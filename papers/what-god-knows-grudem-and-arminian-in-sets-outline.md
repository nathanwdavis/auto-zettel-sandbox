## Before we start: where the two sides come from

Everything below in **Grudem's** picture comes from his own book. Everything in
the **Arminian** picture comes from Grudem quoting an Arminian writer named
Jack Cottrell. Grudem is fair to him — he calls Cottrell's essay "the most
comprehensive and persuasive" of the Arminian essays he read. But we are still
hearing Cottrell through Grudem, not reading Cottrell himself. Keep that in
mind.

## Grudem's three-part list

Grudem defines God's knowledge in one sentence:

> "God fully knows himself and all things actual and possible in one simple and eternal act."

That sentence lists three things God knows. Let's give each one a letter so we
can talk about them.

- **S** — God knows him**s**elf.
- **A** — God knows **all things actual**. Grudem explains this as "all things
  that exist and all things that happen." So: everything that is real, and
  everything that really happens.
- **P** — God knows **all things possible**. Grudem explains this as "events
  that might happen but that do not actually come to pass." So: things that
  *could* happen but never do.

Put all three together and you get **everything God knows**. We'll call that
**K**:

> **K = S ∪ A ∪ P**  (everything God knows = himself, plus what's actual, plus
> what's possible)

We use ∪ here, not ⊎, because the groups can overlap: God himself exists, so he
is in S and also among "all things that exist" in A.

He also says *how* God knows all this: in "one simple and eternal act."
**Simple** here does not mean easy. Grudem says it is "used in the sense 'not
divided into parts.'" He means God knows everything at once, all the time. God
never has to stop and figure something out, and he "never learns and never
forgets anything."

## Two more labels we need

Grudem has a word for one of these. The other is just a label for this guide.

- **D** — the things God **ordains**. *Ordains* is Grudem's word. In this guide
  it means God decides ahead of time that something will happen and makes it
  certain. God knowing about something, or allowing it, is not enough to put it
  in D.
- **F** — people's **future free choices**. This is the part everybody argues
  about. Grudem describes the kind of freedom Arminians want as choices that are
  "undetermined or uncaused" — meaning nothing outside the person makes the
  choice happen.

F is part of A, because your future choices are things that really happen:

> **F ⊆ A**  (your future free choices are part of "all things actual")

## Grudem's picture

For Grudem, God decides everything that happens. He writes that God "ordains
all that we do, and (2) we exercise our personal will and make real, voluntary
choices." He holds both of those at the same time.

> **A ⊆ D**  (everything that actually happens is something God ordained)

Now, where does God's knowledge *come from* in this picture? Two places, and
both of them are God himself:

- God knows what is **possible** because he knows himself. Grudem: "If God
  fully knows himself, he knows everything he is able to do, which includes all
  things that are possible."
- God knows what is **actual** because he decided it.

That is the key thing about Grudem's picture: **nothing God knows depends on
us.** All of it traces back to who God is or what God decided.

## The Arminian picture

Here is the part students usually get wrong, so read it twice.

**The Arminian does NOT say God knows less.** God still knows the whole future,
including every free choice. Cottrell, quoted by Grudem, says God "has a true
foreknowledge of future free-will choices without himself being the agent that
causes them or renders them certain." Translation: God really does see your
future choices ahead of time — he just isn't the one making them happen.

So A has all the same things in it. What changes is that A gets **split into
two piles**:

> **A = D ⊎ F**  (what happens = what God ordained, plus free choices he did
> not ordain)

**Careful: outside D does not mean outside God's plan.** Cottrell's own essay
says: "Man's free acts are included in God's decree, but are not determined by
it. God's decree is all-inclusive, but not all-determinative." So for the
Arminian, free choices are still part of God's plan: he knows them, allows them,
and sets their limits. They sit outside **D** only because D in this guide holds
what God *determines*. Grudem's summary of the Arminian view leaves this out, so
read **A = D ⊎ F** as a claim about what God determines, not about everything
his plan includes.

Cottrell also shrinks D. He says God "does not have a specific, unconditional
purpose for each discrete particle, object, person, and event within the
creation." Plain English: God does not have a specific plan for every single
thing.

And now God's knowledge of F comes from **us**, not from God. God looks ahead
and sees what we will freely choose.

## The whole difference, in one line

> **Grudem:** A ⊆ D — everything traces back to God.
> **Arminian:** A = D ⊎ F — one pile traces back to us instead.

Notice what the argument is *not* about. It is **not** about how much God
knows. Both sides say God knows the entire future. The argument is about
whether what happens splits into two piles, and whether any part of what God
knows depends on people instead of on God.

## The argument about "all things possible"

Some Arminians (called **Molinists**, after a writer named Molina) say there is
a special kind of knowledge in between: God knows what you *would* choose in
any situation he might put you in. They call it **middle knowledge**.

Grudem agrees God has this knowledge. He writes: "God does have this kind of
knowledge, whether we call it 'middle knowledge' or something else." And he
says the fight isn't really about that: "the debate at this point is not about
whether God knows how people would respond if various kinds of circumstances
came about."

What Grudem denies is that it is a *separate* kind. Quoting John Frame, he says
"such knowledge is already part of God's necessary knowledge, or what Craig
calls natural knowledge." In our letters: there is no new box between P and A.
That knowledge was already sitting inside **P** the whole time.

## Open theism: the one view that really does shrink what God knows

**Open theism** is a different view, and it is the only one here that says God
knows *less*. Richard Rice, quoted by Grudem: "All that God does not know is the
content of future free decisions, and this is because decisions are not there to
know until they occur."

Up to now, **K** has meant everything God knows if he knows the whole future,
which is what Grudem and the Arminian both say. Open theism's God knows a smaller
set, so give it its own name:

> **K_open = K \ F**  (open theism's God knows everything in K *except* future
> free choices)

## Grudem's argument that the Arminian picture falls apart

Grudem's main objection is not that the Arminian is unbiblical here. It is that
the Arminian picture doesn't hold together.

His reasoning: once a choice is known ahead of time, it is locked in — no matter
*why* it is known. He writes, "even if God did not plan or cause things to
happen, the fact that they are foreknown means that they will certainly come
about." And then: "If our future choices are known, then they are fixed. And if
they are fixed, then they are not 'free' in the Arminian sense (undetermined or
uncaused)."

If Grudem is right, splitting A into two piles gains the Arminian nothing. F is
locked in just by being known at all. So the only way to keep choices truly
undetermined is to take them *out* of what God knows — and that is open theism.
This is why Grudem says open theism "seems to me to be the most consistent
Arminian position." He is not agreeing with open theism. He is saying it is the
version that at least holds together.

## A second way to slice the same knowledge: what God has shown us

So far we have sorted God's knowledge by **what it is about** — himself, what is
actual, what is possible. Now we sort the *very same knowledge* a different way:
**has God told us about it, or not?**

Two new sets:

- **R** — everything God has **revealed** to human beings.
- **H** — everything **hidden** from us: things God knows that he has not shown
  us.

The Bible states the idea in one verse, which Grudem builds on in chapter 13:

> "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are
> revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the
> words of this law." — Deuteronomy 29:29 (KJV)

### Three rules that connect R and H to K

1. **R ⊆ K.** God can only reveal what he knows. Nothing is revealed that God
   doesn't know.
2. **H ⊆ K.** "Hidden" means God knows it and we don't. If God doesn't know
   something either, it isn't *hidden* — it's just unknown.
3. **K = R ⊎ H.** Every single thing God knows is either revealed or hidden.
   Never both, never neither. (That also means **R ∩ H = ∅** — nothing is in
   both piles.)

Put rules 1 and 3 together and you get a handy shortcut: **H = K \ R**. Hidden is
everything God knows, take away what he has told us.

### The big idea: this slice cuts *across* the first one

Here is the most important thing in this section. R and H do **not** line up
with S, A, and P. The line between "revealed" and "hidden" runs right *through*
every one of Grudem's categories. Each one is partly revealed and partly hidden.

| | Revealed to us (in R) | Hidden from us (in H) |
|---|---|---|
| **S** — God himself | **Some.** God tells us who he is in the Bible — that is what Grudem's whole book is built on. | **Always some, and it can never all be shown to us.** "only he who is infinite can fully know himself in every detail." We are not infinite. |
| **A** — what actually happens | **Some.** Prophecy tells us what will happen. Steven C. Roy, reported by Grudem, "has compiled a list of 4,017 biblical prophecies, of which 2,323 relate to free human decisions." | **The rest of the future**, and much of the past. |
| **P** — what could happen but doesn't | **Some.** God told David what the men of Keilah would do, and Jesus said Tyre and Sidon "would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes." | **Most.** All the "other variations or kinds of animals and fish and other things that God could have created but did not." |
| **D** — what God ordains | **Some.** When prophecy tells us what God has decided will happen. | **Most.** Grudem says God's secret will "includes most of his hidden decrees by which he governs the universe and determines everything that will happen." |

In set language, every row says the same thing:

> **S ∩ R ≠ ∅** and **S ∩ H ≠ ∅**
> **A ∩ R ≠ ∅** and **A ∩ H ≠ ∅**
> **P ∩ R ≠ ∅** and **P ∩ H ≠ ∅**

In plain English: **none of Grudem's categories is all revealed, and none is all
hidden.** The first slice (what knowledge is *about*) and the second slice
(whether we've been *told*) are two different ways of cutting the same cake.

### Careful: Grudem's "revealed will" is smaller than R

In chapter 13, Grudem splits God's *will* into a "secret will" and a "revealed
will." It is easy to think his "revealed will" means the same as R. It doesn't.
He says: "Because God's revealed will usually contains his commands or
'precepts' for our moral conduct, God's revealed will is sometimes also called
God's will of precept or will of command."

So his **revealed will** is mostly *what God tells us to do*. But **R** is
*everything* God has shown us — including facts about himself and prophecies
about the future. His revealed will is only one part of R.

### What R and H do to the three views

**Grudem and the Arminian agree about R and H.** Both say God knows the whole
future, so K is the same size for both. Future free choices God hasn't revealed
are simply *hidden* — God knows them, we don't.

**Open theism changes the picture.** Its God knows only **K_open**, which has no
future free choices in it. The three rules then hold with K_open in place of K,
and since H ⊆ K_open (rule 2), future free choices are **not hidden at all**. They are unknown to
*everyone* — God included.

This is where Grudem's strongest argument against open theism lives, and sets
make it easy to see:

1. Rule 1, for open theism, says **R ⊆ K_open** — whatever God reveals, God knows.
2. Prophecy puts some future free choices into R: **F ∩ R ≠ ∅**.
3. So some future free choices must be in K_open.
4. But K_open has **no** future free choices in it: **F ∩ K_open = ∅**.

Steps 3 and 4 can't both be true. That is Grudem's point when he says open
theism goes against the Bible's "unfailing predictive prophecy." According to
Grudem, open theism answers by treating those prophecies as God's best guess —
"what will most likely occur." Grudem rejects that: Scripture treats them as
"God's firm declarations of what will certainly occur."

## Summary table

| | How A and D relate | Where God's knowledge of F comes from | Is "middle knowledge" its own category? | Does God know the whole future? | Are future free choices hidden from us? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Grudem** | A ⊆ D | from God's own decision | No — it is already inside P | Yes | Mostly hidden; some revealed through prophecy |
| **Arminian** | A = D ⊎ F | from us | Yes, if they are Molinists | Yes | Same as Grudem |
| **Open theism** | Not all of A is in D: future free choices are not | God doesn't know F | Does not apply | No | Not hidden — unknown to everyone, even God |

Two of the three rows say God knows the whole future. The real split between
Grudem and the Arminian is the second column: does any part of what God knows
depend on us? And the revealed/hidden slice cuts across all of it the same way
for both.

## Legend: the set symbols

A **set** is just a collection of things. Here the sets are collections of
things God knows.

| Symbol | Say it out loud | What it means | Example from above |
|---|---|---|---|
| **⊆** | *is a subset of* | Everything in the first group is also in the second group. The two may be equal. | **R ⊆ K** — everything revealed is something God knows |
| **∪** | *union* | Two groups joined together. They *may* overlap. | **K = S ∪ A ∪ P** — God himself exists, so S and A can overlap |
| **⊎** | *split into* (disjoint union) | Two groups joined together, with **nothing in both**. They don't overlap. | **K = R ⊎ H** — everything God knows is revealed or hidden, never both |
| **∩** | *intersect*, or *overlap* | Only the things that are in **both** groups. | **P ∩ R** — possible things God has revealed, like Keilah |
| **\\** | *minus*, or *take away* | Everything in the first group except what's in the second. | **H = K \ R** — hidden is what God knows, minus what he's revealed |
| **∅** | *the empty set* | A group with nothing in it at all. | **R ∩ H = ∅** — nothing is both revealed and hidden |
| **≠ ∅** | *is not empty* | The group has at least one thing in it. | **S ∩ H ≠ ∅** — at least some of God's self-knowledge is hidden from us |
| **⊂** | *is a proper subset of* | Like ⊆, but the first group is definitely *smaller* — it can't be equal. | not used above |

**Reading the letters:**
**K** = everything God knows, if he knows the whole future · **S** = God knows himself · **A** = all things
actual · **P** = all things possible · **D** = what God ordains (decides and makes certain) · **K_open** = what open
theism says God knows · **F** = future
free choices · **R** = what God has revealed to us · **H** = what is hidden from us
