# A Taxonomy of Knowledge: Hidden Knowledge, Middle Knowledge, and Where Human Knowledge Sits — Paper Outline

## What this document is

An outline, dated 2026-09-21, for a short paper that sets out what this
repository knows about two ideas and puts them on one map: the owner's account
of God's hidden knowledge, and Molina's middle knowledge. It is descriptive.
Every point rests on notes already on file; each link opens the note, whose
"Grounding" line names the captured text in `raw/` to quote from. Nothing
below is researched afresh.

## The thesis, in one paragraph

Everything God knows can be cut two ways. The scholastic cut sorts God's
knowledge by its *ground*: what he knows by his nature, what he knows by his
will, and (for Molina) a middle class he knows by neither. The owner's cut
sorts the same knowledge by *disclosure*: what God has made known to creatures
and what he has not, with the undisclosed part divided again by whether we can
even ask the question. Human knowledge is the revealed slice of the second cut
plus what creatures know of themselves and the world; and middle knowledge,
placed on the second cut, turns out to be a known unknown whose very existence
is contested.

## I. The whole: God's knowledge

- Omniscience is the outer set. Grudem's definition, knowledge of "himself and
  all things actual and possible in one simple and eternal act", denies stages
  in the knowing, not parts in the being
  ([[grudems-simple-act-of-knowing-denies-process-not-composition--202609061920]]).
- For Maimonides God's knowledge is identical with his essence, which is what
  dissolves the foreknowledge problem for him; Grudem holds simplicity and
  never uses it that way
  ([[simplicity-is-maimonides-solvent-and-grudem-never-uses-it--202609081915]]).
- Nothing in the hidden tier is hidden *from God*: hiddenness is a fact about
  the creature's share
  ([[the-knowledge-of-god-divides-into-what-he-has-revealed-and--202609151913]]).

## II. The first cut, by ground: natural, middle, free

- Natural knowledge is what God knows by his essence, necessary truths and
  every possibility; free knowledge is what he knows by his will, what will
  actually be. Middle knowledge shares one property with each: it precedes the
  divine will yet its content could have been otherwise
  ([[middle-knowledge-sits-between-natural-and-free-knowledge-because-it-is-prevolitional-yet-contingent--202609090100]]).
- Molina reverses the direction of dependence: God foreknows the free act
  because the creature would do it, not the reverse
  ([[molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101]]).
  Providence then works by choosing a whole order of things, at the cost of
  conceding worlds God cannot actualize
  ([[weak-actualization-claims-meticulous-providence-without-determination-and-concedes-infeasible-worlds--202609090103]]).
- The middle cell is contested. The Reformed reply grounds counterfactuals in
  the decree, so that a counterfactual derived from nature and circumstance is
  natural or free knowledge but never middle
  ([[a-counterfactual-derived-from-nature-and-circumstance-is-natural-or-free-knowledge-but-not-middle--202609111821]],
  [[the-reformed-answer-to-the-grounding-objection-is-the-decree-and-the-confession-runs-opposite-to-molina--202609111825]]).
  Grudem grounds knowledge of the possible in God's self-knowledge, which
  reaches what God could do but not, without a further step, what free
  creatures would do
  ([[grudem-grounds-knowledge-of-the-possible-in-self-knowledge-without-separating-what-creatures-would-do--202609061923]]).

## III. The second cut, by disclosure: revealed and hidden

- The owner's account: a revealed subset, a hidden remainder, and the
  remainder split into known unknowns (a question held, the answer absent or
  partial) and unknown unknowns (neither question nor answer)
  ([[the-knowledge-of-god-divides-into-what-he-has-revealed-and--202609151913]]).
- The anchor verse, Deut. 29:29, licenses the outer partition and nothing
  below it, and only on the Christian reading: the Talmud and five Jewish
  commentators read it as a partition of liability for sins
  ([[deuteronomy-29-29-anchors-the-hidden-knowledge-partition--202609151914]],
  [[five-jewish-witnesses-read-deuteronomy-29-28-as-a-partition--202609182340]]).
- Three postures toward the hidden: Calvin fences it and forbids neglect of
  the revealed; Luther declares it nothing to us; Dionysius approaches it by
  subtraction, without disclosure
  ([[calvin-and-luther-both-make-the-hidden-a-known-unknown-with--202609151916]],
  [[for-dionysius-the-hidden-god-is-reached-by-subtraction-so--202609151917]]).
- The boundary moves: in Pauline usage a mystery is what has crossed from
  hidden to revealed
  ([[in-pauline-usage-a-mystery-is-a-thing-that-has-crossed-from--202609151919]]).

## IV. Where human knowledge sits

- **Revealed knowledge of God**, by two channels. General revelation is thin
  on both its routes, power and deity enough for inexcusability
  ([[natural-knowledge-of-god-is-thin-on-both-routes-for-the-same-reason--202609011042]]);
  special revelation adds what reason cannot reach. Aquinas ranks the rungs,
  natural reason, grace, the beatific vision, each bounded by the knower's
  mode rather than by a withheld portion
  ([[aquinas-bounds-every-knowledge-of-god-below-god-s-own-by--202609151915]]).
- **Ordinary knowledge of creatures and of oneself.** Natural knowledge begins
  from sense (the same Aquinas note). Ramban's reading adds a human-side
  hidden/revealed line: sins hidden from the sinner himself against conscious
  ones, the one Jewish reading of the verse that turns on knowledge, with the
  human as the ignorant party
  ([[five-jewish-witnesses-read-deuteronomy-29-28-as-a-partition--202609182340]]).
- **Four human cells, not three.** Known knowns (the two classes above), known
  unknowns, unknown unknowns, and the false known, the thing believed
  revealed that was not, which Socrates ranks below both unknowns and the
  owner's account lacks
  ([[known-unknowns-can-be-inventoried-and-unknown-unknowns-only--202609151918]]).

## V. Where the two cuts cross: middle knowledge on the disclosure map

- Some middle knowledge is revealed: Keilah, and Tyre and Sidon, are
  scriptural instances of counterfactual knowledge, read in opposite
  directions by Grudem and Molina
  ([[grudem-and-molina-both-argue-from-tyre-and-sidon-and-read-it-in-opposite-directions--202609090105]]).
- The class as a whole is hidden, and its ground is a known unknown: the
  grounding objection is Molinism's own unanswered question, and each reply
  relocates it
  ([[the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104]]).
- Every position concedes such a residue, placed where the other side's
  evidence would land
  ([[the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914]]);
  these residues are the base's inventory of known unknowns.
- The floor of the hidden tier: even the blessed do not see future contingents
  or secret thoughts, which "belongs to God alone"
  ([[aquinas-bounds-every-knowledge-of-god-below-god-s-own-by--202609151915]]).
  The contingent future is precisely the object of free and middle knowledge,
  so the first cut's contested cells are the second cut's permanent remainder.
