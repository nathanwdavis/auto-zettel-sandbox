# Divine Sovereignty and Human Free Will: Paper Outline

**Course:** Systematic Theology (evangelical). **Length:** about 4 pages double-spaced,
roughly 1,100-1,300 words. **Citation style assumed:** Turabian notes-bibliography
(the seminary default; check the syllabus). **This version:** 2026-09-09, refined to the
debate between Grudem's concurrence and Molina's middle knowledge. The earlier, broader
survey version (Arminianism, open theism, the confessions, Maimonides) is in this file's
git history before this date; pull material back from it only if the page budget allows.

**Thesis.** God's sovereign will and human free will coexist, and both are true doctrines.
The two best evangelical-compatible accounts of *how* they coexist, Grudem's concurrence
and Molina's middle knowledge, agree on exactly that and disagree only about the mechanism.
Their disagreement comes down to one thing: the direction of dependence between God's
knowing and the creature's act. Held with the posture Packer calls antinomy, concurrence
carries the doctrine at the lower cost.

**Why the debate format serves the thesis.** A paper that surveys every position spends its
four pages describing. A paper that sets the two most sovereignty-affirming mechanisms
against each other can *show* the thesis: both keep both doctrines whole, so the thesis is
the common ground of the debate, not one party's claim. Grudem's chapter 16 treats
Molinism as "a third Arminian viewpoint"; the paper's first move is to show that on the
thesis itself Molina stands with Grudem, not against him.

## I. Introduction: two mechanisms, one doctrine (about 150 words)

- **Hook.** Acts 2:23 puts both truths in one sentence, the "definite plan and foreknowledge
  of God" and "the hands of lawless men," and Grudem uses exactly this verse to fence his
  own doctrine: "we must never understand it to imply that we are freed from responsibility
  for evil or that God is ever to be blamed for sin. Scripture never speaks that way, and we
  may not either, even though how this can be so may remain a mystery for us in this age."
  **[repo]** [Grudem splits God's will into necessary and free, then secret and revealed](../literature/grudem-splits-gods-will-into-necessary-free-secret-and-revealed--202609081904.md).
- **Thesis** (above), stated as the common ground of the two positions the paper compares.
- **The two parties, in one sentence each.** Grudem: every event is "fully (100 percent)
  caused by God and fully (100 percent) caused by the creature as well," God as primary
  cause and the creature as secondary **[repo]** [Concurrence is the mechanism](../permanent/concurrence-is-the-mechanism-the-wisdom-section-assumed--202609081826.md).
  Molina: God chooses which order of things to create, knowing by a "middle knowledge"
  what every free creature would do in it. In the same decision, in the repository's
  rendering, he decided to cooperate in this or that way with their free will ("simul
  statuit hoc vel illo modo cum libero arbitrio illorum cooperari") **[repo]** [Molina distinguishes three knowledges](../literature/molina-distinguishes-three-knowledges-and-makes-the-middle-one-depend-on-the-creature--202609090055.md).
- **The evangelical stake.** Molinism is not a Catholic curiosity; Craig and Keathley
  defend it inside evangelicalism, and Grudem answers it in the same chapter that answers
  Arminianism **[to verify]** Craig, *The Only Wise God*; Keathley, *Salvation and
  Sovereignty*; **[repo]** [Grudem answers the Arminian by comparing unanswered questions](../literature/grudem-answers-the-arminian-by-comparing-unanswered-questions--202609081825.md).

## II. What both sides affirm: the thesis is the common ground (about 220 words)

Four agreements, each with one witness from each side. This section carries the thesis.

- **Exhaustive foreknowledge, proved from prophecy.** Grudem rests on Isaiah 40-48, where
  God makes declaring the future his own test of deity (Isa 46:9-10) **[repo]** [Grudem answers open theism with Isaiah's test of deity](../permanent/grudem-answers-open-theism-with-isaiahs-test-of-deity-not-with-the-definition-of-omniscience--202609061921.md).
  Molina, one paragraph before defining middle knowledge, argues the same way: without
  foreknowledge of future contingents "perit prophetia," prophecy perishes and the greater
  part of Scripture collapses. **[repo]**, page image only: the sentence is on
  `raw/202609090050-molina-p317-leaf334.jpg` just above the transcribed passage; it is not in
  the transcription, so read it from the image before citing.
- **Meticulous providence.** Grudem: God "works all things according to the counsel of his
  will," and "No event in creation falls outside of his providence" (Eph 1:11) **[repo]**
  [202609081826](../permanent/concurrence-is-the-mechanism-the-wisdom-section-assumed--202609081826.md).
  Molina: "Nihil esse in potestate creaturae, quod etiam non sit in potestate Dei," nothing
  is in the creature's power that is not also in God's; God can bend the will "quocunque
  ipse voluerit, praeterquam in peccatum," anywhere except into sin **[repo]** [Molina's own list of what coheres keeps sovereignty whole](../permanent/molinas-own-list-of-what-coheres-keeps-sovereignty-whole--202609090102.md).
- **God is not the author of sin.** Grudem's Acts 2:23 fence (section I). Molina: "Ne tamen
  intelligas causam ullo modo esse peccatorum," God is in no way the cause of sins; he may
  permit them, "non vero praecipere, aut ad ea incitare vel inclinare," never command or
  incite them **[repo]** [202609090102](../permanent/molinas-own-list-of-what-coheres-keeps-sovereignty-whole--202609090102.md).
- **Real human choice.** Grudem: "we make willing choices, choices that have real effects"
  **[repo]** [The freedom Grudem deferred is compatibilist freedom](../permanent/the-freedom-grudem-deferred-is-compatibilist-freedom--202609081827.md).
  Molina: the will could, if it willed, do the opposite ("cum tamen posset, si vellet,
  facere re reipsa oppositum") **[repo]** [Middle knowledge sits between natural and free knowledge](../permanent/middle-knowledge-sits-between-natural-and-free-knowledge-because-it-is-prevolitional-yet-contingent--202609090100.md).
- **Say what this proves.** Both parties hold both doctrines whole. The thesis is therefore
  not a Calvinist claim that a Molinist must reject; it is what the two disagree *inside*.
  Grudem's label "a third Arminian viewpoint" is about the ground of election, not about
  whether Molina keeps sovereignty **[repo]** [202609081825](../literature/grudem-answers-the-arminian-by-comparing-unanswered-questions--202609081825.md).

## III. Where they part: the direction of dependence (about 320 words)

One difference, shown three ways. This is the paper's analytical core.

- **The kind of freedom.** Grudem keeps Ware's "freedom of inclination": "we are free when
  we choose according to our strongest inclination or deepest desire," a freedom that "does
  not rule out the idea that our choices may ultimately be determined by God in ways
  invisible to us," which his footnote calls "compatibilist freedom." Molina requires the
  libertarian kind Grudem quotes and declines, "the ability, all things being just what they
  are, to choose differently" **[repo]** [202609081827](../permanent/the-freedom-grudem-deferred-is-compatibilist-freedom--202609081827.md);
  the IEP: "God cannot will a free creature to act in a particular way and the act still be
  free" **[repo]** [Laing defines middle knowledge](../literature/laing-defines-middle-knowledge-as-prevolitional-knowledge-of-counterfactuals-of-freedom--202609090056.md).
- **The order of God's knowing and willing.** Grudem: "If our future choices are known,
  then they are fixed," and the decree is what makes them known; foreknowledge follows
  foreordination **[repo]** [The foreknowledge entailment is a weapon in chapter 16](../permanent/the-foreknowledge-entailment-is-a-weapon-in-chapter-16--202609081829.md).
  Molina: three knowledges, natural (what could be), middle (what each free will *would* do
  in any order of things, prior to God's decree yet contingent), free (what will be, after
  the decree); and the marginal gloss that states the reversal outright, "Futura contingentia
  non quia sciuntur a Deo ideo futura sunt, sed e contrario," future contingents are not
  future because God knows them, but the reverse. God foreknows the free act because the
  creature would do it, "et non e contrario" **[repo]** [Molina reverses the direction of dependence](../permanent/molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101.md).
  This is the whole disagreement in one line: for Grudem the decree fixes what God knows;
  for Molina what the creature would do fixes what God knows, and the decree comes after.
- **The verse where it shows: Tyre and Sidon (Matt 11:21).** Both cite it. Grudem cites it
  in chapter 12 to prove God knows "all things possible," then, through Frame, folds that
  knowledge into God's natural knowledge of his own power, so middle knowledge is
  "redundant." Molina cites it to prove the opposite: Christ says Tyre and Sidon *would have*
  repented, a conditional future that is more than a possibility, and the opponents who
  read it as mere possibility refuse, in the repository's rendering, to concede a middle
  between the simply future and the merely possible, and this in order to escape middle
  knowledge ("Renuunt namque concedere medium inter futurum simpliciter et possibile merum,
  idque ut mediam scientiam effugiant") **[repo]** [Grudem and Molina both argue from Tyre and Sidon](../permanent/grudem-and-molina-both-argue-from-tyre-and-sidon-and-read-it-in-opposite-directions--202609090105.md).
  One sentence on Gen 50:20 and Acts 4:27-28: both parties can read them (decree plus
  secondary cause; chosen order plus cooperation), which is why the conjunction texts do not
  decide the debate and this verse does.

## IV. Each side's objection, each side's residue (about 300 words)

Two objections per side, then what each side admits it cannot answer. Keep the ones that
survive the repository's own checks.

- **Grudem against Molina, kept.** (1) The entailment: if foreknown then fixed, and a fixed
  choice is not free "in the Arminian sense (undetermined or uncaused)"; Molina's reply is
  the reversal above, so the dispute is whether God's knowledge can depend on what the
  creature would do **[repo]** [202609081829](../permanent/the-foreknowledge-entailment-is-a-weapon-in-chapter-16--202609081829.md),
  [202609090101](../permanent/molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101.md).
  (2) Feasibility: because middle knowledge is prior to God's will, "He has no control over
  it," and the IEP concedes in its own voice that "there may be some possible worlds that
  God cannot actualize." That is the honest form of Grudem's charge that the rival residue
  "tends to diminish the greatness of God—his omniscience, his omnipotence, and the
  absolute reliability of his promises" **[repo]** [Weak actualization claims meticulous providence and concedes infeasible worlds](../permanent/weak-actualization-claims-meticulous-providence-without-determination-and-concedes-infeasible-worlds--202609090103.md).
- **Grudem against Molina, retired.** Do not use "every situation in which people find themselves is itself the result of
  thousands, in fact millions, of individual human choices." It touches a God who
  assembles circumstances piecemeal; Molina's God chooses a whole order of things and
  foresees the millions of choices inside it. Cite the objection only to set it aside
  **[repo]** [A situation is made of other people's choices](../permanent/a-situation-is-made-of-other-peoples-choices--202609081830.md)
  (amended 2026-09-09 from the Molinist side).
- **Molina against Grudem.** (1) A choice God causes is not a free choice, however
  willing: the IEP's first assumption, and Pohle's report of the Thomist-Molinist charge
  that decrees which determine the will "must logically and necessarily destroy freedom
  and lead to Determinism" **[repo]** [Pohle locates the dispute in the medium of foreknowledge](../literature/pohle-locates-the-molinist-thomist-dispute-in-the-medium-of-foreknowledge--202609090057.md).
  Grudem's reply is a question, "Where does Scripture say that a choice ordained by God is
  not a real choice?" **[repo]** [202609081825](../literature/grudem-answers-the-arminian-by-comparing-unanswered-questions--202609081825.md).
  (2) The coherence point: "proponents of compatibilist freedom must believe that God
  possesses libertarian freedom in order to avoid theological fatalism," so the concept
  Grudem declines for creatures he needs for God **[repo]** [202609090056](../literature/laing-defines-middle-knowledge-as-prevolitional-knowledge-of-counterfactuals-of-freedom--202609090056.md).
- **The residues, side by side.** Grudem names his: "Exactly how can God cause us to
  choose something willingly?" and "Exactly how can God not be blamed for evil when he
  ordains that we do evil willingly?" **[repo]** [The dispute is decided by which residue honours God more](../permanent/the-dispute-is-decided-by-which-residue-honours-god-more--202609081831.md).
  Molina's is the grounding objection, what makes a counterfactual of freedom true before
  the creature exists, and his own word for the faculty that knows it is "inscrutabili";
  each of the five Molinist replies relocates the question rather than removing it
  **[repo]** [The grounding objection is Molinism's own unanswered question](../permanent/the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104.md).
  The point for the thesis: both mechanisms end in an admitted mystery at the same joint,
  the place where God's knowing meets the creature's choosing; they differ on which side of
  the joint the mystery sits.

## V. Verdict, and the posture both sides need (about 220 words)

- **The verdict this outline recommends.** Concurrence carries the doctrine at the lower
  cost. Its residue is about the *manner* of God's causing (how a caused choice is willing);
  Molina's is about the *reach* of God's knowledge into what he does not author, and it
  buys libertarian freedom with worlds God cannot have. The scriptural order runs Grudem's
  way: Isa 46:10 grounds "declaring the end from the beginning" in "my counsel shall stand,
  and I will accomplish all my purpose," foreknowledge in purpose, not purpose in
  foreknowledge; and Acts 4:28 says the actors did what God's "plan had predestined."
  **[repo]** [202609061921](../permanent/grudem-answers-open-theism-with-isaiahs-test-of-deity-not-with-the-definition-of-omniscience--202609061921.md);
  [202609081904](../literature/grudem-splits-gods-will-into-necessary-free-secret-and-revealed--202609081904.md).
  (Isa 46:10b and the Acts 4:28 wording are not captured in the repository; cite them from
  your Bible.)
- **An alternative verdict, if you prefer it.** Treat the 1607 decree as the model: the
  Congregatio de Auxiliis, after "eighty-five conferences in the presence of the popes,"
  ended with a decree that "allowed each party to defend its own doctrine, enjoined each
  from censoring or condemning the opposite opinion," and reserved judgement. Both doctrines
  required, either mechanism permitted **[repo]** [The Congregatio de Auxiliis ended by permitting both schools](../permanent/the-congregatio-de-auxiliis-ended-by-permitting-both-schools-and-condemning-neither--202609090106.md).
  Either verdict supports the thesis; the first commits you to a mechanism, the second only
  to the pair.
- **Why the tie-breaker must be handled with care.** Grudem decides between the two
  residues by which leaves God greater. The repository's own reading is that this is a
  doxological tie-breaker stated once and undefended, not his argument, and that the
  Molinist would say the framing assumes what is at issue. Cite it as his closing
  judgement, not as a proof **[repo]** [202609081831](../permanent/the-dispute-is-decided-by-which-residue-honours-god-more--202609081831.md).
- **Antinomy.** Packer: an antinomy is "an appearance of contradiction between conclusions
  which seem equally logical, reasonable or necessary," to be distinguished from a paradox
  **[second-hand]** *Evangelism and the Sovereignty of God* (IVP, 1961), 18-20. Carson's
  two propositions: God "is absolutely sovereign, but his sovereignty never functions in
  Scripture to reduce human responsibility," and humans "are responsible creatures ... but
  human responsibility never functions in Scripture to diminish God's sovereignty"
  **[second-hand]** *How Long, O Lord?* (Baker, 1990), ch. 11. Both mechanisms need this
  posture at their residue; the paper's thesis is what the posture protects.

## VI. Conclusion (about 90 words)

- Restate: the two mechanisms disagree about the direction of dependence and agree about
  the doctrine; their agreement is the thesis, their residues are the reason for antinomy.
- Close on Grudem's fence (Acts 2:23) and Molina's "praeterquam in peccatum": from opposite
  mechanisms, the same two refusals, that sovereignty never excuses the sinner and never
  implicates God.

**Cut list if over length**, in order: the Isa 46:10 sentence in V; the "coherence point"
in IV; the alternative verdict in V; the Gen 50:20 / Acts 4:28 sentence in III; the
"evangelical stake" bullet in I.

## Connections this repository makes that no single source makes

1. **The thesis is the common ground of the debate.** Grudem and Molina each affirm all
   four of section II's agreements in their own words; neither notices the other doing so.
2. **The dependence reversal is the whole disagreement.** Molina's "non e contrario" is
   aimed at exactly the step Grudem's foreknowledge-entails-fixity argument asserts and does
   not defend ([202609090101](../permanent/molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101.md)).
3. **One verse, opposite directions.** Tyre and Sidon is where the two mechanisms visibly
   part ([202609090105](../permanent/grudem-and-molina-both-argue-from-tyre-and-sidon-and-read-it-in-opposite-directions--202609090105.md)).
4. **Same joint, different side.** Each mechanism admits a mystery where God's knowing meets
   the creature's choosing; Grudem's is about the manner of causing, Molina's about the
   grounding of what is known
   ([202609081914](../permanent/the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914.md),
   [202609090104](../permanent/the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104.md)).
5. **Both argue foreknowledge from prophecy.** Grudem's Isaiah test and Molina's "perit
   prophetia" are the same argument from opposite sides of the Reformation; the Molina
   sentence is on the committed page image and not yet transcribed.
