# Divine Sovereignty and Human Free Will: Paper Outline

**Course:** Systematic Theology (evangelical). **Length:** about 4 pages double-spaced,
roughly 1,100-1,300 words. **Citation style assumed:** Turabian notes-bibliography
(the seminary default; check the syllabus). **Written:** 2026-09-08, on branch
`claude/trusting-einstein-v2es9x`.

**Thesis.** Scripture teaches, often in a single sentence, both that God ordains
whatsoever comes to pass and that human beings make real choices for which they are
responsible. Neither doctrine qualifies the other. The evangelical doctrine of
concurrence shows how both can be true at once, and where explanation runs out the
right response is antinomy, holding both, rather than surrendering either.

**Position the paper defends.** Concurrence plus antinomy: Grudem, Ware, Frame and
Calvin on the mechanism; Packer, Carson and Spurgeon on the posture. Section IV gives
the rival mechanisms a fair hearing in one paragraph each.

**Revision 2026-09-09: the Molinist leg, and what it does to the thesis.** The
repository now holds Molina's *Concordia* in his own Latin (disp. 52-53), the IEP
survey of middle knowledge, and the Catholic Encyclopedia's history of the dispute, all
`[repo]`; see the MOC section "Middle knowledge, added 2026-09-09". Five consequences
for the paper:

1. Molinism is itself a "both are true" position. It affirms meticulous providence (God
   chooses a whole order of things knowing every free act in it) and libertarian
   freedom, and Molina's own list of what "coheres" says that nothing is in the
   creature's power that is not in God's. The thesis is therefore shared across camps.
   The paper must argue that concurrence plus antinomy is the better *mechanism* for
   holding both, not that it is the only home of the thesis.
2. The decisive difference from Grudem is the order of dependence, not the amount of
   sovereignty. Grudem: foreknown, therefore fixed. Molina: God foreknows the free act
   because the creature would do it, "et non e contrario." Section III should name this.
3. Grudem's "a situation is made of other people's choices" should carry no weight in
   the paper: it touches only a God who assembles circumstances piecemeal. The honest
   pressure on Molinism is feasibility, which the IEP concedes in its own voice: there
   may be possible worlds God cannot actualize. That is where Grudem's "diminishes God"
   residue actually lands.
4. Molinism pays its own unanswered question, the grounding objection, at the joint
   between God's knowing and the creature's choosing. Section V's claim that every
   serious position leaves a residue now has three witnesses, and the Church's decree of
   1607, permitting both schools and condemning neither, is a historical precedent for
   the antinomy posture.
5. Grudem and Molina both argue from Tyre and Sidon (Matt 11:21) and read it in opposite
   directions; it is the one verse where the two mechanisms visibly part, and the paper
   can use it as such. Word budget unchanged; the survey paragraph gets tighter.

## I. Introduction and thesis (about 150 words)

- **Hook.** Acts 2:23 puts both truths in one sentence: Jesus was "delivered up
  according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God" and killed "by the hands of
  lawless men." Grudem uses exactly this verse to state the limit of his own doctrine:
  "we must never understand it to imply that we are freed from responsibility for evil
  or that God is ever to be blamed for sin. Scripture never speaks that way, and we may
  not either, even though how this can be so may remain a mystery for us in this age."
  **[repo]** [Grudem splits God's will into necessary and free, then secret and revealed](../literature/grudem-splits-gods-will-into-necessary-free-secret-and-revealed--202609081904.md)
  (ch. 13 sec. D; raw `202609081903`).
- **The pair is older than the Reformation debate.** Rabbi Akiva, *Pirkei Avot* 3:15:
  "Everything is foreseen yet freedom of choice is granted." The mishnah states both
  halves and reconciles neither, then turns straight to judgement and works.
  **[repo]** [Akiva states both halves and reconciles neither](../literature/akiva-states-both-halves-and-reconciles-neither--202609081913.md)
  (raw `202609081911`, Hebrew and English). One sentence in the paper, no more; it
  shows the thesis is not a Calvinist novelty.
- **Thesis** (above). Say explicitly what "equally true" means: neither doctrine is
  the exception clause of the other.
- **Define the terms.**
  - Sovereignty = God's decree: "The decrees of God are the eternal plans of God whereby,
    before the creation of the world, he determined to bring about everything that
    happens" (Grudem ch. 16 sec. D). Anchor text Eph 1:11, where Grudem presses
    *energeo* as continual bringing-about. **[repo]** [Grudem defines providence as preservation, concurrence and government](../literature/grudem-defines-providence-as-preservation-concurrence-and-government--202609081824.md).
  - Free will = willing, responsible choice, not independence from God. Grudem: "we are
    nonetheless free in the greatest sense that any creature of God could be free—we
    make willing choices, choices that have real effects." **[repo]** [The freedom Grudem deferred is compatibilist freedom](../permanent/the-freedom-grudem-deferred-is-compatibilist-freedom--202609081827.md).
  - Flag the vocabulary problem in one clause: Calvin would rather not use the term
    "free will" at all because it is misheard as autonomy (*Institutes* II.2.7-8:
    man "acts voluntarily, and not by compulsion"; "I am unwilling to use it myself"),
    and Grudem declines "compatibilism" and "determinism" for the same reason.
    **[verified]** Calvin, *Institutes* II.2.7-8, Beveridge trans., CCEL,
    https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iv.iii.html. **[repo]** [Grudem takes positions whose standard names he declines](../permanent/grudem-takes-positions-whose-standard-names-he-declines--202609081828.md).

## II. The biblical witness: both taught, often in one verse (about 300 words)

Lead with the conjunction texts; they carry the thesis. The two single-doctrine lists
are context, one sentence each.

- **Sovereignty texts** (one sentence, cite three): Eph 1:11; Ps 115:3; Prov 21:1;
  Dan 4:35; Isa 46:9-10 ("declaring the end from the beginning"). **[repo]** lists in
  [202609081904](../literature/grudem-splits-gods-will-into-necessary-free-secret-and-revealed--202609081904.md)
  and [Grudem answers open theism with Isaiah's test of deity](../permanent/grudem-answers-open-theism-with-isaiahs-test-of-deity-not-with-the-definition-of-omniscience--202609061921.md).
- **Responsibility texts** (one sentence): Deut 30:19; Josh 24:15; Ezek 18:30-32;
  Matt 23:37. **Caution from the repository:** a command proves God's precept, not by
  itself the creature's independent capacity. Maimonides reads "I have set before you
  today life" as proof "that the choice is in your hands"; Grudem assigns commands to
  the revealed will, which is compatible with the decree running otherwise, so the
  inference is blocked before it starts. Do not rest the freedom half of the thesis on
  commands alone; rest it on the conjunction texts. **[repo]** [Commandment texts as capacity or as precept](../permanent/commandment-texts-as-capacity-or-as-precept--202609081916.md).
- **Conjunction texts** (the core paragraph; pick four):
  - Gen 50:20, "You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good." One act, two
    intentions, both real. **[repo]** quoted in full in raw `202609081918`
    ([Pharaoh note](../permanent/on-pharaoh-grudem-names-the-jewish-reading-and-refuses-it--202609081917.md)).
  - Acts 2:23 and 4:27-28: Herod, Pilate, Gentiles and Israel did "whatever your hand
    and your plan had predestined to take place"; Grudem presses the plural *hosa* to
    cover "all the detailed events connected with" the death. **[repo]** [202609081904](../literature/grudem-splits-gods-will-into-necessary-free-secret-and-revealed--202609081904.md).
    Carson: Christians "become compatibilists (knowing or otherwise) when they think
    about the cross." **[second-hand]** Carson, *How Long, O Lord?* ch. 11.
  - Isa 10:5-15: Assyria is "the rod of my anger," sent by God, yet judged for the
    intention in its own heart ("he does not so intend"). The cleanest text for two
    agents, two intentions. **[gap]** not captured in the repository; Carson treats it.
  - Phil 2:12-13: "work out your own salvation ... for it is God who works in you, both
    to will and to work." The command and the enabling in one sentence. **[gap]** not in
    the repository.
  - Prov 16:9 ("the heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps") and
    Luke 22:22 ("as it has been determined, but woe to that man") if space allows. **[gap]**.
- **The hard case: Pharaoh** (Ex 4:21; 7:3; 8:15; 9:16; Rom 9:17-18). Grudem's reply to
  the "Pharaoh hardened himself first" objection is chronological: God's promise to
  harden (4:21; 7:3) "is made long before" the first self-hardening (8:15), and
  "even when Pharaoh hardens his own heart, that is not inconsistent with saying that
  God is causing Pharaoh to do this." **[repo]** raw `202609081918`. Be honest about the
  weak spot the repository found: Maimonides grounds Pharaoh's prior guilt in Ex 1:10,
  three chapters before the first hardening promise, so Grudem's dating counts only the
  narrator's hardening formula. Use Pharaoh as an illustration of concurrence, not as a
  chronology proof. **[repo]** [On Pharaoh Grudem names the Jewish reading and refuses it](../permanent/on-pharaoh-grudem-names-the-jewish-reading-and-refuses-it--202609081917.md).

## III. Framework: concurrence and the two wills (about 350 words)

- **Concurrence, primary and secondary causes.** Grudem: "in one sense events are fully
  (100 percent) caused by God and fully (100 percent) caused by the creature as well.
  However, divine and creaturely causes work in different ways." God is "the 'primary
  cause' that plans and initiates everything that happens"; creaturely properties are
  "the 'secondary causes' ... the causes that are evident to us by observation." Not two
  shares of one quantity but two offices. **[repo]** [Concurrence is the mechanism the wisdom section assumed](../permanent/concurrence-is-the-mechanism-the-wisdom-section-assumed--202609081826.md).
  - Confessional anchor, Westminster Confession 3.1: "God from all eternity did by the
    most wise and holy counsel of his own will, freely and unchangeably ordain
    whatsoever comes to pass; yet so as thereby neither is God the author of sin; nor is
    violence offered to the will of the creatures, nor is the liberty or contingency of
    second causes taken away, but rather established." And 5.2: God "ordereth them to
    fall out according to the nature of second causes, either necessarily, freely, or
    contingently." **[verified]** CCEL, https://www.ccel.org/ccel/anonymous/westminster3.i.iii.html
    and https://www.ccel.org/ccel/anonymous/westminster3.i.v.html. This is the single
    best statement of the thesis in one sentence; consider quoting 3.1 in the paper.
  - Classical root (one sentence): Aquinas, God "moves causes both natural and
    voluntary" and "by moving voluntary causes He does not deprive their actions of
    being voluntary: but rather is He the cause of this very thing in them" (ST I q.83
    a.1 ad 3); "God, while moving the will, does not force it, because He gives the will
    its own natural inclination" (I q.105 a.4 ad 1). **[verified]** New Advent,
    https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1083.htm and https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1105.htm.
  - Calvin: providence "consists in action. What many talk of bare prescience is the
    merest trifling" (I.16.4); "nothing happens without his counsel" (I.16.3).
    **[verified]** https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xvii.html.
- **What kind of freedom.** Edwards: "The plain and obvious meaning of the words
  Freedom and Liberty, in common speech, is The power, opportunity, or advantage, that
  any one has, to do as he pleases," "without taking into the meaning of the word, any
  thing of the cause of that choice." **[verified]** *Freedom of the Will* Part I sec. 5,
  CCEL, https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards/will/will.ii.v.html. Ware's "freedom of
  inclination," as Grudem quotes it: "we are free when we choose according to our
  strongest inclination or deepest desire. In short, we are free when we do what we most
  want to do," against libertarian freedom, "the ability, all things being just what
  they are, to choose differently." Grudem's footnote: freedom of inclination "is also
  called 'compatibilist freedom.'" **[repo]** [202609081827](../permanent/the-freedom-grudem-deferred-is-compatibilist-freedom--202609081827.md);
  **[to verify]** Ware, *God's Greater Glory* (Crossway, 2004), ch. 3, for the page.
  - Why libertarian freedom cannot be kept alongside exhaustive foreknowledge: "If our
    future choices are known, then they are fixed. And if they are fixed, then they are
    not 'free' in the Arminian sense (undetermined or uncaused)." Grudem and Pinnock
    accept the same inference and give up opposite sides of it, so the argument is not
    a Calvinist trick; it is common ground. **[repo]** [The foreknowledge entailment is a weapon in chapter 16](../permanent/the-foreknowledge-entailment-is-a-weapon-in-chapter-16--202609081829.md),
    [Grudem and Pinnock accept the same entailment](../permanent/grudem-and-pinnock-accept-the-same-entailment-and-give-up-opposite-sides-of-it--202609061922.md).
    Note the repository's limit: Grudem asserts the fixed-therefore-not-free step and
    does not answer the classical reply that certainty is not necessity. One clause of
    acknowledgement is enough.
  - The point where concurrence and middle knowledge part is the direction of
    dependence. Molina: future contingents are not future because God knows them, "sed
    e contrario"; God foreknows the free act because the creature would do it. Grudem's
    decree runs the arrow the other way. Both cite Tyre and Sidon, Grudem for God's
    knowledge of possibilities, Molina for a conditional future that is more than a
    possibility. **[repo]** [Molina reverses the direction of dependence](../permanent/molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101.md),
    [Grudem and Molina both argue from Tyre and Sidon](../permanent/grudem-and-molina-both-argue-from-tyre-and-sidon-and-read-it-in-opposite-directions--202609090105.md).
- **Two wills: decree and precept.** Grudem's secret will "includes most of his hidden
  decrees by which he governs the universe and determines everything that will
  happen"; his revealed will is "sometimes also called God's will of precept or will of
  command." Joseph's brothers were commanded not to sell him and decreed to do so.
  **[repo]** [Grudem's two wills are sorted from outside the verse](../permanent/grudems-two-wills-are-sorted-from-outside-the-verse--202609081905.md).
  - Caution: Deut 29:29 partitions what is *disclosed*, not what is *willed*; it
    warrants reticence rather than proving two wills. Cite it for the former.
    **[repo]** [Deut 29:29 partitions knowledge and Grudem partitions will](../permanent/deut-29-29-partitions-knowledge-and-grudem-partitions-will--202609081906.md).
  - Calvin already had the distinction: "while in himself the will is one and
    undivided, to us it appears manifold" (I.18.3), and "there is the greatest
    difference between" God's command and his secret will (I.18.4). **[verified]**
    https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xix.html.
  - Standard modern treatment: Piper, "Are There Two Wills in God?" in *Still Sovereign*,
    ed. Schreiner and Ware (Baker, 2000), 107-31: on Pharaoh, "God commands that Pharaoh
    do a thing that God himself wills not to allow" (114). **[second-hand]** via Sam
    Storms's summary; the essay is at desiringgod.org.
- **The asymmetry.** God ordains sinful acts without being the author of sin (Jas 1:13;
  Gen 50:20; Acts 2:23). WCF 5.4: providence extends "even to the first Fall, and all
  other sins of angels and men, and that not by a bare permission ... yet so, as the
  sinfulness thereof proceedeth only from the creature, and not from God." **[verified]**.
  Grudem's own limit, quoted in I above. **[repo]** [202609081904](../literature/grudem-splits-gods-will-into-necessary-free-secret-and-revealed--202609081904.md).

## IV. Brief survey of rival mechanisms (about 200 words)

One paragraph. Each rival keeps both doctrines but re-scales one of them; the thesis
"equally true" is best served by the model that shrinks neither.

- **Classical Arminianism** (Arminius; Wesley; Cottrell; Olson). Libertarian freedom,
  prevenient grace, sovereignty as God's free self-limitation, foreknowledge without
  foreordination. As Grudem reports Cottrell: "God does not have a specific,
  unconditional purpose for each discrete particle, object, person, and event within
  the creation." Pressure: the foreknowledge entailment (III above), and Grudem's
  question, "Where does Scripture say that a choice ordained by God is not a real
  choice?" **[repo]** [Grudem answers the Arminian by comparing unanswered questions](../literature/grudem-answers-the-arminian-by-comparing-unanswered-questions--202609081825.md).
  **[to verify]** Olson, *Arminian Theology: Myths and Realities* (IVP, 2006), for the
  position stated from inside; the repository holds no Arminian primary text.
- **Molinism** (Molina, *Concordia* 1588; Craig; Keathley). Three knowledges in God:
  natural (what could be), middle (what each free creature *would* do in any order of
  things), free (what will be, after the decree). God chooses a whole order of things
  knowing every free act in it, and exercises providence by placement and cooperation,
  not by determining the act; Molina's own list of what coheres keeps sovereignty whole
  ("nothing is in the creature's power that is not in God's"; God can bend the will
  anywhere but into sin; God permits sin, never incites it). **[repo]** [Middle knowledge sits between natural and free knowledge](../permanent/middle-knowledge-sits-between-natural-and-free-knowledge-because-it-is-prevolitional-yet-contingent--202609090100.md),
  [Molina's own list of what coheres keeps sovereignty whole](../permanent/molinas-own-list-of-what-coheres-keeps-sovereignty-whole--202609090102.md).
  Pressure, stated honestly: not Grudem's "a situation is made of other people's
  choices," which touches only piecemeal bringing-about
  ([the repo's note on that objection](../permanent/a-situation-is-made-of-other-peoples-choices--202609081830.md),
  now amended from the Molinist side), but feasibility, "there may be some possible
  worlds that God cannot actualize," which the IEP concedes in its own voice, and the
  grounding objection, what makes a counterfactual of freedom true before the creature
  exists. **[repo]** [Weak actualization claims meticulous providence and concedes infeasible worlds](../permanent/weak-actualization-claims-meticulous-providence-without-determination-and-concedes-infeasible-worlds--202609090103.md),
  [The grounding objection is Molinism's own unanswered question](../permanent/the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104.md).
  **[to verify]** Craig, *The Only Wise God* (Baker, 1987); Keathley, *Salvation and
  Sovereignty: A Molinist Approach* (B&H, 2010), for the evangelical form.
- **Open theism** (Pinnock, Rice, Sanders, Hasker, Basinger; Boyd). Grudem calls it
  "the most consistent Arminian position": it accepts the entailment and pays with
  God's knowledge of future free choices. Pressure: Isa 40-48 makes declaring the
  future God's own test of deity, and Rom 8:28 can no longer be issued as an
  unconditional promise about today. **[repo]** [202609061921](../permanent/grudem-answers-open-theism-with-isaiahs-test-of-deity-not-with-the-definition-of-omniscience--202609061921.md),
  [Grudem's Romans 8:28 comfort is bought with the freedom](../permanent/grudems-romans-8-28-comfort-is-bought-with-the-freedom--202609081646.md).
  **[to verify]** Pinnock et al., *The Openness of God* (IVP, 1994); Boyd, *God of the
  Possible* (Baker, 2000).

## V. Objections and the posture of antinomy (about 250 words)

- **"A determined choice is not a free choice."** Answer with the definition (Edwards,
  Ware) and Calvin's necessity/compulsion distinction: man sins "not forced or
  unwilling, but voluntarily ... not by violent compulsion, or external force, but by
  the movement of his own passion" (II.3.5, with Augustine and Bernard: "bond in
  respect of necessity, free in respect of will"). **[verified]**
  https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iv.iv.html. Grudem: "God has made us in
  such a way that (1) he ordains all that we do, and (2) we exercise our personal will
  and make real, voluntary choices." **[repo]** [202609081825](../literature/grudem-answers-the-arminian-by-comparing-unanswered-questions--202609081825.md).
- **"This makes God the author of sin."** Secondary causes, the asymmetry (III), Gen
  50:20 and Acts 2:23. Then concede what Grudem concedes: "Exactly how can God not be
  blamed for evil when he ordains that we do evil willingly?" and "Exactly how can God
  cause us to choose something willingly?" are questions "Scripture does not explain to
  us." **[repo]** [The dispute is decided by which residue honours God more](../permanent/the-dispute-is-decided-by-which-residue-honours-god-more--202609081831.md),
  flagged *contested*. The repository warns: Grudem's closing comparison of unanswered
  questions is a doxological tie-breaker, not his argument; do not present it as his
  reason for the position.
- **"Mystery is a contradiction in disguise."** This is where the antinomy posture does
  its work.
  - Packer: an antinomy is "an appearance of contradiction between conclusions which
    seem equally logical, reasonable or necessary," "an apparent incompatibility between
    two apparent truths," and is distinct from a paradox, "a dispensable, comprehensible
    play on words." **[second-hand]** *Evangelism and the Sovereignty of God* (IVP, 1961),
    18-20; confirmed from two summaries, check the page.
  - Carson's two propositions: "God is absolutely sovereign, but his sovereignty never
    functions in Scripture to reduce human responsibility," and "Human beings are
    responsible creatures ... but human responsibility never functions in Scripture to
    diminish God's sovereignty or to make God absolutely contingent." The tension "is
    not a problem to be solved; rather, it is a framework to be explored."
    **[second-hand]** *How Long, O Lord?* (Baker, 1990), ch. 11, 199-228; the monograph
    is *Divine Sovereignty and Human Responsibility* (1981; Wipf and Stock, 2002)
    **[to verify]**.
  - Spurgeon: the two truths "are two lines that are so nearly parallel, that the mind
    that shall pursue them farthest, will never discover that they converge; but they
    do converge, and they will meet somewhere in eternity, close to the throne of God."
    **[verified]** "Sovereign Grace and Man's Responsibility," sermon on Rom 10:20-21,
    1 August 1858, *New Park Street Pulpit* vol. 4,
    https://www.spurgeon.org/resource-library/sermons/sovereign-grace-and-mans-responsibility/.
  - The repository's own strongest connection: Maimonides and Grudem reach the same
    wall and put the mystery on opposite sides of it. Maimonides places it in God's
    knowledge ("He, may His name be praised, and His knowledge are one") and keeps the
    human half "without any doubt"; Grudem places it in the human half and keeps
    exhaustive ordination; Molina places it in God's "inscrutable comprehension" of what
    a creature would freely do, and Molinism's named residue is the grounding objection.
    A residue is therefore what any serious monotheism pays for holding both, not a
    Calvinist embarrassment. **[repo]** [The same wall and the mystery put on opposite sides](../permanent/the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914.md)
    (amended 2026-09-09 to three placements),
    [The grounding objection is Molinism's own unanswered question](../permanent/the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104.md).
  - Historical precedent for the posture: after twenty years and eighty-five conferences
    before two popes, the Congregatio de Auxiliis ended in 1607 with each school
    permitted to defend its doctrine, forbidden to condemn the other, and judgement
    reserved. Holding both doctrines was required; a theory of how they consist was
    permitted. One sentence in the paper. **[repo]** [The Congregatio de Auxiliis ended by permitting both schools](../permanent/the-congregatio-de-auxiliis-ended-by-permitting-both-schools-and-condemning-neither--202609090106.md).
    Optional footnote: divine simplicity is the solvent Maimonides uses and Grudem never
    does. **[repo]** [Simplicity is Maimonides' solvent](../permanent/simplicity-is-maimonides-solvent-and-grudem-never-uses-it--202609081915.md),
    flagged *contested*. In a 4-page paper this is one sentence or a footnote.

## VI. Conclusion and pastoral payoff (about 100 words)

- Both doctrines are needed, and needed whole: for prayer and evangelism (Packer's
  whole book is that sovereignty is the ground of evangelism, not its enemy); for
  comfort, since Rom 8:28 is a promise about a day not yet lived only if all things
  are ordered to a fixed end **[repo]** [202609081646](../permanent/grudems-romans-8-28-comfort-is-bought-with-the-freedom--202609081646.md);
  and for action, "God has ordained that events will come about by our causing them
  ... A hearty belief in God's providence is not a discouragement but a spur to action"

## Connections this repository makes that no single source makes

These are the repository's own findings (marked as such in the notes); citing them
means citing the primary texts they rest on and making the connection yourself.

1. **Same wall, opposite sides.** Maimonides and Grudem each concede a mystery exactly
   where the other's evidence would have to land
   ([202609081914](../permanent/the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914.md)).
2. **Capacity versus precept.** The two traditions cannot be refuted by producing more
   commandment texts, because they disagree about what such a text is evidence of
   ([202609081916](../permanent/commandment-texts-as-capacity-or-as-precept--202609081916.md)).
3. **The price of Romans 8:28.** The concession "some sense in which our choices are
   not absolutely free" (ch. 12) is what the comfort of "all things" (ch. 12 wisdom
   section, ch. 16) is bought with
   ([202609081646](../permanent/grudems-romans-8-28-comfort-is-bought-with-the-freedom--202609081646.md)).
4. **Same inference, opposite functions.** The foreknowledge-to-fixity step is a cost
   Grudem pays in ch. 12 and a weapon he uses in ch. 16, and Pinnock accepts it too
   ([202609081829](../permanent/the-foreknowledge-entailment-is-a-weapon-in-chapter-16--202609081829.md),
   [202609061922](../permanent/grudem-and-pinnock-accept-the-same-entailment-and-give-up-opposite-sides-of-it--202609061922.md)).
5. **Added 2026-09-09. Same verse, opposite directions.** Grudem and Molina both argue
   from Matt 11:21; the verse is where decree-first and creature-first accounts of
   foreknowledge part ([202609090105](../permanent/grudem-and-molina-both-argue-from-tyre-and-sidon-and-read-it-in-opposite-directions--202609090105.md)).
6. **Added 2026-09-09. Three placements of the mystery.** Maimonides in the identity of
   knower and known, Grudem in the willingness of a determined choice, Molina in the
   inscrutable comprehension of what a creature would do; and a fourth party, the Church
   of 1607, that declined to place it at all
   ([202609090101](../permanent/molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101.md),
   [202609090106](../permanent/the-congregatio-de-auxiliis-ended-by-permitting-both-schools-and-condemning-neither--202609090106.md)).
