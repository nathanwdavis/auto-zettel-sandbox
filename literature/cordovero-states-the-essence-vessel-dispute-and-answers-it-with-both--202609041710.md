Own-words summary of [[cordovero-pardes-rimmonim--202609041700]].

Every English phrase below is **this repository's own rendering** of the
Hebrew, which is quoted beside it; Sefaria serves no translation of this work.

**The question, in the gate's first sentence.** Cordovero opens by saying the
gate's title states its business: to settle "in the matter of the sefirot,
whether they are the essence of the Emanator or His vessels, like the vessels
of a craftsman" — אם הם עצמות המאציל או הם כליו ככלים אל האומן — and he
immediately calls the state of the question a scandal: "a great confusion has
fallen among the later kabbalists" (מבוכה רבה נפלה בין המקובלים האחרונים),
naming Menachem Recanati on one side and R. David, author of *Magen David*, on
the other (Gate 4, ch. 1, para. 2).

**The instrumentalist side, chs. 1 and 8 of that chapter.** Cordovero
transcribes Recanati at length before disputing him. Recanati's difficulty is
that neither multiplicity nor change nor limit nor number may be predicated of
the Creator, and yet the sefirot act by judgement and mercy, stand right and
left, and are counted to ten. His resolution is that what is said of the
sefirot is not said of God at all: "the sefirot are like the craftsman's
vessels, with which he performs his work" (הספי' הם כמו כלי האומן שפועל בהם
פעולתו). R. Yehudah Hayyat, in *Minhat Yehudah*, follows him and sharpens the
analogy: as the sun's ray "is not the essence of the sun but a property in it,
so the emanation is a property in the Emanator and is not the essence of the
Emanator" (אינו עצמות השמש אלא סגולה בו כך האצילות סגולה במאציל ואינו עצמות
המאציל).

**The essentialist side, ch. 2.** R. David answers that the difficulty is
illusory, because "the difference of the names, or the number of the sefirot,
does not entail multiplicity in the Creator, God forbid" (שאין חלוף השמות או
מנין הספי' מחייב רבוי בבורא ית' חלילה): the names track differing aspects
under which one simple essence is considered, relative to its differing
operations — "this is nothing but the difference of the aspects, in relation to
differing actions" (זה אינו כי אם חלוף הבחינות בהצטרף אל פעולות מתחלפות). He
grounds it in a Zohar passage on Deuteronomy 32 that lists the sefirot and then
gathers them with the word *Hu*, "He", and in the midrash on Exodus 3 where God
says "according to My deeds I am called" — *Elohim* when judging, and so on.

**Cordovero's decisive objection to the instrumentalists, ch. 2 end.** It is
one argument, and it is short. Everybody in this dispute agrees the sefirot are
*emanated* and not *created* — "the sefirot are emanated and not created"
(כי הספירות הן נאצלות ולא נבראות) — and what is emanated carries the emanator's
power within it, so "the emanated is not separate from the emanator; and if so,
everything said of the sefirot is literally said of the Creator" (ואין הנאצל
נפרד מן המאציל וא"כ כל מה שנאמר בספירות הוא ממש נאמר בבורא ית'). The
instrumentalist's firewall does not hold, because emanation is not manufacture.

**His objection to the essentialists, ch. 3.** If the sefirot are only aspects
discerned relative to what God does for recipients, then they exist only while
the corresponding act is being performed — sometimes ten, sometimes five,
sometimes more — and they have no real existence of their own; and then the
Zohar's whole discourse about them, and the soul's ascent to cleave to them as
to its roots, has nothing to cleave to. R. David, he says, "tore down a wall to
put up a mat."

**The answer, ch. 4: not a choice but a doubling.** Cordovero first grants both
parties their standing — "these and those are the words of the living God, and
the law is as they say, though not for their reasons" (ואלו ואלו דברי אלקים
חיים והלכה כמותם ולא מטעמם) — and then splits the term they were fighting over.
The Ein Sof emanated "ten sefirot which are of His essence, unified with Him,
and He and they are all one complete unity" (עשר ספירות אשר הם מעצמותו מתיחדות
בו והוא והם הכל אחדות שלימה); and "these sefirot are a soul, and they clothe
themselves in ten sefirot designated by names, which are vessels" (והספירות
האלה הם נשמה ומתלבשות בע"ס הנקובות בשמות שהם כלים). So there are ten inner
sefirot that are essence and ten outer sefirot that are vessels, perfectly
unified, the first standing to the second as soul to body. The gain is exactly
what Recanati wanted: "the change and the division are not in Him, but in the
outer sefirot" (אין השנוי והחלוק בו אלא בספירות החיצוניות).

He illustrates it twice: water is colourless but takes the colour of the
vessels it is poured into, the change being accidental and in the eye of the
beholder rather than in the water; and better, "the parable of the sun's ray
passing through a window and passing on its way through ten lamp-glasses of ten
different colours" (המשל אל ניצוץ השמש העובר דרך החלון ודרך מעברו בדרך עשר
עששיות מעשר גוונים משונים). The vessels are what make judgement and mercy; the
light is neither.

**What is denied of the Emanator, ch. 4, para. 1.** The apophasis is total and
it is symmetrical in time: "in Him we can neither speak nor depict nor
predicate — not judgement, not mercy" (ובו אין אנו יכולין לדבר ולא לצייר ולא
לחייב לא דין ולא רחמים), and this holds "neither before the emanation, nor now
after the emanation" (לא קודם האצילות לא עתה אחר האצילות). Even Keter, the
first of the emanated, has name and depiction denied of it.

**How he grounds it, ch. 5, and one bibliographic fact worth recording.**
Cordovero's proof text is *Patach Eliyahu*, which this repository already holds
in translation at [[tikkunei-ha-zohar--202609011531]]. He quotes it in Aramaic,
entire and continuously, and cites it as being in *Sefer ha-Tiqqunim*,
"in the introduction, folio 15" (בהקדמה דף ט"ו) — where the edition Sefaria
serves places the same words at folios 17a-17b. That is an edition difference in
foliation, and the wording differs only in spelling. The two texts were
compared clause by clause and the variation is entirely orthographic —
תקונין / תיקונין, מאלין / מאילין, כאלו / כאילו, כלהו / כולהו, ולאו / ולא,
מלגאו / מלגו — with no clause present in one and absent from the other, and no
word changed for another. That holds for every line this repository builds on:
"You are He that has produced ten constructs", the binding-and-uniting clause,
the separation rule, "You have no specific name", and "left like a body without
a soul". Two differences that are not orthographic: the printed *Tiqqunim*
Sefaria serves carries variant readings in parentheses (נ"א) that Cordovero's
copy does not, and his quotation stops earlier than the folio does — he says so
himself, "thus far his words, for we have no more; and although it is evident
from within it that the end of the passage is missing", and the capture bears
him out, continuing past his last line with "Arise, Rabbi Shim'on". His use of it is precise: the passage's own doubling —
lights that are named and a One who has no specific name, and the line "when
You withdraw from them, all those names are left like a body without a soul" —
is what he takes to license the essence/vessel distinction, and he reuses that
very image for the outer sefirot when the light is withdrawn: "the others are
left like a body without a soul" (ישארו האחרות כגוף בלא נשמה).

**Limit of this note.** It reports Recanati and R. David as Cordovero quotes
them, at length and in their own words, but from his transcription only;
neither *Ta'amei ha-Mitzvot* nor *Magen David* is in this repository, and no
claim here needs his summary to be fair to them, only accurate as a
transcription of what he read.
