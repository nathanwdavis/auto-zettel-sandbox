Own-words summary of the New Testament passages in [[sin-as-act-and-sin-as-indwelling-condition-passages-1-john--202610032100]].

Source and product. Jesus uses the tree: "a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil
fruit", and "A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt
tree bring forth good fruit" (Matt 7:17-18); "make the tree corrupt, and his
fruit corrupt: for the tree is known by his fruit" (Matt 12:33), glossed as
"out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" (12:34). The heart is
the source of the acts: "out of the heart proceed evil thoughts, murders,
adulteries, fornications, thefts, false witness, blasphemies" (Matt 15:19).
James gives a sequence from desire to deed to death: a man is "drawn away of
his own lust, and enticed" (Jas 1:14); "Then when lust hath conceived, it
bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death"
(1:15). Paul
sets "the flesh" that "lusteth against the Spirit" (Gal 5:17) apart from "the
works of the flesh", which are then listed as deeds (5:19-21) and set against
"the fruit of the Spirit" (5:22). "That which is born of the flesh is flesh"
(John 3:6).

Having sin and having sinned. 1 John denies two different claims in parallel:
"If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves" (1:8), and "If we say
that we have not sinned, we make him a liar" (1:10), with confession of "our
sins" between them (1:9). The same letter defines the act, "sin is the
transgression of the law" (3:4), and says "Whosoever is born of God doth not
commit sin" (3:9).

Sin as a power, in Romans 5-8. Sin "entered into the world" by one man (5:12);
"sin hath reigned unto death" (5:21). Believers are told "Let not sin therefore
reign in your mortal body, that ye should obey it in the lusts thereof" (6:12)
and promised "sin shall not have dominion over you" (6:14); there is a "body of
sin" to be destroyed (6:6). In Romans 7 sin acts through the commandment:
"sin, taking occasion by the commandment, wrought in me all manner of
concupiscence" (7:8). Paul then separates himself from his deeds: "it is no
more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me" (7:17, again at 7:20); "in me
(that is, in my flesh,) dwelleth no good thing" (7:18); there is "another law in
my members" bringing him "into captivity to the law of sin which is in my
members" (7:23). And "the carnal mind is enmity against God: for it is not
subject to the law of God, neither indeed can be" (8:7).

Adam and the many. Romans 5:12 reads, "by one man sin entered into the world,
and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned".
Though "sin is not imputed when there is no law" (5:13), death reigned "even
over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam's transgression"
(5:14). Adam's
act is "the offence" (5:15, 17, 18), and "by one man's disobedience many were
made sinners" (5:19). "as in Adam all die" (1 Cor 15:22); "all have sinned"
(Rom 3:23).

Both in one sentence. Ephesians 2 calls its readers "dead in trespasses and
sins" (2:1), recalls them "fulfilling the desires of the flesh and of the mind"
(2:3), and adds that they "were by nature the children of wrath, even as
others" (2:3).
