Own-words summary of
[[mueller-and-oppenheimer-the-pen-is-mightier-than-the-keyboard--202609032201]].
**This summarises an abstract, because the abstract is all this base has.**
Every sentence below is either in that abstract or is flagged as coming from
somewhere else. Where a number appears, it comes from the replication's
re-analysis, not from here.

The claim of the abstract, in the authors' own frame. Laptop note taking is
common and often assumed to be worse than longhand, and previous work put that
down to multitasking and distraction. Their contribution is to argue that
laptops impair learning even when used *only* for notes, and to name the
mechanism: laptop use "results in shallower processing." Across three studies,
laptop note takers did worse than longhand note takers on conceptual questions.
The proposed cause is transcription — laptop takers tend to write the lecture
down verbatim "rather than processing information and reframing it in their own
words," and it is that tendency, not the keyboard, that they call detrimental.

One qualification is inside the abstract itself and is usually dropped when the
study is repeated in the press: "whereas taking more notes can be beneficial."
Volume of notes is not the problem in their account; verbatim volume is.

What the study is *about*, which matters for what it can be cited for. The
manipulation is the medium of capture during a lecture, and the outcome is a
quiz on that lecture. Nothing in it concerns keeping notes, returning to them,
linking them, or using them later — the original's Study 3, the only one with
a delay, is described in the replication as finding the medium effect only
among participants who studied their notes first (see
[[urry-et-al-replicate-the-notes-effect-and-not-the-learning-effect--202609032205]],
ms. p. 1). So even the strongest reading of this paper is a claim about the
first few seconds of a note's life.

What has happened to it since, and why this base does not cite it alone. The
direct replication in the same journal reproduced the notes-content effects and
not the learning effect, and a pooled analysis of eight similar studies put the
quiz-performance effect at *g* = 0.04, statistically equivalent to zero. The
mechanism claim — more verbatim overlap, worse performance — survives only as a
correlation, and the replicators themselves refuse the causal reading of it.
The details, the effect sizes and the authors' own caveats are in
[[urry-et-al-replicate-the-notes-effect-and-not-the-learning-effect--202609032205]].
This base cites the 2014 finding **only** in company with that record; citing
the headline result on its own would be exactly the failure this repository
exists to avoid, and the inquiry that commissioned this work said so before any
of it was read.
