Own-words summary of [[aqam-tafsir-al-aqam-on-2-221-and-5-5--202609041415]].
Translations are this repository's own.

**On Q 2:221 he does not touch the disputed point.** His comment is the
occasion-of-revelation report about Marthad b. Abi Marthad and the woman 'Anaq,
and then two flat rulings: `وهي تدل على تحريم نكاح المشركة` — it indicates the
prohibition of marrying a *mushrika* — and, of the second half,
`الآية تدل على تحريم تزويج الكفار بالمسلمة والاجماع على ذلك`, the verse
indicates the prohibition of marrying unbelievers to a Muslim woman, and there
is consensus on that. Whether *mushrika* reaches a woman of the Book is not
asked. The consensus he invokes is on the uncontested half of the verse.

**On Q 5:5 he maps the positions, and the second one is the finding.**

> { والمحصنات من الذين أُوتوا الكتاب } هم اليهود والنصارى، واختلفوا في معناه
> قيل: نساء أهل الكتاب عن **أكثر المفسرين** والذين آمنوا منهم إزالة الشبهة أي
> **من كانت يهودية فآمنت يجوز أن يتزوج بها** عن **يحيى والقاسم (عليهم السلام)**،
> وقيل: أراد الحرائر من أهل الكتاب فتحلَّ الحرائر، ولا تحل الإِماء عن مجاهد
> وجماعة، وإليه ذهب الشافعي

"And the *muhsanat* of those given the Book" — they are the Jews and the
Christians, and they differed over its meaning. It is said: the women of the
People of the Book — from **most of the commentators**; and [it is said] those
of them who have believed, to remove the doubt, that is, **one who was a Jewess
and then believed may be married** — from **Yahya and al-Qasim**, peace be upon
them; and it is said: he meant the free women of the People of the Book, so free
women are lawful and slave women are not — from Mujahid and a group, and
al-Shafi'i went to this.

Yahya al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, founder of the Zaydi imamate in Yemen, and al-Qasim
al-Rassi are to a Zaydi commentary what al-Baqir and al-Sadiq are to an Imami
one, and `عليهم السلام` marks them as such. The reading credited to them is the
converts-only reading — which is the *first* of the two *ta'wils* al-Tusi and
al-Tabrisi give for exactly this clause, arriving from the other Shi'i tradition
and named to different authorities. On this reading Q 5:5 licenses nothing for an
unconverted woman of the Book, and Q 2:221 is left standing over her.

**The same two imams appear once more on the same verse**, and in the same
direction: on `وطعام الذين أوتوا الكتاب` he records `وقيل: لا تحل ذبائحهم،
والمراد بالمطاعم الحنطة والشعير عن القاسم ويحيى` — it is said their slaughtered
animals are not lawful, and what is meant by "food" is wheat and barley, from
al-Qasim and Yahya. That is the strict line on the food clause of the same verse
and it matches al-Tusi's Imami report almost exactly, which makes the marriage
attribution harder to read as a slip.

**What this is evidence for, stated at its real strength.** It is a Zaydi
commentary attributing to the school's two foundational imams a reading of Q 5:5
that yields the strict result. It is not a Zaydi *fiqh* text, and al-A'qam
reports the position rather than arguing it — so the *content* of the claim
about Yahya and al-Qasim still rests on one witness, even though that witness is
now inside the tradition instead of outside it. And it is a late witness: the
host dates al-A'qam only to the ninth century AH, some five hundred years after
the imams he is reporting.
