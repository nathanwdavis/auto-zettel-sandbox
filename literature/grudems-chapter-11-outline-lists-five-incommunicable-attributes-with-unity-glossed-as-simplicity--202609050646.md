This is an unattributed lesson outline presented on waynegrudem.com accompanying the teaching of Chapter 11 (The Character of God: "Incommunicable" Attributes). The PDF carries metadata showing creation on 2009-03-18 by Mike Langley; the site presents it without author or date attribution. The page numbers and section headings that follow are from the 1st edition (1994) of Systematic Theology.

The outline lists five incommunicable attributes with their page ranges: Independence (aseity/self-existence), pages 160-163; Unchangeableness (immutability), pages 163-168; Eternity, pages 168-173; Omnipresence, pages 173-177; and Unity, labeled "(Simplicity)" as the outline prints it, pages 177-180. For each attribute, the outline prints the definition sentence verbatim: Independence, "God does not need us or the rest of creation for anything, yet we and the rest of creation can glorify him and bring him joy"; Unchangeableness, "God is unchanging in his being, perfections, purposes, and promises, yet God does act and feel emotions, and he acts and feels differently in response to different situations"; Eternity, "God has no beginning, end, or succession of moments in his own being, and he sees all time equally vividly, yet God sees events in time and acts in time"; Omnipresence, "God does not have size or spatial dimensions and is present at every point of space with his whole being, yet God acts differently in different places"; and Unity, "God is not divided into parts, yet we see different attributes of God emphasized at different times."

In the Unity (Simplicity) section (pages 177-180), the outline develops three claims: God's being does not constitute a collection of attributes added together; God's attributes are not additions to his real being; and every attribute of God qualifies every other attribute, such that none stands in isolation.

Under the Unchangeableness attribute, the outline addresses classical debates about divine passibility (pages 163-168). The outline notes that some theologians have denied God has passions or emotions, but asserts that the Bible seems to consistently affirm that God has emotions, citing passages where God rejoices, grieves, and shows compassion.
