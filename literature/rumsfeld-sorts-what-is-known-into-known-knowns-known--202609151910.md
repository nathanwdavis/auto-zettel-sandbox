**Grounding: raw/202609151856-rumsfeld-dod-news-briefing-2002-02-12-washington-file.html, the Washington File reprint of the whole briefing; the one exchange is used, read in its context.**

The question was about absence of evidence: whether "there is no evidence of a direct link between Baghdad and some of these terrorist organizations". The answer begins by refusing the inference: "Reports that say that something hasn't happened are always interesting to me, because as we know, there are known knowns; there are things we know we know. We also know there are known unknowns; that is to say we know there are some things we do not know. But there are also unknown unknowns -- the ones we don't know we don't know. And if one looks throughout the history of our country and other free countries, it is the latter category that tend to be the difficult ones." The barb that follows uses a theological word for the fault being diagnosed: "people who have the omniscience that they can say with high certainty that something has not happened or is not being tried, have capabilities that are" beyond his. Asked whether the case at hand "is an unknown unknown", he answers, "I'm not going to say which it is."

**What the passage supplies and what it does not.** It supplies the three-cell vocabulary in its original form and the claim that the third cell is where the difficulty lives. It does not supply a fourth cell (things known without being known to be known, or errors), it does not say how one could tell that an unknown unknown exists, and it offers the taxonomy as a rule of epistemic hygiene about claims of absence, not as a theory of knowledge. Its interest for this base is that the owner's division of the unrevealed tier of God's knowledge borrows exactly these two cells, and that the passage itself reaches for "omniscience" as the name of the certainty it thinks nobody has.

**Limits.** A press-briefing transcript, mirrored; the DoD's own archive copy could not be reached. The vocabulary predates the briefing (it circulated in engineering and project-management usage), but no earlier primary source is on file, and this note makes no claim about origin.
