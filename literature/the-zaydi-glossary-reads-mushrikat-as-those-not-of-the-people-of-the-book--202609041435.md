Own-words summary of
[[zayd-b-ali-gharib-al-quran-on-2-221-and-5-5--202609041410]]. Translations are
this repository's own.

**The whole comment on Q 2:221 is one sentence, and it is the opposite of what
this cycle went looking for.**

> وقولهُ تعالى: { وَلاَ تَنْكِحُواْ ٱلْمُشْرِكَاتِ حَتَّىٰ يُؤْمِنَّ } معناه
> **مِمَّنْ لسن من أَهلِ الكِتابِ**.

And His words, "and do not marry the *mushrikat* until they believe": its
meaning is, **from among those who are not of the People of the Book**.

That is the *takhsis* reading — the third of the three classical positions, the
one al-Qurtubi credits to Qatada and Sa'id b. Jubayr and al-Tabrisi to the same
pair. The verse is restricted from the start; the women of the Book were never
inside it. It is the *lenient* position on the question, and it is the position
of the oldest commentary in the Zaydi corpus, published under the name of the
school's eponym.

**On Q 5:5 the work is silent where it would matter.** Its three entries on the
verse gloss `الطيبات` as "the lawful", `وطعام الذين أوتوا الكتاب` as "their
slaughtered animals", and — reaching across to Q 4:25 — `محصنات غير مسافحات` as
"chaste women, not fornicators". The clause at issue, `والمحصنات من الذين أوتوا
الكتاب`, draws no comment at all. So the work neither confirms nor denies the
permission on that verse; it simply does not treat it.

**What this does and does not establish.** It does not refute al-Sabuni or
al-Tabrisi. Both of them say *some* Zaydis, and one Zaydi text holding the other
view is exactly what "some" allows for. What it establishes is that the strict
reading is not the Zaydi position *simpliciter*, and that the qualifier in both
attributions is load-bearing rather than a hedge — which is worth knowing, since
a claim about a school is the kind of thing that hardens into a claim about all
of it.

**Two limits, stated rather than buried.** The ascription of *Gharib al-Qur'an*
to the historical Zayd b. 'Ali is a transmitted one and is not established here;
what is claimed above is a fact about the Zaydi commentary corpus as published,
not about a man who died in 122/740. And this is a glossary, so its silence on
a clause is very weak evidence about anything: the genre passes over whatever
presents no lexical difficulty. Both limits are recorded in the reference note.
