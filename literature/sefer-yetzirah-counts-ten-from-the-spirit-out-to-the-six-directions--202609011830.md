Own-words summary of [[sefer-yetzirah--202609011825]], chapter 1 complete.

The chapter opens by saying that God "carved and created His World in
Thirty-Two Wondrous Ways of Wisdom", and then splits those thirty-two into two
sets: ten *Sefirot Belimah* and twenty-two letters (1:1-1:2). Everything after
that, until the letters take over in chapter 2, is about the ten. Eight of the
fourteen mishnayot open with the same drumbeat phrase, "Ten Sefirot Belimah",
and each adds one thing about them.

**What the text says about the ten.** They are counted on the fingers, "five
against five, with the Covenant of the Unique One established in the center"
(1:3). Their number is fixed and the fixing is emphatic: "Ten and not nine, ten
and not eleven" (1:4). Their measure is ten but "they have no edge", and the
mishnah then lists them as ten *depths* — of beginning and end, good and evil,
above and below, and the four compass points (1:5). They look "as the look of
lightning", God's word runs in them "running and returning", and "before His
throne they bow" (1:6). Their end is attached to their start "like the
flame-tongue is bound to the coal" (1:7). And speculation about them is closed
off: "Keep your mouth from speaking and your heart from conceiving" (1:8).

**What the ten actually are, when the text finally enumerates them.** From 1:9
the chapter counts them out in order, and the order is a descent through a
cosmogony, not a list of divine attributes. One is "the Wind of the Living GOD" in the
community translation and "The spirit of the living God" in Kalisch — the
Hebrew is *ruach*, and the two witnesses split on it exactly where English
translators of this book have always split. Two is "Wind from Wind", carved
into the twenty-two letters; three is "Water from Wind", from which *tohu* and *bohu*, mud and clay
are hewn; four is "Fire from Water", from which the throne of glory, the wheels,
the "Fiery Serpents" (Kalisch: "the Seraphim and Ophanim"), the holy creatures
and the ministering angels are made; and five
through ten are the six directions — above, below, east, west, south, north —
each "sealed" with a permutation of the letters of the divine name (1:9-1:13).
The recapitulation at 1:14 lists exactly that series again. So the first member
of the ten is God's own spirit and the last six are the dimensions of space, and
the three between the two ends are elements and what is formed out of them.

**The one thing the chapter says directly about the One.** It says it as the
*reason* for the circle in 1:7, and both English witnesses read the clause
causally. The community translation: "Their end is attached to their start, like
the flame-tongue is bound to the coal. For the Lord is unique, He has no second
— before one, what can you count?" Kalisch, 1877, at his §6: "its end linked to
its beginning and its beginning linked to its end, just as the flame is wedded
to the live coal; because the Lord is one and there is not a second one, and
before one what wilt thou count?" The Hebrew clause behind both —
*she-adon yachid we-ein lo sheni* — is identical word for word in the two Hebrew
witnesses collated for this capture. The same figure appears in 1:5, where after
the ten depths the mishnah adds that "The Unique Lord EL, the faithful king,
governs them all from His Holy Abode" (Kalisch: "the only Lord God, the faithful
King, rules over all from His holy habitation for ever and ever"). In both
places the ten are set under a One who is not one of them.

**Limits of this summary.** Chapters 2-6 are not captured and nothing here
covers them. Two readings that would be easy to slide into are refused. First,
the final clause of 1:7, "before one, what can you count?", is terse and has
carried very different construals in the commentary tradition; this repository
has captured no commentator, and reads it only as denying that the counting
starts anywhere earlier than the One — which is what the causal clause it
follows supports. Second, this book's ten are not the ten sefirot of medieval
Kabbalah, and the translator of the version quoted here says so explicitly in a
note reproduced verbatim in the capture: he refrains from linking them to "the
now-familiar Ten Sefirot found in later texts like the Bahir and Zohar" because
"it is not at all clear that the SY ascribes the same symbolic significance to
the Ten Sefirot Belimah as latter Kabbalistic texts do". The connection that
does hold is one of vocabulary and citation, and it runs the other way: this is
where the word first appears, and *Patach Eliyahu* glosses its own "ten
sephirot" with a pointer back to Sefer Yetzirah 1:2 (see
[[patach-eliyahu-names-the-sefirot-and-leaves-ein-sof-nameless--202609011536]]).
