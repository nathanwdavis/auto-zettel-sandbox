Own-words summary of [[baghawi-maalim-al-tanzil-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609040804]].
English renderings are this repository's own working translations of the
captured Arabic.

**On Q 29:46.** al-Baghawi works clause by clause, each lemma in parentheses.
"Do not dispute with the People of the Book" is `لا تخاصموهم` — do not quarrel
with them; "except in the better way" is by the Qur'an, by calling to God
through His signs and drawing attention to His proofs, and he adds that what is
meant is those who have accepted the *jizya*. The exception is those who
`أبوا أن يعطوا الجزية ونصبوا الحرب` — refused to give the *jizya* and set up
war — `فجادلوهم بالسيف حتى يسلموا أو يعطوا الجزية` (so dispute with them by the
sword until they submit or pay the *jizya*); alternatively, he says, the sense
is "except those who wronged *you*", since all of them are wrongdoers by
unbelief. Sa'id ibn Jubayr's identification (the people of war and those with
no treaty) and Qatada's and Muqatil's abrogation claim follow. On "say: we
believe in what was sent down to us and to you" he gives the rule: if one of
them who has accepted the *jizya* tells you something from their books, do not
dispute it with them, and neither believe them nor disbelieve them; say instead
what the verse says.

**Then the lemma that matters.** He prints `( وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد ونحن له
مسلمون )` — the shared-God clause and its sequel — as a heading in its own
right, exactly as he prints the clauses he glosses. Under it he supplies two
full transmission chains: al-Bukhari's report from Abu Hurayra, and a report
from Abu Namla al-Ansari about the Jew who asked whether a passing bier speaks.
Both reports carry the instruction not to believe and not to disbelieve the
People of the Book. He supplies no gloss on the clause's content, no synonym
for `إله`, and no report offered in support of the identification. The word
`معبود` does not occur.

That is the sharpest form the finding takes in the three classical
commentaries captured this cycle. A commentator who never raised the clause
might simply have been moving quickly. al-Baghawi raises it in the form his
commentary reserves for material to be explained, and then explains something
else.

**On Q 3:64.** He opens with the occasion: the delegation of Najran came to
Medina, met the Jews, and the two parties disputed over Abraham, each claiming
him; the Prophet said both parties are innocent of Abraham, who was a *hanif*
and a Muslim. The Jews then said, "Muhammad, you want only that we take you as
a lord, as the Christians took Jesus as a lord", and the Christians said,
"Muhammad, you want only that we say of you what the Jews said of Ezra" — and
the verse came down. `سواء` he glosses `عدل بيننا وبينكم مستوية` — equitable
between us and you, level — with a philological excursus on the word. The
"word" is then explained by "that we worship none but God", and "let none of us
take others as lords besides God" is glossed `كما فعلت اليهود والنصارى` — as the
Jews and the Christians did — with Q 9:31 in support. "Bear witness that we are
Muslims" is `مخلصون بالتوحيد` — sincere in the affirmation of God's oneness. He
closes with the letter to Heraclius, the verse quoted inside `أسلم تسلم`
(submit and you will be safe).

**On 3:64, then, this commentary reads the verse as arising from a refusal.**
The occasion he reports is two parties telling Muhammad they will not take him
as a lord; the equitable word he expounds is one whose second half names what
the addressees are doing wrong.
