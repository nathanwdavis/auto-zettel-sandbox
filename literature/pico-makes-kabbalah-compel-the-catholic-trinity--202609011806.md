Own-words summary of the cabalistic section of
[[pico-conclusiones-sive-theses-dccccc--202609011801]].

The section announces its purpose in its own heading: these are conclusions
drawn "from the very foundations of the Hebrew sages" that "most greatly
confirm the Christian religion". The programme is stated, not inferred.

Thesis 5 is the load-bearing claim, and its strength is easy to understate.
Pico does not say that Kabbalah *resembles* trinitarian doctrine, nor that it
*permits* a trinitarian reading. He says every Hebrew kabbalist, proceeding
from the principles and sayings of the science of Kabbalah itself, is
"inevitably compelled to concede" -- *cogitur inevitabiliter concedere* --
concerning the Trinity and each divine person, Father, Son and Holy Spirit,
precisely what the catholic faith of Christians holds, "without addition,
diminution, or variation". The three qualifiers do the work: the concession
Pico extracts is not an approximation but the Catholic doctrine exactly. Its
corollary turns the same instrument on Christian heretics -- not only those
who deny the Trinity but those who state it otherwise than the Church does,
"such as Arians, Sabellians and the like", can be manifestly refuted if the
principles of Kabbalah are granted. Kabbalah is thus made to arbitrate an
intra-Christian dispute about the Trinity, which tells you how completely Pico
believed he had annexed it.

Thesis 6 supplies the mechanism in the only place it is made concrete: three
"great fourfold names of God" held in the kabbalists' secrets are to be
assigned to the three persons "by a marvellous appropriation" -- *sekhel* to
the Father, *ha-maskil* to the Son, *ha-muskal* to the Holy Spirit; the
intellect, the one who understands, and the understood. (Peterson supplies
these Hebrew words; Brown's diplomatic transcription leaves gaps where they
stand, so the mapping is attested by one of the two witnesses only.) The
triad is a philosophical one, not a sefirotic one, which is worth noticing:
where the parallel is actually spelled out, Pico reaches for the
knower/known/knowing structure rather than for the ten sefirot.

Theses 7 and 8 do the same work on the name of Jesus, which "no Hebrew
kabbalist can deny", interpreted by kabbalistic method, signifies "this
precisely and nothing else" -- God, the Son of God and the wisdom of the
Father, united to human nature through the third person. Theses 14-16 read the
letter *shin* in that name, and the letters of *shabbat*, to the same end. In
each case the argument runs from a technique the tradition owns to a
conclusion the tradition rejects, and the modal vocabulary never softens:
*potest negare* (can deny), *cogitur* (is compelled), *evidenter elicitur*
(is evidently drawn out).

Two limits on what this source can be asked to show. It is a set of theses,
not the argument for them: Pico offers the conclusions for disputation and
supplies the reasoning almost nowhere, so the *Conclusiones* record what he
claimed rather than how he defended it. And Pico is a Christian apologist
writing to convert; he is a witness to what Christian kabbalism asserted, and
not evidence about what kabbalists held. The Jewish side of this dispute is
not captured in this repository -- see INBOX.
