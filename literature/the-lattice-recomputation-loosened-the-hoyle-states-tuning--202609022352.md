Own-words summary of [[epelbaum-viability-of-carbon-based-life--202609022348]].

The Letter asks how much the constants of nature could move before stars stop
making carbon and oxygen, and answers it from lattice QCD rather than from a
fitted nuclear model. The control parameter throughout is epsilon, the energy
of the Hoyle state relative to the triple-alpha threshold.

The question, in the authors' framing: "We investigate whether this
life-essential condition is robust or delicately fine-tuned by measuring its
dependence on the fundamental constants of nature, specifically the light quark
mass and the strength of the electromagnetic interaction."

The prior state of the argument (p. 1). Two earlier results are reported.
Livio et al. "modified the value of epsilon by hand" and "concluded that a
~= 60 keV change in epsilon could be tolerated, and thus the amount of
fine-tuning required was not as severe as first believed." Oberhummer et al.,
working from a nuclear cluster model, found that "Outside of a narrow window of
~= 0.5% around the observed strong force and ~= 4% around the observed Coulomb
force, the stellar production of carbon and/or oxygen was found to be reduced by
several orders of magnitude." The authors' objection to the latter is
methodological, not numerical: that model "is not readily connected to" QCD, so
"it is not obvious how to translate the findings of Ref. [12] into anthropic
constraints on fundamental parameters."

The result (pp. 3-4). Within an envelope of "+/-100 keV around the observed
value of epsilon", carbon and oxygen abundances hold. Translated into quark
masses: "carbon-based life survives at least a ~= 0.7% shift in mq" as a
worst case, while "Given the central values of A_s,t, we conclude that
variations of the light quark masses of 2 - 3% are unlikely to be catastrophic
to the formation of life-essential carbon and oxygen." The summary states it as
"a change of ~= 2% in mq or ~= 2% in alpha_em."

The sentence that matters most for how a design argument may use this. Between
those two numbers the authors record what their own error bars permit: "we find
that the theoretical uncertainty in A_s,t is also compatible with a vanishing
d(epsilon)/d(M_pi) (complete lack of fine-tuning)." So the best available
calculation does not merely loosen the tuning; its uncertainty is consistent
with there being no tuning in this channel at all.

And what they still grant: "Beyond such relatively small changes, the anthropic
principle appears necessary at this time to explain the observed reaction rate
of the triple-alpha process." The phrase "at this time" is the authors' own —
the verdict is dated, and offered as the current state of a calculation rather
than a settled fact.
