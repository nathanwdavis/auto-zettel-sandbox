**Note on source:** This is al-Ash'ari's own reporting of Mu'tazili doctrine from within al-Ibana, not Klein's introduction and not the Mu'tazilis' own voice. It represents al-Ash'ari's characterization of his opponents' position.

On pages 99-100 of al-Ibana, al-Ash'ari explicitly reports the Mu'tazili position: "The Mu'taziiah deny the attributes of the Lord of the Worlds, and think that the meaning of 'hearing, seeing,' is identical with the meaning of 'knowing,' just as the Christians think that God's hearing is His sight and His visibility and His Word and His knowledge and His Son."

This report shows two things about the Mu'tazili position: (1) they deny that the attributes exist as real properties of God, and (2) they identify the meaning of 'hearing, seeing' with 'knowing'; al-Ash'ari then presses them to extend the identification to power and life. Al-Ash'ari then constructs a reductio ad absurdum: if the Mu'tazilis are right that these attributes all mean the same thing, then by their own logic, everything known would have to be heard, which is clearly false.

On page 50 of al-Ibana, al-Ash'ari also states that "we do not deny it as the Mutazilah, the Jahmiyyah, and the Harijites deny it," referring to the attributes of hearing and sight. On al-Ash'ari's telling the Mu'tazili position is a denial of distinct attributes rather than an affirmation of them under a different name; the Mu'tazila's own formulation is not in evidence here.
