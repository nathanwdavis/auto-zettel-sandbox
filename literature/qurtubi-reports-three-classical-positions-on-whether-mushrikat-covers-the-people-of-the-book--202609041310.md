## What al-Qurtubi is doing on this verse

He divides the verse into seven *masa'il* (numbered questions). The third is the
one this base came for: `وٱختلف العلماء في تأويل هذه الآية` — "the scholars
disagreed about the interpretation of this verse". What follows is a report of
named positions, and the disagreement is not about what Jews and Christians
believe. Every party takes the facts of Christian belief as given. The
disagreement is about whether the Qur'anic term `المشركات` — the associating
women — has those believers inside its extension.

## The three positions, as he reports them

**(1) It covered them, and was then narrowed by abrogation.** "A group said: God
forbade marriage to the *mushrikat* in Surat al-Baqara, then the women of the
People of the Book were abrogated out of that statement and He permitted them in
Surat al-Ma'ida" — `حرّم الله نكاح المشركات في سورة «البقرة» ثم نسخ من هذه
الجملة نساءَ أهل الكتاب فأحلهنّ في سورة «المائدة»`. Reported from Ibn 'Abbas;
held by **Malik b. Anas, Sufyan al-Thawri and al-Awza'i**. On this reading the
People of the Book *are* within the word, and what exempts them is a subsequent
ruling, not their category.

**(2) It never covered them at all.** "Qatada and Sa'id b. Jubayr said: the
wording of the verse is general over every unbelieving woman, but what is meant
by it is specific, in the women of the Book, and the Ma'ida verse made the
specification plain — and the general never comprehended the women of the Book
at any point" — `ولم يتناول العمومُ قطُّ الكتابياتِ`. Al-Qurtubi notes this as
one of al-Shafi'i's two positions.

**(3) It covers them and the permission is abrogated.** Ishaq b. Ibrahim al-Harbi
reports "a group who made the Baqara verse the abrogating one and the Ma'ida
verse the abrogated, and so forbade marriage to every associating woman, of the
Book or not". Ibn 'Atiyya reports a *riwaya* from Ibn 'Abbas in the same
direction: the verse is general over "idolatresses, Magian women and women of the
Book, and everyone not upon Islam is forbidden".

## The sharpest classical sentence on this base's own question

Al-Qurtubi attaches to position (3) a saying of **Ibn 'Umar in Malik's
*Muwatta'***: `ولا أعلم إشراكاً أعظمَ من أن تقول المرأة ربّها عيسى` — "and I know
of no *shirk* greater than a woman saying that her lord is Jesus." That is a
Companion, transmitted in the earliest surviving law book, saying in one line
that Christian belief is not merely *shirk* but the greatest instance of it.

Al-Qurtubi does not let it stand as a legal proof, and his reason is worth
recording because it is procedural rather than doctrinal: Ibn 'Umar "was a man
who held back" (`كان رجلاً متوقِّفاً`) — hearing two verses, one permitting and
one forbidding, and not having received word of the abrogation, he suspended
judgement; "the abrogating and the abrogated are not established by
interpretation" (`وليس يؤخذ الناسخ والمنسوخ بالتأويل`).

## The argument that decides it for the majority, and it is grammatical

Al-Qurtubi gives it as `وقال بعض العلماء` — "some of the scholars said" — that
there is no conflict between the two verses at all, "for the apparent sense of
the word *shirk* does not comprehend the People of the Book"
(`فإن ظاهر لفظ الشرك لا يتناول أهل الكتاب`). The proof is that the Qur'an
conjoins the two:

- Q 2:105 `مَّا يَوَدُّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ مِنْ أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَابِ وَلاَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ`
- Q 98:1 `لَمْ يَكُنِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ مِنْ أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَابِ وَٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ`

"He distinguished between them in the wording, and the apparent force of the
conjunction requires a distinction between the conjoined and that to which it is
conjoined" — `ففرّق بينهم في اللفظ وظاهرُ العطف يقتضي مغايرةً بين المعطوف
والمعطوف عليه`. A second, subordinate move follows: the name *shirk* is a
general term and not an explicit text (`عمومٌ وليس بنصّ`), whereas Q 5:5's
permission is explicit, "so there is no conflict between what is merely possible
and what is not open to interpretation."

## What this note does not claim

Al-Qurtubi is a Maliki reporting a Maliki school position among others, and he is
reporting, not polling. "The majority" here is his word (`الجمهور` appears in
al-Sabuni's parallel section; al-Qurtubi's own summary is `فقهاءُ الأمصار
عليه`, "the jurists of the great cities hold it"), and this base has no
independent measure of how the classical field actually divided. What the
capture supports is that all three positions were held by named authorities and
that the tradition treated the question as open — not a count of who won.

Note also that the exemption at issue is asymmetric and narrow: it concerns a
Muslim man marrying a woman of the Book, and mas'ala 4 records that even that
lapses if the People of the Book are `حَرْباً`, at war. Nothing here is a general
statement that Jews and Christians are outside *shirk* for every purpose; see
the parallel al-Sabuni note
[[sabuni-runs-mushrik-at-two-extensions-inside-one-verse--202609041315]], where
the same author denies the exemption in the other direction.
