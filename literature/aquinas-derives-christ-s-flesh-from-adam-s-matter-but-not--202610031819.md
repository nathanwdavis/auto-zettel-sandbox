Own-words summary of [[aquinas-summa-theologiae-iii-q-31-of-the-matter-from-which--202610031810]].

Article 1 asks whether Christ's flesh was derived from Adam. Aquinas says yes,
and for a soteriological reason: "it was becoming that He should assume flesh
of matter derived from Adam, that the nature itself might be healed by the
assumption."

The reply to the third objection then explains why that descent did not bring
original sin with it. Christ's body was in Adam as to its bodily substance,
"but it was not there by reason of seminal virtue, because it was not
conceived from the seed of man. Thus it did not contract original sin, as
others who are descended from Adam by man's seed." On this account original
sin passes through seminal generation from Adam. Christ shares Adam's matter
but not that mode of descent, so he shares the nature without the inherited
sin. The argument makes no appeal to the condition of Mary.
