In the section headed *Sharh qawl 'Abd Allah ibn Kullab* ("Exposition of the
doctrine of 'Abd Allah ibn Kullab", printed p. 546), al-Ash'ari reports Ibn
Kullab's account of the divine attributes at length. Ibn Kullab holds God to be
eternally (*lam yazal*) knowing, powerful, living, hearing, seeing, and so on
through a long list, and — crucially — to be so **by** real attributes:
"قائلًا متكلّمًا بعلم وقدرةٍ وحياة وسمع وبصر" ("speaking and discoursing by
knowledge, power, life, hearing, and sight"). These are "attributes of the
essence" (*min sifat al-dhat*); God's attributes "are His names"
("صفات الله سبحانه هى اسماؤه"); and they subsist in God without subsisting by
themselves ("لا تقوم بأنفسها وانها قائمة بالله").

The relation of these real attributes to the essence is then given by the exact
formula the base had been unable to ground: "وان صفاته لا هى هو ولا غيره"
("and that His attributes are not He nor other than He"), and likewise the
attributes do not differ among themselves ("لا تتغاير"), "وان العلم لا هو
القدرة ولا غيرها" ("and that Knowledge is not the Power nor other than it").
Some of his companions sharpened it to a rule about speech:
"الصفات لا يقال هى هو ولا يقال غيره" ("the attributes: it is not *said* that
they are He, nor *said* that they are other than He"), forbidding the first
expression.

On p. 548 al-Ash'ari extends the same formula to the "reported" attributes —
the Face, the Eye, and the Hands: "انها صفاتٌ لله لا هى الله ولا هى غيره كما
قال فى العلم والقدرة" ("that they are attributes of God, not being God nor
other than Him, just as he said of Knowledge and Power"), adding only that
here Ibn Kullab affirmed the point "as report" (*khabar*).

This is al-Ash'ari's own doxographical report of the Kullabi position — the
school al-Ash'ari himself joined — not the Mu'tazili view, which the same author
reports elsewhere as denying the attributes and collapsing "hearing, seeing"
into "knowing" (al-Ibana, pp. 99–100; see
[[mutazili-position-as-al-ash-ari-reports-it-denies-distinct-attributes--202609051941]]).
The passage supplies the relation-term between attribute and essence that
al-Ibana withheld under *bila kayfa*. Whether Ibn Kullab *originated* the
formula or the tradition credits him with a position it later systematised is
not settled by this passage, which reports him holding it; the doxography's
"*sharh qawl*" frame is a report of his doctrine, not a chain of transmission.
