Own-words summary of
[[ibn-kathir-tafsir-al-quran-al-azim-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609040802]]. English
renderings are this repository's own working translations of the captured
Arabic, printed beside the Arabic where they carry weight.

**On Q 29:46.** Ibn Kathir opens on the abrogation question: Qatada and others
held the verse abrogated by the sword verse, so that nothing is left with the
People of the Book but Islam, the *jizya* or the sword; others held it standing
for whoever among them wants to see clearly in religion, so that he is disputed
with in the better way. He supports the second with Q 16:125 and with God's
instruction to Moses and Aaron to speak gently to Pharaoh, and then records
whose view this is: `وهذا القول اختاره ابن جرير` — this is the view Ibn Jarir
[al-Tabari] chose. The exception "those of them who do wrong" he glosses as
those who turned from the face of truth and were obstinate, `فحينئذ ينتقل من
الجدال إلى الجلاد` — at that point one moves from disputation to combat — and he
transmits Mujahid's identification of them as the people of war and whoever
refuses to pay the *jizya*.

On "and say: we believe in what was sent down to us and sent down to you" he
gives the epistemic rule at length: when they report something whose truth or
falsity we do not know, we neither declare it false, since it may be true, nor
accept it, since it may be void, but `نؤمن به إيمانا مجملا معلقا على شرط` — we
believe in it with an unspecified belief, suspended on a condition, namely that
it be revealed and neither altered nor reinterpreted. He then supplies the
Bukhari report from Abu Hurayra, the Ahmad report from Abu Namla, Ibn Mas'ud's
prohibition on questioning them, and Ibn 'Abbas's, and closes with his own
judgement: `أكثر ما يحدثون به غالبه كذب وبهتان` — most of what they narrate is
in the main falsehood and calumny, because alteration and reinterpretation have
entered it.

**The absence.** The clause `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد` reaches his page twice, both
times inside the wording of a hadith he is transmitting, and he comments on it
neither time. He is glossing the same verse in which al-Tabari wrote
`ومعبودنا ومعبودكم واحد`, and he names al-Tabari approvingly two paragraphs
earlier on a different clause of it. The word `معبود` does not occur in his
comment.

**On Q 3:64.** The address, he says, covers the People of the Book generally.
`سواء بيننا وبينكم` is `عدل ونصف ، نستوي نحن وأنتم فيها` — justice and fairness,
in which we and you stand equal. Its content is the verse's own "that we worship
none but God and associate nothing with Him", which he expands into a list:
`لا وثنا ، ولا صنما ، ولا صليبا ولا طاغوتا ، ولا نارا ، ولا شيئا` — no idol, no
image, no *cross*, no *taghut*, no fire, nothing — but rather worship for God
alone, and this, he adds, is the call of all the messengers. "Let none of us
take others as lords besides God" he glosses with Ibn Jurayj (obeying one
another in disobedience to God) and 'Ikrima (prostrating to one another). "If
they turn away, say: bear witness that we are Muslims" means: if they turn from
this fairness and this call, `فأشهدوهم أنتم على استمراركم على الإسلام الذي شرعه
الله لكم` — have them witness that you continue in the Islam God legislated for
you. The rest of the comment is a chronological problem about the Najran
delegation and the letter to Heraclius, in which this verse is quoted, and four
possible resolutions of it.

**What that adds up to on 3:64.** The equality he asserts is an equality in the
terms of the proposal, not a shared position already held: the proposal's
content excludes the cross by name, and the fallback if it is refused is a
declaration that the Muslim party stands where it stood.
