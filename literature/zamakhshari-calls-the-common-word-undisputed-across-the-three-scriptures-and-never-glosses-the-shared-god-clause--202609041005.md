Own-words summary of
[[zamakhshari-al-kashshaf-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609041000]]. Every English
rendering below is this repository's own working translation of the captured
Arabic, and the Arabic is printed beside each rendering that carries weight.
That standing translation debt is unpaid here too and is tracked in INBOX.

**On Q 3:64, the "common word" verse.** al-Zamakhshari begins with the address:
`يا أَهْلَ الْكِتابِ` is said to be the people of the two Books, or the
delegation of Najran, or the Jews of Medina — he reports the three and settles
none. Then the word `سَواءٍ بَيْنَنا وَبَيْنَكُمْ`, which he glosses
`مستوية بيننا وبينكم، لا يختلف فيها القرآن والتوراة والإنجيل` — even between us
and you; the Qur'an, the Torah and the Gospel do not differ over it. That is the
strongest inter-scriptural statement any commentator in this base makes about
this clause, and it is made about the *word* — the content that follows — not
about the parties' present positions.

He then says what that word is, and the definition is where the commonality
narrows: `وتفسير الكلمة قوله أَلَّا نَعْبُدَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ وَلا نُشْرِكَ بِهِ
شَيْئاً وَلا يَتَّخِذَ بَعْضُنا بَعْضاً أَرْباباً مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ` — the
explanation of the word is His saying "that we worship none but God, associate
nothing with Him, and take not one another as lords apart from God" — and he
paraphrases the invitation as `يعنى تعالوا إليها حتى لا نقول: عزيز ابن اللَّه،
ولا المسيح ابن اللَّه، لأن كل واحد منهما بعضنا بشر مثلنا`: meaning, come to it
*so that* we may not say "'Uzayr is the son of God" nor "the Messiah is the son
of God", because each of the two is one of us, a human being like us. The
sonship of Christ is thus named, in the gloss of the word itself, as one of the
things the invitation exists to end. (Both hosts spell the name `عزيز` where the
printed editions usually read `عزير`; the spelling is theirs and is left as
served.)

The lordship clause he reads with the Qur'an's own cross-reference to Q 9:31 and
with the report from 'Adi ibn Hatim — "we did not worship them, Messenger of
God"; "did they not make lawful for you and forbid to you, and you took their
word for it?"; "yes"; "that is it" — so that obedience in law is itself the
lordship being renounced.

Then the turn. `فَإِنْ تَوَلَّوْا` he glosses simply `عن التوحيد` — if they turn
away *from tawhid* — and reads `فَقُولُوا اشْهَدُوا بِأَنَّا مُسْلِمُونَ` as
`أى لزمتكم الحجة فوجب عليكم أن تعترفوا وتسلموا بأنا مسلمون دونكم`: the proof has
bound you, so you are obliged to acknowledge and concede that we, not you, are
the submitted ones — `كما يقول الغالب للمغلوب في جدال أو صراع`, as the victor
says to the vanquished in an argument or a wrestling match: concede that I am
the winner. He allows an alternative in which the words are ironic reproach
(`التعريض`), meaning "bear witness that you are unbelievers, since you turned
away from the truth after it had appeared". Neither reading leaves the verse as
a statement of ground already held in common.

**The *mushrikun* remark.** Commenting a few verses on, at `وَما كانَ مِنَ
الْمُشْرِكِينَ` (Q 3:67), he offers as a second possible sense: `أو أراد
بالمشركين اليهود والنصارى لإشراكهم به عزيراً والمسيح` — or He meant by "the
associators" the Jews and the Christians, because they associate 'Uzayr and the
Messiah with Him. He gives it as an option, not a ruling, and does not develop
it; but it is a classical commentator applying the term *mushrikun* to the
People of the Book, inside the commentary on the very verse that issues the
common-word summons.

**On Q 29:46, and what is absent.** His comment is short. `بِالَّتِي هِيَ
أَحْسَنُ` is `بالخصلة التي هي أحسن` — the trait that is better, meeting
roughness with gentleness and anger with restraint. `إِلَّا الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا`
is those who went to excess in hostility and obstinacy and whom gentleness did
not reach, so severity is used with them; he then reports three alternatives —
those who injured the Prophet; `وقيل: إلا الذين أثبتوا الولد والشريك وقالوا يد
الله مغلولة`, those who asserted a son and a partner and said God's hand is
fettered; and a juristic reading on which the verse concerns the *dhimmis* who
pay the *jizya*, the exception being `فنبذوا الذمّة ومنعوا الجزية، فإن أولئك
مجادلتهم بالسيف`, those who repudiated the pact and withheld the tax, whose
disputation is by the sword. He records Qatada's view that the verse is
abrogated by Q 9:29, remarking `ولا مجادلة أشدّ من السيف`, and no disputation is
harsher than the sword. He ends with the hadith "whatever the People of the Book
tell you, do not believe them and do not call them liars; say: we believe in God
and His books and His messengers".

And then he stops. The comment never reaches `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد`. He treats
`وقولوا آمنا بالذي أنزل إلينا` as of a piece with disputation in the better way
and passes over the clause that follows it in the same verse. `معبود` — the word
in which al-Tabari states his identification of the two parties' object of
worship — does not occur; neither does `واحد`, in any form, anywhere in the
comment. Both counts were taken under a normaliser whose controls are recorded
in the capture, on two independently served copies, and both come out zero,
while `واحد` occurs twice in his comment on 3:64 — so the word is not one this
tool cannot see.
