Own-words summary of [[shema-deuteronomy-6-4-5--202608311921]].

The confession is four Hebrew words, and what they assert is genuinely
contested in the tradition itself. The JPS gender-sensitive edition renders 4b
"the ETERNAL is our God, the ETERNAL alone," following Rashbam and Ibn Ezra, and
its own translators' note flags the contrasting traditional rendering, "the LORD
our God, the LORD is one."

The two readings claim different things. "The LORD alone" asserts exclusive
allegiance: this God and no other is Israel's. "The LORD is one" asserts
something about God himself: that he is one. The ambiguity sits in the Hebrew,
not in the translator's choice, so the verse cannot be used to settle by itself
what *kind* of oneness Judaism asserts. Verse 5 then turns the confession into a
command of total love -- heart, soul, might -- which is why the Shema functions
liturgically as an act of allegiance and not only as a proposition.
