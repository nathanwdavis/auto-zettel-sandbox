Own-words summary of [[kiddushin-30b-the-evil-inclination-and-torah-as-its-antidote--202610061549]].

Three consecutive segments of Kiddushin 30b on the evil inclination.

God speaks as its author: "I created an evil inclination, which is the wound,
and I created Torah as its antidote". (The words "which is the wound" are the
translation's gloss, carrying over the parable of the wound and the bandage that
precedes the captured segment.) The cure is assigned with the disease.

Genesis 4:7 is then read clause by clause as a choice. Engaged in Torah, a person
is not handed over to the inclination: "If you do well, shall it not be lifted
up?" Not engaged, he is handed over: "Sin crouches at the door", and its desire
is toward him. Yet he can master it: "But you may rule over it". The verse's
*chatat*, sin, is read as the inclination throughout.

A baraita follows: "So difficult is the evil inclination that even its Creator
calls it evil", from Genesis 8:21. Rav Yitzhak adds that "A person's evil
inclination renews itself to him every day", reading the *kol ha-yom*, all the
day, of Genesis 6:5 as every day.

In these segments the inclination is God's creation, it is renewed daily rather
than fixed once, and it can be ruled with the remedy God gave with it. Adam,
guilt and inheritance do not appear.
