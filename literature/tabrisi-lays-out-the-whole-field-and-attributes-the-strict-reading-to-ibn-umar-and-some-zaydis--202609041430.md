Own-words summary of
[[tabrisi-majma-al-bayan-on-2-221-and-5-5--202609041405]]. Translations are this
repository's own working renderings; the Arabic is printed beside every one that
carries weight.

**The position first, in the same words as al-Tusi's and with the same marker.**

> وهي **عامة عندنا** في تحريم مناكحة جميع الكفار من أهل الكتاب وغيرهم وليست
> بمنسوخة ولا مخصوصة

It is **general in our view** in prohibiting marriage with all the unbelievers,
of the People of the Book and others; and it is neither abrogated nor
restricted.

Al-Tabrisi writes a century after al-Tusi and from him, so on the position
itself he is corroboration rather than an independent witness. What he adds is
the thing al-Tusi does not supply.

**He maps the whole dispute and says whose each position is.** The first
division is over the word:

> فقال بعضهم **لا يقع اسم المشركات على أهل الكتاب** وقد فضل الله بينهما فقال
> { لم يكن الذين كفروا من أهل الكتاب والمشركين } و { ما يود الذين كفروا من أهل
> الكتاب ولا المشركين } وعطف أحدهما على الآخر **فلا نسخ في الآية ولا تخصيص**

Some said the name *mushrikat* does not fall on the People of the Book — God
distinguished the two, saying [Q 98:1] and [Q 2:105], coordinating one with the
other — so there is neither abrogation nor restriction in the verse.

This is the coordination argument this base already holds from the Sunni side in
[[the-qurans-own-syntax-is-the-classical-proof-that-the-people-of-the-book-are-not-mushrikun--202609041320]],
and its appearance here is worth registering carefully: it reaches "no
abrogation" by the opposite route from al-Tabrisi's own. On this reading the
verse never covered the women of the Book, so nothing had to be abrogated out of
it. On his, it covers them and stays.

The second division is among those who take the verse to reach everyone —
`الآية متناولة جميع الكفار والشرك يطلق على الكل`, the verse takes in all the
unbelievers and *shirk* is predicated of them all — and it is a three-way split
with names attached:

> فمنهم من قال إن الآية **منسوخة** ... عن ابن عباس والحسن ومجاهد، ومنهم من قال
> إنها **مخصوصة** بغير الكتابيات عن قتادة وسعيد بن جبير ومنهم من قال أنها **على
> ظاهرها** في تحريم نكاح كل كافرة كتابية كانت أو مشركة عن **ابن عمر وبعض
> الزيدية**، وهو **مذهبنا**

Some said the verse is abrogated — from Ibn 'Abbas, al-Hasan and Mujahid; some
said it is restricted to non-Scripturary women — from Qatada and Sa'id b.
Jubayr; and some said it stands on its plain sense, forbidding marriage to every
unbelieving woman, whether of the Book or an idolatress — from **Ibn 'Umar and
some of the Zaydis** — and that is **our school's** position.

Three things sit in that one sentence. The three-way split is the same one
al-Qurtubi reports and this base already holds. **Ibn 'Umar** is the same
dissenting Companion the base has from Malik's *Muwatta'*, arriving now through
an Imami chain. And `بعض الزيدية` — *some of the Zaydis* — is word for word the
phrase the modern al-Sabuni uses, written here in the sixth/twelfth century by a
Shi'i author.

**On Q 5:5, the same reading down of the permission, with the school named
differently.**

> وقال **أصحابنا**: لا يجوز عقد نكاح الدوام على الكتابية لقوله تعالى: { ولا
> تنكحوا المشركات حتى يؤمن } ولقوله: { لا تمسكوا بعصم الكوافر }

And **our companions** said: a contract of permanent marriage with a woman of
the Book is not permitted, because of [Q 2:221] and [Q 60:10].

He gives the same two *ta'wils* al-Tusi gives — that the women meant are those
who converted (`اللاتي أسلمن منهن`, again credited to Abu al-Qasim al-Balkhi),
or that the permission is confined to *mut'a* and concubinage,
`فإن عندنا يجوز وطؤهن بكلا الوجهين` — for in our view intercourse with them by
both routes is permitted. The same qualification therefore applies to him as to
al-Tusi: what is forbidden is the permanent contract, not every relation.

**What is not here.** He does not repeat al-Tusi's *'illa* argument — that the
Qur'an's stated reason, "they call to the Fire", carries over to the *dhimmi*
woman — and he does not raise al-Tusi's question about how a monotheist
unbeliever comes to be called a *mushrik*. The *Majma'* is the better witness to
who held what; the *Tibyan* is the better witness to why.
