Own-words summary of [[quran-shared-god-passages--202609040056]]. This note is
the counterpart to
[[the-quran-asserts-tawhid-and-names-the-formula-it-rejects--202608311933]] and
should be read with it; neither capture is the whole picture and the base now
holds both.

**29:46 is the verse.** In Pickthall's rendering the instruction is not to
dispute with the People of the Scripture except in the better way, and then to
say: "We believe in that which hath been revealed unto us and revealed unto
you; our Allah and your Allah is One, and unto Him we surrender." Three things
are worth separating in that sentence. It is a *command about speech* — the
Qur'an tells Muslims what to say to Jews and Christians. It affirms a shared
God in the plainest available terms. And it does so in the same breath as
affirming shared revelation, so the oneness claimed is not merely numerical
("there happens to be one God") but identifying ("the one you were given is the
one we were given").

The immediate context does not undercut this. 29:45 is about prayer and
remembrance; 29:47 says that those given the Scripture before will believe in
what was revealed to Muhammad, and that some among "these" believe also. The
surah is Meccan. The verse sits in a passage about continuity with earlier
revelation rather than in a polemical passage, which matters, because the
verses in the first capture (4:171, 5:73) are polemical and were captured for
that reason.

**The qualification inside 29:46 is real and should not be dropped.** The
instruction to argue in the better way is given "save with such of them as do
wrong" — the concession is not unconditional, and the verse is addressed to a
situation of dispute, not of agreement. What it does is regulate the dispute
and, in doing so, hand Muslims a formula of shared reference to use in it.

**3:64** is the invitation to the People of the Scripture to come to an
agreement — Pickthall's word here is "agreement", where the 2007 letter's
translation has "a common word" — on three terms: worship of God alone, that
"we shall ascribe no partner unto Him", and that none take others as lords
beside God. Read beside 29:46, it shows the two registers running together: the
same address to the same audience can affirm a shared God and stipulate a
condition that bears against trinitarian language.

**2:136 and 5:48** are the frame. 2:136 has Muslims profess belief in what was
revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, the tribes, Moses and Jesus, "We
make no distinction between any of them". 5:48 supplies the limit from within
the same scripture: "For each We have appointed a law and a way. Had God willed
He could have made you one community. But that He may try you by that which He
hath given you (He hath made you as ye are). So vie one with another in good
works." Difference of law and way is presented as intended, not as failure —
which is a different thing from difference of God.

**What this note does not establish.** That the Qur'an contains 29:46 does not
settle how any school of Islamic theology or law has read it, and this capture
contains no commentary at all. The classification of Christians in the juristic
tradition — whether *ahl al-kitab* status coexists with *shirk*, and what
follows either way — is not addressed by any verse captured here, and Pickthall
is a translation whose choices ("agreement" at 3:64, "Allah" left untranslated
at 29:46 where the same word is rendered "God" by others) are doing visible
work in exactly the places this note leans on.
