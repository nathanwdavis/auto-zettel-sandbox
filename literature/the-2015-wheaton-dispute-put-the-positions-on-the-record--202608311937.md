Own-words summary of [[gjelten-do-christians-and-muslims-worship-the-same-god--202608311927]], which is used only for the directly
attributed statements of its named speakers.

The occasion: Wheaton College placed a political science professor on paid
administrative leave after she wore a headscarf in solidarity with Muslims
during Advent and, quoting Pope Francis, said Christians and Muslims "worship
the same God."

Four positions, each argued on a different basis:

- **Albert Mohler** (Southern Baptist Theological Seminary) argues
  christologically: "whether one can reject Jesus Christ as the Son and truly
  know God the Father," and holds that Christ settled it -- "to reject the Son
  means that one does not know the Father."
- **Zeki Saritoprak** (John Carroll University) argues genealogically from the
  Qur'an's own account of Jacob's sons: "this God that Jacob worshipped, this
  God that Abraham, Isaac worshipped, is the same God that Muslims worship
  today."
- **Amy Plantinga Pauw** (Louisville Seminary) draws the distinction the dispute
  keeps failing to draw: "To say that we worship the same God is not the same as
  insisting that we have an agreed and shared understanding of God."
- **Hamza Yusuf** (Zaytuna College) answers apophatically: "Whatever you imagine
  God to be, God is other than that."

Wheaton's own statement shows the pressure the question puts on an institution:
"our compassion must be infused with theological clarity."
