**Grounding: the raw capture of Ramban's comment in Chavel's English from the Sefaria API, read whole. The Hebrew is not captured (Sefaria declares its licence unknown), so no Hebrew is quoted here.**

**Two readings reported.** Ramban opens with the reading he attributes to "the commentators" (Chavel's note names Ibn Ezra): "It is for the Eternal our G-d to execute judgment upon those who worship idols secretly, for all hidden matters are revealed before Him, but the things that are revealed are upon us and our children that we may do to them all the words of this Law", that is, to punish idolaters by the law of the Torah. He adds that the Rabbis read it the same way: "According to the interpretation of the Rabbis, too, it is so." Chavel's note refers this to Sanhedrin 43b.

**His own reading.** "By way of the simple meaning of Scripture it is my opinion that the secret things are the sins that are hidden from those who commit them", with Psalm 19:13 as the proof, "Who can discern errors? Cleanse Thou me from hidden faults." So the verse says, in his paraphrase: "[Our] hidden sins belong to G-d alone; for them we bear no guilt for having transgressed [unknowingly]. But the things that are revealed, namely our conscious sins, belong unto us and to our children forever". The point of the verse, on this reading, is to limit the covenant curse: because the oath bound Israel to perform all the commandments, "he excluded from the ban whoever commits a sin unintentionally". His witness is Onkelos, who rendered the first clause "The secret things are before the Eternal our G-d," where the commentators' reading would have required the preposition "to".

**What differs, and what does not.** Ramban moves the concealment from the community to the sinner: the hidden sin is not one done in secret but one the doer does not know he did. That is a reading on which the verse turns on knowledge, but the knower whose ignorance matters is the human sinner, not God, and the object is still a sin and the consequence still a question of liability. On every reading he reports or holds, the verse partitions responsibility for wrongdoing; none of them makes the "secret things" a body of knowledge God has withheld.
