**Grounding: excerpt-only, from cross-verified secondary sources (raw/202609050546-grudem-trinity-definition-excerpts.txt); not full-text reading of the 2nd edition.**

Grudem formulates the Trinity doctrine as three theological claims: (1) God eternally exists as three persons—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; (2) each person is fully God; (3) there is one God. This formulation holds together the trine plurality of persons with the oneness of the Godhead.

**Gap: The full text would clarify what Grudem means by "eternally exists" and whether he defends a timeless eternal generation of the Son or a different model of eternal relation. The excerpts do not show Grudem's scriptural support for each of the three claims, nor do they reveal his handling of the classical problem of divine simplicity (unity of essence) versus real distinction of persons—a central theological tension in historical treatments of the Trinity. One review of the 2nd edition (spoiledmilks.com, pages 305-307) reports that Grudem teaches the Son maintains eternal submission to the Father's authority while remaining equal in deity and honor, and that the three persons share one will expressed in three ways, but this lead is unconfirmed by a second independent witness and should not be cited as Grudem's stated position without direct verification from the text.**
