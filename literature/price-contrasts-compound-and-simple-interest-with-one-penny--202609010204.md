Own-words summary of pp. 18–19 of
[[price-an-appeal-to-the-public-on-the-national-debt--202609010203]]:
Price makes the difference between compound and simple interest vivid with a
single thought experiment. The same penny, lent at the same 5 per cent for
the same ~1,772 years, ends as either an astronomical sum or pocket change —
the only variable being whether the interest is reinvested. He stresses the
shape of the curve: compounding starts slowly and accelerates, so its late
values defeat intuition. And he draws an institutional moral: individual
lives are too short to collect much of this acceleration, while long-lived
polities can, because 50–100 years is "little in the duration of kingdoms."

- "Money bearing compound interest increases at first slowly. But, the rate
  of increase being continually accelerated, it becomes in some time so
  rapid, as to mock all the powers of the imagination." (p. 18)
- "One penny, put out at our Saviour's birth to 5 per cent. compound
  interest, would, before this time, have increased to a greater sum, than
  would be contained in a hundred and fifty millions of earths, all solid
  gold. — But if put out to simple interest, it would, in the same time,
  have amounted to no more than seven shillings and four pence half-penny."
  (pp. 18–19)
- "The duration of the lives of individuals is confined within limits so
  narrow, as not to admit, in any great degree, of the advantages that may
  be derived from this increase. But a period of 50 or 60 or 100 years being
  little in the duration of kingdoms, they are capable of securing them in
  almost any degree" (p. 18)

The penny quote directly supports [[reinvested-returns-compound--202701010011]]
(reinvestment is the mechanism separating the two outcomes) and
[[linear-growth-lacks-a-feedback-loop--202701010012]] (simple interest, the
no-feedback case, stays negligible over even eighteen centuries). Price's
argumentative purpose is not curiosity but policy: the pamphlet argues a
sinking fund left to compound could redeem the national debt.
