Own-words summary of [[nae-statement-of-faith--202608311920]].

The statement's second article is the direct answer to "Who is God?": there is
one God, "eternally existent in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit."
Oneness and threeness are asserted together, in one clause, with no attempt to
soften either.

What the rest of the statement does is worth noticing as much as that clause. Of
seven articles, three are given directly to the Son and the Spirit (arts. 3-5):
the deity of Christ, his virgin birth, sinless life, miracles, "vicarious and
atoning death," bodily resurrection, ascension and personal return; then the
Spirit's necessity for regeneration, and his indwelling ministry. A fourth names
Christ again, in "the spiritual unity of believers in our Lord Jesus Christ."
And the first article puts the Bible, "the inspired, the only infallible,
authoritative Word of God," ahead of the doctrine of God itself.

So the identification is not a list of attributes that any monotheism could
share. God is identified by naming a tri-personal life, by a particular history
of acts, and by a particular text taken as the authority for knowing him.
