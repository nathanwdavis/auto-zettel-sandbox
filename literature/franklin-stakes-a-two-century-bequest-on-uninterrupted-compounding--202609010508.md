Own-words summary of the codicil (pp. 501–510) in
[[franklin-last-will-and-testament-and-codicil--202609010507]]: Franklin
converts his unspent presidential salary into a designed compounding
machine. Each of two £1,000 funds — one for Boston, one for Philadelphia —
is to be lent at 5 per cent to young married artificers fresh out of
apprenticeship; each borrower repays a tenth of the principal yearly with
interest, and the repayments are immediately relent to fresh borrowers, so
the loop's output is fed straight back into its input. Franklin states the
projections himself: £131,000 after the first hundred years (most of it then
spent on public works, £31,000 relent), and £4,061,000 at year two hundred.
The scheme is deliberately staged, is expected to run on volunteer
management, and — notably — its own author names uninterrupted execution,
not the arithmetic, as the thing that could fail: he hedges with fallback
clauses (either city refusing, both refusing) and closes by conceding the
projection may be a "vain fancy" given two centuries of accidents.

Verbatim quotations, taken from the Smyth-edition block of the raw capture
and normalized as follows: the OCR's double word-spacing is collapsed,
line-break hyphenation joined, running heads omitted, and three clear OCR
misreads of "in" as "hi" (pp. 503, 505) corrected; where the Smyth text and
the shared web-transcription lineage differ, the quote follows Smyth and
the capture records the variant ("and of all the utility" vs. "and all the
utility"; "hereinafter" vs. "herein after"):

- "I wish to be useful even after my death, if possible, in forming and
  advancing other young men, that may be serviceable to their country in
  both these towns." (p. 503)
- "To this end, I devote two thousand pounds sterling, of which I give one
  thousand thereof to the inhabitants of the town of Boston, in
  Massachusetts, and the other thousand to the inhabitants of the city of
  Philadelphia, in trust, to and for the uses, intents, and purposes
  hereinafter mentioned and declared." (p. 503)
- the managers are to "let out the sum upon interest, at five per cent, per
  annum, to such young married artificers, under the age of twenty-five
  years" (p. 503), and "each borrower shall be obliged to pay, with the
  yearly interest, one tenth part of the principal" (p. 504)
- "it is hoped that no part of the money will at any time be dead, or be
  diverted to other purposes, but be continually augmenting by the
  interest" (p. 505)
- "If this plan is executed, and succeeds as projected without interruption
  for one hundred years, the sum will then be one hundred and thirty-one
  thousand pounds" (p. 505)
- "At the end of this second term, if no unfortunate accident has prevented
  the operation, the sum will be four millions and sixty one thousand
  pounds sterling" (p. 505)
- "Considering the accidents to which all human affairs and projects are
  subject in such a length of time, I have, perhaps, too much flattered
  myself with a vain fancy that these dispositions, if carried into
  execution, will be continued without interruption and have the effects
  proposed." (p. 507)

The relending of repayments to fresh borrowers is an explicit reinvestment
loop, supporting [[reinvested-returns-compound--202701010011]]; the whole
design — a person arranging for institutions to collect growth he cannot
live to see — is a real enactment of
[[compounding-favors-horizons-longer-than-one-life--202609010205]], and the
codicil's own conditionals ("without
interruption", "if no unfortunate accident") ground the constraint note
built on it.
