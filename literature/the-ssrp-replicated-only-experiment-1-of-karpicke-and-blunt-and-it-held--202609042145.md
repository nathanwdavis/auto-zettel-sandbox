Summary of
[[the-ssrp-replicated-only-experiment-1-of-karpicke-and-blunt-and-it-held--202609042145]]'s
source — the SSRP's own per-study replication report — in my own words. This is
the document the base's Camerer et al. 2018 note
([[the-ssrp-replicated-one-result-per-paper-and-said-so-in-its-limitations--202609041900]])
said it did not have; the per-study numbers the summary Letter withholds are all
here.

**What was targeted.** Exactly one result: Karpicke & Blunt's Experiment 1
(Study 1), and within it the single comparison of Retrieval Practice against
Concept Mapping on the one-week short-answer test — the M = 0.67 vs M = 0.45
result, which the original reported as F(1,38) = 21.63, p = 0.000039, r = 0.602.
The report says plainly it followed the project protocol to "select the first
study in the paper reporting treatment effects," so **Experiment 2 was not
tested** — not the within-subject design, not the enumeration/sequence texts,
not the final concept-map test, not the 84% per-subject advantage. It also
dropped the study-once and repeated-study conditions and compared only the two
conditions of interest with a t-test rather than the original's ANOVA.

**What happened.** It replicated at the first stage. The replicators (Buttrick,
Gampa, Hummer, Nosek) collected 52 participants at the University of Virginia
(2016-2017), analysed 49 after three no-shows for the one-week session: 23 in
retrieval, 26 in concept mapping. Retrieval recalled M = 62.3% (SD 19%) vs
concept mapping M = 46.9% (SD 19%), t(47) = 2.8825, p = 0.006, r = 0.38
[95% CI 0.12, 0.60]. Same direction, p < 0.05, so by the project's criterion it
replicated and no second-stage collection was needed. Two of the original's
secondary findings also reappeared: initial learning did not differ reliably
between conditions (p = 0.097), and the metacognitive reversal held — the
concept-mapping group predicted remembering 69% and the retrieval group only
57.8% (p = 0.025), the wrong way round.

**How big, and how the replicators read it.** The replication effect was smaller
than the original (r = 0.39 vs r = 0.60), but the original point estimate fell
within the replication's confidence interval, and the report's verdict is a
"successful replication" that "adds confidence and precision to the original
observation, and suggests generalizability to a new sample and setting." There
were no unplanned protocol deviations. The one planned deviation of note:
because the original E-Prime program could not be recovered, the replication ran
on paper rather than screen, a change the original authors agreed should not
matter for the effect.

**The boundary this source draws.** This is a single direct replication of the
first experiment of a two-experiment paper. It confirms that Karpicke & Blunt's
central retrieval-over-concept-mapping effect is real, on a fresh sample, at
about two-thirds the original magnitude. It says nothing, and by design could
say nothing, about the paper's Experiment 2 or about retrieval practice as a
programme — the limit the base already stated in the abstract in
[[a-replication-project-that-tests-one-study-per-paper-cannot-settle-the-paper--202609041905]].
