Own-words summary of [[maimonides-mishneh-torah-yesodei-hatorah--202608311922]].

Maimonides takes up exactly the question the Shema leaves open: granted that God
is one, what sort of one? His answer is that God is "unified in a manner which
[surpasses] any unity that is found in the world," and he rules out the two
worldly models by name. God is not one the way a general category is one while
containing many individuals, and not one the way a body is one while divisible
into portions and dimensions.

His argument for this is compact: if there were many gods they would have body
and form, because like entities are distinguished from one another only by
circumstances of body and form -- and a creator with body and form would be
bounded, which he takes as impossible for God.

The upshot for comparison is that this is a positive claim about God's internal
structure, not merely a count. Any account on which the one God contains real
internal distinctions has to answer it.
