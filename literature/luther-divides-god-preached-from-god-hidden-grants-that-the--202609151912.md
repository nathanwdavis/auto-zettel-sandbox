**Grounding: the raw capture of CCEL's page for Section LXIV, read entire, together with the two sections before it (LXII-LXIII, read but not captured) for the context of the Ezekiel dispute.**

**The occasion.** Erasmus had argued from Ezekiel 18:23 that if God does not desire the sinner's death, the sinner's perishing must lie with the sinner's own will, and had asked whether "the righteous Lord" would "deplore that death of His people, which He Himself works in them". Luther's answer is that the verse belongs to one register and the question to another. Ezekiel "is speaking of THE PREACHED AND OFFERED MERCY OF GOD, not of that SECRET AND TO BE FEARED WILL OF GOD, who, according to His own counsel, ordains whom, and such as He will, to be receivers and partakers of the preached and offered mercy: which WILL, is not to be curiously inquired into, but to be adored with reverence as the most profound SECRET of the divine Majesty, which He reserves unto Himself and keeps hidden from us".

**The distinction.** "we are to argue in one way, concerning the WILL OF GOD preached, revealed, and offered unto us, and worshipped by us; and in another, concerning GOD HIMSELF not preached, not revealed, not offered unto us, and worshipped by us." (The capture repeats "and worshipped by us" in the second clause; the sense requires "not".) The rule attached to it is severance rather than reverence: "In whatever, therefore, God hides Himself and will be unknown by us, that is nothing unto us", with the maxim "What is above us, does not concern us." Luther claims the distinction is Paul's, from 2 Thessalonians 2:4, "above all that is God, as preached and worshipped", and states it a second time as a rule about where the creature stands: "God, therefore, is to be left to remain in His own Nature and Majesty; for in this respect, we have nothing to do with Him, nor does He wish us to have, in this respect, anything to do with Him: but we have to do with Him, as far as He is clothed in, and delivered to us by, His Word".

**Two wills, one of them hidden.** "GOD PREACHED desires this: — that, our sin and death being taken away, we might be saved". But "GOD HIDDEN IN MAJESTY neither deplores, nor takes away death, but works life and death and all things: nor has He, in this Character, defined Himself in His Word, but has reserved unto Himself, a free power over all things." Erasmus's error is "not making a distinction between GOD PREACHED and GOD HIDDEN: that is, between the word of God and God Himself. God does many things which He does not make known unto us in His word: He also wills many things which He does not in His word make known unto us that He wills." So God does not will the sinner's death in his word, "but He wills it by that will inscrutable".

**What may be known of the hidden will.** Exactly one thing, its existence: "It is enough to know only, that there is in God a certain will inscrutable: but what, why, and how far that will wills, it is not lawful to inquire, to wish to know, to be concerned about, or to reach unto — it is only to be feared and adored!" The reason given is practical, "for who can direct himself according to a will inscrutable and incomprehensible?", and the closing sentence on the why of it is Paul's "Who art thou that repliest against God!" with the warning that "though you should inquire much, yet you will never find out".

**What this supplies.** The partition is of God's *will* (preached against hidden, word against decree) rather than of knowledge as such, but it yields a doctrine of hidden knowledge at once: the hidden will is a known unknown whose existence is affirmed and whose content is placed under a prohibition. It is the same shape as the secret/revealed will this base holds from Grudem, three centuries earlier and stated harder, and it differs from Calvin's fence in one word: where Calvin says adore, Luther says the hidden God is "nothing unto us" and is "not ... worshipped by us".

**Limits.** Cole's English of 1823, with Atherton's silent alterations; the Latin is not on file, so *Deus absconditus* and *Deus praedicatus* are supplied from the literature and are not in the capture. One section of a long book, read for one distinction.
