Summary of
[[pyc-and-rawson-why-testing-improves-memory-mediator-effectiveness-hypothesis--202609042320]]
in my own words. This note reports only what the paper's own authoritative
abstract states of itself; the article's full text was not obtainable, so the
finer design and the exact statistic are reported in the companion note on the
project's replication report rather than claimed here.

**The question the paper set.** By 2010 the *effect* was settled — practice
tests improve later memory for the tested material — but the *mechanism* was
not. Pyc and Rawson propose the **mediator effectiveness hypothesis**: what a
successful test-plus-restudy practice does that plain restudy does not is
generate better *mediators*, the intermediate bits of information that link a
cue to its target. A keyword that sounds like the foreign word and points to its
meaning is such a mediator.

**The two components of an effective mediator.** The hypothesis is stated as a
pair of necessary conditions, and this is its analytic content: a mediator earns
its keep only if it can itself be *retrieved* at test time, and, once retrieved,
if it actually *elicits the target response*. A mediator that cannot be recalled,
or one that is recalled but fails to call up the answer, buys nothing. The claim
is therefore not "testing helps" but "testing helps by improving both of these
specific properties of the link the learner forms."

**What was measured, and the result.** The two components were evaluated for
learning foreign-language translations under two practice schedules —
test-restudy versus restudy-only. Supporting the hypothesis, test-restudy
practice produced mediators that were *both* more likely to be retrieved at the
final test *and* more likely to elicit the target once retrieved. So the benefit
of testing is localised to the quality of the cue-to-target links it builds,
rather than left as an unexplained property of "testing."

**Scope, for this base.** The abstract fixes the paradigm (paired-associate
foreign-vocabulary learning, mediators reported by the learner) and the
direction of the effect, but not its size or its durability; those are in the
full text this capture could not reach and, for the one comparison the SSRP
tested, in the replication report. Nothing here concerns note-taking, external
memory, or retention beyond the single delayed test the paradigm uses.
