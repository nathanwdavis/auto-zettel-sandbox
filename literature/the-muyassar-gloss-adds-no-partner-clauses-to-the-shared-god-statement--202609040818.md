Own-words summary of [[al-tafsir-al-muyassar-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609040808]].
English renderings are this repository's own working translations.

**On Q 29:46.** The commentary paraphrases the verse in a single sentence, and
where the Qur'an says "and our God and your God is one" it writes: `وإلهنا
وإلهكم واحد لا شريك له في ألوهيته، ولا في ربوبيته، ولا في أسمائه وصفاته` — and
our God and your God is one, *having no partner in His divinity, nor in His
lordship, nor in His names and attributes*. Everything after "one" is the
commentary's addition; the Qur'anic clause ends at `واحد`.

The three qualifications are the standard tripartite division of *tawhid* in
modern Salafi teaching. That trinitarian doctrine falls under the third of them
is this repository's own gloss and is not stated in the capture; it is why the
expansion reads as pointed, but nothing here rests on it.

So the gloss does not deny that the God named is the same. It takes a
clause whose surface says *one and the same* and immediately spends its
expansion on what may not be predicated of Him, in terms that the addressees of
the verse are held to violate.

The rest of the paraphrase runs the same way as the classical commentators: do
not dispute with the Jews and Christians except in the best manner, except
those who turned from the truth, were obstinate and declared war on you, `فجالدوهم
بالسيف حتى يؤمنوا، أو يعطوا الجزية عن يدٍ وهم صاغرون` — fight them with the
sword until they believe or give the *jizya* by hand in a state of
subjection — and "we are submitters to Him" is glossed as humbly obedient in
what He commanded and forbade.

**On Q 3:64.** "Come to a word of justice and truth which we all hold to:
that we single out God alone for worship, and take no partner with Him,
`من وثن أو صنم أو صليب أو طاغوت أو غير ذلك` — no image, no idol, no *cross*, no
*taghut* or anything else; and that none of us render obedience to another
instead of God." If they turn away from `هذه الدعوة الطيبة` (this good call),
say: bear witness that we are Muslims, compliant to our Lord in servitude and
sincerity. The call, it adds, is directed to whoever stands in the same position
as the Jews and Christians.

**What this source is good for and what it is not.** It is a paraphrase, not an
argument, and it reports no dissent, so it cannot show what the tradition
debated. What it can show is what the gloss on this clause looks like in the
text most widely distributed with the Qur'an today: the shared-God clause is
not passed over, as in the classical three, and it is not read as an
identification of what two parties worship, as in al-Tabari. It is turned into
an assertion of *tawhid* against partners — with the cross named among the
partners in the neighbouring verse.
