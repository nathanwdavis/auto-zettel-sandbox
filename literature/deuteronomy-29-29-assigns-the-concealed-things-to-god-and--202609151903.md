**Grounding: raw/202609151849-deuteronomy-29-29-hebrew-kjv-jps.txt, the verse in the Masoretic Hebrew (Miqra according to the Masorah, via Sefaria), the King James Version (via bible-api.com) and the 2023 JPS Tanakh (via Sefaria). Nothing outside that one verse is used here.**

**The shape of the verse.** Two clauses, each built on a plural niphal participle used without a noun and a *lamed* of possession. The first is הַנִּסְתָּרֹת (*ha-nistarot*; the KJV's "The secret things", the JPS's "Concealed acts"), assigned "unto the LORD our God"; the second is הַנִּגְלֹת (*ha-niglot*; KJV "those things which are revealed", JPS "overt acts"), assigned "unto us and to our children", with עַד־עוֹלָם (KJV "for ever") and then a purpose clause introduced by לַעֲשׂוֹת (KJV "that we may do"). The transliterations are this note's; the glosses are the two translations' and not the note's own. The purpose clause belongs to the second half only: it is the revealed things that are given so that all the words of this Torah may be done. The concealed things receive no purpose clause and no content. The verse says that they exist and whose they are, and nothing else about them.

**What the participles leave open.** Neither participle has a head noun, so what is concealed and what is revealed is not stated in the Hebrew. English translation has to supply it, and the two witnesses captured here supply different things. The KJV reads "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law." The JPS Tanakh reads "Concealed acts concern the ETERNAL our God; but with overt acts, it is for us and our children ever to apply all the provisions of this Teaching." Where the KJV's "things" leaves the reference open, and so lets the verse be read of knowledge, the JPS's "acts" settles it on conduct. The choice is not forced by the grammar; it is an interpretive decision made in translation, and the rabbinic reading behind the JPS choice is recorded in [[rashi-on-deuteronomy-29-28-the-secret-things-and-the--202609151851]].

**The extraordinary points.** The Masoretic text carries dots over לָנוּ וּלְבָנֵינוּ עַד (the words the KJV renders "unto us and to our children" and the first word of its "for ever"); the capture preserves them as combining marks. They are one of the ten (by some counts fifteen) places in the Torah so marked, and the tradition treats them as a signal that the marked words are to be interpreted, which is where Rashi's second comment starts.

**What is and is not here.** The verse is a partition of what belongs to whom, with an assignment of duty on the human side. It does not say whether the concealed things are concealed for a time or for ever (the "for ever" is on the revealed side); it does not say whether they could be known in principle; and it does not say what they are. Every use of this verse as an epistemology of divine hiddenness, Grudem's and Calvin's included, supplies those three things from outside the verse.
