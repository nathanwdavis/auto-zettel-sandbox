Own-words summary of [[beckwith-why-muslims-and-christians-worship-the-same-god--202609010832]].

The follow-up, after critical replies from Mohler, Walker, Cochran, and
Leithart. Beckwith concedes their documentation of Islam's theological
inadequacies from a Christian standpoint and claims it is "actually consistent
with my argument."

The machinery is the distinction between general revelation (truths about God
available to unaided reason) and special revelation (truths available only
through scripture or tradition). Natural theology -- his exhibit is the kalam
cosmological argument, which he credits to the Muslim philosopher al-Ghazali --
delivers by reason alone "the uncaused, perfect, unchanging, self-subsistent,
eternal Creator." His thought experiment: Abdullah, an atheist persuaded by
the argument, believes in this Creator; he then joins a mosque and adds
Qur'anic doctrines (no Trinity, begets not). A Christian adds trinitarian
doctrines from the Bible. They still worship the same God, "but with a
caveat: ... they cannot both be right about the Trinity and the Incarnation" --
the disagreement lives entirely in the rival special revelations.

The reference theory is made explicit by the Superman case: Lois Lane loves
Kal-El and thinks him non-human; Lana Lang loves Clark Kent and thinks him
human; they love the same man, because "there is only one being that is
essentially Kal-El." Likewise "there is only one being that is essentially
God." Reference rides on the essence being unique, not on the lovers'
descriptions being correct.

Worth flagging: an aside grants that William Lane Craig is "not a classical
theist," which Beckwith says raises "the even more awkward question of whether
all Christians worship the same God" -- the uniqueness argument cuts within
traditions as well as across them.
