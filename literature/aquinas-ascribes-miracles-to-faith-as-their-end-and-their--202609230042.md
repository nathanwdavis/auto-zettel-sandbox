Own-words summary of [[summa-theologiae-secunda-secundae-question-178-the-gift-of--202609230037]].

Article 1 files the working of miracles among the gratuitous graces of
1 Cor 12, by which "the Holy Ghost provides sufficiently for the Church in
matters profitable unto salvation". Their job is to make preaching credible. The spoken word "needs to be confirmed in order that it be rendered
credible", and miracles do this (Mark 16:20). Aquinas treats that as
continuous with natural theology: just as natural reason reaches "some
knowledge of God through His natural effects", so a person is "brought to a
certain degree of supernatural knowledge of the objects of faith by certain
supernatural effects which are called miracles" (a.1 co.).

The miracle-worker is never the cause. The cause of a miracle is "the divine
omnipotence which cannot be communicated to any creature", so the power
cannot be "a quality abiding as a habit in the soul". What happens is that
the worker's mind "be moved to do something resulting in the miraculous
effect which God causes by His power", sometimes after prayer and sometimes
"by authority" without it (Gregory). In both cases "God is the principal
worker", who uses a man's inward movement, speech, gesture, or even a dead
body's bones as an instrument (a.1 ad 1).

Objection 5 puts the faith question directly: miracles "result from faith",
either the worker's (1 Cor 13:2, faith that removes mountains) or that of the
people "for whose sake miracles are wrought" (Matt 13:58, "He wrought not
many miracles there, because of their unbelief"). So why count a separate
grace of miracles? The reply gives two reasons why "the working of miracles
is ascribed to faith": "because it is directed to the confirmation of
faith", and "because it proceeds from God's omnipotence on which faith
relies". Faith is therefore not enough by itself. Just as the grace of the
word is needed besides the grace of faith, "the grace of miracles [is]
necessary that people may be confirmed in their faith" (a.1 ad 5).

Article 2 grants that the wicked can work true miracles "for the
confirmation of truth declared", because the miracle answers the invoking of
Christ's name, not the worker's merit (Jerome on Matt 7:22). Only miracles
worked "in proof of a person's holiness" are confined to the saints. That
settles the faith question from the other side. To the objection that dead
faith (Jas 2:20) cannot work, Aquinas answers that "nothing hinders a living
thing from working through a dead instrument, as a man through a stick. It
is thus that God works while employing instrumentally the faith of a sinner"
(a.2 ad 2).

Scope: q.178 only, as captured. Aquinas's general treatment of miracles
(ST I q.105 and q.110) is not on file.
