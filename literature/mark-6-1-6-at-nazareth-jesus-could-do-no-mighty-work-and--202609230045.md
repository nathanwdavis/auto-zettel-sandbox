Own-words summary of [[mark-6-1-6-no-mighty-work-there-because-of-their-unbelief--202609230040]].

In his home town the synagogue hearers are "astonished" at Jesus' teaching
and at "such mighty works ... wrought by his hands", yet they take offence
because they know his family: "Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary?"
(6:2-3). Jesus answers with the proverb of the prophet without honour in his
own country (6:4). Then comes the verse that matters here: "he could there
do no mighty work, save that he laid his hands upon a few sick folk, and
healed them. And he marvelled because of their unbelief" (6:5-6).

Three details should be kept precise. First, Mark says "could" (inability),
not "would not". Second, the exception is real: a few sick people are
healed. Third, Mark sets the inability and the unbelief side by side without
the explicit causal "because" that Matthew's parallel supplies (13:58,
quoted in [[aquinas-ascribes-miracles-to-faith-as-their-end-and-their--202609230042]]).
The unbelief in question belongs to the townspeople, the would-be
beneficiaries, not to the miracle-worker.

Scope: Mark 6:1-6 in the KJV only. The Synoptic sayings in which faith is
credited with a healing ("thy faith hath made thee whole", Mark 5:34, 10:52)
are not captured.
