Own-words summary of [[maimonides-kings-and-wars-8-10-11--202609042022]].

The seven commandments given to Noah's descendants — against idolatry,
blasphemy, murder, theft, forbidden unions and eating flesh from a living
animal, and the requirement to establish courts — are the standard by which
gentiles are judged, and Maimonides states the standing they earn in 8:11.

The halachah then attaches a condition that is not about conduct at all:
"Anyone who accepts upon himself the fulfillment of these seven mitzvot and is
precise in their observance is considered one of 'the pious among the gentiles'
and will merit a share in the world to come. This applies only when he accepts
them and fulfills them because the Holy One, blessed be He, commanded them in
the Torah and informed us through Moses, our teacher, that Noah's descendants
had been commanded to fulfill them previously. However, if he fulfills them out
of intellectual conviction, he is not a resident alien, nor of 'the pious among
the gentiles,' nor of their wise men."

Two gentiles may therefore behave identically — same seven prohibitions, same
precision — and stand in different categories, because one takes them as
commanded through Moses and the other has reasoned his way to them. What
separates them is neither the act nor the object but the *ground*: whose
authority the obedience is referred to.

The condition is famously hard, and Maimonides' own reading of it has been
disputed since; what is recorded here is only the text of the halachah as it
stands in this translation.
