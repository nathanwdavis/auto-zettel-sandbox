Own-words summary of
[[saadi-taysir-al-karim-al-rahman-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609040806]]. The whole
comment on both verses was read; the capture holds the lemma-and-gloss
sequences summarised here. English renderings are this repository's own working
translations.

**On Q 29:46, the clause this cycle went looking for.** al-Sa'di prints the
lemma `{ وَقُولُوا آمَنَّا بِالَّذِي أُنْزِلَ إِلَيْنَا وَأُنْزِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ
وَإِلَهُنَا وَإِلَهُكُمْ وَاحِدٌ }` — including the shared-God clause — and
glosses the whole of it as a rule for how disputation is to be conducted: `ولتكن
مجادلتكم لأهل الكتاب مبنية على الإيمان بما أنزل إليكم وأنزل إليهم، وعلى الإيمان
برسولكم ورسولهم، وعلى أن الإله واحد` — let your disputation with the People of
the Book be built on belief in what was sent down to you and to them, on belief
in your messenger and theirs, and on *that the deity is one*.

The grammatical shape of that is the finding. Three things are named as what
the disputation is to be *built on*: two of them are acts of belief, and the
third is a proposition, `أن الإله واحد` — that the deity is one. He does not
say the two parties' object of worship is one; he says the oneness of the deity
is a premise the Muslim disputant must not argue in a way that undermines. What
follows confirms the direction: one must not, in disputing, impugn any divine
book or any messenger, as the ignorant do; the obligation is to reject the
falsehood the opponent holds and accept the truth he holds, `ولا يرد الحق لأجل
قوله، ولو كان كافرا` (and truth is not to be rejected because of who said it,
even if he is an unbeliever). And the purpose of proceeding this way, he says
plainly, is `إلزام لهم بالإقرار بالقرآن` — obliging them to acknowledge the
Qur'an and the messenger who brought it.

He glosses `{ وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ }` as `منقادون مستسلمون لأمره` —
compliant, submitting to His command — and adds that whoever believes in Him,
takes Him as God, believes in all His books and messengers and follows them is
the happy one, and whoever deviates from this way is the wretched one.

**On Q 3:64, where he uses the language of sharing.** al-Sa'di glosses `{ تعالوا
إلى كلمة سواء بيننا وبينكم }` as: come, let us gather on it — the word all the
prophets and messengers agreed on, which none opposed but the obstinate and the
astray, `ليست مختصة بأحدنا دون الآخر، بل مشتركة بيننا وبينكم` (not the property
of one of us to the exclusion of the other, but shared between us and you), and
`هذا من العدل في المقال والإنصاف في الجدال` — this is justice in speech and
fairness in dispute. Then he fills its content: worship God alone and associate
nothing with Him, `ولا نشرك به نبيا ولا ملكا ولا وليا ولا صنما ولا وثنا` — not a
prophet, not an angel, not a saint, not an image, not an idol; and obedience is
all to God and His messengers. If they answer the call, he says, they are like
you, with the same rights and duties; if they turn away, they are obstinate
followers of their whims, so bear witness to them that you are Muslims.

**The two verses pull in different directions in this commentary, and that is
worth recording as it stands.** On 3:64 he uses `مشتركة` — shared — of the
common word, which is the vocabulary the modern interfaith use of the verse
wants. On 29:46, where the Qur'an itself says our God and your God is one, he
converts the clause into a premise about the oneness of the deity and the
conduct of argument. The sharing he grants is a shared *word to be agreed to*,
whose content is the exclusion of every rival object of worship and whose
alternative is that the addressee is obstinate.
