Own-words summary of
[[tantawi-al-tafsir-al-wasit-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609041110]]. English
renderings are this repository's own working translations; the Arabic is printed
beside those that carry weight.

**Why this note exists at all.** The 08:00Z cycle found this gloss on one host
and deliberately declined to build on it. Two hosts now carry it, and they agree
against the third — see the reference note for the correction that came with the
cross-check.

**On Q 29:46, the clause.** After the address, and after quoting al-Shawkani on
the Torah and the Gospel (`وأنهما شريعة ثابتة إلى قيام الشريعة الإسلامية
والبعثة المحمدية ولا يدخل فى ذلك ما حرفوه وبدلوه` — the two were a standing law
until the rise of the Islamic law and the Muhammadan mission, and what they
altered and changed is not included in that):

> { وَإِلَـٰهُنَا وَإِلَـٰهُكُمْ وَاحِد } **لا شريك له لا فى ذاته ولا فى صفاته**
> { وَنَحْنُ } جميعاً معاشر المؤمنين { لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ } أى مطيعون وعابدون له
> وحده، ولا نتخذ أربابا من دونه - عز وجل -.

"And our God and your God is one": **He has no partner, neither in His essence
nor in His attributes**; "and we", all of us, the company of the believers, "are
submitted to Him": obedient, worshipping Him alone, and we take no lords apart
from Him.

Three things worth separating. First, the register is the same as al-Tusi's and
al-Tabrisi's — the clause is read as *tawhid*, the denial of partnership — and
not al-Tabari's, whose `معبود` does not occur here either (0 in both sections,
against a `واحد` count of 2 in the same commentary). Second, Tantawi does not
stop at `لا شريك له`: he extends the denial `لا فى ذاته ولا فى صفاته`, into the
essence *and the attributes*. Third, he attaches to the same clause the
renunciation of taking lords apart from God, which the Qur'an places in the
*other* verse of this pair — so the two passages are read together.

That second point is this repository's own observation about wording and is
recorded as wording: the phrase `لا فى ذاته ولا فى صفاته` says more than
`لا شريك له` says, and where the additional denial falls — on the divine
attributes — is where the Christian doctrine of the Trinity locates its
distinctions. Whether Tantawi intends the clause to exclude trinitarian
Christians is *not* stated in this capture, and this note does not say that he
does. What the capture supports is narrower and still worth having: the phrase
that the modern reading adds to the classical one is a phrase about the
attributes.

**On Q 29:46, the exception.** `{ إِلاَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ مِنْهُمْ }` is
those who wronged you, who behaved badly in disputing; he then reports the
second construal on which "People of the Book" means the believers among them
and "those who wronged" the ones who remained in unbelief. He gives Mujahid
against abrogation, and quotes al-Qurtubi at length. There is no *jizya* here at
all: `الجزية` is 0 in both his sections, where al-Tusi has it once and
al-Tabrisi twice. The modern commentator drops the tax and the sword from a
verse where both classical readers put them.

**On Q 3:64.** `وَٱلسَّوَاءُ العدل والنصفة` — *sawa'* is justice and fairness —
and, as an alternative, `السواء مصدر مستوية أى هلموا إلى كلمة لا تختلف فيها
الرسل والكتب المنزلة والعقول السليمة`: come to a word over which the messengers,
the revealed books and sound minds do not differ. That is a wider claim of
agreement than al-Zamakhshari's (who named the Qur'an, the Torah and the Gospel)
because it adds `العقول السليمة`, sound reason, to the list of things that do not
differ over it.

He glosses the word itself as the renunciation of worshipping any but God, of
associating anything with Him, and of taking one another as lords — the last
with the 'Adi ibn Hatim report, as the Sunni commentaries do. The close is
severe: `فإن أعرض هؤلاء الكفار عن دعوة الحق ... فلا تجادلوهم ولا تحاجوهم، بل
قولوا لهم اشهدوا بأنا مسلمون مذعنون لكلمة الحق، بخلافكم أنتم فقد رضيتم بما أنتم
فيه من باطل` — if *these unbelievers* turn away from the call of truth, do not
dispute or argue with them; say to them, bear witness that we are the submitted,
yielding to the word of truth, unlike you, who have been content with the
falsehood you are in. The addressees of the common-word summons are called
`هؤلاء الكفار` in the sentence that ends it.
