Own-words summary of [[bogardus-urban-how-to-tell-whether-christians-and-muslims--202609010833]].

**Grounded in the published abstract only** -- the full text could not be
retrieved in this environment (see the reference note), so this records the
paper's stated framework and conclusion, not its internal argumentation.

To "Do Muslims and Christians worship the same God?" the authors answer: *it
depends*. They set aside "some specious arguments surrounding this issue" to
isolate the question that actually decides the matter: what determines the
reference of a name, and when do names shift reference? Their tool is Gareth
Evans's theory of reference, on which a name refers to the *dominant source of
information* in the name's "dossier" -- the body of information its users
associate with it. Whether "Allah" as Muslims use it co-refers with "God" as
Christians use it then "depends on how much weight is given to what type of
information in the dossiers of these two names."

Their practical upshot is a two-part counterfactual test the reader applies to
their own semantic judgments: if Christianity were true and Islam false, might
"Allah" still refer to God? And if Islam were true and Christianity false,
might "God" still refer to Allah? The abstract closes by separating worship
from reference -- there may be conditions on worship beyond referring to the
right being -- and by asking whether salvation turns on the issue at all.

For this repository the load-bearing point is methodological: on their account
co-reference is not settled by any single shared description, however exalted,
but by which information *dominates* each name's dossier -- a question the
traditions' semantics, not their doctrines alone, must answer.
