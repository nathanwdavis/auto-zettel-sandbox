Own-words summary of [[aquinas-st-i-q3-simplicity--202609042070]].

Question 3 proceeds as an enumeration. Its eight articles ask, one at a time,
whether God is a body; composed of matter and form; of nature and *suppositum*;
of essence and existence; of genus and difference; of subject and accident;
composite in any way at all; and whether He enters into composition with other
things. The doctrine is built by exclusion, kind of composition by kind of
composition.

Article 7 gathers the result and gives five arguments for it, of which two are
the load-bearing ones and both are about dependence. "Every composite is
posterior to its component parts, and is dependent on them; but God is the first
being." And: "every composite has a cause, for things in themselves different
cannot unite unless something causes them to unite. But God is uncaused... since
He is the first efficient cause." Augustine supplies the authority on the
contrary: "God is truly and absolutely simple."

The list in article 7 is worth reading for what it contains: "neither composition
of quantitative parts in God, since He is not a body; nor composition of matter
and form; nor does His nature differ from His 'suppositum'; nor His essence from
His existence; neither is there in Him composition of genus and difference, nor
of subject and accident."

Six kinds of composition are denied. A plurality of *persons* is not among them,
and the question does not raise it — the Trinity is treated much later in the
Prima Pars. So the doctrine as stated here excludes metaphysical composition and
is silent, in this question, about the divine three.
