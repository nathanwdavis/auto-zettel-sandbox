Own-words summary of [[constantinople-381-canon-1--202609041956]].

The whole of Canon I, in the NPNF English, is two sentences: "The Faith of the
Three Hundred and Eighteen Fathers assembled at Nice in Bithynia shall not be
set aside, but shall remain firm. And every heresy shall be anathematized,
particularly that of the Eunomians or [Anomœans, the Arians or] Eudoxians, and
that of the Semi-Arians or Pneumatomachi, and that of the Sabellians, and that
of the Marcellians, and that of the Photinians, and that of the Apollinarians."

Three things follow from the form of it.

**It is a canon, not a creed.** Canons are the council's disciplinary decrees,
printed and numbered separately from the creed it promulgated. The creed of 381
that the page's companion entry carries has no anathema clause at all.

**It condemns by party name.** The canon says "that of the Sabellians". It does
not say what the Sabellians hold. Every other entry in the list is the same: a
name, no proposition. Compare 325, which condemned six stated propositions and
named nobody.

**The list itself is unstable.** The editor notes that the manuscripts disagree
— "There is a difference of reading in the list of the heretics" — and prints
the variant, in which the Arians and Eudoxians are separate items rather than
identified, adding that Dionysius substitutes "Macedonians" for "Semi-Arians"
and that "It would seem that this was the correct reading." The editor prints
the reading he judges incorrect, "as being the more usual."
