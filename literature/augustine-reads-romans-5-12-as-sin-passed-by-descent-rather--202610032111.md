Own-words summary of [[augustine-on-merit-and-the-forgiveness-of-sins-and-the--202610032103]], Book I, chapters 9-13.

Augustine reports the reading he is answering. His opponents take the death of
Romans 5:12 to be the soul's death "which takes place in actual sin", and hold
that "this actual sin has not been transmitted from the first man to other
persons by natural descent, but by imitation"; hence they deny that infants
have any original sin to be remitted in baptism (ch. 9).

His first reply is from the verse's choice of first offender. Had Paul meant
imitation, he would have named the devil, who "sins from the beginning" (1 John
3:8), not Adam; naming the progenitor shows that "propagation, not imitation" is
meant (chs. 9-10). Adam "is one thing as an example to those who sin because
they choose; and another thing as the progenitor of all who are born with sin"
(ch. 10).

Chapter 11 is headed "Distinction Between Actual and Original Sin". Augustine's
text of the clause is "In which all have sinned". Whether "which" is taken as
the sin or as the one man, the clause separates two things: "the sins which are
peculiar to every man, which they themselves commit and which belong simply to
them, mean one thing; and that the one sin, in and by which all have sinned,
means another thing; since all were that one man".

Chapters 12-13 carry the distinction through Romans 5:13-14. Under the law of
nature, "every man when arrived at years of discretion only proceeds to add his
own sins to original sin" (ch. 12). Those who "had not sinned after the
similitude of Adam's transgression" are those "who had not yet sinned of their
own individual will, as Adam did, but had drawn from him original sin" (ch. 13).
He notes that some Latin copies drop the "not" in 5:14, but says the Greek
copies have it.
