Own-words summary of [[john-20-24-31-these-are-written-that-ye-might-believe--202609230041]].

Thomas makes sight and touch the condition of belief: "Except I shall see
in his hands the print of the nails ... I will not believe" (20:25). Eight
days later Jesus offers exactly that evidence, and adds "be not faithless,
but believing". Thomas confesses "My Lord and my God" (20:27-28). Jesus'
answer both accepts and ranks it: "because thou hast seen me, thou hast
believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed"
(20:29).

The next two verses state the Gospel's purpose, and they run the relation
between miracle and faith the opposite way from Mark 6. Jesus did "many
other signs" not recorded, "But these are written, that ye might believe
that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have
life through his name" (20:30-31). Here faith is the product of the signs,
not their precondition. The signs reach the reader as written testimony,
not as sight, and that matches the blessing of 20:29 on those who believe
without seeing.

Scope: John 20:24-31 in the KJV only. John's other sign-sayings (e.g. 4:48,
"Except ye see signs and wonders, ye will not believe") are not captured.
