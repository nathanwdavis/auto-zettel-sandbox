Own-words summary of the method in
[[locke-a-new-method-of-making-common-place-books--202609010602]] (core
description pp. 4–9): Locke's unit of storage is the *entry filed under a
head* — a topic word — and his machinery exists to guarantee the head can
always be found again. A two-page index divides the alphabet into
letter-plus-first-vowel classes (twenty letters by five vowels; K, Y, W
folded into C, I, U; Q exiled to the spare Z space). Each new head claims
the next blank two-page opening, whose index cell records its page number;
every later entry on that head goes to the same opening. When an opening
fills, its last page and the next free page point at each other by page
number, so one topic's scattered pages read as if contiguous. The system is
mature practice, not speculation — Locke had run it for a quarter century
by the French printing of 1686 — and it is deliberately minimal: heads need
no planned taxonomy, only a "considerable word" whose first letter and
vowel are stable.

- "If I would put any Thing in my Common-Place Book, I look a Head to
  which I may refer it, that I may be able to find it when I have
  Occasion." (pp. 4–5)
- "Every Head ought to begin with some Considerable Word that is Essential
  to the Matter treated of, and of this Word one must carefully observe
  the First Letter, and the Vowel which follows it; for upon these Two
  Letters depends the whole Use of our Index." (p. 5)
- On never once needing the ZU cell: "I have not found so much as One for
  the Space of Five and Twenty Years, during which Time I have made use of
  this Method." (p. 5)
- "When one has thus preserv'd the Margin clear, the Heads present
  themselves at First Sight" (p. 6)
- On the page-number pairs that chain one head across openings: "By these
  Numbers, which refer to one another … one reads the Matter which is
  separated as well as if there was nothing between them. For by this
  Reciprocal Reference of Numbers, one turns over as one Leaf all those
  which are between them, as if they were join'd together." (p. 8)

A quirk of the printing worth recording: the tract's specimen is the book
itself — the ruled two-page index and worked example entries precede the
prose (the text's own examples cite "the Second Page of this Writing" and
the class EI "the Second and Third Page"), and the prose description
("I take a White Paper Book of what Size I think fit…") begins on p. 4
under the running margin note "Adversariorum Methodus, or the Method of
Common Places", verified from the page image.

What the design conspicuously lacks is any device relating one head to
another: the only cross-reference primitive in the method points a topic's
pages at *each other*. That observation grounds
[[topic-head-filing-optimizes-retrieval-not-connection--202609010604]].
