Own-words summary of
[[najafi-jawahir-al-kalam-vol-30-on-marriage-to-a-woman-of-the-people-of-the-book--202609041605]].
English renderings are this repository's working translations; the Arabic stands
beside every rendering that carries weight. Quotations were matched exactly as
the OCR has them against the capture's **excerpt body**, whitespace normalised
and nothing else, with controls in the same pass — `الزيدية` occurs nowhere in
this capture, so nothing in it is evidence about the Zaydis. Corrected readings
are in square brackets and are this base's.

**The *matn* first, because the two authors disagree.** Al-Muhaqqiq al-Hilli
(d. 676/1277) states the agreed part and then the disputed part:

> لا يجوز للمسلم تكاح غير الكتابية اجماعاً […] تم #ني تحر يم‌الكتا بية منالهود
> والتصادى روایتان

(The `[…]` skips four lines the OCR has damaged past reading. `تم #ني تحر يم` is
the OCR of `ثم في تحريم`, and the `#` is the print's half-bracket marking the
resumption of the *matn* inside the commentary.)

[read: `نكاح`, `الكتابية من اليهود والنصارى روايتان`] — a Muslim may not marry a
non-*kitabi* woman by consensus; and concerning the prohibition of the woman of
the People of the Book, Jewish or Christian, **there are two narrations**. That
is the base's first sight of the question stated as *fiqh* rather than as
commentary, and the first thing to notice is that the school's own manual does
not state a school position. It states a disagreement.

**The position the base already knew, named as the later majority.** Al-Najafi
records the resolution most later scholars adopted, and it is exactly the rule
the base found in al-Tusi, al-Tabrisi and al-Tabataba'i — prohibition of the
permanent contract, permission in the temporary one and by ownership. He refers
to it as the *matn*'s own choice: the distinction `بالدائم دالمؤجنل وملك اليمين
الذي اختاره المصشف وغیره` [read: `بالدائم والمؤجل وملك اليمين الذي اختاره المصنف
وغيره`], between the permanent, the temporary and ownership, which the author
and others chose, and which is said to be the well-known view.

**The strict wing is named, and it is not a fringe.** `ذهب المفيد والمر تی
دابن إدديس فيماحكى عنه إلى المشع مطلقا` [read: `ذهب المفيد والمرتضى وابن إدريس
فيما حكي عنه إلى المنع مطلقاً`] — al-Mufid, al-Murtada and Ibn Idris went to
prohibition absolutely, extending it even to concubinage; `بل اد عىالمرتضى منهم
الاجماع على ذلك` [read: `بل ادعى المرتضى منهم الإجماع على ذلك`], indeed
al-Murtada among them claimed consensus for it. Before rejecting them al-Najafi
gives their whole case, including the reports the base already holds from
al-Tusi: Zurara from al-Baqir, `لا ينبغى نكاح أهل الكتاب` — marriage to the
People of the Book is not fitting — grounded, when Zurara asks where the
prohibition is, in `قال : قوله : ولا تمسكوا بعصم الكوافر` (Q 60:10); and the
*naskh* report, `قال : هذه منسوخة بقوله : ولاتمسكوا بعصم الكوأفر`, this verse
(Q 5:5) is abrogated by *do not hold to the ties of unbelieving women*.

**Then he rejects all of it, in one sentence.**

> إلا أن التحقيق الجواز مطلقا وفاقاً للحسن د السدوفين على كراحية متفاوئة

[read: `إلا أن التحقيق الجواز مطلقاً وفاقاً للحسن والصدوقين على كراهية متفاوتة`]
— except that **the verified position is permission absolutely**, in agreement
with al-Hasan [Ibn Abi 'Aqil] and the two Saduqs, subject to a dislike that
varies in degree: stronger for the permanent contract than the temporary,
stronger for a man who can find a Muslim wife than for one who cannot. The
prohibition becomes a *karaha* — disapproval — rather than a bar.

**His four arguments, in the order he uses them.**

*First, Q 5:5 is not abrogated but abrogating.* Sura al-Ma'ida is `اتی هي من سو
رةالمائدة. المشهورة ف اها محكمة` [read: `التي هي من سورة المائدة المشهورة في
أنها محكمة`], from the sura famous for being *muhkama*, and he cites the
Prophetic report `إن" سورة المائدة آخرالفى آن نزولا فأحلوا حلالها` [read: `إن
سورة المائدة آخر القرآن نزولاً`], that al-Ma'ida is the last of the Qur'an to be
revealed, so treat its permissions as permitted. This is the same chronological
argument al-Tabataba'i would use a century later, and al-Najafi is using it
first.

*Second — and this is the argument that connects to the base's oldest note in
this cluster — a woman of the People of the Book is not covered by the word
"al-mushrikat" at all.*

> فيتو جه عليه مضع دخولالكتابية نيالمشر كةء لان المتبادد من الشرك في إطلاق الثر
> ع غير أهلالكتاب, كما یؤیده عطف المشر كبن على اهل الكتاب د بالعمكس في كثير من
> الابات

[read: `فيتوجه عليه منع دخول الكتابية في المشركة، لأن المتبادر من الشرك في إطلاق
الشرع غير أهل الكتاب، كما يؤيده عطف المشركين على أهل الكتاب وبالعكس في كثير من
الآيات`] — the objection stands that the *kitabiyya* is not included in "the
*mushrika*", because what comes first to mind from *shirk* in the law's usage is
someone other than the People of the Book, as is confirmed by the coupling of
*al-mushrikun* with *ahl al-kitab*, and the reverse, in many verses. That is the
grammatical argument from conjunction which this base held from al-Qurtubi and
al-Sabuni and then found in al-Tabataba'i — here in Imami *fiqh*, a century
before *al-Mizan*.

*Third, he draws the distinction that keeps the argument honest.* `وهذا لا ينافي
اعتقادهم ما بوجب الشرك › إذ ليس الغرض فى الشرك عنهم » پل عدم تبادده من إطلاق
لفظ المشرك` [read: `وهذا لا ينافي اعتقادهم ما يوجب الشرك، إذ ليس الغرض نفي الشرك
عنهم، بل عدم تبادره من إطلاق لفظ المشرك`] — and this does not conflict with their
holding what entails *shirk*; the purpose is not to deny *shirk* of them, but
that it is not what the word *mushrik* first conveys. The claim is about the
extension of a term, not about the theology of the people it fails to reach. Note
that the OCR has dropped `نفي` from this sentence; the reading is marked and the
sense is fixed by the contrast with `بل`.

*Fourth, even granting the conflict, specification beats abrogation.* `ولا روب
في أن التخصيص أولى من النسخ` [read: `ولا ريب`] — there is no doubt that
specification is preferable to abrogation. The same maxim al-Tabataba'i uses, in
the opposite direction: al-Tabataba'i specifies to *keep* the restriction on
permanent marriage, al-Najafi specifies to *dissolve* it.

**The corroborating reports, which are the part that changes the picture most.**
Against the Zurara reports he sets a body of narrations permitting, including
al-Baqir in the opposite sense: `وصحيح ل بن مسلم` [read: `وصحيح محمد بن مسلم`]
— asked about marrying a Jewish or Christian woman, `فقال : لا بأس به » أما علمت
أنه كان تحت طللحة` [read: `طلحة`] — there is no harm in it; do you not know that
Talha had a Jewish wife in the Prophet's time? The same Companion precedent the
Zaydi manual cites on its own strict side. He also reports from al-Murtada's
opponents' side of the ledger that `قد اُجاز اصحابنا كلهم التمتع بالكتابية`, all
our associates permitted temporary marriage with a *kitabiyya* — which is why the
disagreement in the school is about the permanent contract specifically.

**He closes the question, in the voice of someone who thinks he has.** After
dismissing the six or seven graded positions as `ضعف الافوال الستثة أو السبعة`
[read: `ضعف الأقوال الستة أو السبعة`] and the claimed consensus as demonstrably
false, `فام ببق بحمداله سبحا نه فياللسألة بعداليوم من … إشكال » والحمد لله
المتعال` [read: `فلم يبق بحمد الله سبحانه في المسألة بعد اليوم من إشكال`] — no
difficulty remains in the question after today. Whether Imami law followed him is
not something this capture can show; that the tradition's largest legal
commentary states the permission as the verified position, is.

**One report he cannot place, and prints anyway.** Ibn al-Jahm asks al-Rida about
a man who married a Christian woman alongside a Muslim wife; told it is not
permitted on the authority of Q 2:221, the Imam's response is `فتبسم` — he
smiled — and said nothing. Al-Najafi will not build on a smile: he notes the
smile may have been at the questioner's confusion, since the question was about
marrying a Christian *in addition to* a Muslim wife, which presupposes that
marrying her is possible.

**What this note does not establish.** One work, one section, one host, and a
commentary rather than a code. It shows the tradition contains this position at
its highest level of legal elaboration and that the strict position is held by
named authorities against it; it does not show which position any Imami
jurisdiction operates today. Nothing here bears on the Zaydis — the control run
against this capture is what licenses saying so.
