In al-Ibana's preface (p. 43), al-Ash'ari grounds the work by asserting that God "has always had the attributes of primacy and power, and He will always continue to be knowing and cognizant." The preface thus asserts the permanence of the attributes before any argument is made.

On page 50 of al-Ibana, al-Ash'ari states that "anybody who thinks that the names of God are other than He is in error." This is his direct rejection of any doctrine that treats God's names as external to Him. In the same passage, he affirms that "God has knowledge" and that "we do not deny it as the Mutazilah, the Jahmiyyah, and the Harijites deny it; and we assert that God has hearing and sight" and "God has prowess (quwwah)." These assertions ground the attributes (knowledge, hearing, sight, power) as real predicates of God, not reducible to identity with a single principle.

On pages 99-100, al-Ash'ari reports the Mu'tazili position: they "deny the attributes of the Lord of the Worlds, and think that the meaning of 'hearing, seeing,' is identical with the meaning of 'knowing.'" He then constructs a logical argument to refute this reduction: if hearing and seeing collapse into knowing, then by the Mu'tazilis' own logic, power and life would also collapse into knowing, which leads to absurdity ("every known thing would certainly be heard; but since that is impossible, your belief is false"). Al-Ash'ari refuses the Mu'tazili reduction; these passages do not state the relation of the attributes to the essence.
