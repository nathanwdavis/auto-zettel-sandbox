Own-words summary of [[westminster-confession-of-faith-ch-6-of-the-fall-of-man-of--202610032101]].

The chapter moves from the first sin to its consequences in six sections, each
with its proof texts as printed on the page.

6.1-6.2. Our first parents sinned in eating the forbidden fruit, and by that sin
fell from original righteousness, "became dead in sin", and were "wholly defiled
in all the faculties and parts of soul and body" (proofs include Gen 2:17; Eph
2:1 for the death in sin, and Gen 6:5; Jer 17:9 for the defilement).

6.3. Being "the root of all mankind", their posterity received two things: "the
guilt of this sin was imputed", and "the same death in sin and corrupted nature"
was "conveyed to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary
generation". Imputation is proved from Rom 5:12, 15-19 and 1 Cor 15:21-22 among
others; the conveyed corruption from Gen 5:3; Job 14:4; 15:14; Psa 51:5.

6.4. The order of source and product is stated outright: "From this original
corruption" (whereby we are disabled and opposite to all good, and "wholly
inclined to all evil") "do proceed all actual transgressions". The inclination
is proved from Gen 6:5; 8:21; Rom 3:10-12; the proceeding of actual
transgressions from Mat 15:19; Eph 2:2-3; James 1:14-15.

6.5. The corruption "doth remain in those that are regenerated", and though
"through Christ pardoned and mortified, yet both itself and all the motions
thereof are truly and properly sin". The proofs: Prov 20:9; Eccl 7:20; Rom 7:14,
17-18, 23; James 3:2; 1 John 1:8, 10 for its remaining, and Rom 7:5, 7-8, 25;
Gal 5:17 for its being sin.

6.6. "Every sin, both original and actual" is a transgression of God's law
(proof 1 John 3:4) and brings guilt, wrath and death.

The chapter's two terms for the distinction are "original corruption" (also
"corruption of nature", "corrupted nature") and "actual transgressions". It
never uses the phrase "sin nature".
