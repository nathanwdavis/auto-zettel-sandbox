Own-words summary of [[maimonides-avodah-zarah-1--202609042050]].

Maimonides' history of idolatry begins with an argument, not an apostasy. "In
the times of Enosh, mankind made a great mistake, and the wise men of that
generation gave thoughtless counsel." What they said was this: "God created
stars and spheres with which to control the world. He placed them on high and
treated them with honor, making them servants who minister before Him.
Accordingly, it is fitting to praise and glorify them and to treat them with
honor."

And they took that to be piety, not defection: "[They perceived] this to be the
will of God, blessed be He, that they magnify and honor those whom He magnified
and honored, **just as a king desires that the servants who stand before him be
honored. Indeed, doing so is an expression of honor to the king.**"

Every premise there is one a monotheist holds. God exists, God created the
heavenly bodies, God assigned them their office, honour rendered to a great
lord's ministers redounds to the lord. The conclusion is idolatry. The
translator's note underlines who reached it: "the Rambam does not attribute the
rise of paganism to simple commoners, but to the 'wise' of the generation."

Halachah 2 then makes the decline institutional rather than intellectual. False
prophets arise who claim a command — "Serve this star, sacrifice to it, offer
libations to it, build a temple for it and make an image of it so that all
people... could bow to it" — and only later, in a third stage, do people lose
the knowledge of God entirely. Touger's note sets out the three stages
explicitly: mistaken individuals; institutions whose leaders "still recognized
God"; and finally worship "without any awareness of God."

So on this account the *knowledge* of God survives two full stages into
idolatry. What goes wrong first, and what goes wrong at all, is something else.
