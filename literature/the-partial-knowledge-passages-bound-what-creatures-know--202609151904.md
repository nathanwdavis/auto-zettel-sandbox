**Creaturely knowledge as bounded.** Five passages state a limit from the creature's side. Paul: "For we know in part, and we prophesy in part" (1 Cor. 13:9), and "now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known" (13:12). The doxology: "how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out!" (Rom. 11:33). Isaiah: "as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts" (55:9). Zophar to Job: "Canst thou by searching find out God? canst thou find out the Almighty unto perfection?" (Job 11:7). The psalmist: "Such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain unto it" (Ps. 139:6). Two of these are asymmetric comparisons (heavens and earth; height and depth) rather than bare denials, and one, 1 Cor. 13:12, gives the partiality a terminus: "then face to face". The bound is stated as a present condition, not as a permanent one.

**God as the one who conceals and who reveals.** Four passages put both verbs on God. "It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter" (Prov. 25:2) pairs concealing and searching out as two honours, not as a prohibition. Daniel: "He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the darkness" (Dan. 2:22). Amos: "Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets" (3:7). Paul: "the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God" and "the things of God knoweth no man, but the Spirit of God" (1 Cor. 2:10-11), with the revealed portion given "that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God" (2:12). In each the movement is one way: from a knowledge that is God's own into a creature's possession, by an act of God.

**"Mystery" as the disclosed, not the hidden.** In the two Pauline passages the word names something that was hidden and is hidden no longer: "the mystery which hath been hid from ages and from generations, but now is made manifest to his saints" (Col. 1:26); that "Which in other ages was not made known unto the sons of men, as it is now revealed unto his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit" (Eph. 3:5). A *mysterion* in this usage is a thing that has crossed from the concealed side to the revealed side, and is called a mystery because of where it came from, not because of where it is.

**One announced withholding.** John 16:12 is the only passage in the set in which the withholder says that there is more and that it is being withheld, and gives the reason: "I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now." The next verse promises the remainder through the Spirit, who "will guide you into all truth" (16:13). It is the single scriptural case here of a known unknown declared as such by the one who knows.

**Limits.** These are twelve verses chosen for this inquiry, not a survey; the KJV's italicised supplied words are not marked in the capture; and no passage here says anything about a class of things that are neither asked about nor known, which is the owner's fourth category. Scripture in this set names the hidden as a whole ("the deep and secret things", "the deep things of God") and never subdivides it.
