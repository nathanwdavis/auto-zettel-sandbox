Own-words summary of [[calvin-institutes-ii-13-christ-clothed-with-the-true--202610031811]].

In II.13.4 Calvin answers opponents who denied that Christ took real flesh
from Mary. He reads Romans 8:3 as Paul's way of placing Christ inside the
human race yet outside its corruption: Paul "distinctly separates him from the
common lot, as being true man, and yet without fault and corruption".

The opponents had pressed a dilemma: if Christ is pure yet of Mary's seed,
then the orthodox must say only the man's seed is impure. Calvin calls that
"childish trifling" and denies that his position rests on the absence of a
human father at all: "We do not hold Christ to be free from all taint, merely
because he was born of a woman unconnected with a man, but because he was
sanctified by the Spirit, so that the generation was pure and spotless, such
as it would have been before Adam's fall." He adds that Scripture's language
about Christ's purity concerns his human nature, "since it were superfluous to
say that God is pure."
