Own-words summary of [[bereshit-rabbah-34-10-antoninus-and-the-inclination-of-the--202610061548]].

Genesis Rabbah on Genesis 8:21, where God speaks "to His heart" and gives as his
reason that the inclination of man's heart is evil from his youth.

The section opens on the phrase "to His heart": "the wicked are under the
control of their hearts" (the scoundrel, Esau, Jeroboam, Haman all speak *in*
their hearts), "But the righteous, their hearts are subject to them" (Hannah and
David speak *to* their hearts, Daniel "placed in his heart", and God speaks to
His).

Three sayings then take the verse's verdict on the inclination as the verdict of
the one who made it. Rabbi Hiyya: "Wretched is the dough whose baker attests in
its regard that it is inferior". Abba Yosei HaTorati says the same of leaven and
its maker, citing Psalm 103:14, "For He knows our inclination"; the Rabbis say
it of a sapling and its planter, citing Jeremiah 11:17. In each image the
inferior thing is the maker's product and the maker is the witness against it.

Then Antoninus's two questions. "From what point is the evil inclination
implanted in a person?" Rabbi answers before birth; Antoninus objects that the
fetus would then dig through her innards and force its way out; "Rabbi conceded
to him", and the agreement is grounded in Genesis 8:21. Rabbi Yudan adds a
reading of the verse's spelling: the inclination is there "from the moment that
he pushes out [ninar] to emerge from his mother's womb". The second question,
when the soul is placed in a person, is answered the other way, before birth,
from Job 10:12.

The verse for the inclination is Genesis 8:21 throughout. Nothing in the
section speaks of Adam the first man, of guilt, or of inheritance; *adam*
appears only in the generic sense, man's heart.
