Own-words summary of sections 6-8 of
[[bush-as-we-may-think--202609010800]]. Bush's diagnosis is that retrieval
fails at the index, not at the storage: filed data "can be in only one
place, unless duplicates are used; one has to have rules as to which path
will locate it, and the rules are cumbersome" — and "Having found one
item, moreover, one has to emerge from the system and re-enter on a new
path." (sec. 6). Against that he sets the mind: "The human mind does not
work that way. It operates by association." (sec. 6). The mind's known
defect is decay — "trails that are not frequently followed are prone to
fade, items are not fully permanent, memory is transitory" (sec. 6) — and
that defect is exactly where a machine can win: "it should be possible to
beat the mind decisively in regard to the permanence and clarity of the
items resurrected from storage." (sec. 6). His proposed selection-by-
association machine may yet be mechanized (sec. 6; the two captured
copies differ by one word here, "rather than indexing" vs. "rather than
by indexing" — variant recorded in the raw capture and unresolved).

The memex embodies this: "A memex is a device in which an individual
stores all his books, records, and communications, and which is
mechanized so that it may be consulted with exceeding speed and
flexibility. It is an enlarged intimate supplement to his memory."
(sec. 6). Its one non-conventional feature is "associative indexing, the
basic idea of which is a provision whereby any item may be caused at will
to select immediately and automatically another. This is the essential
feature of the memex. The process of tying two items together is the
important thing." (sec. 7). Joined items form a named, stored trail that
reads like a purpose-built book ("It is more than this, for any item can
be joined into numerous trails.", sec. 7), and where the brain's trails
decay, "his trails do not fade" (sec. 7): years later a trail is recalled
by name, replayed, and even copied out — the owner "photographs the whole
trail out, and passes it to his friend for insertion in his own memex,
there to be linked into the more general trail." (sec. 7). Section 8
scales this from a private aid to an economy of links: "There is a new
profession of trail blazers, those who find delight in the task of
establishing useful trails through the enormous mass of the common
record.", so that "The inheritance from the master becomes, not only his
additions to the world's record, but for his disciples the entire
scaffolding by which they were erected." (sec. 8).
