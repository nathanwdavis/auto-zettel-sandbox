Own-words summary of [[aquinas-summa-theologiae-i-q2--202609011201]].

Question 2 sets the terms for every later natural-theology argument in
three moves. First (a.1), the existence of God is not self-evident *to
us*: the proposition is self-evident in itself, "because God is His own
existence", but since we do not know God's essence it "needs to be
demonstrated by things that are more known to us, though less known in
their nature--namely, by effects". Along the way Aquinas rejects the
Proslogion-style move from the meaning of the word: granted that "God"
signifies "something than which nothing greater can be thought", "it
does not therefore follow that he understands that what the word
signifies exists actually, but only that it exists mentally" (a.1, ad
2). Second (a.2), demonstration from effects is legitimate -- a
posteriori argument runs "from what is prior relatively only to us" --
and its results are "not articles of faith, but are preambles to the
articles". Back in a.1 (ad 1) Aquinas had already conceded a
non-demonstrative natural awareness: "To know that God exists in a
general and confused way is implanted in us by nature", which is not
yet knowing "absolutely that God exists".

Third (a.3), the demonstration itself: "The existence of God can be
proved in five ways" -- from motion, efficient causation, contingency
and necessity, gradation of perfections, and "the governance of the
world". The fifth way's datum is regularity in unintelligent things:
"things which lack intelligence, such as natural bodies, act for an
end, and this is evident from their acting always, or nearly always, in
the same way, so as to obtain the best result", so that "not
fortuitously, but designedly, do they achieve their end"; what lacks
intelligence must "be directed by some being endowed with knowledge and
intelligence; as the arrow is shot to its mark by the archer". Each way
closes with an identification formula rather than an argument for it:
"this everyone understands to be God" (first way), "to which everyone
gives the name of God" (second), "This all men speak of as God" (third),
"and this being we call God" (fifth).

Scope: this note covers ST I q.2 only, as captured; nothing beyond
Question 2 is quoted or summarized.
