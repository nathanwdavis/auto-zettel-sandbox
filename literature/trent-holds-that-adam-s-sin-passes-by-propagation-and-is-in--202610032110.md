Own-words summary of [[council-of-trent-session-v-decree-concerning-original-sin--202610032102]].

The decree states five anathemas and then a closing declaration.

1. Adam, by transgressing, lost holiness and justice, incurred wrath and death,
and was "changed, in body and soul, for the worse".

2. His prevarication injured his posterity and not himself alone. The decree
condemns the view that he transfused into the whole race only death and bodily
pains, "but not sin also, which is the death of the soul", and gives Romans
5:12 as the proof, in the form "in whom all have sinned".

3. This sin of Adam, "which in its origin is one", is transfused into all "by
propagation, not by imitation", and "is in each one as his own"; it is removed
only by the merit of Christ, applied in baptism to adults and infants.

4. Infants are baptized for the remission of sins, because "even infants, who
could not as yet commit any sin of themselves" have something to be cleansed:
"that in them that may be cleansed away by regeneration, which they have
contracted by generation". Committing sin of oneself and contracting it by
generation are here two different ways of having sin.

5. Baptism remits the guilt of original sin and takes away "the whole of that
which has the true and proper nature of sin"; it is not merely "rased, or not
imputed". Yet "in the baptized there remains concupiscence, or an incentive (to
sin)", left for struggle, harmless to those who do not consent. The decree then
reads Paul: "This concupiscence, which the apostle sometimes calls sin", the
Church "has never understood it to be called sin, as being truly and properly
sin in those born again, but because it is of sin, and inclines to sin".

The decree does not name which Pauline passages call concupiscence sin.
