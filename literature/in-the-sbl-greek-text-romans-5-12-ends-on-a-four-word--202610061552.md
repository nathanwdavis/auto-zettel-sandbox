Own-words summary of [[romans-5-12-21-in-the-sbl-greek-new-testament--202610061545]].

What the Greek of Romans 5:12-21 shows on its face, without any commentator.

Romans 5:12 names no one. Sin entered the world "διʼ ἑνὸς ἀνθρώπου", through one
man, and death through sin, and so death passed to all men, and the verse ends on
the clause the translations divide over: "ἐφʼ ᾧ πάντες ἥμαρτον". The clause has
four words: the preposition *epi* (elided before the vowel), the relative *hō*,
*pantes* (all) and *hēmarton* (sinned). It contains no noun, so what *hō*
refers to, or whether *eph' hō* works as a fixed connective, is not printed in
the clause and has to be supplied by a reader.

The edition ends 5:12 with a dash, not a stop. The sentence opened with
"ὥσπερ" (just as) is not finished in the verse.

Adam is first named in 5:14: death reigned "ἀπὸ Ἀδὰμ μέχρι Μωϋσέως", even over
those who had not sinned "ἐπὶ τῷ ὁμοιώματι τῆς παραβάσεως Ἀδάμ". The verse in
between, 5:13, says that sin is not counted where there is no law: "ἁμαρτία δὲ
οὐκ ἐλλογεῖται μὴ ὄντος νόμου".

At 5:19 the many were made, or constituted, sinners through the one man's
disobedience: "ἁμαρτωλοὶ κατεστάθησαν οἱ πολλοί". The verb is passive, and the
cause named is the one man's act, not the many's.

The capture holds the Greek only. The KJV of the same verses is on file under a
separate reference and is not summarised here.
