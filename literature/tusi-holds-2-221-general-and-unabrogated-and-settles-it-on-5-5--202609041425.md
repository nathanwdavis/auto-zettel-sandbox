Own-words summary of [[tusi-al-tibyan-on-2-221-and-5-5--202609041400]]. Every
English rendering below is this repository's own working translation of the
captured Arabic, and the Arabic is printed beside each rendering that carries
weight. The standing translation debt is unpaid here too and is tracked in
INBOX.

**On Q 2:221, the position, in one sentence and marked as his school's.** After
the lexical entry on *nikah* he opens the sense section with it:

> وهذه الآية على عمومها - **عندنا** - في تحريم مناكحة الكفار، وليست منسوخة ولا
> مخصوصة.

This verse stands on its generality — **in our view** — in prohibiting marriage
with the unbelievers; it is neither abrogated nor restricted.

The two dashes around `عندنا` are the site's own punctuation and they are doing
the work this cycle came for: the position is not reported, it is claimed. He
then lists the alternatives and rejects each. Ibn 'Abbas, in Shahr b. Hawshab's
transmission, has 'Umar separate Talha and Hudhayfa from their wives; another
transmission from Ibn 'Abbas, followed by al-Hasan, Mujahid and al-Rabi', has
the verse general but abrogated by Q 5:5; Qatada and Sa'id b. Jubayr have it
restricted from the start. His reason for preferring his own:
`لأنه لا دليل على نسخها، ولا على خصوصها` — because there is no evidence for
either its abrogation or its restriction.

**The argument he actually gives, and it is an *'illa* argument.** Marriage to a
*wathaniyya* is forbidden by consensus, he says, `لأنها تدعو الى النار` —
because she calls to the Fire, as God relates. Then:

> وهذه العلة بعينها قائمة في **الذمية من اليهودية والنصارى**، فيجب أن لا يجوز
> نكاحها.

And this very same ratio stands in the case of **the *dhimmi* woman, Jewish or
Christian**; so it must be that marrying her is not permitted.

This is the whole of the strict position in one move: the reason the Qur'an
gives for the prohibition is not idolatry as a category but the direction a
spouse pulls, and that reason does not stop at the border of the Book.

**He does not finish here, and says so.**
`وسنبين وجه الآية في المائدة اذا انتهينا إليها` — we shall explain the sense of
the verse in al-Ma'ida when we come to it. That deferral is why this capture
carries Q 5:5.

**On Q 5:5, the same position stated against the verse that seems to defeat it.**

> **وعندنا** لا يجوز العقد على الكتابية نكاح الدوام، لقوله تعالى: { ولا تنكح
> المشركات حتى يؤمن } ولقوله: { ولا تمسكوا بعصم الكوافر }

**In our view** a contract of permanent marriage with a woman of the Book is not
permitted, because of His words "and do not marry *mushrikat* until they
believe" and His words "and do not hold to the bonds of unbelieving women"
[Q 60:10].

So Q 2:221 is not merely left unabrogated; it is one of the two verses by which
Q 5:5 is read down. He then offers two *ta'wils* of the Q 5:5 permission:
either the women meant are `اللائي أسلمن منهن`, those of them who have converted
(reported from al-Balkhi), or the permission is confined to `نكاح المتعة أو ملك
اليمين` — temporary marriage or concubinage — `لانه يجوز عندنا وطؤهن بعقد
المتعة، وملك اليمين`, since intercourse with them by either route is permitted
in our view.

**And the inversion, in two reports, attributed to the Imams.** Inside the second
*ta'wil* he adds:

> على أنه روى أبو الجارود عن أبي جعفر (ع) أن ذلك **منسوخ** بقوله: { ولا تنكح
> المشركات حتى يؤمن } روى عن ابي عبد الله (ع) انه قال: هو منسوخ بقوله: { ولا
> تمسكوا بعصم الكوافر }

Abu al-Jarud narrated from Abu Ja'far [al-Baqir] that it — the Q 5:5 permission
— is **abrogated** by "do not marry *mushrikat* until they believe"; and it is
narrated from Abu 'Abd Allah [al-Sadiq] that he said it is abrogated by "do not
hold to the bonds of unbelieving women."

That is the abrogation running backwards, from the later verse to the earlier
one, on the authority of the fifth and sixth Imams — which is precisely the
inversion this base had on al-Sabuni's one clause and nothing else.

**One thing that complicates any flat reading of the position.** On Q 5:5 the
prohibition he defends is of `نكاح الدوام`, *permanent* marriage, and in the
same breath he grants that *mut'a* and concubinage with the same women remain
permitted. The strictness is about the contract, not about contact. That
qualification is absent from the one-clause Sunni report this capture was taken
to test, and a note that repeated the report without it would be repeating
something narrower than what the Imami source says.

**A second thing, on the word itself.** Al-Tusi does not simply assume that a
monotheist unbeliever is a *mushrik*; he raises the difficulty in his own voice
— `فان قيل: كيف قيل للكافر الموحد مشرك؟` — and answers it twice: that unbelief
in God's favour equals association in the gravity of the offence, or, from
al-Zajjaj and `وهو الأقوى` (the stronger of the two), that one who disbelieves
in the Prophet has ascribed to another than God what can come only from God,
namely the Qur'an. So the extension of *mushrik* is argued for here, not
stipulated — which is the same question al-Qurtubi's *mas'ala* runs and the
answer arrives from the other side.
