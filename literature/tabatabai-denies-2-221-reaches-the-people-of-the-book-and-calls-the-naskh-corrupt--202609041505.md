**Shirk is a scale, and the word is not the deed.** He opens by grading it:
polytheism and idols are `شرك ظاهر`, manifest *shirk*; below that lies what the
People of the Book hold; below that, believing secondary causes act
independently; and at the bottom `الغفلة عن الله`, heedlessness of God, from
which only the sincere escape. But — and this is the move the argument turns on —
committing an act is not being named by the description:

> غير أن إطلاق الفعل غير إطلاق الوصف والتسمية به

except that predicating the deed is not the same as predicating the description
and naming by it.

His example is a Muslim who neglects the pilgrimage: Q 3:97 calls him one who
`كفر`, yet `ليس تارك الحج كافرا` — the one who omits the *hajj* is not an
unbeliever. So a Jew or Christian may fall under *shirk* in the graded sense
without falling under the Qur'an's *name* `مشرك`.

**The Qur'an's own usage is his evidence, and he lists it.**

> على أن لفظ المشركين في القرآن غير ظاهر الإطلاق على أهل الكتاب بخلاف لفظ
> الكافرين

Moreover the word *al-mushrikin* in the Qur'an is not apparently applied to the
People of the Book, unlike the word *al-kafirin*.

He then walks the passages that name both together or name the *mushrikun*
alone — Q 98:1, Q 9:28, Q 9:7, Q 9:36, Q 9:5 — and handles the two verses that
look like counter-examples (Q 2:135 and Q 12:106) by argument rather than by
silence: in Q 2:135 the *mushrikun* are a third party, shown by Q 3:67, and in
Q 12:106 the verse is not in the business of naming at all, since the description
would then catch most believers too.

**The conclusion, in his own words.**

> فقد ظهر من هذا البيان على طوله ان ظاهر الآية … قصر التحريم على المشركات
> والمشركين من الوثنيين دون أهل الكتاب

It has emerged from this exposition, for all its length, that the apparent sense
of the verse … restricts the prohibition to the idolatrous women and men, not to
the People of the Book.

**And the abrogation is not merely refused, it is called corrupt.**

> ومن هنا يظهر فساد القول بأن الآية ناسخة لآية المائدة

and from this the corruptness appears of the claim that this verse abrogates the
verse of al-Ma'ida.

He kills it in every direction the tradition runs it. There is no conflict to
abrogate, because Q 2:221 does not reach the *kitabiyya* and Q 5:5 speaks only of
her — `فلا نسبة بين الآيتين بالتنافي`. Q 60:10 is disposed of separately, as
being about keeping a wife who stayed behind rather than about contracting a new
marriage. And even granting a conflict, the chronology forbids it:

> على أن سورة البقرة أول سورة نزلت بالمدينة بعد الهجرة، وسورة الممتحنة نزلت
> بالمدينة قبل فتح مكة، وسورة المائدة آخر سورة نزلت على رسول الله ناسخة غير
> منسوخة ولا معنى لنسخ السابق اللاحق

al-Baqara is the first sura revealed at Medina after the Hijra, al-Mumtahana was
revealed at Medina before the conquest of Mecca, and al-Ma'ida is the last sura
revealed to the Messenger of God, abrogating and not abrogated — and there is no
sense in the earlier abrogating the later.

He says it in the other direction too, in both sections. On Q 2:221: Q 5:5 is
`واردة مورد الامتنان والتخفيف`, delivered in the register of favour and relief,
`فهي أبية عن المنسوخية` — so it refuses to be abrogated; and if abrogation were
insisted on, `كانت آية المائدة هي الناسخة`, al-Ma'ida would be the abrogator. On
Q 5:5, the same, from the other end: being `واقعة موقع الامتنان والتخفيف`, it
`لم تقبل النسخ بمثل قوله` — does not admit abrogation by the likes of Q 2:221 or
Q 60:10.

**He knows the Imami narrations and he answers them.** This is not a commentator
unaware of his school's reports. In the *bahth riwa'i* on Q 5:5 he cites Zurara
in *al-Kafi* and Mas'ada b. Sadaqa in al-'Ayyashi, asking al-Baqir about "and the
chaste women of those given the Book before you": `فقال منسوخة بقوله { ولا تمسكوا
بعصم الكوافر }` — he said, it is abrogated by "and do not hold to the bonds of
unbelieving women." His reply is the chronological one, now aimed at a report
from an Imam:

> ويشكل بتقدم قوله { ولا تمسكوا } الآية على قوله { والمحصنات } الآية نزولاً ولا
> يجوز تقدم الناسخ على المنسوخ

it is problematic, because "do not hold" precedes "and the chaste women" in
revelation, and the abrogator may not precede the abrogated.

He notes the other report — `وفي بعض الروايات أيضاً أن الآية منسوخة بقوله { ولا
تنكحوا المشركات }` — and refers back to the same objection.

**What he keeps, and it is exactly the shape the earlier Imami capture found.**
The dissent is not a swing to permissiveness. He offers the strict rule a
different foundation and a narrower reach. If Q 60:10 is taken as a *prior
specifier* rather than an abrogator, then

> خرج به النكاح الدائم من إطلاق قوله { والمحصنات من الذين أُوتوا الكتاب من قبلكم }

permanent marriage is excluded by it from the generality of "and the chaste women
of those given the Book before you."

That is the same `نكاح الدوام` line al-Tusi and al-Tabrisi draw — the permanent
contract forbidden, *mut'a* left standing — reached by *takhsis* instead of
*naskh*. And he grounds the surviving permission in practice, not only in theory:
the report allowing *mut'a* with a *kitabiyya* is itself evidence the verse is
unabrogated, `وقد عمل بها الأصحاب` — and the companions of the school have acted
on it — with a cross-reference to his own treatment of Q 4:24, `أن المتعة نكاح
كالنكاح الدائم`, that *mut'a* is a marriage like permanent marriage.

Read on its own terms, Q 5:5 for him licenses both: `من غير شرط آخر من نكاح دوام
أو انقطاع`, without any further condition of permanent or temporary marriage.

**The narrative material he transmits without endorsing.** The Q 5:5 section also
carries the *fiqh*-adjacent reports the base should not mistake for his position:
al-Baqir's `إنما يحل منهن نكاح البله`, al-Sadiq's answer that a man who has a
Muslim wife has no business with a Jewish or Christian one and that `عليه في دينه
غضاضة`, there is a diminishment in his religion — dissuasion, not prohibition,
and he lets them stand as reports.

**One limit worth stating.** Al-Tabataba'i is a *mufassir* writing in the
twentieth century, not a *faqih* stating the school's operative rule in a legal
compendium, and he twice sends the reader elsewhere for the rest —
`وللكلام تتمة تطلب من الفقه`, the discussion has a remainder to be sought in
jurisprudence. What this capture establishes is what a major twentieth-century
Imami commentary argues, not what Imami law codifies. The base still holds no Imami
*fiqh* text.
