Own-words summary of [[christ-s-sinlessness-and-the-transmission-of-sin-passages--202610031815]].

The passages fall into four groups.

Holiness from the start, and sinlessness as a state. The child to be born is
"that holy thing which shall be born of thee" (Luke 1:35), holy before any
act. Christ "knew no sin" (2 Cor 5:21); "in him is no sin" (1 John 3:5); he is
"holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners" (Heb 7:26); the prince of
this world "hath nothing in me" (John 14:30); and he can ask "Which of you
convinceth me of sin?" (John 8:46). 1 Peter 2:22 states the record of acts:
he "did no sin".

Likeness without identity. God sent his Son "in the likeness of sinful flesh"
(Rom 8:3). "In all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren"
(Heb 2:17), and he "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin"
(Heb 4:15).

Shared weakness. He is "touched with the feeling of our infirmities" (Heb
4:15), and the narratives show it: "Jesus therefore, being wearied with his
journey, sat thus on the well" (John 4:6).

Why it matters, and what he is set against. He "needeth not daily, as those
high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins" (Heb 7:27); his
blood is that of "a lamb without blemish and without spot" (1 Pet 1:19). The
human condition he stands apart from is stated in Romans 5:12, "for that all
have sinned", and Psalm 51:5, "in sin did my mother conceive me".

None of the passages uses the later vocabulary of original sin or sin nature,
and none says how the inheritance was blocked in Christ's case. That question
belongs to the theologians.
