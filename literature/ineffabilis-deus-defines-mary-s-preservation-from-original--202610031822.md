Own-words summary of [[pius-ix-ineffabilis-deus-apostolic-constitution-defining--202610031813]].

The constitution defines that "the most Blessed Virgin Mary, in the first
instance of her conception, by a singular grace and privilege granted by
Almighty God, in view of the merits of Jesus Christ, the Savior of the human
race, was preserved free from all stain of original sin". It is declared "a
doctrine revealed by God and therefore to be believed firmly and constantly by
all the faithful."

The reason the document gives is the dignity of the divine maternity, argued
from fittingness. It was "wholly fitting that so wonderful a mother" should be
"so completely free from all taint of original sin", and Mary, differing from
others, "had only nature in common with them, not sin." The privilege is
granted "in view of the merits of Jesus Christ", so Mary is presented as
redeemed by her Son, not as a precondition of his redemption. The text does
not argue that Christ needed a mother free of original sin in order to be born
free of it; that use of the doctrine belongs to its interpreters and critics.
