Own-words summary of [[nicene-creed-325--202609041955]].

The creed of 325 ends with a single anathema clause, and every proposition in
it is one an Arian would assert. In the NPNF English: "And whosoever shall say
that there was a time when the Son of God was not, or that before he was
begotten he was not, or that he was made of things that were not, or that he is
of a different substance or essence [from the Father] or that he is a creature,
or subject to change or conversion — all that so say, the Catholic and
Apostolic Church anathematizes them."

Six condemned propositions, listed. They condemn temporal posteriority, origin
out of nothing, difference of substance, creaturehood, and mutability. There is
no seventh clause, and nothing in the six condemns the opposite error — saying
that Father, Son and Spirit are one and the same, differing only in aspect or in
what they do for us. The word Sabellian does not occur, nor any equivalent.

The positive text does its work on the same side: the Son is "of the substance
of the Father", "begotten, not made", "of one substance (*homoousion*,
*consubstantialem*) with the Father". Those phrases exclude a Son of different
substance. A modalist could recite every one of them without discomfort — which
is exactly the complaint the fourth-century Arians made against the
*homoousion*, that it sounded Sabellian.

The page also prints the rival creed Eusebius of Caesarea brought to the
council. Its wording is looser at precisely the contested point ("the first-born
of all creatures"), which is what the council's version was written to exclude.
