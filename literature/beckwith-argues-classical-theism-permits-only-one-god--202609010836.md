Own-words summary of [[beckwith-do-muslims-and-christians-worship-the-same-god--202609010831]].

Written two days into the Wheaton College dispute, in defense of Larycia
Hawkins (while correcting her "people of the book" claim, which he notes is a
Qur'anic category for Jews and Christians, not a Christian self-description).

Beckwith's positive argument runs through reference. Different terms can pick
out one thing -- "Muhammad Ali" and "Cassius Clay" -- so "Yahweh" and "Allah"
naming different Gods does not follow from the names differing; it would have
to follow from the properties differing. He then plays the classical-theist
card: Aquinas, Maimonides, and Avicenna -- one thinker per Abrahamic tradition
-- shared classical theism, on which "there can only in principle be one God."
His conclusion is stated as a necessity: classical theists of the three
traditions "must be worshipping the same God. It simply cannot be otherwise."

The remainder answers the Trinity objection with an epistemic principle:
disagreement over a property is not disagreement over the referent. Fred and
Bob disagree about whether Jefferson fathered Sally Hemings's children, yet
both believe things about the same third president; Abraham and Moses held no
trinitarian beliefs, yet Augustine and Billy Graham worship the God of Abraham.
"The fact that one may have incomplete knowledge or hold a false belief about
another person -- whether human or divine -- does not mean that someone who has
better or truer knowledge about that person is not thinking about the same
person."

Note what carries the weight: the *in-principle uniqueness* of the classical
God. The argument nowhere requires that Muslim and Christian descriptions
agree beyond the classical core, and expressly tolerates one side being wrong.
