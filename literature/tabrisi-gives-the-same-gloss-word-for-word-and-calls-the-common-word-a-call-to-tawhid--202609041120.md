Own-words summary of
[[tabrisi-majma-al-bayan-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609041105]]. English renderings
are this repository's own working translations; the Arabic is printed beside
each one that carries weight.

**On Q 29:46, the clause.** Al-Tabrisi's gloss:

> { وقولوا } لهم في المجادلة وفي الدعوة إلى الدين { آمنا بالذي أنزل إلينا
> وأنزل إليكم } أي بالكتاب الذي أنزل إلينا وبالكتاب الذي أنزل إليكم
> { وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد } **لا شريك له** { ونحن له مسلمون } أي مخلصون طائعون.

"And say" to them, in disputation and in the summons to the religion, "we
believe in what was sent down to us and sent down to you" — that is, in the Book
sent down to us and the Book sent down to you — "and our God and your God is
one": **He has no partner**; "and to Him we are submitted": sincere, obedient.

The operative words are al-Tusi's, letter for letter: `لا شريك له`. That is a
datum and it is recorded as one; what it *means* — whether al-Tabrisi is
transmitting al-Tusi or independently reaching the same gloss — is a question
about the textual relation of the two works, which this repository has captured
no source on and therefore does not assert. The two are treated here as two
attestations of one reading, not as two independent witnesses to it.

What is around the gloss differs from al-Tusi in one telling way: al-Tabrisi
locates the saying in two settings, `في المجادلة وفي الدعوة إلى الدين` — in
disputation *and in the summons to the religion*. The formula is not just what
you say when arguing; it is part of the call.

**On Q 29:46, the exception, which is harder than al-Tusi's.** Where al-Tusi
gives Mujahid's `بمنع الجزية` in four words, al-Tabrisi spells the ruling out:

> { إلا الذين ظلموا منهم } أي إلا من أبى أن يقرَّ بالجزية منهم ونصب الحرب
> فجادلوا هؤلاء بالسيف حتى يسلموا أو يعطوا الجزية عن مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير.

Except those who refuse to acknowledge the *jizya* and set up war: dispute with
*these* by the sword, until they submit or pay the tax — from Mujahid and Sa'id
b. Jubayr. He then reports Abu Muslim (wronging by obstinacy and by concealing
the description of the Prophet after knowing it) and Ibn Zayd, and prefers the
conduct reading: `والأولى أن يكون معناه إلا الذين ظلموك في جدالهم`, the better
sense is those who wronged *you* in their disputing. On abrogation he is
explicit where al-Tusi is brief: `وقيل: إن الآية منسوخة بآية السيف عن قتادة
والصحيح أنها غير منسوخة لأن الجدال على الوجه الأحسن هو الواجب الذي لا يجوز
غيره` — it is said, from Qatada, that the verse is abrogated by the sword-verse;
the correct view is that it is not abrogated, because disputing in the better
way is the obligation and nothing else is permitted.

So the same paragraph that glosses "our God and your God is one" as "He has no
partner" also carries the sword and the *jizya*, and registers no tension
between them. That is the fourth family in this base to do it — and the first
outside the Sunni fold.

**On Q 3:64.** He gives the language and grammar sections first (al-Zajjaj on
`كلمة` as a whole utterance, hence the Arabs' calling a *qasida* a "word"), then
the occasion of revelation with the same three views al-Tusi reports and the
same Imami marker (`وقد رواه أصحابنا أيضاً`) — but unlike al-Tusi he chooses:
`وثالثها ... عن أبي علي الجبائي وهذا أولى لعمومه`, the third, from al-Jubba'i,
that it was revealed about both parties, and this is the better view **for its
generality**.

Then the sense, and it is the sharpest one-sentence statement of purpose any
commentator in this base gives the verse: `لما تمّ الحجاج على القوم دعاهم تعالى
إلى التوحيد وإلى الاقتداء بمن اتفقوا أنه كان على الحق` — when the argument
against the people was complete, He called them to *tawhid* and to following the
one they agreed had been upon the truth. The summons is issued *after* the
argument has been won, not as a search for common ground before it. And
`{ إلى كلمة سواء } أي عدل` — a word of justice — is the equity reading, not the
"held in common" one.

He carries the same Ja'far al-Sadiq report on the lordship clause that al-Tusi
does, in nearly the same words.
