Own-words summary of the Old Testament passages in [[sin-as-act-and-sin-as-indwelling-condition-passages-1-john--202610032100]].

The passages fall into four groups.

Sinfulness before any deed, placed at conception, birth and youth. The psalmist
was "shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me" (Ps 51:5). "The
wicked are estranged from the womb" (Ps 58:3), and in the same verse the deeds
follow the condition: "they go astray as soon as they be born, speaking lies".
Israel "wast called a transgressor from the womb" (Isa 48:8). Job ties
uncleanness to being born: "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? not
one" (Job 14:4), and "he which is born of a woman, that he should be
righteous?" (Job 15:14).

The heart as a standing disposition. Before the flood "every imagination of the
thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (Gen 6:5); after it, God gives
nearly the same verdict as a reason for sparing rather than smiting, "for the
imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth" (Gen 8:21). "The heart is
deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked" (Jer 17:9). None of these
lists deeds; they describe what the heart is like.

Sin as something other than the deed. To Cain: "if thou doest not well, sin
lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over
him" (Gen 4:7). Doing well or not is Cain's act; sin is pictured as a thing
crouching outside him, wanting him, which he is told to master.

Acts, universal and individually answerable. Ecclesiastes states the
universality of sinning as something done: "there is not a just man upon
earth, that doeth good, and sinneth not" (Eccl 7:20). The law and Ezekiel make liability personal:
"every man shall be put to death for his own sin" (Deut 24:16); "The soul that
sinneth, it shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father"
(Ezek 18:20), given as the answer to the question "doth not the son bear the
iniquity of the father?" (Ezek 18:19). These speak of answering for sin
committed; they say nothing either way about a condition from the womb.

None of the passages uses the terms original sin or sin nature. The
condition is spoken of in terms of womb, birth, youth and heart; the acts in
terms of doing, going astray, transgressing and sinning.
