**Grounding: the raw capture of Rashbam's two comment segments, Hebrew and Munk's English, from the Sefaria API, read whole.**

Rashbam refers back to his comment on the curses of Deuteronomy 27 and gives the verse a procedural sense. On the secret things: "It is precisely because sins committed in secret cannot be dealt with by the courts, that the Torah has to go on record that the sinner will still not get away with his sin." The Hebrew puts the contrast as a matter of whose hand the punishment is in, the court's or the Holy One's. On the second lemma, the clause assigning the revealed things to Israel and its children: "it is up to us to administer the penalties prescribed by the Torah subject to the offender being convicted on the basis of reliable witnesses." (לעשות דיני עונשין על פי עדים.)

**What this adds.** The division is drawn exactly where a court's competence ends. What witnesses can establish, the community judges; what they cannot, God does. The verse, so read, is about evidence and jurisdiction, and the *secret things* are secret only relative to a court. Nothing in it concerns what God has or has not disclosed.

**Limits.** Two short segments; Munk's translation is undated on Sefaria and is quoted as served.
