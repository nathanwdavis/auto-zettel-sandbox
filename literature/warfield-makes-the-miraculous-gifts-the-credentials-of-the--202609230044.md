Own-words summary of [[counterfeit-miracles--202609230039]].

Warfield grants that "The Apostolic Church was characteristically a
miracle-working church". In the church of 1 Cor 12 and 14 the exception
"would be, not a church with, but a church without, such gifts" (p. 5). He
denies that this made the gifts the Christian's possession as such. They
"were not the possession of the primitive Christian as such ... they were
distinctively the authentication of the Apostles. They were part of the
credentials of the Apostles as the authoritative agents of God in founding
the church", and so "they necessarily passed away with it" (p. 6).

He argues this from the way the gifts were passed on. Outside Pentecost and
Cornelius, the charismata are never recorded as conferred "without the
laying on of the hands of Apostles", and never by anyone else's hands. The
Samaritans (Acts 8:14-17) are the "cardinal instance". Their conversion under
Philip is taken for granted, yet the gifts come only when Peter and John
arrive. The episode teaches the church "to distinguish between the gifts of
grace and the gifts of power", so that believers without extraordinary gifts
are still counted Christians, and it fixes the gifts' function: "to
authenticate the Apostles as the authoritative founders of the church"
(pp. 21-23). The link between miracle and faith is therefore located in the
Apostle's commission. The recipient's faith has nothing to do with it.

In the Faith-Healing lectures Warfield takes up Gordon's definition, "a
miracle is the immediate action of God, as distinguished from His mediate
action through natural laws", and calls it one "than which no definition
could be clearer or better". He then objects that faith-healing writers
blur the issue by pleading "every instance of answer to prayer" as a
miracle. On this account the doubter is wrongly taken to deny "any real,
answer to prayer, instead of merely the continuance through all time of the
specific mode of answer to prayer which comes by miracle" (pp. 160-161). The
praying faith of the present is thus answered, but by God's mediate
providence rather than by miracle.

Scope: the first lecture's thesis and the definitional section of the
Faith-Healing lecture. Warfield's historical surveys (patristic,
ecclesiastical, Irvingite, Lourdes) are in the capture but not summarized.
