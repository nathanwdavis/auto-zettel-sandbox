**Grounding: the raw capture of Ibn Ezra's two comment segments, Hebrew and the Strickman-Silver English, from the Sefaria API, read whole.**

**The plain reading.** On the lemma "THE SECRET THINGS": "The reference is to one who serves idols in secret." On "BELONG UNTO THE LORD OUR GOD": "Its meaning is that his punishment is in the hands of God, and the Lord will exact punishment from him. However, if the sin was committed in public there is an obligation on us and our children to do in accordance with that which is written in the Torah." The Hebrew is as compact: הטעם מי שיעבוד עבודת כוכבים בסתר, and משפטו ביד השם והוא יפרע ממנו.

**The reading he rejects.** He then reports another: "the secret and the revealed things belong to God. It is incumbent upon us only to observe the words of the Torah." His verdict: "Now this interpretation has no head or foot." (בלא ראש ורגל.) His objection is contextual, "What reason is there to mention this here?", and he goes on to read the verse as the hinge to the promise of return from exile that follows in chapter 30.

**Why the rejected reading matters here.** It is the only reading on file in the Jewish tradition that removes the division of jurisdiction from the verse, giving both participles to God and leaving Israel only the duty of obedience, and it is reported in order to be dismissed. Even so it is not an epistemic reading: it does not say that the secret things are knowledge God has kept back, only that both the hidden and the open are God's concern. So the nearest the tradition on file comes to reading the verse without the jurisdiction axis is a reading that still says nothing about divine knowledge withheld, and that its reporter calls headless.

**Limits.** The translators' footnotes are embedded in the served text and are theirs; the second half of the comment, on the grammar of Deuteronomy 30:3, is not used.
