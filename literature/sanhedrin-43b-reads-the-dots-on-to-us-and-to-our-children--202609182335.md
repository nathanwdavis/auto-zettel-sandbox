**Grounding: the raw capture of three segments of Sanhedrin 43b from the Sefaria API, read whole; the rest of the folio is not captured and not used.**

**The question.** The Gemara has been discussing Achan, whose theft of the devoted things brought defeat on Israel at Ai, and asks why the nation had not been punished for his sin before. Rabbi Yohanan answers in the name of Rabbi Elazar: "Because God did not punish the nation as a whole for hidden sins committed by individuals until the Jewish people crossed the Jordan River."

**The verse, and the dots.** The Gemara then cites Deuteronomy 29:28, in a rendering that already decides what the participles refer to: "The hidden matters belong to the Lord our God, but those matters that are revealed belong to us and to our children forever [ad olam], that we may do all the words of this Torah". It asks why a Torah scroll carries dots over "to us and to our children" and over the *ayin* of "forever", and gives Rabbi Yehuda's answer: "The dots, which function like erasures that weaken the force of the words, teach that God did not punish the nation for hidden sins until the Jewish people crossed the Jordan River."

**The dispute.** Rabbi Nehemya objects that the nation is never liable for individuals' hidden sins: "And does God ever punish the nation as a whole for hidden sins committed by individuals?", citing the verse's "forever" as "indicating that the Jewish people will never be collectively held responsible for the secret sins of individuals". On his reading the dots weaken the *revealed* half instead: "so too, He did not punish the entire nation for sins committed publicly by individuals until the Jewish people crossed the Jordan River." That is the line Rashi's comment on the verse follows, and the crossing of the Jordan is where the tradition dates the mutual responsibility (*arvut*) accepted at Gerizim and Ebal.

**What the passage settles.** Both tanna'im, and the Gemara framing them, take the verse to be about sins and collective punishment; neither reads it of anything God knows and Israel does not. The hidden things are what individuals do in secret, the revealed things what they do in public, and the whole exegesis concerns when the community becomes answerable for either. The passage is the primary source behind Rashi's reading and confirms that it is the rabbinic reading and not Rashi's own.

**Limits.** Three segments of a long discussion, in one translation; the surrounding material on Achan and the continuation of the dispute are not captured.
