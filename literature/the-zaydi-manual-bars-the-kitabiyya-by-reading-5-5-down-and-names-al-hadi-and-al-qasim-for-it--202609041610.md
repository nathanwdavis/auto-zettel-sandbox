Own-words summary of
[[ibn-miftah-sharh-al-azhar-vol-4-on-the-unbelieving-wife--202609041600]]. Every
English rendering is this repository's own working translation of the captured
Arabic, and the Arabic is printed beside each rendering that carries weight. The
standing translation debt is unpaid here too and is tracked in INBOX.

**How the quotations were checked, and why the check is unusually load-bearing
here.** The capture is OCR of a scanned print, not clean digital text. Every
Arabic string below was matched **exactly as the OCR has it** against the
capture's excerpt body — not against the capture header, which contains Arabic of
this base's own writing and would otherwise match a quotation to itself.
Whitespace was normalised, nothing else. Controls were run in the same pass: the
undamaged form `الهادي` occurs **nowhere** in the capture, which is the positive
evidence for the OCR-damage claim made below rather than an excuse for it.
Corrected readings appear in square brackets and are this base's, not the source's.

**The rule, in the *matn*.** The spouses must agree `(في الملة'“)`, in religion —
the two glyphs inside the parenthesis are footnote markers, not text:

> فلا تحل الكافرة للمسلم» ولا المسلمة للكافرء ولا اليهودية […] للنصراني» ولا
> العكس» وكذلك كل امرأة تخالف الرجل في ملته فإنها تحرم عليه

(The `[…]` is not an ellipsis of this base's choosing: the *matn* is interrupted
at that point by a page of the commentary's own footnote apparatus, and the two
halves are quoted here as they stand on either side of it.)

the unbelieving woman is not lawful to the Muslim, nor the Muslim woman to the
unbeliever, nor the Jewish woman to the Christian, nor the reverse; and likewise
every woman who differs from the man in his religion is forbidden to him. The bar
is symmetrical and it is not about Islam in particular — a Christian may not
marry a Jew either, `الكفر ملل ختلفة يكفر بعضهم بعضاً` [read: `ملل مختلفة`],
unbelief being different religions which declare each other unbelieving.

**The interesting move is what the commentary does with Q 5:5, and it is not
abrogation.** Every Shi'i witness this base has read handled the clash between
Q 2:221 and Q 5:5 as a question about *naskh* — which verse cancels which, and
in which order they were revealed. Al-Tusi and al-Tabrisi ran the abrogation
backwards; al-Tabataba'i called the whole abrogation claim `فساد` and rebuilt the
rule as *takhsis*. The Zaydi manual does neither. It leaves Q 5:5 standing and
narrows what it says:

> وأما قوله تعال … والشحصتات من الذي ووا اكاب … فالمراد به من آمن من آهل
> الكتاب» كذا ذكره المادي والقاسم وغيرهما من أئمة أهل البيت

[read: `وأما قوله تعالى … والمحصنات من الذين أوتوا الكتاب … فالمراد به من آمن من
أهل الكتاب، كذا ذكره الهادي والقاسم وغيرهما من أئمة أهل البيت`] — as for God's
word *and the chaste women of those given the Book*, what is meant by it is
**those of the People of the Book who believed**; so said al-Hadi and al-Qasim
and others of the imams of the People of the House. The verse is not cancelled;
its subject is read as converts. A permission that survives intact and applies to
nobody the dispute is about does the same work as an abrogation, without
incurring the chronological objection that al-Tabataba'i used to destroy the
Imami *naskh* route.

Two words in that sentence are OCR-damaged, and both matter, so both are marked:
the Qur'anic lemma is set in a decorative font that the OCR renders as
`والشحصتات من الذي ووا اكاب`, identified here as Q 5:5's `والمحصنات من الذين
أوتوا الكتاب` — the identification is this base's, from the wording and the
context, not something the capture states cleanly — and `المادي` is `الهادي`,
Yahya b. al-Husayn al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, the school's founding imam in Yemen.

**Who is named, and why this closes something.** `الهادي والقاسم` are the two
imams the base's only previous witness for this attribution, *Tafsir al-A'qam*,
also named. That witness was a *tafsir*, five centuries after them and reachable
from one host only. This is a work of Zaydi *fiqh*, from a different century and
a different host, naming the same two men for the same reading. The attribution
no longer rests on one witness.

**The school is split, and the manual is where the split is visible.** Having
given the rule, the commentary records the dissent by name:

> وعن الصادق والباقر وأبي حنيفة والشافعي: أنه يجوز نكاح الكتابيات
>
> واختاره في الانتتصار قال فيه: وهو إجماع الصدر" الأول

(Two sentences, quoted separately because a footnote marker stands between them
in the print. The first carries an invisible right-to-left mark after `الصادق`
in the OCR, and the second carries a footnote marker between `الصدر` and
`الأول`; the mechanical check therefore normalises whitespace and invisible
bidirectional marks, and nothing else. Read `الانتصار` for the OCR's doubled
`الانتتصار`.)

From al-Sadiq, al-Baqir, Abu Hanifa and al-Shafi'i: that
marrying women of the People of the Book is permitted; and the author of
*al-Intisar* chose that, saying it is the consensus of the first generation. A
marginal note adds `والناصر`, al-Nasir, to the permitting side. So within Zaydi
*fiqh* the strict reading is the school's rule and the lenient one is a position
held by named Zaydi authorities — which is what `بعض الزيدية`, "some Zaydis", has
been claiming since al-Tabrisi wrote it in the twelfth century.

**The apparatus argues with itself.** A gloss beside the consensus claim disputes
it: `وهذه نكتة» أعني: دعوى الإجاع؛ لمخالفة كثير من العلهاء الأخيار» والجحهابذة من
أئمة الآل الكبار» فينظر في ذلك» ويبحث عنها هنالك` [read: `الإجماع`, `العلماء`] —
this is a difficulty, I mean the claim of consensus, because many excellent
scholars and great imams of the Family disagreed; so look into it and investigate
it there. The gloss is tagged `(انتصار بلفظه)`, "*al-Intisar*, in its own
wording", and this base cannot tell from the excerpt whether that means
*al-Intisar* qualified its own consensus claim or the commentator is quoting it
against itself. Either way the manual prints the objection rather than the claim
alone.

**Two further details worth carrying.** First, a note from *al-Thamarat*: `وقد
قال الإمام يحبى بن حمزة … إنم من العرب تهودوا» فلا تنکح نساؤهم على قول من يجوز
نکاحهم` [read: `يحيى بن حمزة`, `إنهم`] — Imam Yahya b. Hamza said of the Jews of
Yemen that they are Arabs who Judaised, so their women are not to be married even
on the view that permits it. The permission, where held, is held about a
historically continuous People of the Book and not about anyone who now professes
the religion. Second, the same apparatus cites the Companions' practice the Imami
*fiqh* cites on the opposite side: `ونكح طلحة نصرانية» ونكح حذيفة بهودية`, Talha
married a Christian woman and Hudhayfa a Jewish one. The precedent is common
property; what differs is which way it is made to cut.

**Al-Hadi's own argument, on the converse case.** For the prohibition on a Muslim
woman marrying a non-Muslim the commentary quotes al-Hadi's *al-Manzila bayna
al-manzilatayn* directly: `أجع المسلمون أنه لا يحل لامرأة مؤمنة أن تزوج رجلا
يهودياً ولا نصرانياً` [read: `أجمع`], the Muslims agree that a believing woman
may not be married to a Jew or a Christian, with the argument from the absence of
inheritance between people of two religions. This base should note that this
quotation is about the *converse* direction, on which all the schools it has read
agree, and is not evidence for the contested direction.

**What this note does not establish.** One manual, one section, one host. The
work is a commentary, so what it reports of al-Hadi and al-Qasim is transmission
rather than their own text — better than *Tafsir al-A'qam* by being *fiqh* and by
being independent of it, but still not al-Hadi's or al-Qasim's own writing, which
this base has never seen. And *al-Intisar*, the lenient authority, is known here
only through this commentary's citations of it.
