Own-words summary of
[[mueller-and-oppenheimer-the-pen-is-mightier-than-the-keyboard--202609040155]].
**This note records the abstract's stated design and stated findings only.**
The article could not be read from this environment, so nothing here reports
how the studies were run, how many students took part, how large any
difference was, or how the authors argued from their data to their conclusion.
Where the abstract's own words carry the claim they are quoted exactly; where
this note characterises, it says so.

The paper's target is a comparison people usually make on other grounds. The
abstract opens by setting aside the familiar objection to laptops in
classrooms — that they invite multitasking and distraction — and stakes out a
claim that survives its removal: "even when laptops are used solely to take
notes, they may still be impairing learning because their use results in
shallower processing." The interesting move is that the medium is not the
explanation offered. Shallower processing is.

The reported result, across what the abstract calls three studies, is that
"students who took notes on laptops performed worse on conceptual questions
than students who took notes longhand." Two things in that sentence are worth
holding onto, because both are load-bearing and both are easy to lose in
citation. It is *conceptual* questions that the abstract names, not recall of
facts. And the comparison is between two note-taking conditions; on the
abstract's evidence this paper says nothing about taking no notes at all.

The mechanism is where this paper touches the practices this repository
documents. The abstract's closing sentence is the one to keep: "whereas taking
more notes can be beneficial, laptop note takers' tendency to transcribe
lectures verbatim rather than processing information and reframing it in their
own words is detrimental to learning." Note what is and is not being blamed.
Not the keyboard, not the volume of notes — the abstract concedes in the same
breath that more notes can help. What is blamed is *transcription in place of
reframing*: the note taker who copies has not processed, and it is the
processing whose absence costs.

That is a claim about a rule this repository actually follows — a literature
note here must be in the reader's own words and must never paste source prose.
It is not, however, a claim about a slip-box. A lecture, immediate testing, and
two ways of writing during it are the whole of what the abstract describes.
Anything about linking, retrieval years later, or accumulation is outside what
this note can support, and
[[the-note-taking-experiments-do-not-reach-the-slip-box-claims--202609040215]]
is where that limit is argued rather than left implicit.

Two limits on the note itself, both from the reference note and neither
inferred here. The abstract is one witness to its own paper's findings and
cannot be checked against the article. And a corrigendum to this paper was
published in 2018 whose content is not retrievable from this environment — so
whether any part of what is summarised above was later corrected is a question
this repository has open, not one it has answered.
