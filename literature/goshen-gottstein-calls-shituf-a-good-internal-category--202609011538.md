Own-words summary of
[[goshen-gottstein-shituf-critical-and-constructive-reflections--202609011533]].
**This records the chapter's stated conclusion only, from the publisher
abstract; the chapter body was not obtained, so nothing here reports how the
argument is made.**

The abstract sets up a two-sided verdict on shituf. On the appreciative side,
shituf "provides the basis for the few contemporary efforts at recognizing
Hinduism as a valid spiritual path for its followers," and the author credits
what its application "offers for contemporary interfaith relations."

On the critical side, the stated conclusion is that the category earns its keep
in one direction and fails in the other: "Shituf is a useful internal category,
but one that does not do justice to the theological nuances of the religions it
seeks to describe." He proposes keeping it "as a purely internal one by means
of which Jews come to terms with another religion," and warns that extending it
to Hinduism, "as previously made in relation to Christianity," requires asking
"how adequate it really is to a description of Hindu religious reality" --
concluding that it may need reinterpretation, or at minimum use "with awareness
of the discrepancy between our own internal theological and halachic categories
and what should be an adequate description of the religious reality of other
religions."

Two things this repository can take from the abstract. First, the description
of shituf as *internal* is the author's own word, not a critic's gloss on him.
Second, the phrase "as previously made in relation to Christianity" is his
acknowledgement that the Christian case is the precedent the Hindu case is
being argued from -- which is the case the same-God cluster here is built on.
