Own-words summary of the codicil (pp. 501–510) in
[[franklin-last-will-and-testament-with-codicil--202609010406]]: Franklin
leaves £1,000 sterling each to Boston and Philadelphia, not as gifts to
spend but as loan funds to be compounded for two hundred years. The money
is to be lent at 5 per cent to young tradesmen just out of apprenticeship;
the repayment terms are engineered so the fund re-lends itself — each
borrower repays a tenth of the principal yearly along with the interest,
and those repayments go straight out to new borrowers. Managers serve
unpaid, and the design's stated ideal is that the capital never sit idle.
Franklin then states the compound-growth projections explicitly, with
century checkpoints: £131,000 at year 100 (of which £100,000 is spent on
public works and £31,000 continues), and £4,061,000 at year 200 — while
conditioning both on the loop actually running uninterrupted.

Verbatim quotations, normalized as follows and cross-checked word-for-word
against the independent transcription named in the reference note (with a
third witness, Sparks's edition, arbitrating the one divergence): the OCR's
scattered "hi" for "in" is corrected; line-break hyphenation is rejoined;
the running head interrupting a sentence at the p. 504/505 break is
omitted; "per cent, per annum" is reproduced with the comma both witnesses
print; the original's capitalization is kept as printed:

- "I wish to be useful even after my death, if possible, in forming and
  advancing other young men, that may be serviceable to their country in
  both these towns." (p. 503)
- "to let out the sum upon interest, at five per cent, per annum, to such
  young married artificers, under the age of twenty-five years, as have
  served an apprenticeship in the said town" (p. 503)
- "These aids may, therefore, be small at first, but, as the capital
  increases by the accumulated interest, they will be more ample." (p. 504)
- "each borrower shall be obliged to pay, with the yearly interest, one
  tenth part of the principal, which sums of principal and interest, so
  paid in, shall be again let out to fresh borrowers." (p. 504; the
  constitution.org transcription eye-skips ", which sums of principal" —
  Smyth and Sparks agree on the reading quoted here)
- "it is hoped that no part of the money will at any time be dead, or be
  diverted to other purposes, but be continually augmenting by the
  interest" (pp. 504–505)
- "If this plan is executed, and succeeds as projected without
  interruption for one hundred years, the sum will then be one hundred and
  thirty-one thousand pounds" (p. 505)
- "At the end of this second term, if no unfortunate accident has
  prevented the operation, the sum will be four millions and sixty one
  thousand pounds sterling" (p. 505)

The repayment-recycling clause directly supports
[[reinvested-returns-compound--202701010011]] — the codicil is a working
specification of the reinvestment step, performed by people rather than a
bank. The deliberate 200-year horizon, chosen by a testator who knew he
would see none of it, supports
[[compounding-favors-horizons-longer-than-one-life--202609010205]] with a
designed instance rather than an argument. Note the codicil credits no
source for the scheme; this base records no influence claim (Price or
otherwise) for it.
