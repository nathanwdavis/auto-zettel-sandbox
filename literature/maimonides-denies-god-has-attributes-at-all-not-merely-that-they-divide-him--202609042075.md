Own-words summary of [[maimonides-guide-1-57--202609042071]].

The chapter begins from a distinction about existence: for anything whose
existence has a cause, "existence is an element superadded to their essence."
God is the exception, "for His existence, as we have said, is absolute" — and so
in Him "existence and essence are perfectly identical; He is not a substance to
which existence is joined as an accident, as an additional element."

Then the sentence that gives the doctrine its reach: "Consequently God exists
without possessing the attribute of existence. Similarly He lives, without
possessing the attribute of life; knows, without possessing the attribute of
knowledge; is omnipotent without possessing the attribute of omnipotence; is
wise, without possessing the attribute of wisdom: all this reduces itself to one
and the same entity; there is no plurality in Him."

Two features are worth keeping apart. The *argument* is about composition: what
is composed of essence plus something else depends on a cause, and God has none.
The *conclusion* is about predication: the terms we use of God do not name
attributes He possesses. Maimonides does not say that God's attributes are
identical with each other and with Him and leave it there; he says that He is
wise "without possessing the attribute of wisdom."

He then generalises the point to number itself: "unity and plurality are
accidents supervening to an object according as it consists of many elements or
of one," and "in the same way as number is not the substance of the things
numbered, so is unity not" the substance of God. Even calling Him one is
predicating an accident, on this account.
