Own-words summary of [[tusi-al-tibyan-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609041100]]. Every
English rendering below is this repository's own working translation of the
captured Arabic, and the Arabic is printed beside each rendering that carries
weight. The standing translation debt is unpaid here too and is tracked in
INBOX.

**On Q 29:46, and the sentence this cycle went looking for.** Al-Tusi walks the
verse clause by clause. Then, on the clause four cycles of this base have
watched eight commentators pass over:

> ثم قال تعالى للمؤمنين { وقولوا آمنا بالذي أنزل إلينا } من القرآن
> { وأنزل إليكم } من التوراة والانجيل، وقولوا { وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد }
> **لا شريك له** { ونحن له مسلمون } طائعون.

Then God said to the believers, "and say: we believe in what was sent down to
us" — of the Qur'an — "and what was sent down to you" — of the Torah and the
Gospel — and say, "and our God and your God is one": **He has no partner**; "and
to Him we are submitted": obedient.

So the clause is glossed, and the gloss is `لا شريك له`. Two things follow, and
they pull in different directions. It is not the reading al-Tabari gave: `معبود`
— the word in which al-Tabari identifies the two parties' *object of worship* —
does not occur anywhere in al-Tusi's comment on either verse, while `واحد`
occurs twice in the same comment, so the tool that reports the absence can see
words where they are. And it is exactly the reading this base had found only in
the modern register: oneness taken as the denial of partnership, i.e. *tawhid*,
rather than as the identification of a shared object of worship.

**On Q 29:46, the rest of the comment, which is where the tension sits.** He
reports Qatada's view that the verse is abrogated by fighting and rejects it:
`وقال غيره: هي ثابتة، وهو الأولى، لانه لا دليل على ما قاله` — others say it
stands, and that is the better view, because there is no evidence for what he
said. On the exception `إلا الذين ظلموا منهم` he reports Mujahid —
`قال مجاهد: { إلا الذين ظلموا منهم } بمنع الجزية`, by withholding the *jizya* —
and Ibn Zayd, those who persist in unbelief after the proof has been established
against them.

And he raises, in his own voice, the objection the earlier commentaries in this
base never state: `فان قيل: لم استثنى الذين ظلموا؟ وكلهم ظالم لنفسه بكفره!` —
if it be asked: why did He except *those who do wrong*, when every one of them
wrongs himself by his unbelief? His answer narrows the exception to conduct:
the wrongdoing meant is `في جدالهم أو في غيره مما يقتضي الاغلاظ لهم`, in their
disputing or in something else calling for harshness towards them. The exception
is thus about how the other party behaves, not about what they believe — which
is a different result from the one the 09:00Z cycle recorded from al-Razi, who
read the same exception as excluding trinitarians *as associators*.

**On Q 3:64, the common-word verse.** He reports three views on the addressees
without settling them: the Christians of Najran (al-Hasan, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd,
Muhammad b. Ja'far b. al-Zubayr); the Jews of Medina (Qatada, al-Rabi', Ibn
Jurayj) — `وقد روى ذلك أصحابنا`, "and our companions have also narrated that",
the first place in this base where an Imami transmission marker appears in this
cluster; and, from Abu 'Ali al-Jubba'i, both parties on the plain sense.

The lordship clause he reads with a report from Abu 'Abd Allah (Ja'far
al-Sadiq): `ما عبدوهم من دون الله وإنما حرموا لهم حلالا وأحلوا لهم حراماً، فكان
ذلك اتخاذ الارباب من دون الله` — they did not *worship* them apart from God;
rather they forbade to them what was lawful and made lawful what was forbidden,
and that was the taking of lords apart from God. This is the same substance as
the 'Adi ibn Hatim report the Sunni commentaries use for the clause, arriving
through an Imami chain: obedience in law is itself the lordship being renounced.

He closes the verse mildly. `فإن تولوا فقولوا اشهدوا بأنا مسلمون` is
`أي أقيموا على إسلامكم، وقولوا لهم: { اشهدوا بأنا مسلمون } مقيمون على الاسلام` —
persist in your submission and say to them, bear witness that we are the
submitted, persisting in submission. There is no victor-and-vanquished framing
of the kind al-Zamakhshari gives the same words, and no reproach read into them.

**What is not here.** `معبود`: 0 in both sections. `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد` occurs
once in the commentary on 29:46 — the gloss above — and not at all in the
commentary on 3:64, where the clause does not appear. The counts, their
controls, and the single-host limitation on this capture are in the capture
header; the `معبود` absence in particular rests on one host and is filed to
INBOX for confirmation rather than treated as closed.
