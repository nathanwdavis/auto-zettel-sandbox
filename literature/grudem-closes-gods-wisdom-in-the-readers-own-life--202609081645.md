The passage does two things, and they are different in kind.

**The attribute in a life (paras. 1-2).** Grudem turns from wherever God's wisdom has been shown before to "our individual lives," quotes Rom. 8:28 in full, and reads it as Paul affirming that God "does work wisely in all the things that come into our lives," advancing us through those things toward the goal named in the next verse, conformity to the image of Christ (Rom. 8:29). The end is stated twice and given a further end: that we might be like Christ *and thereby bring glory to him*. The register is pastoral throughout — "our great confidence," "a source of peace day by day," and a second paragraph whose whole business is to "quiet our discouragement," with the assurance issued about the present day ("even today") and made conditional on one thing only, "if we are his children."

The worked case is Paul's thorn. Grudem cites 2 Cor. 12:7 for the thorn and 12:8-10 for Paul's acceptance of it — citing, not quoting — and describes what was accepted as "something that, though painful, God in his wisdom had chosen not to remove." So the instance offered for the attribute is a request refused and an affliction left in place; the wisdom is located in the not-removing.

**The attribute shared (paras. 3-4).** "God's wisdom is, of course, in part communicable to us." Grudem then supplies, in four compressed moves, everything the passage says about the shared portion:

- *How it is asked for.* Petition, on a promise: James 1:5, quoted in full, with its "generously" and "without reproach."
- *Where it mainly comes from.* "primarily from reading and obeying his Word," on Ps. 19:7 ("The testimony of the Lord is sure, making wise the simple") with Deut. 4:6-8 as a cross-reference.
- *What it is.* Glossed in an appositive as it goes past: "This wisdom, or skill in living a life pleasing to God." The gloss is of the human possession, not of God's own wisdom.
- *Where it starts, and how it is checked.* "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom" (Ps. 111:10; Prov. 9:10; cf. Prov. 1:7), explained by a because-clause that is entirely motivational — fearing to dishonour or displease God, and fearing "his fatherly discipline," yields "the motivation that makes us want to follow his ways." And the possession is tested by conduct: humility and not pride (Prov. 11:2; James 3:13), "a gentle and peaceful spirit" and not arrogance (James 3:14-18), a continual walk "in dependence on the Lord and with a desire to exalt him."

**One thing the passage does that is worth recording exactly.** The ESV wording Grudem quotes makes "all things" the grammatical subject: *all things work together for good*. Both of his restatements of it supply God as the agent of that working — "God does work wisely in all the things that come into our lives," and "God causes all things to move us toward his ultimate goal for our lives." Whether Rom. 8:28 should be construed with God as subject is a real and contested question about the verse, turning on a textual variant this base has not examined; the point here is only that Grudem's paraphrase settles it in one direction without marking that it has, in a passage where the quoted rendering does not.
