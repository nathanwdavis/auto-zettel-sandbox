Grudem organizes divine attributes into two categories in his systematic theology. Incommunicable attributes are qualities of God that God does not share with creation. Grudem identifies five incommunicable attributes, each defined with a primary characterization balanced by a note about God's acts or feelings. Independence refers to God's self-sufficiency and lack of need for the created order, though creation may glorify God and bring God joy. Unchangeableness means God remains constant in his fundamental being, perfections, purposes, and promises, even though God acts and responds with emotion to different circumstances. Eternity refers to God's existence without beginning, ending, or succession of moments in God's own being, with God perceiving all time with equal vividness, though God also acts within time and responds to temporal events. Omnipresence means God has no spatial dimensions or size yet is fully present at every point in space, even though God acts differently in different locations (this definition is verified by the unattributed lesson outline only; other secondary sources quote it with one-word variations, which per house standard do not meet the two-witness threshold). Unity refers to God not being divided into parts, though different divine attributes may be emphasized in different contexts.

The excerpts do not capture Grudem's definition of communicable attributes, so the term is used here only as the label for the other category in Grudem's taxonomy.
