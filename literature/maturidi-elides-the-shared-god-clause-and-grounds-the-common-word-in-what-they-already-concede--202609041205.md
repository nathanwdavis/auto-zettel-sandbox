## On Q 29:46 — the clause, and what he does with it

Al-Maturidi opens by saying the verse admits three construals:
`الآية تخرج على وجوه ثلاثة`. The first two concern the exception
`إلا الذين ظلموا منهم`: either those who wrong are to be disputed with in no
manner at all, since they are the ones `لا يقبلون الحجة` — who do not accept the
proof — and are `أهل عناد ومكابرة`, people of obstinacy and contention; or the
phrase is not an exception from what precedes it at all but a fresh imperative
(`ليس على الثنيا من الأوّل، ولكن على الابتداء`), on the model of Q 2:150.

The third construal is the one that matters here, and it is the reason the
silence in this comment is worth a note rather than a line in a tally. On the
third reading, the *saying* is itself the good disputation the verse commands:

> والثالث: جائز أن يكون قوله: { وَقُولُوۤاْ آمَنَّا بِٱلَّذِيۤ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْنَا
> وَأُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ... } إلى آخر ما ذكر: هي المجادلة الحسنة التي أمروا بها؛
> لأن تلك مما يقبلها العقل والطبع، وبها جاءت الكتب والرسل، فلا سبيل إلى ردّ ذلك.

The third: it may be that His words, "and say: we believe in what was sent down
to us and what was sent down to you…" — **to the end of what was mentioned** —
are themselves the good disputation they were commanded to make; because that is
of the sort reason and natural disposition accept, and with it the Books and the
Messengers came, so there is no way to reject it.

So al-Maturidi does take up the very sentence in which the clause
`وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد` stands, and makes it the content of the command — and he
quotes it as far as `وَأُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ` and then writes `إلى آخر ما ذكر`, "to
the end of what was mentioned". He does the same thing once more in the same
comment. The clause is not passed over in the sense of never being reached; it is
reached, abbreviated away, and the abbreviated remainder is then said to be
something reason itself accepts. `معبود` — al-Tabari's word — does not occur in
this comment, and neither does any gloss of the clause; the mechanical counts and
their controls are in the capture header, and the two occurrences of the clause
in the captured section are both the host's own verse block, printed identically
at the head of each page.

## On Q 29:46 — what he supplies instead

**A defence of disputation itself.** `ثم فيه دلالة جواز المناظرة والمجادلة مع
الكفرة في الدين` — then in it is an indication of the permissibility of debate
and disputation with the unbelievers in religion. He adds that this is not as
some people say, that debate with them is impermissible, and puts their
prohibition down to `جهلهم بحجج الإسلام وبراهينه`, their ignorance of Islam's
proofs and demonstrations. That is a theologian defending his own discipline out
of a verse about how to argue with the People of the Book.

**And the juristic register, reported as opinions.** Three positions are relayed
in sequence and none is adjudicated:

> وقال بعضهم: من لا عهد معهم فجادلهم بالسيوف، ومن كان معه عهد وكتاب فجادلهم
> بالحجج. وقال بعضهم: هو منسوخ بقوله: { قَاتِلُواْ ٱلَّذِينَ لاَ يُؤْمِنُونَ
> بِٱللَّهِ... } الآية [التوبة: 29]. ومنهم من يقول: من أدّى إليكم الجزية فلا
> تغلظوا له القول وقولا لهم قولا حسناً، ومن لم يؤدّ فاغلظوا لهم وجادلوهم
> بالسيوف، والله أعلم.

Some said: those with whom you have no covenant — dispute with them with swords;
whoever has a covenant and a book — dispute with them with proofs. Some said: it
is abrogated by His words, "fight those who do not believe in God…" [Q 9:29].
And among them are those who say: whoever pays you the *jizya*, do not be harsh
with him in speech but speak well to them; and whoever does not pay, be harsh
with them and dispute with them with swords. And God knows best.

Two features of that passage are worth keeping. It is the earliest attestation in
this base of the sword-and-*jizya* reading of Q 29:46, earlier by more than a
century than al-Tusi's, in whose comment this base first found it. And unlike
al-Tusi, who reports the abrogation view and rejects it (`هي ثابتة، وهو الأولى،
لانه لا دليل على ما قاله`), al-Maturidi leaves all three standing.

**The cross-reference, in his own voice.** Reporting a further view that the
verse directs disputation towards those of the People of the Book who confirm
rather than conceal, he adds:

> والأوّل كقوله: { تَعَالَوْاْ إِلَىٰ كَلِمَةٍ سَوَآءٍ بَيْنَنَا
> وَبَيْنَكُمْ... } الآية [آل عمران: 64]، والمجادلة الحسنة هي التي جاء بها
> الكتاب ويوجبها العقل.

And the first is like His saying, "Come to a word common between us and you…"
[Q 3:64]; and the good disputation is that which the Book brought and which
reason requires.

## On Q 3:64 — the common word, and where its commonality comes from

`سواء` is glossed `عدل`, just — the same gloss the four Sunni classical
commentators in this base give — and the "word" is `كلمة الإخلاص والتوحيد`, the
word of sincere devotion and of divine oneness. (The clause immediately after,
as served, reads `أي: تلك الكلمة عدم بيننا وبينكم`, where `عدم` is almost
certainly a corruption of `عدل`; it is quoted here as served and nothing rests on
it.) Then comes the sentence this note was written for:

> لأنهم كانوا يقرون أن خالق السماوات والأرض: الله… وكذلك يقرون أن خالقهم الله…
> فدعاهم رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى ألا يجعلوا عبادتهم لغير الذي أنعم
> عليهم؛ **إذ العبادة لا تكون إلا لله الذي أقروا جميعاً أنه خالق السماوات
> والأرض، وأنه ربهم**.

Because they used to acknowledge that the creator of the heavens and the earth
is God… and likewise they acknowledge that their creator is God… so the
Messenger of God called them to render their worship to none but the one who
bestowed favour upon them; **since worship is due to none but God, whom they all
acknowledged to be the creator of the heavens and the earth and to be their
Lord**.

The two acknowledgements are anchored in scripture rather than asserted: Q 31:25
and Q 43:87, the verses in which the addressees, asked who created the heavens
and the earth and who created them, answer "God". What he charges them with is
not misidentifying the deity but misdirecting the worship — `لكن منهم من يعبد
دون الله أوثاناً`, some of them worship idols besides God, on the excuse of
Q 39:3, and some assign Him partners in His worship.

The argument he then gives for the "word" is rational rather than scriptural, in
two steps: `لأن العبادة لواحد أهون وأخف من العبادة لعدد` — worship of one is
easier and lighter than worship of a number — and

> إذ ذاك جور وظلم في العقل أن ينعم أحد على آخر، فيشكر غيره.

since it is, in reason, injustice and wrong that one should bestow favour on
another and that other thank someone else.

Worship, on this account, is `لشكر وجزاء ما أنعم عليهم` — gratitude and requital
for benefit received — which is why the argument runs through who the benefactor
is rather than through what the deity is like.

Finally, `فإن تولوا` is given two readings: turning away from obedience and
*tawhid*, or `ويحتمل: فإن تولوا عن المباهلة والملاعنة`, turning away from the
mutual imprecation — which locates the verse in the Najran delegation's visit.
And `اشهدوا بأنا مسلمون` is glossed `أي: مخلصون العبادة له، صادقون الشكر على ما
أنعم علينا`: sincere in worship to Him, truthful in gratitude for what He has
bestowed on us.

**One caution about attribution.** Two sentences in the Q 3:64 section are marked
`قال الشيخ - رحمه الله -` and belong to the recension's transmitter-editor rather
than to al-Maturidi. Neither is quoted above as his.
