Own-words summary of [[nostra-aetate--202608311924]].

Section 3 says the Church "regards with esteem also the Moslems," and then makes
the affirmative claim plainly: "They adore the one God, living and subsisting in
Himself; merciful and all-powerful, the Creator of heaven and earth, who has
spoken to men." It ties their submission to Abraham's, "with whom the faith of
Islam takes pleasure in linking itself."

What makes the passage useful is that it does not purchase the affirmation by
suppressing the disagreement. In the same paragraph: "Though they do not
acknowledge Jesus as God, they revere Him as a prophet." The declaration holds
both -- a shared object of adoration, and an unresolved christological
difference -- without treating the second as cancelling the first.

That combination is the structural move the whole same-God dispute turns on, and
it is why this Catholic text keeps appearing in an argument among evangelicals.
