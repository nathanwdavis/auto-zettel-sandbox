Own-words summary of [[hodge-systematic-theology-volume-1--202609011033]].

Three positions from one volume, and the interest is in how they cohere.

On origin (ch. I): the knowledge of God is innate -- "By innate knowledge is
meant that which is due to our constitution, as sentient, rational, and moral
beings," opposed to knowledge "founded on experience," to "ab extra
instruction," and to "that acquired by a process of research and reasoning."
Section 2 is titled against the alternative: the knowledge of God "is not due
to a Process of Reasoning." Hodge's argument is distributional -- deriving the
idea of God from a quasi-scientific inference fails because "such
generalizations are possible only for cultivated minds," and belief in God is
found "in the minds of all men, even the least educated."

On the argument (ch. II): Hodge nonetheless states and defends the
teleological argument in full syllogistic dress: "Design supposes a designer.
The world everywhere exhibits marks of design. Therefore the world owes its
existence to an intelligent author" -- design meaning selection of an end,
choice of means, and their actual application.

On knowability (ch. IV): "It is the clear doctrine of the Scriptures that God
can be known," with Paul's statement about the heathen -- "they knew God" --
cited from Romans 1:19-21, 28 as proof. The claim is immediately bounded:
"our knowledge of God is partial and inadequate. There is infinitely more in
God than we have any idea of; and what we do know, we know imperfectly."

The coherence: the design argument cannot be what *originates* belief (ch. I
rules that out) and cannot deliver *complete* knowledge (ch. IV bounds it),
yet it remains worth stating -- its office is to vindicate and articulate, in
argumentative form, a knowledge the constitution already possesses. Note the
chapter order: the theistic arguments (ch. II) come *before* the knowability
chapter (ch. IV) opens with "Having considered the arguments in favor of the
doctrine that God is... we come now to consider the question, Can God be
known?"
