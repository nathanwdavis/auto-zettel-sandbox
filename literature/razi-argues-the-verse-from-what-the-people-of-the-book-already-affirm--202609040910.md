Own-words summary of
[[razi-mafatih-al-ghayb-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609040905]]. Every English
rendering below is this repository's own working translation of the captured
Arabic, and the Arabic is printed beside each rendering that carries weight.

**On Q 29:46.** al-Razi frames the verse as the second half of a pair: having
set out the way to guide the idolaters, God now sets out the way to guide the
People of the Book. He reports the reading that "do not dispute" means "do not
dispute them with the sword", then offers what he calls a subtler sense of his
own: the idolater came with what is objectionable, so it is fitting that he be
disputed with roughly and that his doctrine be denigrated — `وأمّا أهْلُ
الكِتابِ فَجاءُوا بِكُلِّ حَسَنٍ إلّا الِاعْتِرافَ بِالنَّبِيِّ`, but as for the
People of the Book, they came with everything good except acknowledgement of the
Prophet, `فَوَحَّدُوا وآمَنُوا بِإنْزالِ الكُتُبِ وإرْسالِ الرُّسُلِ
والحَشْرِ` — they professed [God's] oneness and believed in the sending down of
books, the sending of messengers, and the gathering. Because of that, he says,
they are disputed with in the better way first, their views are not made light
of and their forefathers are not charged with error, unlike the idolater.

The exception "except those of them who do wrong" he then reads, on that same
construction, as `إلّا الَّذِينَ أشْرَكُوا مِنهم بِإثْباتِ الوَلَدِ لِلَّهِ
والقَوْلِ بِثالِثِ ثَلاثَةٍ` — except those of them who associated partners by
asserting a son for God and by the doctrine of a third of three — `فَإنَّهم
ضاهَوْهم في القَوْلِ المُنكَرِ فَهُمُ الظّالِمُونَ؛ لِأنَّ الشِّرْكَ ظُلْمٌ
عَظِيمٌ`, for they have resembled them (the idolaters) in the objectionable
statement, so they are the wrongdoers, because association is a great wrong.
Those, on his reading, are argued with roughly after all.

Only then does the shared-God clause appear, and it appears as an item on the
credit side: `ثُمَّ إنَّهُ تَعالى بَيَّنَ ذَلِكَ الأحْسَنَ فَقَدَّمَ
مَحاسِنَهم بِقَوْلِهِ: وقُولُوا آمَنّا بِالَّذِي أُنْزِلَ إلَيْنا وأُنْزِلَ
إلَيْكم وإلَهُنا وإلَهُكم واحِدٌ ونَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ` — then God set out
that better way, putting their merits first, with His words "and say: we believe
in what was sent down to us and sent down to you, and our God and your God is
one, and we are submitted to Him". He quotes the clause and moves on. He does
not lemmatise `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد` separately, does not ask what it asserts, and
reports no one else's gloss on it. The rest of his comment is spent on the
following verse: the analogical proof that revelation to Muhammad is of a piece
with revelation before him, a three-way dispute over who "those to whom We gave
the Book" are (his own preference: the prophets, since God gave the Book to
prophets), and an analogy in which two factions quarrelling over the merits of
two kings are best answered by being told the two kings agree with each other.
He closes: `وامْتَزْتُمْ عَنِ المُشْرِكِينَ بِكُلِّ فَضِيلَةٍ، إلّا هَذِهِ
المَسْألَةَ الواحِدَةَ` — you were distinguished from the idolaters by every
merit, except this one question, and by denying it you join them.

**What is absent, and it is the point.** The word `معبود` — "object of
worship", the word in which al-Tabari states his identification of the God of
the two parties — occurs nowhere in al-Razi's comment on 29:46. It was searched
for, after normalisation, in three independently served copies of that comment
and occurs in none. The absence is not a genre effect: al-Razi is writing the
*kalam* commentary, he is discussing exactly this verse, he has just used the
verse to distinguish the People of the Book from idolaters — and he still does
not gloss the clause.

**On Q 3:64.** Here he comments at length, and the reading is a summons. The
setting he gives is the Najran delegation: after the proofs and the offer of
mutual imprecation, which they declined, God tells the Prophet to leave that
manner of speech `واعْدِلْ إلى مَنهَجٍ آخَرَ` and turn to another built on
fairness. "Come to a word equal between us and you" he glosses `هَلُمُّوا إلى
كَلِمَةٍ فِيها إنْصافٌ مِن بَعْضِنا لِبَعْضٍ، ولا مَيْلَ فِيهِ لِأحَدٍ عَلى
صاحِبِهِ` — come to a word in which there is fairness from each of us to the
other, with no leaning of one over his fellow. `السَّواءُ هو العَدْلُ
والإنْصافُ`, equality here is justice and fairness, which he derives from
*insaf* as the giving of a half: to be fair is to give the other his half, and
so to level. He quotes al-Zajjaj that `سواء` describes the word itself — a just,
upright word — so that `فَإذا آمَنّا بِها نَحْنُ وأنْتُمْ كُنّا عَلى السَّواءِ
والِاسْتِقامَةِ`, if we and you believe in it we shall be on level and upright
ground. The verb is future and conditional throughout: the equality is what
believing the word would produce, not a state the two parties are found in.

He records three views of the addressee — the Christians of Najran, the Jews of
Medina, or both — and prefers the Christians. He also notes that the address
"O People of the Book" is itself part of the fairness: it is among the finest of
titles, since it makes them people of God's Book, and such a title is used when
a man turns from wrangling with his opponent to seeking fairness.

**The three exclusions, and why those three.** al-Razi says the verse names
three things — that we worship none but God, associate nothing with Him, and
take not one another as lords besides God — `وإنَّما ذَكَرَ هَذِهِ الثَّلاثَةَ
لِأنَّ النَّصارى جَمَعُوا بَيْنَ هَذِهِ الثَّلاثَةِ`, because the Christians
combined all three. They worship other than God, namely the Messiah; they
associate others with Him, because they say he is a third of three, Father, Son
and Holy Spirit, `فَأثْبَتُوا ذَواتًا ثَلاثَةً قَدِيمَةً سَواءً` — so they
affirmed three eternal essences on a par. His argument that these are three
*independent* essences runs through the hypostases: the hypostasis of the Word
clothed itself in the humanity of the Messiah and the hypostasis of the Holy
Spirit in the humanity of Mary, and were they not independent this could not
have happened; `ولَمّا أثْبَتُوا ذَواتًا ثَلاثَةً مُسْتَقِلَّةً فَقَدْ
أشْرَكُوا` — and since they affirmed three independent essences, they have
committed association. The third item, taking rabbis and monks as lords, he
supports with four reasons, the last being that obeying them in disobedience is
all that lordship means.

For the rational warrant that all three are void he gives, among others: `وذَلِكَ
لِأنَّ قَبْلَ المَسِيحِ ما كانَ المَعْبُودُ إلّا اللَّهَ، فَوَجَبَ أنْ يَبْقى
الأمْرُ بَعْدَ ظُهُورِ المَسِيحِ عَلى هَذا الوَجْهِ` — because before the
Messiah the object of worship was none but God, so the matter had to remain so
after the Messiah appeared. This is the one place the word `معبود` occurs in
either section, and it is doing the opposite of al-Tabari's work: it states what
the object of worship was before Christ in order to argue that the Christians
have since added to it, not to identify the God of the two parties.

He ends on the turning-away clause: if they persist, say we are submitted to
God, `يَعْنِي أظْهِرُوا أنَّكم عَلى هَذا الدِّينِ، ولا تَكُونُوا في قَيْدِ أنْ
تَحْمِلُوا غَيْرَكم عَلَيْهِ` — that is, show that you are on this religion, and
do not be under obligation to carry anyone else onto it.

**Two limits on what this note may be used for.** First, al-Razi's account of
what Christians assert — in particular that the hypostasis of the Holy Spirit
clothed itself in the humanity of Mary — is his description of the doctrine, not
a Christian statement of it, and this base holds a separate note on how the
doctrine is stated from inside. Whether his premise matches its target is a
further question and is not settled here. Second, the closing sentence about
being distinguished from the idolaters by every merit is carried by two of the
three copies of the 29:46 comment (tafsir.app and islamweb.net); the third is
truncated before it. That still leaves two independent witnesses, which is this
repository's standard, but it is not three.
