Own-words summary of [[augustine-enchiridion-handbook-on-faith-hope-and-love--202610031808]].

Chapter 41 gives two separate reasons why Christ had no sin, one for original
sin and one for personal sin. Christ was "Begotten and conceived, then,
without any indulgence of carnal lust, and therefore bringing with Him no
original sin". He was united with the Word as "a son by nature, not by grace,
and therefore having no sin of His own". The first reason turns on the manner
of the conception: for Augustine, original sin passes with the lust that
attends ordinary generation, and a conception without it passes none on.

Augustine then reads Romans 8:3 and 2 Corinthians 5:21 together. Because of
"the likeness of sinful flesh in which He came, He was called sin, that He
might be sacrificed to wash away sin". The likeness is what qualifies him to
be the sacrifice; it is not a share in the sin.
