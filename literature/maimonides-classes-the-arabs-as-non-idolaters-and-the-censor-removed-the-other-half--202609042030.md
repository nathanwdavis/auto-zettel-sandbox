Own-words summary of [[maimonides-forbidden-foods-11-7--202609042020]].

The halachah sorts gentiles by a single question — do they serve false deities?
— and the sorting has legal consequences for their wine. Maimonides then gives
his example of the category that does not: "With regard to any gentile who does
not serve false deities, e.g., the Arabs: It is forbidden to drink his wine, but
it is permitted to benefit from it. The Geonim rule in this manner."

Two things to notice about that clause. It is *incidental*: Maimonides is not
arguing about Islam, he is stating a rule about wine, and the classification of
the Arabs comes in as an uncontroversial illustration. And it is *legal*: the
category is doing work in a live prohibition, not in a comparative essay.

The next clause is where the printed tradition breaks. The standard text
continues with an anonymous "With regard to those who worship false deities,"
and the translator's note 24 says why: "Our translation follows the standard
version of the Mishneh Torah. The uncensored text reads: 'Christians, by
contrast, are idolaters. It is forbidden to benefit....'" The same note carries
the later softening: "The Rama (Orach Chayim 155:1) rules that Christianity
violates only the prohibition against shituf."

So the sentence as Maimonides wrote it made a *contrast*, with Islam on one side
of the idolatry line and Christianity on the other, and the printed editions
preserve only the half that names Islam. A reader of the standard text sees
Maimonides being generous to Muslims and cannot see whom he is being generous by
comparison with.

Touger's note 23 also records that the commentators found the wording awkward:
the halachah opens by requiring a gentile to accept all seven Noahide laws, and
this clause seems to require only the rejection of idolatry. The Kessef Mishneh
resolves it by making the test national rather than individual — where the whole
nation does not worship false deities, no further acceptance is needed.
