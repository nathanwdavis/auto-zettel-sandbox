**The question, and who is asking it.** Laing, the Molinist whose IEP survey is on file, asks "whether or not a compatibilist view of freedom (as opposed to a libertarian view of freedom) can be reconciled with the doctrine of middle knowledge", because "More and more Calvinist thinkers are attempting to incorporate middle knowledge into their systems of thought." Tiessen's model is taken as representative. Tiessen (*Providence and Prayer*, 290) is quoted as "somewhat reluctant to use the term" because "it is usually associated with a commitment to libertarian freedom", yet using it "even though I do not believe human freedom to be libertarian." "In a similar fashion, compatibilists John Feinberg and Bruce Ware have also claimed that God possesses middle knowledge." Note 11 is the article's only Ware material: Feinberg "is skeptical about the value of such knowledge", whereas "Ware, like Tiessen, seems to believe it to be quite promising"; Ware "claimed to hold to a Calvinistic Middle Knowledge position at the EPS session (2000 annual meeting, Nashville, TN)" and "has confirmed this in personal conversation."

**The compatibilists accept the grounding objection.** After the uncaptured paragraphs in which Tiessen, Crabtree and Feinberg call libertarian choice arbitrary (Laing replies that most libertarians claim only that the agent could have chosen otherwise, not that the alternatives were equally likely), Laing records that this view "has led many compatibilists who are intrigued by middle knowledge to accept the grounding objection", that "Ware has also noted that he finds the grounding objection to be convincing", and that Tiessen holds Basinger's limits on the Molinist God "do not apply to my own compatibilist model". So the question: "What can ground the truth of counterfactuals of compatibilist creaturely freedom?"

**The dilemma worked on one counterfactual.** Take "(1) If John were offered an extra night’s stay for free, he would (freely) decline." The middle-knowledge proponent "must claim that (1) was true prior to the creation of the world." A moderate Calvinist can say so: "It may be the case that God determined (1) to be true from all eternity." But "the proponent of middle knowledge must make the further claim that the truth of (1) is in no way dependent upon the will of God", and most Calvinists want God able to bring about his will through free acts even when the creature does not want to perform them; Feinberg is quoted to the effect that the decree "includes not only God’s chosen ends but also the means to such ends". Hence Feinberg's "use of the phrase, middle knowledge, only refers to a divine knowledge of counterfactuals which is not prevolitional. But this is not middle knowledge at all! Rather, it is merely a part of God’s free knowledge! Interestingly, Molina’s chief opponent, Domingo Bañez, also held to a belief that God knows all true counterfactuals of freedom (which are true in virtue of an act of his will)."

**Where the two views really differ.** "Many compatibilists claim that (1) is true because God created my will in such a way that I would freely choose to decline instead of accept", the choice being free because self-determinative "in accordance with how my will was created to function"; the libertarian says "God created the human will in such a way that I can freely choose to either accept or decline." So "the fundamental difference between these views of libertarianism and compatibilism lies in the implications drawn from the belief that God creates the individual with a free will." A compatibilism of that strong kind "requires divine knowledge of counterfactuals to be part of God’s free knowledge"; a weaker compatibilism, on which freedom means only that sufficient reasons can be given, runs into the grounding objection: the counterfactual "was true prior to God’s decision to actualize a world where I am faced with such a decision, prior to his decision to actualize a world where I exist, and prior to his decision to actualize a world at all!" Grounding it in the creature as an idea in God's mind leaves as the only sufficient reason "simple self-determination", and Laing asks whether this view is "any different from a libertarian conception of freedom", and whether it can properly "be called a Calvinistic approach? It does not seem to be."

**Nor does it escape Basinger's limits.** If Adam would compatibilistically eat, then "According to Tiessen, God can actualize a world where (6) is true and (5) is false. But how can he, apart from Adam’s choice being in some way dependent upon the will of God? If it is dependent upon the will of God, then the truths of (5) and (6) are not part of God’s middle knowledge. If it is not dependent upon the will of God, then it seems that God is just as constrained in terms of the kind of world he can actualize in the Calvinist Molinist model as he is in the traditional (libertarian) Molinist model". The compatibilist escape is to hold "that any possible free action can be elicited from any given creature, if the proper circumstances and influences obtain", which Laing finds hard to believe of every mother and Lamentations 2:20.

**The conclusion.** The Calvinist middle-knowledge proponent "seems to be caught between the horns of a dilemma": if the counterfactual's truth "is grounded in the will of God or in the way God created the creaturely will, then she has denied the prevolitional character of divine knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom and therefore, her position is not in the middle of anything"; if it is grounded in the creature's character as pre-existing in God's mind, or needs no grounding, "then her view of freedom is virtually indistinguishable from libertarian freedom." Either way she loses the free-will defence. "The soft determinist may claim that God possesses knowledge of counterfactuals of compatibilist freedom, but she cannot claim that such knowledge is prevolitional; it must be part of God’s free knowledge."

**Gaps.** The article predates *God's Greater Glory* by two months and never cites a text of Ware's; the Basinger passages on the limits of a Molinist God (section II) are summarised, not captured; Laing's own grounding proposal (his dissertation on supercomprehension) is named in a footnote and unread. Laing puts the compatibilist's counterfactual in free knowledge; Helm's 2009 post puts it in natural knowledge ([[helm-shunning-middle-knowledge--202609111810]]), and Van Horn says it can be either ([[van-horn-on-incorporating-middle-knowledge-into-calvinism--202609111812]]).
