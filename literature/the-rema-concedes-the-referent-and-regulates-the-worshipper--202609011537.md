Own-words summary of [[shulchan-arukh-orach-chayim-with-the-gloss-of-rema--202609011532]].

Note first what kind of text this is, because it is routinely quoted as though
it were a treatise on comparative theology. Orach Chayim 156 is a paragraph of
practical advice about earning a living -- work, but do not let work displace
Torah; trade honestly; avoid oaths. Karo's ruling is a commercial one: a Jew
should be wary of "partnering with the gentiles, for perhaps [the gentile] will
be obligated to take an oath" and the Jew will then have caused the name of
another god to be "heard on your lips."

The Rema's gloss loosens that ruling, and the theology arrives as the reason
for a business permission. "Some are lenient with partnering with gentiles
these days because the gentiles nowadays do not take an oath by idols. And
even though they mention their foreign god, nevertheless **their intention is
to the Creator of heaven and earth**, except that they associate the name of
Heaven with other forces." The permission is completed by a rule about who is
bound: there is no violation of "before a blind person you shall not place a
stumbling block," "because the gentiles are not warned regarding partnership."
The gloss cites the Ran on Avodah Zarah ch. 1, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Tosafot
on Bekhorot ch. 1.

Three things are worth holding apart in that sentence. The **intention** of the
gentile worshipper is granted to terminate on the Creator of heaven and earth
-- the referent is conceded, not contested. The **association** ("the name of
Heaven with other forces") is described as an addition to that intention, not a
substitution for it. And the **verdict** is indexed to the worshipper: the same
association is a permitted addition for one class of person and forbidden for
another, which is why the gloss decides a question about Jewish liability
rather than about gentile salvation.

The gloss says "their foreign god" without naming a religion; the ruling has
been read since as being about Christians. It names the Ran on Avodah Zarah
ch. 1, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Tosafot on Bekhorot ch. 1 as its authorities,
but only as citation strings -- none of the three is in this repository, so
what they argue is not reported here.
