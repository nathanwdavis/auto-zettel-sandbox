Own-words summary of [[vallicella-divine-simplicity-sep--202609042072]].

Three claims in this passage bear on what the base was asking.

**The doctrine is not one tradition's.** "DDS is represented not only in
classical Christian theology, but also in Jewish, Greek, and Islamic thought."
The entry's illustration is Maimonides, quoted from *Guide* I:57 in the same
words this repository now holds in its own capture of that chapter.

**Its content is a claim about the manner of possession.** "God is not only
radically non-anthropomorphic, but radically unlike creatures in general, not
only in respect of the properties he possesses, but also in his manner of
possessing them... God has his properties by being them."

**Its place in the Summa is structural, and the entry says so**: "The centrality
of DDS to medieval philosophical theology is shown by its position in the order
of topics in Aquinas' Summa Theologica. It occurs as Question 3 right after
Question 2 on the existence of God." This base already holds question 2; the
neighbour it never read is the one that states what kind of thing has just been
shown to exist.

**And the Trinity is reported as a motive for the doctrine, not an obstacle to
it.** "the triune nature of the God of classical Christian theism may also be
taken as motivating DDS within Christian theology, and this despite the initial
suspicion that Trinity is incompatible with divine simplicity." The entry cites
Wedgeworth (2019) for the claim that "Athanasius of Alexandria consistently used
the doctrine of divine simplicity to argue that the Son's eternal Generation
from the Father proved his absolute deity," and then declines to pursue it.

That last is a lead recorded at the strength it has: a survey's report of one
secondary author's claim about Athanasius, not a reading of Athanasius. The
entry itself says pursuing it "lies beyond the scope."
