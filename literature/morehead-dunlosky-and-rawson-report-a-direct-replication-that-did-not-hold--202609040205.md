Own-words summary of
[[morehead-dunlosky-and-rawson-how-much-mightier-is-the-pen--202609040156]].
**This note records the abstract's stated design and stated conclusion only**,
and the abstract is the whole of what could be obtained. Nothing here reports
sample sizes, materials, statistics or how the authors reasoned from their
results — none of that was read. The abstract is unusually explicit about its
own outcomes, which is why an abstract-only note can still carry a real
finding; it is also why every characterisation below stays close to its
wording.

The design is stated as a **direct** replication of
[[mueller-and-oppenheimer-attribute-the-longhand-advantage-to-not-transcribing--202609040200]],
extended in two ways: a group taking notes on eWriters, and — this is the
consequential one — "a group who did not take notes."

The result is reported in a form that resists a one-line summary in either
direction, and the abstract's own hedging is worth preserving. "Some trends
suggested longhand superiority; however, performance did not consistently
differ between any groups (experiments 1 and 2), including a group who did not
take notes (experiment 2)." So the direction of the original finding was not
absent from the data; what was absent was a difference that held up. And the
abstract puts the group who took no notes inside the same statement: it too is
among the groups performance did not consistently differ between.

Two further results are stated. Group differences "were further decreased after
students studied their notes," which is the condition closest to how anyone
actually uses notes. And a meta-analysis combining direct replications "revealed
small (nonsignificant) effects favoring longhand" — small, in the direction
Mueller and Oppenheimer reported, and not statistically significant.

The authors' own conclusion is the sentence this repository should cite, because
it is more careful than either camp's version of it: "Based on the present
outcomes and other available evidence, concluding which method is superior for
improving the functions of note-taking seems premature." That is not a finding
that longhand does not help. It is a finding that the question is open — which
is a different and, for a base that wants to cite this literature, more
demanding thing.

This note is linked `contradicts` to the Mueller and Oppenheimer literature
note. The relation is exact rather than polemical: what the two disagree about
is whether the reported difference is there to be found, and a direct
replication that does not recover it contradicts the original's finding without
impugning the original's argument. Whether the *mechanism* Mueller and
Oppenheimer proposed — transcription in place of reframing — is also
undermined is a further question, and the abstract does not settle it: an
effect can fail to replicate while the process it was offered as evidence for
remains real and shows up elsewhere. This repository has not read anything that
tests that, and
[[a-failed-replication-leaves-the-mechanism-standing-and-the-effect-unproven--202609040210]]
is where the distinction is worked out.
