Own-words summary of [[westminster-larger-catechism--202610031812]].

Three answers in the Larger Catechism bear on the question. Q. 22 says that
"all mankind descending from him by ordinary generation, sinned in him, and
fell with him in that first transgression." Q. 26 says that "Original sin is
conveyed from our first parents unto their posterity by natural generation,
so as all that proceed from them in that way are conceived and born in sin."
Q. 37 then describes the incarnation: Christ became man "by taking to himself
a true body, and a reasonable soul, being conceived by the power of the Holy
Ghost in the womb of the virgin Mary, of her substance, and born of her, yet
without sin."

The catechism does not argue the point; it frames it. Both the imputation of
Adam's sin and the conveyance of corruption are stated for those who descend
"by ordinary generation" and "proceed from them in that way". Christ's
conception is described in other terms, by the Holy Ghost and of Mary's
substance, so the rule is worded so as not to reach him. The answers do not
say which of the two named factors does the work.
