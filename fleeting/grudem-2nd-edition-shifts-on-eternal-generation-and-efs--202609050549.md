# Grudem's 2nd edition (2020) doctrine changes on Trinity

**Finding (from secondary sources, not direct text access):**

Between 1994 (1st ed.) and 2020 (2nd ed.), Grudem shifted his position on eternal generation:
- 1st edition: denied eternal generation of the Son
- 2nd edition: affirms eternal generation of the Son

The 2nd edition continues to defend eternal functional subordination (EFS) — the idea that the Son is eternally subject to the Father's authority while remaining equal in deity and honor (pages 305, 307 per the spoiledmilks.com review; single-witness report, does not meet two-witness standard for citation without direct text verification).

**Significance for internal-distinction thread:**
This shift toward affirming eternal generation may reflect engagement with broader evangelical and Reformed trinitarian discussions. EFS remains contested in evangelical theology (by the mid-2020s, some prominent evangelicals had rejected EFS as incompatible with genuine equality). Worth tracking whether this bears on how Grudem grounds the real distinction between persons in the Godhead.
