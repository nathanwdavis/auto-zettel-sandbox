Bibliographic record. Richard Price's 1772 pamphlet on the national debt,
second edition (London: T. Cadell) — the primary source of the famous
compound-versus-simple-interest illustration ("one penny, put out at our
Saviour's birth", pp. 18–19), and the first primary text behind this base's
"compound growth" topic, which until this note rested on Housel's 2020
secondary presentation and unsourced fixture notes.

The raw capture is the Internet Archive OCR plain text of the John Carter
Brown Library copy of the second edition (public domain; no access
restriction). The OCR renders the period's long s as "f" and breaks
hyphenated words; quotations in notes built on this reference normalize
those artifacts only after cross-checking the quoted wording word-for-word
against an independent Internet Archive scan of the 1772 first edition
(item price-an-appeal-to-the-public), and each quoting note records that
policy. One adjacent sentence ("Our government has chosen hitherto…")
differs in word order between the two editions and is therefore excluded
from cross-verified quotation. The work carries no ISBN or DOI (1772), so
verification rests on the raw capture; the edition, printer, place, and
year are transcribed from the captured title page itself.
