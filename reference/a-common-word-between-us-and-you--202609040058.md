Bibliographic record for the 2007 open letter from Muslim scholars to the
leaders of the Christian churches. This is the source the base was missing:
until now the same-God cluster — 26 permanent notes, the largest thing this
repository has built — argued the Islamic side almost entirely in Christian and
secular voices, with the Qur'an capture its only Islamic text and scripture
rather than anyone's argument (inquiry 202609032122).

Tiered `primary-text`, not `reputable-secondary`. The letter is not a report
*about* what Muslims think; it is a normative theological statement issued by
the scholars themselves, addressed to named recipients, and published by the
institute that convened it. That is the same footing on which this base holds
Nostra Aetate — a conciliar declaration, captured as what a tradition said in
its own voice.

**Provenance, and one distinction the capture enforces.** The capture holds two
texts and marks them apart. Part A is the letter as published on the
initiative's document page, complete: the Summary and Abridgement, the three
sections (I Love of God, II Love of the Neighbour, III Come to a Common Word
Between Us and You), and the full NOTES apparatus. Part B is the site's own
editorial introduction page, fetched at the same time. Part B is where the date
(13 October 2007), the signatory count (138), the circumstances (one year after
the 2006 Open Letter to the Pope, itself a response to the Regensburg address),
and the presentation venue (the September 2007 "Love in the Quran" conference
under the patronage of King Abdullah II) come from. Claims about what the
letter *says* must rest on Part A; claims about who signed it and when rest on
Part B. The two are not interchangeable and the capture does not let them blur:
Part B calls the letter a statement that Islam and Christianity "share the same
Divine Origin", and that phrasing is the initiative describing its own letter,
not a sentence the letter contains.

Access. `https://www.acommonword.com/the-acw-document/` returned HTTP 200

Limits worth recording. The letter is a consensus document, and consensus
documents are shaped by what a large and internally various group could all
sign — it is evidence of a mainstream position offered to Christians, not of
the range of Islamic opinion, and it is not a hostile witness. Ibn Taymiyya's
al-Jawab al-Sahih and al-Ghazali's Faysal al-Tafriqa, both named as candidates
by inquiry 202609032122, remain uncaptured and would supply the argumentative
and the classical registers this letter does not.
