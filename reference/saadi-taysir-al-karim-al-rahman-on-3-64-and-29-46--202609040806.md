Bibliographic record for al-Sa'di's commentary on Q 3:64 and Q 29:46. Arabic,
verbatim **excerpts** at
`raw/202609040806-saadi-taysir-al-karim-al-rahman-3-64-and-29-46-excerpts.txt`.

**Why a modern commentator is in a classical test.** The INBOX entry asked
whether al-Tabari's `maʿbud` gloss is traditional or idiosyncratic, and the
three classical commentators answered by not glossing the clause at all. That
answer leaves a second question standing: does anyone in the tradition gloss it,
and if so how? al-Sa'di (d. 1376/1956) does, which is why he is here. He is
also the most-reprinted modern Salafi commentary in Arabic, so what he does
with the clause is not a marginal reading.

**Access.** Rung (a), first request: Quran.com API v4, tafsir resource 91
(`slug: ar-tafseer-al-saddi`).

**Excerpt capture, and why.** This work is modern and in copyright. The
2026-09-04 INBOX entry about all-rights-reserved full texts sitting in a public
repository is awaiting a human decision, so the capture holds only the
lemma-and-gloss sequence the notes use, with its immediate context, and marks
its omissions `[...]`. What is omitted from the 29:46 comment is al-Sa'di's
argument that the People of the Book are thereby bound to accept the Qur'an; no
note in this base quotes it. Under the house triage rule this is a *full-text
read, excerpt-captured* source, not an abstract-limited one: the whole comment
was read before the excerpt was cut.

**Independent cross-check.** The quoted passages were normalised and matched
against `quran.ksu.edu.sa`, which serves this commentary independently of
Quran.com. Both quoted strings matched.

**A complication this record states rather than leaves to the notes.** al-Sa'di
is also the one commentator captured this cycle who calls the "common word" of
Q 3:64 `مشتركة بيننا وبينكم` — shared between us and you — which reads at first
against the base's note that the classical reading is a summons rather than
shared ground. Read in place, his sentence says the word is one *all the
prophets agreed on* and that it is not the property of one party, and he
immediately fills its content with the exclusion of every rival object of
worship and ends with `فاشهدوهم أنكم مسلمون`. So it qualifies the note rather
than refuting it, and the qualification belongs in the open: a modern
commentator does use the vocabulary of sharing about this verse, and the
classical three do not.
