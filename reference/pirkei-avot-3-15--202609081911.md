One mishnah, and the classical rabbinic statement of the problem this base has
been circling in Christian dress: *ha-kol tzafui, ve-ha-reshut netunah* —
"Everything is foreseen yet freedom of choice is granted." Captured with both
the Hebrew (Torat Emet, public domain) and an English translation (Kulp, CC-BY)
for inquiry 202609081908.

`scripture: false` deliberately, although Avot is part of the Mishnah: the house
rule reserves `scripture: true` for the biblical text cited by book-chapter-verse
per SBL, and a mishnaic tractate is cited as a work with an edition and
translator, as the Mishneh Torah references here are.

It is worth recording what this text does and does not do, since it is short
enough to be over-read. It asserts both halves and offers no reconciliation;
*tzafui* ("foreseen", from *tzafah*, to look out or watch) is not obviously the
same claim as exhaustive foreknowledge of future contingents, and the saying's
next clauses move to judgement and works rather than to metaphysics. Whether
Akiva means the pair to be a paradox held in tension, or simply two truths whose
joint consistency he does not raise, is not settled by the line, and this base
has captured no commentary on it — not Maimonides' own commentary on the
Mishnah, nor Bartenura, nor the standard modern treatments.
