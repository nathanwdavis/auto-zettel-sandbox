Bibliographic record. Plantinga's notes for a 1986 Bellingham summer-seminar
lecture, circulated informally for two decades and first published -- "while
shortened a bit ... unrevised", with a new "Preface to the Appendix (July
2006)" -- as the appendix to Deane-Peter Baker's edited volume *Alvin
Plantinga* (Cambridge University Press, 2007), pp. 203-227; `original-date`
1986 marks the lecture. Tiered `primary-text`, not `peer-reviewed`: although
the host volume is a scholarly CUP book, the appendix is by Plantinga's own
description unrevised lecture notes -- "argument sketches, or maybe only
pointers to good arguments" -- cited in this repository as the primary
statement of his own assessment of the theistic arguments, in his own words.
The capture is bounded excerpts per the excerpt policy for in-copyright
works: the preface's two opening paragraphs, the 1986 headnote complete, and
the two teleological sections -- (E) The Argument from Physical Constants
and (F) The Naive Teleological Argument -- complete, with the bare heading
of (G). All other sections are cited only as attributed summary and never
quoted. Access provenance, recorded honestly: the PDF is served by "Papers
by Alvin Plantinga" (andrewmbailey.com/ap/), the long-standing paper archive
maintained by the philosopher Andrew M. Bailey; the archive page does not
state a hosting permission, a residual recorded in the capture header rather
than hidden. The book itself is in print; a purchased or library copy
remains the canonical access path.
