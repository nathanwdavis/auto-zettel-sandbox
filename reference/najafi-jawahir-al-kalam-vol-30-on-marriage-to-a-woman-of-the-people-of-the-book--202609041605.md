Bibliographic record for the section of *Jawahir al-kalam* on unbelief as a bar
to marriage — *kitab al-nikah*, the *nazar* on *al-kufr*, first *maqsad*, on
marrying a woman of the People of the Book. Arabic, verbatim, at
`raw/202609041605-najafi-jawahir-al-kalam-30-nikah-al-kitabiyya-arabic.txt`.

**Two texts in one capture.** *Jawahir al-kalam* is a commentary on
*Shara'i' al-Islam*, so al-Muhaqqiq al-Hilli's *matn* (d. 676/1277) is quoted
inside al-Najafi's commentary (d. 1266/1850) and is marked in the print by
half-brackets, which the OCR preserves as `#`, `€` and similar glyphs. Where the
literature note quotes the *matn* it says so, because the two authors are six
centuries apart and disagree.

**Why this source, and why *fiqh* specifically.** Every Shi'i witness this base
held on the *mushrikun* question was a *tafsir*, and the strongest of them said
in as many words that the operative rule belongs elsewhere: al-Tabataba'i twice
defers it with `وللكلام تتمة تطلب من الفقه`, the discussion has a remainder to be
sought in *fiqh*. The INBOX entry open since the 13:00Z cycle asked for exactly
that and, at 15:00Z, was narrowed until *fiqh* was the only thing it still
wanted. This is a work of that *fiqh*.

**What the base may say about this work's standing, and what it may not.** That
*Jawahir al-kalam* is the most comprehensive work of Imami jurisprudence is a
widely held view for which this repository has **no source**, exactly as with
*al-Mizan* one cycle earlier, and the same discipline applies: nothing in the
notes rests on it. What the notes rest on and what the capture shows are the
author, the date, the school, and the fact that this is a commentary on the
*Shara'i'* — all of which are on the scanned front matter. Where a note needs a
description it says "a nineteenth-century Imami work of *fiqh* commenting on the
*Shara'i'*", every part of which is sourced. Any sense that the work is *major*
is this base's characterisation and should not be read as a sourced claim.

**Access.** archive.org, item `BBib-Alex-09185` (a Bibliotheca Alexandrina scan),
OCR text downloaded complete in one request. The Shi'i-library hosts do not
resolve from this environment; the failure modes are recorded in the capture
header and in
[[ibn-miftah-sharh-al-azhar-vol-4-on-the-unbelieving-wife--202609041600]].
Volume 29 was probed first and is the wrong volume — it covers fosterage and
marriage to slave women — which is worth recording because volume numbering
across editions of this work is not stable and the next run should not assume it.

**Exhaustion.** Not an inference. The whole volume was downloaded as a single
file whose sha256 and length are in the capture header; the excerpt is a byte
range inside it. The range was chosen by content: it opens at the section heading
for *al-kufr* and closes on al-Najafi's own closing of the question and the
transition to the Magians. The volume continues past the excerpt and the excerpt
says so.

**The OCR is imperfect and the notes say so at every quotation.** Damage of the
same classes as the Zaydi capture — `تكاح` for `نكاح`, `المشع` for `المنع`, `»`
and `ء` for the printed comma, words broken across a line with an inserted
space. Every quotation is reproduced exactly as the OCR has it, was checked
mechanically against this capture, and carries its corrected reading in square
brackets where the damage would mislead. The controls were run in the same pass;
one of them is load-bearing for a claim the notes deliberately do **not** make:
`الزيدية` occurs nowhere in this capture, so nothing here is evidence about the
Zaydis, and the Zaydi claims are grounded only in the Zaydi capture.

**Rights and extent.** A nineteenth-century work, long out of copyright, in a
1981 Beirut reprint. One section is taken — 24,750 characters — rather than the
volume, for the reason given in the companion reference note: the base has an
open question about full texts of modern editions sitting in a public repository,
and an excerpt sufficient to check the quotations is the smallest thing that does
the job.

**Locators.** Printed page numbers do not survive the OCR usably; the literature
note locates by work, volume and section. Al-Najafi's own internal references
(to *al-Wasa'il*, *al-Bihar*, *al-Tahdhib*) do survive and are quoted where they
identify a report, which gives a reader with a physical copy a way in that a page
number this base cannot see would not.
