Bibliographic record. John Meyendorff's article in Theological Studies 50,
no. 3 (September 1989), 481-499, written at St Vladimir's Orthodox Seminary,
Crestwood, N.Y.; metadata from Crossref (DOI 10.1177/004056398905000304).
It is the fullest statement on file, in Meyendorff's own words, of the
Orthodox account this base needs for the question whether Christ assumed a
fallen nature without inherited guilt: Eastern patristic thought "ignores
the notion of a transmission" of guilt from Adam, what passes from parents
to children is mortality, and "the mortal, corruptible, and fallen humanity"
is what the Logos assumed. Footnote 45 summarises his reading of Romans 5:12
and cites Byzantine Theology pp. 143-46 (reference
byzantine-theology-historical-trends-and-doctrinal-themes--202610081431).

The article is in copyright and is served openly by the journal on its own
website, so the capture is bounded excerpts per the repository's excerpt

block), 490 (Cyril of Alexandria), 492-495 (the end of "Christ, the Savior"
and the section "The 'New Adam'" through "the Emmanuel"), with footnotes
41-49. NOT captured: the history of modern Orthodox theology (pp. 481-489),
the treatment of deification and hypostasis on pp. 490-492 outside the
