Bibliographic record. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on
middle knowledge, by John D. Laing (Southwestern Baptist Theological
Seminary). The IEP prints no date on the article, so the record carries the
access date and no `issued` date, which is how the IEP itself asks to be
cited. The IEP is an editorially refereed reference work; recorded as
reputable-secondary because the entry is a survey of the doctrine and the
objections to it, not a primary statement of Molina's position. Its author is
a Baptist theologian writing in an evangelical seminary, which is worth
knowing when the entry is read alongside the Catholic Encyclopedia's account
of the same doctrine.

The entry is in copyright (the IEP and the author), and the IEP's terms permit
attributed quotation but forbid reposting copies, so the capture is bounded
excerpts per this repository's excerpt policy, following the precedent set by

and `laporte-rigid-designators-sep--202609041950`: seven labelled blocks
covering the definition and placement of middle knowledge, the assumptions,
the three kinds of knowledge, weak actualization, the objection from
providence with the libertarian reply, the argument from divine risk, and the
grounding objection with its five replies, together 46 percent of the text.
