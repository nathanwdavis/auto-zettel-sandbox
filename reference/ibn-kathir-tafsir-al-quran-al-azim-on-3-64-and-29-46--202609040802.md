Bibliographic record for Ibn Kathir's commentary on Q 3:64 and Q 29:46,
captured in full in Arabic at
`raw/202609040802-ibn-kathir-tafsir-al-quran-al-azim-3-64-and-29-46-arabic.txt`.

**Why this source.** The second name in the INBOX entry this cycle worked. Ibn
Kathir (d. 774/1373) writes the standard *tafsir bi-l-ma'thur* — commentary by
transmitted report — and his relation to al-Tabari is direct: he cites him by
name (`وهذا القول اختاره ابن جرير`) inside the very comment captured here. A
commentator who quotes al-Tabari's judgement on one clause of a verse and does
not repeat his gloss on another clause of the same verse is the sharpest
available test of whether that gloss travelled.

**Access.** Rung (a), first request: Quran.com API v4, tafsir resource 14
(`slug: ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir`).

**Public domain.** Author died 774/1373.

**Completeness, checked rather than assumed.** As with the al-Qurtubi record,
this note supports a claim about an absence. The comment served by Quran.com on
29:46 was compared with the independently served copies at `quran.ksu.edu.sa`
and `tafsir.app`: same opening (Qatada on abrogation by the sword verse), same
closing (Ibn Kathir's own remark that Kaʿb narrated from scrolls he thought
well of). The string `معبود` appears in none of the three copies.

**Independent cross-check, done mechanically.** Every quoted Arabic passage was
normalised and substring-matched against hosts independent of Quran.com. The
29:46 passages matched on **both** `quran.ksu.edu.sa` and `tafsir.app`; the
3:64 passages matched on `quran.ksu.edu.sa`.

**Edition.** The API names no printed edition; no page of a printed edition is
claimed and locators are by *sura:aya*. `issued` is the author's death year.

**Translation.** No published English translation was used, and there are
several in print for this work — a run with access to one should use it to
calibrate the renderings here rather than assume them. English in the
literature and permanent notes is this repository's own working translation,
printed beside the Arabic where it carries weight.
