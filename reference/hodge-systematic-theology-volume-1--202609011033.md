Bibliographic record. The classic Princeton systematic theology, public
domain, via CCEL's plain-text etext. `primary-text` because it is cited here
as a primary statement of the Reformed doctrine of the knowability of God,
not as secondary scholarship about someone else's. Three excerpts captured
verbatim: Part I ch. I §§1-3 (knowledge of God innate, not due to a process
of reasoning, not due exclusively to tradition), Part I ch. II §3 opening
(the teleological argument, its syllogistic statement), and Part I ch. IV §1
complete ("God can be known", including "Our Knowledge of God Partial").
That the knowability doctrine and the teleological argument sit thirty pages
apart in the same volume is itself a datum this repository uses.
