Bibliographic record. The classic Princeton systematic theology, public
domain, via CCEL's plain-text etext. `primary-text` because it is cited here
as a primary statement of the Reformed doctrine of the knowability of God,
not as secondary scholarship about someone else's. Three excerpts captured
verbatim: Part I ch. I §§1-3 (knowledge of God innate, not due to a process
of reasoning, not due exclusively to tradition), Part I ch. II §3 opening
(the teleological argument, its syllogistic statement), and Part I ch. IV §1
complete ("God can be known", including "Our Knowledge of God Partial").
**Second capture, added 2026-09-04.** `excerpt_captures` now lists
raw/202609041705-hodge-st1-trinity-nicene-excerpts.txt, four further excerpts
from Part I ch. VI, "The Trinity": § 3's accounts of the Sabellian theory and
of Arianism, § 4 A (the objects of the Council of Nice, with the creed of 325
and its anathemas), and § 5 entire (what the Councils decided against
Sabellianism, against the Arians, and about the mutual relation of the
persons). The first capture contains nothing from that chapter, so no claim
about what the Church doctrine of the Trinity asserts or denies could rest on
it. Cited for that chapter, Hodge is still `primary-text` in the sense this
note already uses -- a Reformed dogmatician stating the doctrine he holds --
but with one difference the notes observe: in ch. VI he is also *reporting*
conciliar history, and where a note here turns on what a council decided, the
note says that it has Hodge's report of it and not the acts themselves.

That the knowability doctrine and the teleological argument sit two chapters
apart in the same volume is itself a datum this repository uses.
