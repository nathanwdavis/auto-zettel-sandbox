Bibliographic record. The Social Sciences Replication Project (SSRP): 21
experimental social-science papers published in *Nature* and *Science* between
2010 and 2015, each replicated once to a pre-registered protocol with a large
sample, alongside prediction markets and surveys that asked researchers to
forecast which would replicate.

**Read in full; committed as an excerpt.** This repository has cited the SSRP
at second hand since 2026-09-04 — through the Google-effect replication that
ran alongside it — without any note here claiming to have read it. This record
closes that gap.

The article was reached at rung (b) of the access ladder on the first request:
Unpaywall for DOI `10.1038/s41562-018-0399-z` returns a copy in Erasmus
University Rotterdam's institutional repository (`pure.eur.nl`), a 10-page PDF
carrying the complete main text, figures and Methods. The publisher's own page
at `nature.com` was not needed and was not tried.

Two things about that copy matter enough to record here. First, **Unpaywall
mislabels it**: the API calls the location `acceptedVersion`, and the file's
own EUR cover sheet says "Document Version: Publisher's PDF, also known as
Version of record", made available under Article 25fa of the Dutch Copyright
Act. Second, and consequently, its stated terms of use permit downloading "for
your personal use only" and sharing the portal link, and forbid making the
material available to third parties. This repository is public, so the whole
file is not committed. What is in `raw/` is an excerpt capture of the passages
the notes use, with the source URL and the SHA-256 of the exact PDF read
(`23f01465…40a34e`), following the excerpt rule the Rowland cycle proposed and
the 08:00Z cycle applied. This is the first source in this base whose host
states those terms explicitly rather than by an inferred publisher notice.

What is *not* in the PDF at all is the Supplementary Information — the
Supplementary Methods, Tables 1–7 and Figs. 1–9 where every per-study sample
size, P value, effect size and Bayes factor lives. Figures 1–3 plot those
values without printing them. So this source grounds statements about the
project, its aggregate results and the enumerated membership of its replication
lists, and it does not ground any individual study's replication statistics
beyond the one worked example the main text prints.
