The chapters of the Mishneh Torah where Maimonides sets out free will
(*bechirah chofshit*), meets the objection from divine foreknowledge, and
explains the hardening of Pharaoh's heart. Captured for inquiry 202609081908,
because this repository held Maimonides on the divine attributes and on the
status of Christianity and Islam but nothing at all from any Jewish source on
free will — the doctrine the inquiry turns on.

Fetched from the Sefaria API in the Touger translation (CC-BY-NC), the same
edition and route this base used for Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah
([[maimonides-mishneh-torah-yesodei-hatorah--202608311922]]) and the Hilchot
Melachim passages. Sefaria is a republication, not the editio princeps; the
Hebrew of these halachot was not separately captured here, so anything turning
on Maimonides' precise Hebrew — *reshut* for the granted choice, for instance —
should be checked against the Hebrew before it is asserted.

`primary-text` because Maimonides is cited here as a primary statement of the
doctrine he is setting out, on the same footing as Grudem's *Systematic
Theology* in the Christian material.

Two limits worth carrying into any note that cites this. First, Hilchot Teshuvah
5:5 refers its own resolution back to *Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah* chapter 2 on the
identity of God and his knowledge; that chapter is on file here only at 1:7, so
the crucial premise is present in this base by a neighbouring halacha rather
than by the one Maimonides points to. Second, the *Guide for the Perplexed* III
treats providence and God's knowledge at much greater length and sometimes in a
different register from the *Mishneh Torah*; only *Guide* I:57 is on file
([[maimonides-guide-1-57--202609042071]]), so nothing here should be read as
Maimonides' whole position.
