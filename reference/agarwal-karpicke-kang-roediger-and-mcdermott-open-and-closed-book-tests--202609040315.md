**Full text.**

Bibliographic record, and why this source matters more here than its citation
count would suggest. Inquiry 202609032123 asked whether anything measures the
cost of externalising memory, and permanent note
[[the-note-became-an-external-memory-only-when-memorizing-came-to-be-seen-as-a-cost--202609032115]]
records a three-century argument about it conducted entirely without
measurement. This is the closest thing to a measurement that this base has
found. Its independent variable is *whether the learner has the written
material in front of them*, and its authors define the open-book condition, on
p. 861, as one "in which students view notes and textbooks while taking the
test" — notes, named by the authors, not stretched into the frame by this
repository. The dependent variable is recall a week later.

That it was found at all is worth recording as method. It does not appear in
any note-taking literature; it was reached by reading
[[karpicke-retrieval-based-learning--202609040325]], noticing that a survey
which never mentions notes nonetheless summarises a study about consulting
them, and following the citation to the primary paper. Following the citation
rather than trusting the summary is what produced the finding — see the reader's
note in `raw/202609040325-karpicke-2025-retrieval-based-learning-excerpts.txt`,
where the review's sentence and the paper's own summary turn out to report the
same data on two different measures.

Tiered `peer-reviewed`: *Applied Cognitive Psychology* is a refereed Wiley
journal, and the captured PDF is the published version (Wiley copyright line,
"Published online 19 September 2007 in Wiley InterScience", printed pages
861–876 in the 2008 print issue).
