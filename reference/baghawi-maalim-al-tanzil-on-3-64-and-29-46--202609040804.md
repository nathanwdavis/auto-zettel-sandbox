Bibliographic record for al-Baghawi's commentary on Q 3:64 and Q 29:46,
captured in full in Arabic at
`raw/202609040804-baghawi-maalim-al-tanzil-3-64-and-29-46-arabic.txt`.

**Why this source, which the INBOX entry did not name.** The entry named
al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir and left "the other classical commentaries" open.
al-Baghawi (d. 516/1122) was added because he sits between al-Tabari and the
other two in date, and because his commentary is an abridging transmitter of
the earlier tradition — the kind of text through which a gloss would pass if it
were traditional. He also turns out to be the decisive witness on the point at
issue, for a formal reason: he is the only one of the three who sets the
shared-God clause up as a lemma of its own, `( وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد ونحن له
مسلمون )`, and then comments on it. What he supplies under that heading is two
chains of hadith about not believing and not disbelieving the People of the
Book, and nothing about the clause's content. A commentator who passes over a
clause he did not lemmatise might merely have been moving fast; one who
lemmatises it and still says nothing about it is making a choice.
