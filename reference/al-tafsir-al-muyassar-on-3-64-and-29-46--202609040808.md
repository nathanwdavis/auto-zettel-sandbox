**What this source is, and why an anonymous committee text is worth a
reference note.** *al-Tafsir al-Muyassar* is a short official paraphrase
commentary produced by a committee of scholars and published by the King Fahd
Complex for the Printing of the Holy Qur'an, which distributes it inside the
Qur'an editions given away worldwide and in most Arabic Qur'an software. It has
no authorial voice to speak of, and that is exactly its value here: it is the
gloss a reader with no scholarly training is most likely to meet, so what it
does with the shared-God clause of Q 29:46 is a fact about the tradition as it
currently reaches people, not about one scholar.

**Authorship, stated honestly.** The work is corporate and the committee is not
named on the served text. `csl_json.author` records it as a committee rather
than inventing a person, and the publisher is recorded because the publisher is
the identifying fact about this edition. The `issued` date 2009 is the year of
the widely distributed King Fahd Complex printing; the API names no edition, so
this is a conventional date for a corporate text under continuous reprint, not
a verified imprint year. A run that can consult a physical copy should correct
it.

**Access.** Rung (a), first request: Quran.com API v4, tafsir resource 16
(`slug: ar-tafsir-muyassar`).

**Quantity taken.** The served comment is a single short paraphrase per verse,
about 470 and 435 characters, and both are reproduced in the capture, because
there is no smaller unit that still shows what the gloss is doing. The work is
modern and in copyright; two sentences is the whole of what this base holds of
it.

**Independent cross-check.** The quoted passage was normalised and matched
against `tafsir.app`, which serves this commentary independently of Quran.com.
`quran.ksu.edu.sa` does not carry it — the URL slug tried returned the site
chrome without a commentary body, and that non-result was not counted as a
witness either way.
