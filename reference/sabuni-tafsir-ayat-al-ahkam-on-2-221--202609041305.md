Bibliographic record for the section of Muhammad 'Ali al-Sabuni's *Tafsir ayat
al-ahkam* on Q 2:221, headed by him `[13] نكاح المشركات` ("marriage to
*mushrikat*"). Arabic, verbatim, all four served pages, at
`raw/202609041305-sabuni-tafsir-ayat-al-ahkam-2-221-arabic.txt`.

**Why a modern work, alongside the classical one.** This capture is the second
half of a deliberate pair. Al-Qurtubi (202609041300) is the classical *ahkam*
commentary; al-Sabuni is a twentieth-century Syrian author writing in the same
genre, and he sets out the majority's proofs in numbered form (`أ`, `ب`, `جـ`,
`د`, `هـ`) where al-Qurtubi reports them discursively. The pair is what lets the
permanent notes say that an argument is classical **and** still current, rather
than saying it twice.

**A caution about what "modern" buys and what it does not.** Al-Sabuni is a
compiler in this section, not an innovator: his `حجة الجمهور` reproduces
arguments that al-Qurtubi already has, and he closes the first ruling by quoting
al-Tabari at length. So the two are not fully independent witnesses to the
*argument* — they are independent witnesses to the argument's **standing**, six
and a half centuries apart, which is the weaker claim the notes actually make.
Where al-Sabuni adds something of his own it is marked: the second ruling, and
one first-person aside (`سألني طالب غير مسلم…`, "a non-Muslim student asked
me…") which is his own voice and is not used as evidence of anything classical.

**Dating.** *Tafsir ayat al-ahkam* was first published in Damascus in the early
1970s; the `issued` year in the CSL record is that approximate first-publication
date and not an edition this run has seen. altafsir.com names no printed edition
and the served body carries no page markers, so no printed locator is claimed
and none should be inferred from the citation. Al-Sabuni's own dates are given
by the host's selector as `مـ 1930م -`.
