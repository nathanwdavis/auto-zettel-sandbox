Bibliographic record. The 1798 first edition (London: printed for J.
Johnson, in St. Paul's Church-Yard), published anonymously; authorship by
Thomas Robert Malthus is the settled scholarly attribution and the record
carries his name on that basis while the captured title page itself names
no author. This first edition is the short polemical form of the argument —
the heavily rewritten 1803 second edition is in effect a different book —
and it is the edition in which the geometrical-versus-arithmetical ratio
argument (Chapters I–II) appears in its original form.

This is the base's primary source on what happens when a compounding
process depends on a resource that grows at best linearly: population
"when unchecked" doubling each generation against subsistence gaining a
fixed increment. It complements the base's existing growth sources —
Price's penny (the upside of the geometric series) and Franklin's bequest
(compounding bounded by institutional survival) — with the collision case.

The raw capture holds two textually independent copies of the front
matter and Chapters I–II: the Project Gutenberg hand-keyed transcription
of the 1798 edition (eBook #4239, block 1) and the Internet Archive OCR of
the University of Toronto / Thomas Fisher Rare Book Library copy (item
essayonprincipl00malt, block 2), neither access-restricted, both public
domain (1798 publication). Wording on which both blocks agree is treated
as verified first-edition text; page locators in notes are the printed
page numbers visible in block 2's running heads. The work predates ISBN
and DOI, so verification rests on the raw capture; publisher, place, and
year are transcribed from the captured title page.
