Bibliographic record for the two sections of Abu al-Qasim Mahmud ibn 'Umar
al-Zamakhshari's *al-Kashshaf 'an haqa'iq ghawamid al-tanzil* that this cycle
captured: the commentary on Q 3:64 and on Q 29:46 — the same pair captured from
al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, al-Sa'di, al-Muyassar and
al-Razi by the three cycles before it. Arabic, verbatim, in full, at
`raw/202609041000-zamakhshari-al-kashshaf-3-64-and-29-46-arabic.txt`.

**Why this source, and what it was brought in to test.** The 09:00Z cycle
captured al-Razi and found that he does not gloss the shared-God clause
`وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد` as a shared `معبود` either, which disposed of the
suggestion that the silence of al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and al-Baghawi was an
effect of their *genres* (jurist, traditionist, abridger). That cycle then
raised the next objection against its own result and left it open: the
commentators in the base were by then all Sunni traditionalists of one sort or
another, so the shared silence might be a *school* effect. It named
al-Zamakhshari as the test — the Mu'tazili, the one major classical commentator
outside that theological family — and this capture is that test, taken up in the
next cycle.

**Access.** tafsir.app, resource slug `kashaf`, one request per verse, both
successful on the first attempt. This is a fourth consecutive rung-(a) success
for `source-access-triage` on a classical Arabic commentary, and it is recorded
as such: the ladder's expensive rungs have not been needed for this class of
source in this environment.

**Edition, stated honestly.** tafsir.app names no printed edition, and — unlike
the al-Razi capture, whose served page markers matched islamweb's and so
evidenced a shared printed exemplar — the served body carries no page markers
for these two sections. Nothing here keys the text to a printed edition, so none
is claimed and all locators are by sura:aya.

**Cross-check.** quranpedia.net serves the *Kashshaf* as book 346 on whole-sura
pages, independently of tafsir.app. Every passage this repository quotes was
normalised and substring-matched against that copy, and all matched; the
document hashes of both hosts' pages are in the capture header. Because this
capture also grounds a NEGATIVE claim — that the shared-God clause is never
glossed — the normaliser used for those counts is written out by code point in
the capture header, with its positive controls, per the standing INBOX finding
that a defective normaliser can silently swallow the letters it is meant to
keep. The absence reproduces on both hosts.
