Bibliographic record for the two sections of al-Qurtubi's *al-Jami' li-ahkam
al-Qur'an* that this cycle captured: the commentary on Q 3:64 and on Q 29:46,
the same pair the 07:00Z cycle captured from al-Tabari. Arabic, verbatim, in
full, at
`raw/202609040800-qurtubi-al-jami-li-ahkam-al-quran-3-64-and-29-46-arabic.txt`.

**Why this source.** The INBOX entry this cycle worked names al-Qurtubi first
among the commentators that would show whether al-Tabari's `maʿbud` gloss is
traditional or idiosyncratic. He is a Maliki jurist of the seventh/thirteenth
century writing three and a half centuries after al-Tabari, in a commentary
organised around legal questions — a different genre inside the same tradition,
which is what makes his silence or his agreement informative rather than
merely repetitive.

**Access.** Rung (a), first request: Quran.com API v4, tafsir resource 90
(`slug: ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi`), `/api/v4/tafsirs/90/by_ayah/<sura>:<aya>`. No
paywall, no bot challenge, no renderer. This is the route the previous cycle
wrote into the INBOX entry, and it worked exactly as recorded.

**Public domain.** The author died in 671/1273, so the Arabic text is out of
copyright and the capture holds the full sections rather than excerpts.

**The completeness question, which matters here more than usual.** This note
supports a claim about what al-Qurtubi does *not* say, and an absence can be an
artefact of an abridged electronic text. Two checks were run rather than
assumed. First, internal: his comment on 29:46 opens `فيه مسألتان` — "it
contains two questions" — and then delivers both, so the text announces its own
extent and reaches it. Second, external: the comment served by Quran.com ends
on the same sentence (the report from Muʿawiya about Kaʿb al-Ahbar) as the
independently served copies at `quran.ksu.edu.sa` and `tafsir.app`. The absence
of a gloss on the shared-God clause is therefore a property of the commentary,
not of this capture.

**Independent cross-check, done mechanically.** Every Arabic passage this
repository quotes from this capture was normalised (diacritics, tatweel,
alef/ya/ta-marbuta forms and punctuation removed) and substring-matched against
hosts independent of Quran.com. The 29:46 passages matched on **both**
`quran.ksu.edu.sa` and `tafsir.app`; the 3:64 passages matched on
`quran.ksu.edu.sa`. In addition, the string `معبود` — the word that carries
al-Tabari's identification — was searched for across all three copies of the
29:46 comment and occurs in none of them.

**Edition.** The API names no printed edition. The 3:64 section carries the
served text's own page markers (`[ ص: 100 ]`, `[ ص: 101 ]`) and the 29:46
section one (`[ ص: 323 ]`); these are preserved in the capture as markers
present in that text, and no page of any printed edition is claimed. Locators
are by *sura:aya*. `issued` is the author's death year, a conventional terminus,
not an edition date.

**Translation.** No published English translation was used. English renderings
in the literature and permanent notes are this repository's own working
translations of the captured Arabic, printed beside the Arabic wherever they
carry weight.
