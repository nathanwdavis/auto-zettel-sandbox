Bibliographic record for one section of the standard Zaydi commentary on the
*Azhar*: *kitab al-nikah*, the *fasl* detailing who may not be married, first
case — the unbelieving woman. Arabic, verbatim, at
`raw/202609041600-ibn-miftah-sharh-al-azhar-nikah-kafira-arabic.txt`.

**This is the first work of *fiqh* from either Shi'i tradition in this
repository.** That matters because of what the base has been holding without it.
Since the 13:00Z cycle it has asserted, on one clause of one modern Sunni author,
that the Imamiyya and some Zaydis hold the People of the Book to be *mushrikun*
and marriage to their women forbidden. Three cycles of work answered that from
*tafsir* — al-Tusi, al-Tabrisi, Zayd b. 'Ali's glossary, al-A'qam,
al-Tabataba'i — and each of them left the same two gaps open, which the 15:00Z
cycle finally stated as the entry's only remaining ask: no Imami or Zaydi *legal*
text was in the base at all, and the attribution of the strict reading to the
Zaydi imams Yahya and al-Qasim rested on al-A'qam alone. This capture and the
Imami one taken beside it ([[najafi-jawahir-al-kalam-vol-30-on-marriage-to-a-woman-of-the-people-of-the-book--202609041605]])
close the first gap. This one also closes the second.

**What the work is.** The *Azhar* is the *matn* of al-Mahdi Ahmad b. Yahya
al-Murtada (d. 840/1437); *Sharh al-Azhar*, properly *al-Muntaza' al-mukhtar min
al-Ghayth al-midrar*, is Ibn Miftah's commentary on it (d. 877/1472) and is the
Zaydi school's ordinary teaching text of *fiqh*. The volume captured is the
fourth, on marriage and divorce. Both attributions are read off the scanned
title matter and the Internet Archive item title, not inferred; what the base
may claim about the work's *standing* — "the school's ordinary teaching text" —
is its own characterisation from general knowledge and is not sourced here, so
no note rests on it. What the notes rest on is that this is a Zaydi work of
*fiqh* by a fifteenth-century author, which the text itself shows on every page
by whom it cites as authoritative.

**Access, and a host this base has not used before.** archive.org, item
`1_20191217_20191217_1729`, OCR text of a scanned print, downloaded complete in
one request. Rung (a) of the child skill's ladder did not apply, since there is
no publisher page for a fifteenth-century text; what was tried and failed first
is worth recording because it is a *new* failure mode for this base:
`shiaonlinelibrary.com`, `lib.eshia.ir`, `noorlib.ir` and `ar.wikishia.net` —
the four hosts a search surfaced for Shi'i legal texts — **do not resolve from
this environment at all**. This is DNS failure, not a block page and not a
paywall, so "unreachable" here means unreachable from this container rather than
absent from the web. `al-maktaba.org` answers with 403. archive.org answers.

**The pagination question does not arise, and that is itself the finding.**
Every altafsir capture this base holds had to establish that a section was read
to its end, and the last two cycles were spent discovering that the host's own
controls do not mean what they appear to mean. Here the whole volume arrives as
one file; the excerpt's bounds are byte offsets inside a file whose sha256 is
recorded, so exhaustion is a matter of arithmetic rather than of inference.

**The OCR is imperfect and the notes say so at every quotation.** This is
machine-read Arabic print. Observed damage in this excerpt includes Persian kaf
for Arabic kaf, ب for ي, ه/ة confusion, doubled letters, `»` and `ء` standing in
for the printed comma, and — the one that matters most here — `المادي` for
`الهادي`. Every quotation the base takes is reproduced **exactly as the OCR has
it** and was checked mechanically against this capture; corrected readings appear
in square brackets and are marked as this repository's reading. The check was run
with controls in the same pass, and one of those controls is the evidence for the
damage claim: the undamaged string `الهادي` occurs **nowhere** in the capture,
which is why the name is quoted in its damaged form.

**Rights.** The text is fifteenth-century and long out of copyright. The scan is
of a modern printing whose typesetting is not, and the base's standing worry
about full texts of in-copyright editions sitting in a public repository (INBOX,
2026-09-04) is the reason this capture is **one section** rather than the volume:
3,600 characters, taken for comment and criticism, kept so that the quotations
built on it are checkable.

**Locators.** No printed page numbers survive the OCR usably, so the literature
note locates by work, volume and section rather than by page. That is a real
limitation on anyone wanting to check this against a physical copy, and it is
stated rather than papered over with a page number this base cannot see.
