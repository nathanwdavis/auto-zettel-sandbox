Bibliographic record. A post of 1 November 2019 on Paul Helm's own blog,
Helm's Deep, whose title begins with five full stops on the page itself
(".....And more on Molinism"); the citation record drops them so that the
rendered citation is not mangled. It
sets out the seventeenth-century Reformed objection to middle knowledge
(Twisse, Rutherford, Turretin, van Mastricht) under the heading of the
Westminster Confession's chapter on the decree, and names Bruce Ware, for
his "Reformed Molinism" in *God's Greater Glory* pp. 110-112, as "an odd and
an inexplicable exception". Tiered `primary-text` for the same reason as the
2009 post (`helm-shunning-middle-knowledge--202609111810`): Helm's own
statement, self-published, no editorial review; used for Helm's view of the
Reformed tradition and for Ware only as Helm characterises him. Helm labels
the Confession's paragraph he discusses "III"; it is chapter 3, paragraph 2,
which his 2009 post cites correctly as 3.2 and which is on file, as Van Horn
quotes it, at `van-horn-on-incorporating-middle-knowledge-into-calvinism--202609111812`.

The post is in copyright (Helm), so the capture is bounded excerpts per the
repository's excerpt policy: four whole paragraphs, 528 words of 1,236 (43
percent), covering what the Confession's paragraph does to middle knowledge,
Twisse and Rutherford cutting the doctrine off at the root, the Reformed
answer to the grounding objection, and the paragraph on Ware. The van
Mastricht quotation (in Rester's 2019 translation), the Confession's text as
Helm quotes it, the paragraph drawing lessons from van Mastricht, the
paragraph on the current debate as "a debate within the libertarian guild",
and the closing note on Charles Rennie's article were NOT captured; notes
cite them only as attributed summary. Helm's post of 1 October 2019, "A
review of a book on Molinism", repeats the paragraph on Ware in nearly the
same words and is not captured.
