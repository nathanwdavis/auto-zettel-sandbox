Bibliographic record. Chapter 1 only, captured complete. That chapter is the
whole of the book's treatment of the ten *Sefirot Belimah*; chapters 2-6 concern
the twenty-two letters and are not captured, so nothing in this repository may
rest on them.

**What is being cited, and what the `issued` date means.** The base text is the
Hebrew of the Warsaw 1884 printing, digitized by Sefaria from the National
Library of Israel copy (NNL_ALEPH001310968) and marked public domain. `issued`
1884 is that printing — the edition actually read — and nothing else. There is
deliberately no `original-date`, against this repository's convention of giving
one for primary texts (Anselm, Aquinas, Calvin, Ramban, Tiqqunei ha-Zohar): for
those works a first composition or first printing is known well enough to state,
and for *Sefer Yetzirah* it is not. Proposals in the captured witness run from the
second or early third century to the Geonic period (700–1000), and the dispute
was already old when Kalisch surveyed it in 1877 — he reports Graetz
placing it in the early Christian centuries and then withdrawing that, Zunz
placing it in the Geonic period, and the traditional ascription to Abraham,
which he calls "fabulous" (capture, Section G). Putting any one of those in a
citation string would assert what the sources do not settle. The prose states
the dispute instead.

**Four witnesses, so that no quoted phrase rests on one transcription.** Two
Hebrew digitizations were collated verse by verse: the Warsaw 1884 text above,
and Hebrew Wikisource's `ספר יצירה א` at revision 2870897 (2024-08-04). The two
are independent transcriptions, and probably of different recensions: the
Wikisource project page lists the Warsaw 1884 "nusach ha-Ari" separately, under
other versions, from the text it serves as the book itself. That inference is
this repository's, from the project page's own arrangement. Mishnayot 1:2, 1:8, 1:9 and 1:10 are
identical word for word; the three verses this repository quotes (1:4, 1:5, 1:7)
agree word for word apart from orthography (*beli mah* as one word or two, plene
versus defective spelling, *we-hashev* / *we-hoshev*). The witnesses diverge in
substance only from 1:11 onward, where recensions differ; nothing here is quoted
from that range. The full collation is in the capture, Section E.

English is quoted from the Sefaria Community Translation (CC0). It is an open
community translation rather than a named published one, which is a weaker
witness than this repository normally prefers, so a second, independent English
rendering was captured alongside it: Isidor Kalisch's published translation of
1877 (*Sepher Yezirah: A Book on Creation; or, The Jewish Metaphysics of Remote
Antiquity*, New York: L. H. Frank & Co.), public domain, from the Internet
Archive scan `1877-kalisch-sepher-yezirah`. The two are 149 years and one
translating tradition apart and were made to agree on nothing; where this
repository leans on a sentence, both renderings of it are set out in the capture
and in the literature note. Kalisch divides the chapter into nine sections
against the fourteen mishnayot of the other witnesses, and that mapping is
recorded in the capture rather than smoothed away: the sentence most used here,
1:7, is his §6.

**A limit the translator himself states, and this repository adopts.** The E1
translator's note on 1:2 — quoted verbatim in the capture, Section F — holds
that although "later interpretors have, of course, explicitly linked the Ten
Sefirot Belimah with the now-familiar Ten Sefirot found in later texts like the
Bahir and Zohar", he refrains from doing so, because "it is not at all clear
that the SY ascribes the same symbolic significance to the Ten Sefirot Belimah
as latter Kabbalistic texts do". No note in this repository may treat this
book's ten as the ten of *Patach Eliyahu* or of the Zohar. What can be said, and
is said, is narrower: this is where the word first appears, and later kabbalah
cites it — the *Patach Eliyahu* capture's own gloss on "the ten sephirot" points
back to "Sepher Yetzirah 1:2" (see [[tikkunei-ha-zohar--202609011531]]).
