Bibliographic record for the *Gharib al-Qur'an* carried under the name of Zayd
ibn 'Ali on Q 2:221 and Q 5:5. Arabic, verbatim, in full, at
`raw/202609041410-zayd-b-ali-gharib-al-quran-2-221-and-5-5-arabic.txt`. **This
is the first Zaydi text of any kind in this repository.**

**Attribution, and the limit it puts on what may be claimed.** Works carried
under the name of a figure of the first Islamic century are transmitted
ascriptions, and this one is not independently established here. Zayd b. 'Ali
(d. 122/740), grandson of al-Husayn, is the eponym of the Zaydiyya; whether he
wrote this glossary is a question this base has not tested and does not need to.
What the capture supports without qualification is a claim about **the Zaydi
commentary tradition** — what the school's own canonical tafsir corpus, as
published by the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute in its Zaydi group, says on these
two verses. Every note drawn from it is worded to that, and nothing here is
asserted about the historical Zayd.

**Why it was captured.** To test from inside the Zaydi tradition a claim this
base held on the report of one modern Sunni author: that the Imamiyya and *some
Zaydis* forbid marriage to the women of the People of the Book. A tradition's
oldest commentary is the cheapest first probe, and the answer it gave was not
the expected one.

**The comment is short, and that is the text rather than a short read.** *Gharib
al-Qur'an* is a glossary: it explains difficult words and passes over the rest.
Its entire comment on Q 2:221 is one sentence. The exhaustion probes recorded in
the capture header are what distinguishes a one-sentence comment from a
truncated capture, and they matter more here than in any other capture this
cycle took, because a *brief* source is exactly where a paging bug would be
invisible.

**Access.** altafsir.com, `tMadhNo=5` (تفاسيرالزيدية), `tTafsirNo=89`. The
host's own selector dates the author to 120 AH where the standard date is
122/740; the discrepancy is the host's and is reproduced in the capture header
rather than silently corrected. `issued` above carries 740 CE, the standard
date, because that is what a bibliography should render.

**Edition.** None named by the host, none claimed; locators are by sura:aya.

**One host only,** and here that limitation is sharper than usual: none of the
eight hosts this base has tried carries a Zaydi group at all, so no second-host
confirmation of any kind is currently available for Zaydi tafsir. The one
negative fact taken from this capture — that this commentary does *not* hold the
strict position — is stated in the notes as a fact about this text, not about
the school.
