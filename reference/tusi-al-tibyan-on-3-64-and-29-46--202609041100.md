Bibliographic record for the two sections of Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn al-Hasan
al-Tusi's *al-Tibyan al-jami' li-'ulum al-Qur'an* that this cycle captured: the
commentary on Q 3:64 and on Q 29:46 — the same pair captured from al-Tabari,
al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, al-Sa'di, al-Muyassar, al-Razi and
al-Zamakhshari by the four cycles before it. Arabic, verbatim, in full, at
`raw/202609041100-tusi-al-tibyan-3-64-and-29-46-arabic.txt`.

**Why this source, and what it was brought in to test.** Two explanations for
the classical silence about the shared-God clause `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد` had by
now been generated by this base and both had been spent. The 09:00Z cycle killed
the *genre* explanation with al-Razi; the 10:00Z cycle killed the *school*
explanation with al-Zamakhshari, the Mu'tazili. That cycle then named where to
look next, and named it precisely: every commentator in the base was Sunni, so
the cheapest place left to find a commentator who *does* gloss the clause was
the Shi'i tradition — al-Tusi's *al-Tibyan* and al-Tabrisi's *Majma' al-bayan* —
"which no host checked so far carries and which belong to neither family the
base has now ruled out". This capture is that test. Al-Tusi is the natural first
choice: the *Tibyan* is the first complete Imami tafsir and the work every later
Imami commentary is built on.

**Access — a new host, and the one that made this cycle possible.**
altafsir.com, published by the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute for Islamic Thought
(Amman), indexes its commentaries by *madhhab* and carries a Twelver Shi'i
category (`tMadhNo=4`) alongside Sunni, Sufi, Salafi, Zaydi and Ibadi ones. This
is the first host in this base's experience that carries Shi'i tafsir at all,
and it also carries three other works this cluster has wanted. Both verses came
back on the first request. Rung (a) of `source-access-triage` again, a fifth
consecutive success on a classical Arabic commentary — but only after the
*absence* of Shi'i tafsir on the previously used hosts had been established
positively rather than assumed; those checks are recorded in the capture header.

**Pagination is load-bearing here, and nearly cost the result.** altafsir.com
splits a commentary section across `&Page=N`. The gloss this cycle was looking
for sits on page 1 for al-Tusi but on page 2 for al-Tabrisi and for Tantawi. A
first-page-only read of this host would have reported two of the three
commentaries silent — a false absence manufactured by paging rather than by
normalisation, but the same class of error as the standing INBOX finding on the
Arabic normaliser. How exhaustion was established (the `التالي` control plus a
body-repeat check) is written into the capture header.

**Edition, stated honestly.** altafsir.com names no printed edition for these
sections and the served body carries no page markers, so nothing keys the text
to a printed edition and none is claimed. All locators are by sura:aya.

**One host only, and what that does and does not threaten.** No independent host
for the *Tibyan* was found from this environment. Eight rungs were tried and
each failure mode is recorded in the capture header: tafsir.app and
quranpedia.net were shown by their own embedded resource lists to carry no Shi'i
tafsir; quran.ksu.edu.sa carries five Sunni tafsirs; al-maktaba.org returned
403; shiaonlinelibrary.com and ar.lib.eshia.ir did not connect; shamela.ws and
hodaalquran.com are reachable but not usable for a verse-level check. The
direction of the risk is stated plainly rather than smoothed over: what this
repository takes from this capture is a POSITIVE quotation — a gloss that is
present — and a single institutional host is a weak threat to that. The single
negative count taken from it (`معبود` does not occur) is marked as single-host
in the notes and filed to INBOX for second-host confirmation.
