Bibliographic record. A post of 1 May 2009 on Paul Helm's own blog, Helm's
Deep, which the post itself calls a "short Analysis" of Bruce Ware's
"compatibilist middle knowledge" as set out in Ware's *God's Greater Glory*
(Crossway, 2004), cited throughout by the abbreviation "GGG" and a page
number. Tiered `primary-text`, not `peer-reviewed`, on the Behe precedent: it
is cited here as the primary statement of Helm's own objection, in his own
words, published by himself with no editorial review. It is used for Helm's
view, and for Ware's view only as Helm quotes him, with the page numbers Helm
gives; Ware's book is not on file in this repository. The post also
announces the conversation between Helm and Terrance Tiessen that appeared
as "Does Calvinism Have Room for Middle Knowledge? A Conversation",
*Westminster Theological Journal* 71 (2009); that article is behind a
paywall and is known here only as Van Horn reports it
(`van-horn-on-incorporating-middle-knowledge-into-calvinism--202609111812`).

The post is in copyright (Helm), and the blog states no licence, so the
capture is bounded excerpts per this repository's excerpt policy for
in-copyright web sources (the IEP and SEP captures are the precedents): five
whole paragraphs, 728 words of 1,691 (43 percent), covering what Ware offers
middle knowledge for, the Reformed tradition's three replies to the charge of
authoring sin and the mystery that remains, what is left of middle knowledge
once libertarian freedom is abandoned (with Ware's own sentences from GGG
112-114), why that is natural knowledge, and the closing verdict. The
epigraph (Westminster Confession 3.2), the opening paragraph, the paragraph
defining middle knowledge, the paragraph on the freedom of indifference, the
first paragraph of "Enter Bruce Ware" (GGG 78), and the three paragraphs on
evil, deliberation, temporalism and "occasioning" (GGG 119-123) were NOT
captured;
notes cite them only as attributed summary and never quote them.
