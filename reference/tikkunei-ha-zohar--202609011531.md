Bibliographic record. Folios 17a-17b, the second introduction known from its
opening words as *Patach Eliyahu* ("Elijah opened"). It is widely recited and
is printed in many Sephardic and Hasidic rites, so it is not an esoteric
fragment but a working formula for how the hidden God stands to the ten
sefirot. (That liturgical reception is background this repository has not
captured a source for; no note should rest on how widely the passage is said,
only on what it says.)

**A term the passage does not use.** *Ein Sof* -- the tradition's standard
designation for the hidden Infinite -- does not occur anywhere in these two
folios. Elijah addresses "Master of the Worlds" and thereafter simply "You".
Reading that addressee as Ein Sof is the standard construal, and the notes
here follow it, but it is a construal: every claim in this repository about
what the passage denies is a claim about the One it addresses, and the
identification with Ein Sof adds nothing to those claims.

Authorship and date are contested in the way that matters for citation. The
work is traditionally ascribed to Shimon bar Yochai (2nd c.) and is presented
in the text as his circle's; scholarship since Scholem places it in the
Castilian zoharic circle of the late thirteenth to early fourteenth century,
by a hand later than the main body of the Zohar. `Anonymous` is therefore the
honest CSL author, and the `issued` date is the 2024 Solomon translation this
repository actually read -- the Aramaic was first printed at Mantua in 1557.
No note here rests on the traditional ascription being correct.

`original-date` 1557 is the Mantua first printing, not a composition date;
composition is placed by scholarship in the late thirteenth to early
fourteenth century, which no citation string can express and which the prose
above states instead.
