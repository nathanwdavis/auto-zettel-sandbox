Bibliographic record. Luis de Molina, S.J. (1535-1600), *Concordia liberi
arbitrii cum gratiae donis, divina praescientia, providentia, praedestinatione
et reprobatione*, first published Lisbon 1588 and enlarged Antwerp 1595; the
work that introduced the term *scientia media* and set off the *de auxiliis*
controversy. This reference is to the Paris 1876 reprint (P. Lethielleux),
digitised by PIMS / University of Toronto and held at archive.org as
`concordialiberia00moli`; title, publisher and date are taken from the item
metadata and confirmed by the running heads and signature marks on the scanned
pages. The text is public domain. Recorded as primary-text: it is Molina's own
statement of his position, not a report of it.

**Capture note.** The item's machine OCR of this nineteenth-century Latin is
usable for search but not for quotation, so the passages used were read
directly from the scanned page images, which are committed to `raw/` as the
visual record: `raw/202609090050-molina-p317-leaf334.jpg`,
`-p318-leaf335.jpg`, `-p323-leaf340.jpg`, `-p324-leaf341.jpg` and
`-p337-leaf354.jpg` (printed pages 317, 318, 323, 324 and 337; archive.org leaf
index = printed page + 17 for this item). The Latin is transcribed in
`raw/202609090050-molina-concordia-disp-52-53-excerpt.txt`, whose header
records the image URLs and SHA-256 checksums, the SHA-256 of the two OCR
witnesses used for a word-by-word check (this item's, and the 1588 Lisbon
edition scanned as `imgmar3873MiscellaneaOpal`), and five printing anomalies
kept as printed. Only the passages the literature note uses are transcribed:
disputation 52 §§ on the three kinds of divine knowledge (pp. 317-318), the
passage on which knowledge is the cause of things and how God is the cause of
free acts (pp. 323-324), and disputation 53 membrum 1 on Tyre and Sidon
(p. 337). The full text is not stored.

**Access and edition.** Alfred J. Freddoso's English translation of Part IV,
*On Divine Foreknowledge* (Cornell, 1988), is in copyright and was not used;
every English rendering in this repository's notes is the repository's own and
is marked as such. The section numbering Freddoso uses (disp. 52 §9 etc.) is
not printed in the 1876 edition, which numbers only disputations and membra,
so locators here give disputation, membrum and printed page.
