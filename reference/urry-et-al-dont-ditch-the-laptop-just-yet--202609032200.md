Bibliographic record. A preregistered direct replication of the single most
influential experiment on note taking, published in the same journal as the
original. This is the first *experimental* source in this base: everything the
`zettelkasten method` topic previously rested on was a how-to
([[ahrens-how-to-take-smart-notes--202608301000]]), a practitioner describing
his own file ([[luhmann-communicating-with-slip-boxes--202609010111]]), that
practitioner's archivist ([[schmidt-niklas-luhmanns-card-index--202609010302]]),
a seventeenth-century method ([[locke-a-new-method-of-making-common-place-books--202609010602]]),
or a historian of the practice ([[blair-note-taking-as-an-art-of-transmission--202609032100]]).
Four kinds of witness, and not one of them a test. This one is a test.

Tiered `peer-reviewed`: *Psychological Science* is the flagship refereed
journal of the Association for Psychological Science, and the manuscript's own
title page records acceptance for publication there on 30 July 2020.

Access and provenance, which matter more than usual here. The version of
record is paywalled — Unpaywall reports the DOI closed, OpenAlex agrees, and
Semantic Scholar's `openAccessPdf` status is CLOSED. What this base holds is
the authors' own accepted manuscript, deposited by them on PsyArXiv (OSF
Preprints) and pulled through the OSF API, captured at
`raw/202609032200-urry-et-al-dont-ditch-the-laptop-manuscript-excerpts.txt`.

Journal pagination 326–339 comes from Crossref and PubMed metadata alone, and
no claim in this base is pinned to it.

The identity of manuscript and article was checked rather than assumed: the
publisher-deposited abstract, retrieved independently by DOI from Crossref and
by PMID 33539228 from NCBI E-utilities, agrees word for word with the
manuscript's abstract on every substantive word, differing only in `n` versus
`N`, "As in" versus "Like", and one hyphen. The capture header records the
comparison in full, along with the one genuinely confusing sentence in the
published abstract ("laptop participants did not perform better on the quiz")
and why the body of the paper, not that sentence, is what the notes cite.

ran the data collection as a class project, plus three authors at other
