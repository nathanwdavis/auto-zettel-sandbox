Bibliographic record. The 2007 open letter from 138 Muslim scholars, clerics
and intellectuals to the leaders of the Christian churches, issued under the
auspices of the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute for Islamic Thought in Amman and
still published by them at acommonword.com. Its title is a quotation of
Qur'an 3:64.

**Why this source and not another.** This repository holds no argued Muslim
source on the same-God question -- the gap that inquiry 202609032122 was filed
to close. Among the candidates that inquiry listed, this one is first because
it is *collective* rather than idiosyncratic: it carries 138 signatories
across the Sunni and Shi'i schools, muftis, and academics, so it is evidence
of a position that Muslim scholars were willing to sign jointly rather than of
one theologian's view. It is also openly published by its own authors, so it
could be captured in full and verified from the capture, which several
better-known candidates (Al-Ghazali's *Faysal al-Tafriqa*, Ibn Taymiyya's
*al-Jawab al-Sahih*) could not be within this cycle.

**What was captured.** Section (III), "Come to a Common Word Between Us and
You", complete, in
`raw/202609032300-a-common-word-2007-section-iii.txt`. Sections (I) and (II),
which set out the two commandments within each scripture, were not captured;
no note here cites them, and a claim about them would not be checkable from
the capture. The endnotes are not captured either.

**Provenance and the limits of this record.** The text captured is the
English text as the Institute publishes it, which is how the letter was
circulated to its Christian addressees and is the form under discussion in the
responses to it; the letter also exists in Arabic. The signatory list is on a
separate page of the same site and was not captured, so this record does not
support any claim about a *particular* named signatory -- only about what the
letter says over the signature of the body as a whole.

**Genre.** This is a public theological appeal addressed to named church
leaders, not a peer-reviewed article and not a legal ruling. It is filed as
`primary-text` because that is the role it plays here: it is the object being
read, evidence of what Muslim scholars said, not a secondary description of
what Muslims think.
