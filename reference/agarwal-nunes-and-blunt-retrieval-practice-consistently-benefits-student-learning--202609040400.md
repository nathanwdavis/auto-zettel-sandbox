**Full text of the authors' preprint; published abstract verified separately
from two independent indexes.** This is not the ordinary "full text" of this
base's other 2026-09-04 captures and the difference is load-bearing, so it is
stated here before anything else.

What this base holds is two documents, not one. The **published** article
(*Educational Psychology Review* 33, no. 4, 1409–53) is represented only by its
abstract, recovered from ERIC (EJ1319572) and from OpenAlex and shown to be
word-identical between them. The **preprint** — the same three authors' own
manuscript, deposited by them on the Open Science Framework on 10 June 2020 and
labelled there "Manuscript under review" — is held in full, all 67 pages.

**The two disagree on their own headline figures**, which is why every note
drawing on this source names which document it is drawing on:

| figure | preprint (2020) | published (2021) |
|---|---|---|
| effect sizes yielded | 60 | 49 |
| share medium or large | 62% | 57% |
| non-WEIRD experiments | "10 out of 50" | "6%" |

The published numbers are the article's findings; the preprint's are
superseded. Nothing in either document explains the first two changes, so the
preprint's per-experiment detail — its tables, its appendix, its counts of
comparison conditions — cannot be assumed to correspond one-for-one to the
published article's, which counts eleven fewer effect sizes. Where a note in
this base quotes a per-experiment figure from this source it is a **preprint**
figure and says so. The third row does reconcile, but only by an inference this
repository is making rather than a statement the authors make; the reasoning is
set out in the capture header and repeated in
[[agarwal-nunes-and-blunt-screened-two-thousand-abstracts-and-counted-what-they-found--202609040405]].

Tiered `peer-reviewed` on the strength of the published article: *Educational
Psychology Review* is a refereed Springer journal, the ERIC record carries
`peerreviewed: T`, and Crossref confirms volume, issue, pagination and an issue
date of 14 March 2021. The document read in full is *pre*-peer-review, and the
gap between the two versions is exactly the evidence that this journal's review
process changed the paper.

**Why the access record matters here more than usual.** The INBOX entry that
commissioned this source (filed 2026-09-04 by the retrieval-practice cycle)
reported it as gold open access — "It is free, legitimately, right now" — and
recommended it as the cheap first item of three. The first half of that turned
out to be wrong in an instructive way, so all six rungs are recorded in the
capture header and summarised here:

- **Publisher.** Springer returned its standing 3,038-byte JavaScript/auth
  shell for both the article page and the `content/pdf` path. That shell is
  served identically for open and closed articles, so this rung cannot tell
  "paywalled" from "bot-blocked", and this note claims neither.
- **Unpaywall, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar** all three report the article
  gold and CC-BY — and all three name, as the openly readable copy, the same
  OSF URL, which is an author *manuscript*, versioned `submittedVersion`. All
  three also report the publisher's own location as not open. Crossref carries
  `springer.com/tdm` as the licence for the DOI, which is a text-and-data-mining
  licence and not CC-BY. The "gold" label is therefore an index judgement that
  no rung of this ladder could confirm at the publisher.
- **The OSF URL the indexes named resolves to nothing downloadable.**
  `osf.io/cfsb6` is a *registration*, not a preprint and not a file: the
  preprint API 404s on it and `osf.io/cfsb6/download` returns HTTP 500. The PDF
  sits two API hops deeper, in the registration's archived storage folder. A
  fetcher that follows the Unpaywall URL and stops gets nothing at all, which
  is very likely why three earlier cycles recorded this paper as unread.
- **The author site is dead where it counts.** retrievalpractice.org's research
  page serves fine and links its PDFs to `pdf.poojaagarwal.com`, which now
  answers with a DreamHost "has not yet uploaded their website" placeholder;
  every filename tried 404s. This is worth recording against the standing house
  proposal to promote the author-page rung for experimental psychology: on the
  previous cycle that rung returned three full texts in one pass, and on this
  one the same rung is a dead host. It is a good rung, not a reliable one.
- **Europe PMC** has no record of the DOI, and **ERIC** holds the abstract only
  (`e_fulltextauth: 0`).

What would close the remaining gap: institutional access to the published
*Educational Psychology Review* article, which would let the eleven vanished
effect sizes be accounted for and the published tables be read against the
preprint's. Filed as an INBOX follow-up rather than left implicit here.
