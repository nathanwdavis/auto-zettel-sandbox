Bibliographic record. A commissioned survey chapter in the third edition of
Elsevier's *Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference*, updating the
author's own 2017 chapter in the second edition. This base uses it for two
things and no others: what the field says about the durability of the testing
effect twenty years on, and what a current survey of that field does not cover.

**The conflict of interest is on the face of it and must travel with every
citation.** Karpicke is the second author of
[[roediger-and-karpicke-test-enhanced-learning--202609040305]], the 2006 paper
this chapter assesses, and a co-author of
[[agarwal-karpicke-kang-roediger-and-mcdermott-open-and-closed-book-tests--202609040315]].
When p. 434 reports that the benefits of retrieval practice "have been
replicated hundreds of times", this repository is reading a researcher's
account of his own effect, which is a different evidential object from an
independent audit. The independent audits exist and the chapter names them —
Rowland (2014), Adesope et al. (2017), Agarwal et al. (2021) — and **none of
them was read this cycle**. So what is grounded here is that the field's
current survey claims robustness and points at three meta-analyses; not that
this base has verified the claim. Reading at least one of those meta-analyses
is filed in INBOX as the work that would close the gap.

That limit is worth stating sharply because of the company this note keeps. The
base's other measured note-taking source,
[[morehead-dunlosky-and-rawson-how-much-mightier-is-the-pen--202609040156]], is
a *direct replication by an independent team* that did not hold up. The
contrast between an effect audited by outsiders and an effect vouched for by
its discoverer is exactly the kind of thing this repository exists to keep
visible, and it is why
[[retrieval-not-re-exposure-is-what-makes-learning-last--202609040340]] states
the replication claim as attributed rather than as fact.

Tiered `peer-reviewed`: a solicited, edited reference-work chapter — editorially
reviewed rather than blind-refereed like a primary report. It is used here for
survey and attribution, never as the source of a measurement; every measurement
this base cites comes from the two primary papers above, read in full.
