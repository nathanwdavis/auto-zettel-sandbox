**Why this source was sought, and what it settles.** It is the third and last of
the three audits named in
[[karpickes-survey-vouches-for-the-effect-and-never-mentions-notes--202609040330]]
(p. 412), and the only one
written from outside the Roediger–Karpicke line: Rowland was at Colorado State
University and has no authorship on any other source this base holds. Two
cycles of INBOX have named it the highest-value outstanding item for exactly
that reason, and the sentence in
[[agarwal-nunes-and-blunt-retrieval-practice-in-schools-and-classrooms--202609040505]]
recording it as unread has been amended accordingly. What it delivers is the base's
first *independent* quantitative estimate of the effect it has been building on:
g = 0.50, CI [0.42, 0.58], from 159 effect sizes in 61 studies reported between
1975 and 2013 (advance online, pp. 8, 16). It is a genuine meta-analysis, which
matters because the survey chapter called all three audits meta-analyses and was
wrong about at least one of them
([[agarwal-nunes-and-blunt-retrieval-practice-in-schools-and-classrooms--202609040505]]).

**The limit that turned out to be the most useful thing in it.** Rowland
excluded classroom studies on purpose — criterion (d), thirteen studies
eliminated, with readers referred to a different meta-analysis for them (p. 8).
So this base's two audits do not overlap at all: this one is laboratory-only by
construction, and
[[agarwal-nunes-and-blunt-retrieval-practice-in-schools-and-classrooms--202609040505]]
is classroom-only by construction. That is worked out in
[[the-laboratory-and-classroom-evidence-were-separated-on-purpose--202609040610]].
