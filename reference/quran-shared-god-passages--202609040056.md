Scripture, cited in-text per SBL and excluded from the bibliography (FR-9).
Same edition and same API as [[quran-tawhid-passages--202608311923]] —
Pickthall's 1930 translation, public domain and therefore capturable in full —
so the two captures are directly comparable. The doctrinal weight in Islam
rests on the Arabic; this is a translation.

**Why a second Qur'an reference note exists.** This one is a correction to a
selection, and the correction is deliberate rather than incidental. Inquiry 202609032122 found
that the first capture holds Surah 112, 4:171 and 5:73 — divine oneness, the
instruction to "say not" the word Pickthall renders as Three, and "They surely
disbelieve who say: Lo! Allah is the third of three" — and that all three mark
*difference*. It omits 29:46, "our Allah and your Allah is One, and unto Him we
surrender", which is the most on-point verse in
the Qur'an for the same-God question and which runs the other way. A capture
assembled to bear on that question which holds the three rejecting verses and
omits the affirming one is selection-shaped whether or not it was meant that
way, and every note resting on it inherits the shape.

`raw/` is immutable, so this is a new capture rather than an edit of the old
one. Neither capture supersedes the other: the Qur'an says both things, and the
pair of reference notes is the honest way to hold that.

Passage selection. 29:46 is the verse at issue; 29:45 and 29:47 are captured
with it so it is not read out of context. 3:64 is captured because it is the
verse from which the 2007 Muslim scholars' letter takes its name (see
[[a-common-word-between-us-and-you--202609040058]]). 2:136 bears on shared
revelation, and 5:48 — "For each We have appointed a law and a way. Had God
willed He could have made you one community" — is captured as the counterweight
inside the same set, so this capture is not itself one-sided in the opposite
direction.
