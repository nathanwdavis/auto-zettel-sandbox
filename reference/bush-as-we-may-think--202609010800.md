Bibliographic record. Vannevar Bush's July 1945 Atlantic Monthly essay,
written as he directed the U.S. Office of Scientific Research and
Development — the canonical statement, between Locke's commonplace method
(1686/1706) and Luhmann's zettelkasten, that "artificiality of systems of
indexing" is the bottleneck of recorded knowledge and that selection should
instead work by association: stored, named, permanent trails of links
between items, embodied in the imagined "memex" desk. Sections 6-8 carry
the argument this base uses; the earlier sections survey 1945
instrumentation (microfilm, dry photography, calculating machinery).

The article is under copyright, so the raw capture holds bounded excerpts
of sections 6-8 only (about 700 words per block), not the full text, per
this base's excerpt policy for in-copyright works. It holds them TWICE,
from two textually independent copies: block 1 from The Atlantic's own
web publication of the full text (the copyright holder's copy, this
record's URL), block 2 from Denys Duchier's 1994 ASCII transcription
hosted by the W3C, whose header records The Atlantic's permission. Five
one-phrase variant readings between the two copies are listed in the
capture header and left unresolved (the July 1945 print scan is
lending-restricted online, so no third witness was accessible); quoting
notes quote only text on which both copies agree word-for-word, except
where a variant is explicitly flagged. A 1945 magazine article carries no
DOI or ISBN, so verification rests on the raw capture; the page range
101-108 is the standard citation for the printed issue and is not
independently confirmed by either captured copy, which carry no page
