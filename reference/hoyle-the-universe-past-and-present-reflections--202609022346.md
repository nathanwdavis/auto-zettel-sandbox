Bibliographic record. Hoyle's essay in Caltech's own alumni science magazine,
the source of the much-quoted "superintellect has monkeyed with physics"
sentence. Tiered `primary-text`, not `peer-reviewed`: *Engineering and Science*
is an institutional magazine, and this piece is an essay stating Hoyle's own
view rather than a refereed report of results. It is cited here for exactly
that — what Hoyle said he concluded — so the tier matches the use.

Served by Caltech's own repository (item record
https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/3312/, persistent identifier
https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechES:45.2.Hoyle), whose rights statement
grants "permission for individual, educational, research and non-commercial
reproduction". The capture is bounded excerpts nonetheless, per the excerpt
policy: print p. 12 entire, plus the title and byline from p. 8.

Provenance worth knowing before quoting: the PDF is a **scan with an OCR text
layer**, not digital typesetting. pdfminer.six and poppler's pdftotext return
byte-identical text for p. 12 *including its errors*, because both read the
same OCR layer — their agreement says nothing about the printed page. The page
was therefore rendered to an image and read directly, and the capture records
the printed text with every OCR divergence listed in its header. Four of those
divergences touch the physics: the OCR renders "7.65 MeV" with a spurious space,
"7.12 MeV level in 16O" as "7 .12 MeV level in 160", "nucleosynthesis" as
"nucleosyhthesis", and "put-up" as "put-Lip". A note quoting this article from
the OCR layer rather than the capture would misquote the nuclide.
