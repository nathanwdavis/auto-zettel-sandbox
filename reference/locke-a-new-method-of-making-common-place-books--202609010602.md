Bibliographic record. The anonymous 1706 English translation (London:
printed for J. Greenwood) of John Locke's "Méthode nouvelle de dresser des
recueils", first published in French in the *Bibliothèque universelle et
historique*, vol. II (1686) — the canonical statement of the commonplace-book
indexing method Locke says he had then used for twenty-five years. The 1706
volume adds an extract from Jean Le Clerc on the same subject and two
unrelated letters of John Wallis on teaching the deaf; the raw capture
covers the Locke tract, its front matter, and the Le Clerc matter, and
stops before the Wallis letters. The printing is itself the specimen: its
early pages are laid out as a working common-place book whose entries the
text refers back to.

This is the base's first pre-Luhmann primary source on note-keeping
method, giving the "zettelkasten method" topic a historical baseline: the
topical-head tradition that Luhmann's fixed-address, link-based file later
departed from.

The raw capture holds two independent Internet Archive OCR plain texts of
the same 1706 edition — the Georgetown University Library copy (block 1,
the copy this record's URL points at) and the Wellcome Collection copy
(block 2) — both marked Creative Commons Public Domain Mark 1.0, neither
access-restricted. The OCR of 1706 letterpress is rough (long s as "f",
ligature misreads); quotations in notes built on this reference normalize
those artifacts only where both independently scanned copies agree
word-for-word, and each quoting note records that policy. The work carries
no ISBN or DOI (1706), so verification rests on the raw capture; publisher,
place, and year are transcribed from the captured title page itself.
