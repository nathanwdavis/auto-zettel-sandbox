Scripture, cited in-text per SBL and excluded from the bibliography (FR-9).
Pickthall's 1930 translation is used because it is in the public domain and so
could be captured in full; it is a translation, and the doctrinal weight in
Islam rests on the Arabic. Surah 112 (al-Ikhlas) is captured complete; 4:171
and 5:73 are captured complete as single ayat. Together they give both the
positive statement of tawhid and the explicit Qur'anic rejection of trinitarian
language.

SECOND CAPTURE ADDED 2026-09-04, and the reason matters more than the addition.
The original capture was assembled to bear on the same-God question and holds
only verses that mark DIFFERENCE. It omits 29:46 — "our Allah and your Allah is
One, and unto Him we surrender" — which is the most on-point verse in the
Qur'an for that question and runs the other way. A selection with the three
rejecting verses and none of the affirming ones is selection-shaped whether or
not it was meant that way, and every note resting on it inherited that shape.
raw/ is immutable, so the correction is a second capture rather than an edit:
raw/202609032356-quran-common-ground-passages.txt holds 29:46, 3:64, 2:136-137
and 5:48, from the same public-domain Pickthall edition via the same API. The
two captures are complements and neither is the Qur'an's whole word on the
subject; anything citing this reference on the same-God question should be
read against both.

Note for anyone citing 3:64: Pickthall renders its key phrase "Come to an
agreement between us and you", whereas *A Common Word Between Us and You*
(reference 202609032358) renders the same Arabic "Come to a common word between
us and you" and takes its title from that. The familiar wording is the letter's,
not Pickthall's.
