[export p. 18 | Joseph Gen 50:20]
“You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring it about that
many people should be kept alive, as they are today” (Gen. 50:20).

[export p. 19 | Pharaoh hardening texts]
The story of the exodus from Egypt repeatedly affirms that God hardened the
heart of Pharaoh: God says, “I will harden his heart” (Ex. 4:21), “I will
harden Pharaoh’s heart” (Ex. 7:3)

[export p. 19 | the self-hardening objection]
It is sometimes objected that Scripture also says that Pharaoh hardened his
own heart (Ex. 8:15, 32; 9:34), and that God’s act of hardening Pharaoh’s
heart was only in response to the initial rebellion and hardness of heart that
Pharaoh himself exhibited of his own free will.

[export p. 19 | the chronological reply]
But it should be noted that God’s promises that he would harden Pharaoh’s
heart (Ex. 4:21; 7:3) are made long before Scripture tells us that Pharaoh
hardened his own heart (we read of this for the first time in Ex. 8:15).

[export p. 19 | concurrence reply on Pharaoh]
even when Pharaoh hardens his own heart, that is not inconsistent with saying
that God is causing Pharaoh to do this, and thereby God is hardening the heart
of Pharaoh.

[export p. 19 | intensifying reply]
if someone would object that God is just intensifying the evil desires and
choices that were already in Pharaoh’s heart, then this kind of action could
still in theory at least cover all the evil in the world today
