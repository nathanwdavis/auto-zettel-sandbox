Addressing the People of the Scripture, the Qur'an says that the God in
question is the same one, and says that their doctrine about him is wrong, in
the same breath and sometimes in the same sentence. It does not treat the two
as competing answers.

The co-reference is stated flatly. Muslims are told to say to the People of
the Scripture "our Allah and your Allah is One, and unto Him we surrender"
(29:46), and elsewhere to ask them "Dispute ye with us concerning Allah when
He is our Lord and your Lord?" (2:139). The possessive is doing the work in
both: *your* Lord, and He is the same one. The second verse goes further than
a bare identity claim -- it treats the shared referent as the *premise* that
makes the dispute odd, and then locates the residue elsewhere, in works
("Ours are our works and yours your works").

The difference is stated just as flatly, in the passages this repository
captured first: "say not 'Three'" and the warning against exaggeration in
religion (4:171), and the condemnation of those who say "Allah is the third of
three" (5:73). Nothing here is softened by 29:46, and 29:46 is not softened by
them.

Two things follow, and only these two.

**The reference question has an explicit Islamic answer, and it is yes.** That
is worth stating because the affirmative side of this dispute has, in this
repository until now, been carried by Christian and secular voices -- Nostra
Aetate, Pauw in the NPR piece, Saritoprak at second hand. The Qur'an makes the
claim in its own voice. Nostra Aetate's move is structurally the same one from
the other side: it affirms that Muslims adore the one God and records in the
same paragraph that they do not acknowledge Jesus as God (see
[[nostra-aetate--202608311924]]). Each text holds shared referent and unshared
description together; neither treats holding both as a difficulty.

**It does not follow that the Qur'an settles the question a modern disputant
is asking.** The verses are addresses -- instructions about what to say to the
People of the Scripture and how to argue with them -- issued in a seventh-century
setting to communities the text names. Reading "our Allah and your Allah is One"
as a metaphysical thesis about reference, rather than as the opening move of a
prescribed argument, is an interpretation, and it is this note's, not the
text's. What can be said without interpretation is narrower and still
load-bearing: no reading of the Qur'an can present it as answering the
reference question in the negative, because it answers it in the affirmative
in as many words.

A caveat about this note's own evidence. The three affirming verses were not in
this repository's original Qur'an capture, which held only the three passages
marking difference; they were added on 2026-09-03 in a second capture after the
gap-filling walk found the omission (see
[[quran-tawhid-passages--202608311923]]). A note built on a capture assembled to
answer one side of a question is worth exactly as much as the capture is
complete, and the repository's first one was not.
