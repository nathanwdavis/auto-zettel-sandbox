Ahrens argues that a *plan* and a *structure to work in* solve different
problems, and only one suits open-ended intellectual work. A plan is a
fixed sequence imposed on yourself in advance; keeping to it under changed
circumstances takes willpower, which is exactly why plans buckle in
research and writing, where each new insight can change what should happen
next. A structure you can trust does something else: because you know
nothing is being lost by switching tasks, you don't have to hold everything
in mind or push yourself through a sequence — you can drop one thread and
pick up another without spending willpower to do it.

This is why the slip-box is a *system*, not a filing habit layered on top of
willpower: its job is to make the switch costless, so effort goes to
[[atomic-notes-compound-over-time--202701010001]] rather than to
remembering what to do next.
