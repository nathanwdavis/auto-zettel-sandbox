The experimental literature on note-taking measures a different thing from what
slip-box advocates claim, on a different time scale, with a different outcome
in view — so it cannot confirm those claims and it cannot refute them either.
The gap is not a shortage of studies. It is a mismatch of construct.

Set the two side by side. What the experiments manipulate is **how a person
writes during a single lecture**, and what they score is **a test taken
shortly after**: Mueller and Oppenheimer compared longhand against typing and
measured conceptual questions; Morehead, Dunlosky and Rawson replicated that
and added a group who took no notes and a condition where students studied
their notes first
([[mueller-and-oppenheimer-attribute-the-longhand-advantage-to-not-transcribing--202609040200]],
[[morehead-dunlosky-and-rawson-report-a-direct-replication-that-did-not-hold--202609040205]]).
What this repository's slip-box notes claim is about something else entirely:
that value **accumulates**, that surprise arrives only past a **critical mass**
of accumulated notes, that a single idea per note is what makes a note reusable
in contexts unforeseen when it was written, that a structure worth trusting
changes what attention is spent on. Every one of those is a claim about a
corpus growing over years and a later self encountering it. Not one of them is
a claim about the minutes in which a note is written.

So the experiments do not test the slip-box, and no honest reading makes them.
The point of contact is narrower and stranger than it first looks. The single
claim in the base that the experiments actually bear on is not a claim about
slip-boxes at all — it is the house rule that a literature note must be written
in the reader's own words and must never paste the source's prose. That rule
is, near enough, the mechanism Mueller and Oppenheimer proposed: transcription
in place of reformulation is what costs. And the state of the evidence for it
is exactly as
[[a-failed-replication-leaves-the-mechanism-standing-and-the-effect-unproven--202609040210]]
describes — the proxy effect did not replicate, so the mechanism is neither
supported nor refuted. The one house practice with any experimental purchase
has that purchase held in suspension.

That is why the two links out of here to
[[atomic-notes-compound-over-time--202701010001]] and
[[a-slip-box-surprises-only-after-critical-mass--202609010113]] are typed
`contradicts`, which needs justifying because nothing measured contradicts what
they assert. What this note contradicts is not their content but their standing:
both are stated in this base as things that happen, and after this cycle the
base holds a peer-reviewed finding that the nearest measured neighbour of such
claims did not hold up — and that among the groups whose performance did not
consistently differ was one that took no notes at all. A reader arriving at
those notes should not be able to reach them without meeting this one. Both remain the base's position; neither is now the base's
*established* position.

Two things this note is careful not to say. It does not say the slip-box fails —
untested is not disproved, and the honest report of an untested practice is that
it is untested. And it does not stretch the note-taking findings to cover
ground they never touched: the no-notes result is a finding about one lecture
and one test, and reading it as evidence
that note-taking has no value would be the same error, run in the opposite
direction, as citing the 2014 headline alone.

What would actually close the gap is worth stating so a later run can look for
it rather than re-deriving it: a study that operationalises accumulation and
linking rather than the medium of writing, and that measures an outcome months
or years out rather than a post-lecture test. The general reading — the testing
effect, the generation effect, transactive memory — is named in inquiry
202609032123 as the direction to go, and this cycle read none of it. Until
somebody does, the finding is that almost nothing measures this — that is this
note's own phrasing, not anyone's quoted words — and it is a real finding
rather than a failed cycle: the base's largest claims about its own method now
rest on the strongest available evidence being about something else.

**Strengthened 2026-09-04 by the classroom review**, which shows that for one
of those three directions the mismatch is not an accident of what got studied
but a screening rule: the retrieval-practice literature defines its own
intervention as retrieval *without* notes or external aids, so it will not come
to bear on a slip-box however much it grows
([[the-classroom-retrieval-literature-excludes-notes-by-its-own-inclusion-criteria--202609040420]]).
*Nobody has measured this yet* and *this field has ruled the measurement out of
scope* call for different responses, and the second is now established for the
retrieval branch.
