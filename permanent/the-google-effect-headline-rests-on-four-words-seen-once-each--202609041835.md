The result that named the Google effect is an interaction reported as
`F(1,66) = 5.02, P < 0.03`. It was computed over four of the experiment's
twenty-four words — Google and Yahoo against Target and Nike — chosen after the
data were in, and, because the experiment deliberately avoided repeating words,
each of those four contributed exactly one reaction time per participant
([[hesselmann-failed-to-recover-the-priming-effect-and-audited-the-original--202609041815]]).

Each element of that is ordinary on its own and ruinous together. A post-hoc
subset is a researcher degree of freedom: the audit's judgement is that
"without further information, it remains possible that the choice was made
after seeing the data", and that the authors "might have capitalized on chance
by selecting a subset of words". A single trial per word per participant is a
measurement with almost no precision. Selecting the best-looking cells out of a
noisy matrix and then testing them is a procedure that manufactures
significance from nothing, and the auditor makes the point by turning it on his
own null data: "we might find a 'Wikipedia Stroop effect' or 'Firefox Stroop
effect' in our data set."

Two further discrepancies in the same paper, from the same audit, and they
compound rather than repeat. Its degrees of freedom fit neither of its own
stated sample sizes — the supplement says forty-six participants, the article
reports `t(68)`, and the audit's finding is that "the reported dfs are
incorrect in both scenarios". And its supplement claims that *removing* the
four privileged words leaves an interaction of `F(1, 66) = 9.49` — larger than
the `5.52` it attributes to the subset — which the audit calls "difficult to
reconcile with the reported data" and then declines to settle, because settling
it would need the original data. That refusal is the right one and worth
imitating: a discrepancy identified is a finding, and a discrepancy explained
without the data is a guess.

What this licenses is a claim about warrant, not about honesty. Nothing above
shows that anyone fabricated anything, and this note does not say so. It shows
that the headline number carries far less evidential weight than its `P < 0.03`
advertises, which is a separate matter from how it came to be reported. That
distinction is why this repository can record the audit without accusing
anybody, and it is the reason the audit itself is worth more here than the null
result it accompanies: a failed replication tells you the effect did not
reappear, while an audit tells you how little there was to reappear.

This is the direct support for treating the whole paper as a weak report rather
than a Science result with one bad experiment
([[the-google-effects-replication-record-reaches-only-its-priming-experiment--202609041820]]).
A reporting standard is a property of a paper, not of an experiment: if the
degrees of freedom in Experiment 1 cannot be reconciled with its own
supplement, the sample sizes given for Experiments 2 through 4 in the same
supplement are being read on trust — and this repository has not read that
supplement, which is stated here rather than left to be discovered.
