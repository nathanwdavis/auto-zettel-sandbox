A dissent can reject an argument and keep its conclusion, and that is what
happens here. It is worth isolating, because the base's summary of the Imami
position would otherwise swing further than the sources do.

**The classical route was *naskh*.** Q 2:221 or Q 60:10 abrogates Q 5:5, on
reports from al-Baqir and al-Sadiq, so that what Q 5:5 permits is cancelled —
except that what is actually forbidden is `نكاح الدوام`, the permanent contract,
with *mut'a* and concubinage left standing
([[the-imami-commentaries-claim-the-strict-reading-of-2-221-as-their-own-and-run-the-abrogation-backwards--202609041445]]).

**Al-Tabataba'i destroys the route and rebuilds the destination.** He calls the
abrogation `فساد` and blocks it in both directions on chronology. But he then
offers the strict rule a different foundation:

> نعم لو قيل بكون قوله { ولا تمسكوا بعصم الكوافر } الآية مخصصاً متقدماً خرج به
> النكاح الدائم من إطلاق قوله { والمحصنات من الذين أُوتوا الكتاب من قبلكم }

yes — if Q 60:10 is said to be a *prior specifier*, permanent marriage is thereby
excluded from the generality of "and the chaste women of those given the Book
before you."

Same line, different instrument. *Takhsis*, specification, where the classical
commentaries used *naskh*. And it is available to him precisely because he has
just argued that "abrogation" in the Qur'an's own idiom `يعم غير النسخ المصطلح
كالتخصيص` — covers more than technical abrogation, specification included. The
chronological objection that killed *naskh* does not touch *takhsis*: a specifier
may precede what it specifies, where an abrogator may not.

**The surviving permission is not theoretical.** He grounds it in what the school
has actually done. The report permitting *mut'a* with a *kitabiyya* is itself his
evidence that Q 5:5 stands unabrogated — `وقد عمل بها الأصحاب`, and the
companions of the school have acted on it — cross-referenced to his own treatment
of Q 4:24, `أن المتعة نكاح كالنكاح الدائم`, that *mut'a* is a marriage like
permanent marriage. Read on its own terms and before any specifier, Q 5:5 for him
licenses both: `من غير شرط آخر من نكاح دوام أو انقطاع`.

**Why this is the note and not a footnote.** Three cycles of this base have now
found the same shape from three directions: what the Imami tradition restricts is
the *contract*, not the association. Al-Tusi and al-Tabrisi restrict it by
abrogation; al-Tabataba'i, having refused abrogation, restricts it by
specification and by a report he holds the school practises. The rule is more
stable than any of the arguments offered for it — which is a reason to state the
base's finding as being about the rule, and to treat the *naskh* claim as one
tradition's machinery for reaching it rather than as the position itself.

**And it sharpens what the outside summary lost.** Al-Sabuni's clause reports the
Imamiyya as holding the People of the Book to be *mushrikun* and marriage to them
forbidden. Neither half survives contact with al-Mizan intact: al-Tabataba'i
denies the naming, and what he restricts is one contract out of three available
routes to the same women. The report was not false about the outcome for permanent
marriage. It was false about everything that makes the outcome intelligible.

**What limits this note.** It rests on one commentator read on two verses from a
single host, and on his own cross-references to parts of *al-Mizan* this base has
not captured (his treatment of Q 4:24 and of the abrogation verse in al-Baqara).
The claim that the school *practises* the *mut'a* permission is his report of the
`أصحاب`, not something verified here, and he defers the operative rule to *fiqh*
twice over. What is established is the structure of his argument, not the state of
Imami law.
