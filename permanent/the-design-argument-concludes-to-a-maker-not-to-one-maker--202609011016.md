Paley's own statement of the watch inference is explicitly indifferent to
number: the watch "must have had a maker: that there must have existed, at
some time, and at some place or other, an artificer or artificers who
formed it" (see [[paley-natural-theology--202609011004]], ch. 1). The
plural is not carelessness -- the inference runs from observed contrivance
to the existence of contriving intelligence, and nothing in the marks of
contrivance fixes how many contrivers there were, or which candidate is
the one a tradition worships. The SEP survey's framing preserves the same
gap: the phenomena incline people to see "a deliberative and directive
mind behind those phenomena", which is "typically taken to be
supernatural" -- typically, because the inference itself does not force it
-- and the entry makes the gap explicit: "A standard but separable second
step—the natural theology step—involves identifying the designer as God"
(see
[[ratzsch-koperski-teleological-arguments-for-gods-existence--202609011003]],
preamble and sec. 2).

This puts design arguments on the opposite side of a divide from Anselm's
formula. The greatest-conceivable description carries uniqueness in its
content -- at most one bearer, so worshippers who aim by it co-refer (see
[[maximal-greatness-has-at-most-one-bearer-so-perfect-being-worshippers-co-refer--202609010841]]).
A designer identified only as *whatever made this* carries no such
guarantee: identifying the designer with the God of a creed -- or with the
unique God of perfect-being theology -- is a further step the teleological
argument does not perform, exactly parallel to the further step Anselm
takes in prayer (see
[[anselms-formula-identifies-god-by-description-alone--202609010840]]).
Natural-theology arguments therefore differ not just in strength but in
what they deliver for the reference question: Anselm's route
under-determines existence but secures uniqueness; the design route argues
for existence but leaves uniqueness -- and identification -- open.
