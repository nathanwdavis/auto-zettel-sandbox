The passages that most plainly separate sin done from sin as a condition use
one figure, source and product, in four forms.

The tree and its fruit: "a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit" (Matt
7:17), and "the tree is known by his fruit" (Matt 12:33). The heart and what
leaves it: "out of the heart proceed evil thoughts, murders, adulteries,
fornications, thefts, false witness, blasphemies" (Matt 15:19), a list of
deeds with the heart as their origin. The flesh and its works: "the flesh
lusteth against the Spirit" (Gal 5:17), and then "the works of the flesh are
manifest" (5:19), followed by a list of acts. Desire and its offspring: "when
lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin" (Jas 1:15). In each the outward
sin is the second term and something prior and inward is the first. See
[[the-new-testament-passages-derive-sinful-deeds-from-a--202610032107]].

The Old Testament places the first term in the heart in the same way: "the
imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth" (Gen 8:21); see
[[the-old-testament-passages-place-sinfulness-at-conception--202610032106]].

The Westminster Confession turns the figure into doctrine. "From this original
corruption", it says, "do proceed all actual transgressions" (6.4), and its
proof texts for that clause are Matthew 15:19, Ephesians 2:2-3 and James
1:14-15, two of them from the passages above
([[the-westminster-confession-derives-all-actual--202610032108]]). The tree
sayings of Matthew 7 and 12 and the list in Galatians 5:19-21 are not among
its proofs for that clause; reading them as nature and deed is this note's
grouping, not the Confession's.
