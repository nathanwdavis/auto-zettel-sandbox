Because compound growth accelerates late — the curve is flattest at the
start and steepest beyond most planning horizons — the agents best placed to
harvest it are the ones that outlive a human career: endowments, states,
long-lived institutions. An individual mostly experiences the slow early
segment; an entity that persists for centuries collects the segment that
"mock[s] all the powers of the imagination."

The point is as old as the compound-interest literature itself: Price's 1772
penny illustration was built to carry exactly this institutional moral —
individual lives are "confined within limits so narrow" to profit much,
while 50–100 years is "little in the duration of kingdoms," so a state
sinking fund can compound where a person cannot
([[price-contrasts-compound-and-simple-interest-with-one-penny--202609010204]],
[[price-an-appeal-to-the-public-on-the-national-debt--202609010203]]).

This sharpens [[time-horizon-dominates-rate--202701010013]]: if horizon
dominates rate, then differences in feasible horizon — not skill or rate —
predict who benefits most from the mechanism in
[[reinvested-returns-compound--202701010011]]. It is also the financial twin
of the slip box's threshold regime
([[a-slip-box-surprises-only-after-critical-mass--202609010113]]): both
systems are back-loaded, spending years looking like mere containers of what
was put in before the feedback loop becomes visible.
