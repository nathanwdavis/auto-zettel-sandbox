Before the doctrine of middle knowledge is a page old, Molina forestalls the
reading that it makes God a spectator of free choices. "Ne vero primo suo
aspectu te haec doctrina perturbet," he writes, and lists what "apertissime
inter se convenire et cohaerere": that "Nihil esse in potestate creaturae,
quod etiam non sit in potestate Dei"; that "Deum sua omnipotentia inflectere
posse nostrum liberum arbitrium quocunque ipse voluerit, praeterquam in
peccatum," since bending it into sin "contradictionem implicat"; that God can
do by himself whatever he does through a secondary cause; that "Deum
permittere posse peccata, non vero praecipere, aut ad ea incitare vel
inclinare"; and then the reversal of dependence recorded in
[[molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101]].
On p. 324 the causal claim turns positive. God is the cause of what free
creatures do, remotely through both natural and middle knowledge and
proximately "per determinationem suae voluntatis, qua statuens collocare
homines et angelos in eo ordine rerum in quo eos collocavit, simul statuit
hoc vel illo modo cum libero arbitrio illorum cooperari" — with one
exclusion, "Ne tamen intelligas causam ullo modo esse peccatorum," because
fault and defect "in solum arbitrium creatam tanquam in causam reducuntur"
([[molina-distinguishes-three-knowledges-and-makes-the-middle-one-depend-on-the-creature--202609090055]],
from [[molina-concordia-liberi-arbitrii--202609090050]]).

Three of those items are what a Reformed reader expects to find in a
Reformed text. Grudem's providence chapter has God as "the 'primary cause'
that plans and initiates everything that happens"
([[concurrence-is-the-mechanism-the-wisdom-section-assumed--202609081826]]),
and his section on God's will, in the breath that affirms the decree over the
crucifixion, refuses to "imply that we are freed from responsibility for evil
or that God is ever to be blamed for sin"
([[grudem-splits-gods-will-into-necessary-free-secret-and-revealed--202609081904]],
from [[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]]). Molina's fourth item is that asymmetry in a Jesuit hand:
permit, never command or incite. His first item is stronger than anything in
the Arminian position Grudem surveys; Cottrell, as Grudem quotes him, denies
that God has "a specific, unconditional purpose for each discrete particle,
object, person, and event within the creation"
([[grudem-answers-the-arminian-by-comparing-unanswered-questions--202609081825]]),
whereas Molina says nothing is in the creature's power that is not in God's.
And the cooperation clause on p. 324 is concurrence under another name.

What the list does not contain is as telling. Molina's God can bend the will
"quocunque ipse voluerit," but the middle knowledge is a knowledge of what
the will would do *without* being bent, and the point of choosing an order
of things is that God reaches his ends through free acts he foresees rather
than acts he inflects. The affirmations are affirmations of power; the
providence actually described is providence by placement. Pohle's Thomists
press exactly this, that the Molinists "seriously circumscribe and diminish
the supremacy of the Creator over His creatures"
([[pohle-locates-the-molinist-thomist-dispute-in-the-medium-of-foreknowledge--202609090057]],
from [[pohle-molinism-catholic-encyclopedia--202609090052]]). The list shows that Molina does not concede the charge; it does
not show that he answers it.

**Limits.** The items are a summary Molina gives of results argued in
disputations 31, 47 and 48, none of which is on file, and the note reports
the summary. "Sovereignty" is this base's word; Molina's are *potestas* and
*omnipotentia*. The comparisons with Grudem and with Cottrell are this
base's, and Cottrell is known only as Grudem quotes him.
