Al-Sabuni's one clause — `وإلى هذا ذهب الإمامية، وبعض الزيدية`, the Imamiyya and
some Zaydis went to this — carries a qualifier on the Zaydis and none on the
Imamiyya. The 14:00Z cycle showed the qualifier was earned: the Zaydi corpus is
genuinely divided. This note is the other half of that result, and it runs the
other way. The Imami side needed a qualifier too, and did not get one.

**The classical commentaries hold the strict reading and say so.** That is
established and not disturbed here:
[[the-imami-commentaries-claim-the-strict-reading-of-2-221-as-their-own-and-run-the-abrogation-backwards--202609041445]].
Al-Tusi (d. 460/1067) and al-Tabrisi (d. 548/1153) mark it `عندنا` and `مذهبنا`,
and al-Tusi carries the inversion as *naskh* reports from al-Baqir and al-Sadiq.

**The major twentieth-century Imami commentary rejects it, and rejects it at the
root.**
Al-Tabataba'i (d. 1401/1981) in *al-Mizan* does not soften the position or
distinguish it away. He denies the premise the whole strict reading stands on:

> فقد ظهر من هذا البيان على طوله ان ظاهر الآية … قصر التحريم على المشركات
> والمشركين من الوثنيين دون أهل الكتاب

the apparent sense of the verse restricts the prohibition to the idolaters, not
to the People of the Book. And he names the abrogation claim as an error rather
than a minority view: `ومن هنا يظهر فساد القول بأن الآية ناسخة لآية المائدة` —
from this the *corruptness* appears of the claim that this verse abrogates the
verse of al-Ma'ida.

**He is answering his own school's Imams, not a Sunni objection.** The Imami
*naskh* reports are the load-bearing thing here, and he quotes them. Zurara in
*al-Kafi* and Mas'ada b. Sadaqa in al-'Ayyashi have al-Baqir say of Q 5:5
`منسوخة بقوله { ولا تمسكوا بعصم الكوافر }`, it is abrogated by Q 60:10; another
report abrogates it by Q 2:221. His answer is chronological and he applies it to
an Imam's report without flinching: `ولا يجوز تقدم الناسخ على المنسوخ` — the
abrogator may not precede the abrogated — al-Baqara being the first Medinan sura
and al-Ma'ida the last.

**So the disagreement is internal, dated and about grounds.** Not a modern
liberalisation of a classical rule: al-Tabataba'i disputes what `المشركات`
denotes in the Qur'an's own usage, and what the order of revelation permits
anyone to claim. Two Imami commentaries eight and nine centuries earlier read the
word one way; the twentieth-century one reads it the other, and says the first
reading cannot be got from the text.

**What this does to the base's claim.** "The Imamiyya hold the People of the Book
to be *mushrikun*" was already thinly sourced and is now positively too strong. A
sentence that named the classical Imami commentaries would be true; one that
names the school is not, unless it also says that a major twentieth-century
commentary of that school argues the opposite. This matters beyond bibliography, because the
finding those notes were built to support is a *sectarian reversal* — the Shi'i
commentaries glossing the shared-God clause where the Sunni pass over it, the
Shi'i jurists taking the stricter line on marriage. Half of that reversal is now
a statement about a period, not about a confession.

**The symmetry is the interesting part.** Both branches of Shi'ism the base has
read turn out to be internally divided on exactly this verse:
[[the-zaydi-tradition-is-split-on-2-221-so-the-qualifier-some-is-load-bearing--202609041455]]
found Zayd b. 'Ali's glossary lenient against al-A'qam's report of Yahya and
al-Qasim. Two traditions, two splits, and in both the outside summary flattened
them. The generalisation this suggests — and it is a suggestion, not a result —
is that a one-clause attribution of a legal position to a *school* is a claim
about a corpus that has not been read.

**What limits this note.** One commentator, one host, one work; *al-Mizan* is a
*tafsir* and not a legal compendium, and al-Tabataba'i twice defers the remainder
to *fiqh* (`وللكلام تتمة تطلب من الفقه`). It shows the tradition contains the
dissent; it does not establish which position Imami law operates. No Imami *fiqh*
text is in this base, which is the same gap the 13:00Z cycle filed and the 14:00Z
cycle could not close.

**Strengthened 2026-09-04 16:00Z, and its closing limit is discharged.** This
note ends by saying that *al-Mizan* is a *tafsir*, that it shows the tradition
contains the dissent but not which position Imami law operates, and that no Imami
*fiqh* text is in the base. The third of those is no longer true and the second
is answered as far as a legal commentary can answer it: al-Muhaqqiq's *Shara'i'*
states the question as `روایتان` rather than as a school position, and
al-Najafi's commentary on it calls permission `التحقيق`
([[an-imami-work-of-fiqh-holds-marriage-to-a-kitabiyya-permitted-so-the-imamiyya-names-a-party-not-the-school--202609041625]]).
The split this note found in the commentaries is therefore not a
classical-versus-modern divide, as its own framing suggested: it runs through the
legal literature too, in the century before al-Tabataba'i, with named jurists on
both sides.
