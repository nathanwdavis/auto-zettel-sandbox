The Qur'anic objection to trinitarian language is an objection to *shirk*,
association: 4:171 says "say not 'Three'" and immediately grounds it in "Allah
is only One Allah"; 5:73 condemns those who say "Allah is the third of three"
on the same ground, "when there is no Allah save the One Allah"
([[quran-tawhid-passages--202608311923]],
raw/202608311910-quran-tawhid-passages.txt).

Qur'an 3:64 — the verse *A Common Word Between Us and You* takes its title from
and builds its whole invitation on — contains that same clause. Its terms are
"that we shall worship none but God, and that we shall ascribe no partner unto
Him, and that none of us shall take others for lords beside God." The letter's
gloss makes the identification explicit: "the blessed words: *we shall ascribe
no partner unto Him* relate to the Unity of God"
([[a-common-word-between-us-and-you--202609032358]], part III;
[[a-common-word-grounds-common-ground-in-shared-commandment--202609040001]]).

So one clause does both jobs. Read as doctrine, "ascribe no partner unto Him" is
what rules trinitarian formulations out. Read as an invitation, it is the first
of the three things the letter asks Christians to come together with Muslims
*on*. The letter is not quoting a conciliatory verse and passing over a
polemical one; it is putting the anti-association clause itself forward as
common ground, and asking Christians to accept it as something they already
hold.

That is a sharper move than it looks, and it is why the same-God question is so
resistant. The Muslim invitation and the Muslim objection are not two attitudes
that might be weighed against each other — they are one sentence read twice.
Any Christian acceptance of the invitation on its own stated terms is acceptance
of the clause that, on the Qur'an's own reading of it at 4:171 and 5:73, its
trinitarian confession violates. The generosity is real and so is the demand;
they are not separable, and a reading that keeps only one of them has taken half
the verse.

This supports
[[abrahamic-monotheisms-divide-over-internal-distinction-not-oneness--202608311941]]
from the Muslim side and in the traditions' own words. What 3:64 offers is
oneness, which is not what divides; what the same clause withholds is internal
distinction within that oneness, which is. The line falls exactly where that
note puts it, and here a single Qur'anic phrase sits astride it.

A limit worth stating. The letter quotes 3:64 in its own rendering ("Come to a
common word between us and you"); Pickthall renders the same Arabic phrase
"Come to an agreement between us and you"
(raw/202609032356-quran-common-ground-passages.txt). Nothing in this note turns
on that difference — the clause at issue, "ascribe no partner unto Him," is the
same in both — but "a common word" is the letter's wording and should not be
cited as Qur'anic phrasing simpliciter.
