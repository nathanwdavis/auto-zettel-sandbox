When a direct replication fails to recover an effect, two different things
were on offer in the original paper and only one of them falls. The
*measurement* loses its warrant. The *mechanism* the measurement was offered as
evidence for is left where it was — neither supported nor refuted, which is a
worse position for anyone who wanted to cite it than being refuted, because it
looks like nothing has changed.

The worked case is the best-known experiment on note-taking. Mueller and
Oppenheimer reported that students taking notes by hand outperformed students
typing, on conceptual questions, across three studies, and they proposed a
reason: laptop note takers transcribe verbatim "rather than processing
information and reframing it in their own words," and it is the missing
processing that costs
([[mueller-and-oppenheimer-attribute-the-longhand-advantage-to-not-transcribing--202609040200]]).
Morehead, Dunlosky and Rawson ran a direct replication and did not recover it:
"performance did not consistently differ between any groups," and — the detail
that does most of the damage — that included a group who took no notes at all.
Their meta-analysis of direct replications revealed "small (nonsignificant)
effects favoring longhand," and their own verdict is that saying which method is
better "seems premature"
([[morehead-dunlosky-and-rawson-report-a-direct-replication-that-did-not-hold--202609040205]]).

Notice what that does and does not settle. Anyone who wanted the finding —
*write by hand and you will understand more* — no longer has it; the effect is
not established, and the honest report of the pair is that the question is
open. But the proposed mechanism was never the same proposition as the effect.
Transcribing instead of reformulating might well be worse for understanding
and simply not show up as a difference between two writing implements in a
single lecture, because the implement is a poor proxy for the behaviour: people
can copy by hand and reformulate on a keyboard. A null result on the proxy is
weak evidence about the process. So the mechanism keeps whatever independent
support it has and gains none from this pair.

The practical rule this yields is asymmetric, and it is the reason this note
exists rather than a note simply recording that a famous study failed to
replicate. **You may not cite the original for the effect. You may not cite the
replication against the mechanism.** A base that did the first would be
repeating a finding that no longer stands; a base that did the second would be
claiming a refutation nobody has performed.

This is the same shape as
[[design-arguments-can-confirm-belief-they-do-not-ground--202609011510]],
where a piece of evidence can raise the standing of a belief without being what
the belief rests on — and the two are `analogous` rather than one supporting
the other, because the domains share no premises. What they share is the
discipline of not letting the strength of one relation be borrowed for another.

A limit this note owns rather than hides: both papers behind it were read
**abstract only** — neither full text is reachable from this environment — so
the reasoning above is built on stated designs and stated conclusions, not on
either paper's internal argument. That is enough to support the asymmetry
claimed here, which turns on what a null result on a proxy can and cannot show,
and it is not enough to adjudicate between the two teams on any point of
method. A further gap, recorded because it bears directly: a corrigendum to
Mueller and Oppenheimer was published in 2018 and could not be read, so this
repository does not know whether the original's own account was later amended.
