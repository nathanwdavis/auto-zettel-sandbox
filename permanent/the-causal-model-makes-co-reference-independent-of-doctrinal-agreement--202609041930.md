The same-God dispute assumes that settling co-reference takes *some* shared
content, and then argues about how much. On the causal model of reference it
takes none.

The model has two parts (see
[[michaelson-sets-out-four-models-of-reference-fixing-and-declines-to-make-them-exclusive--202609041926]]).
Reference is fixed once, by a dubbing. Thereafter it is *borrowed*: a later
speaker refers because "underlying their uses of the name are links in a causal
chain stretching back to the initial dubbing of the object with that name." The
survey states the consequence in the theory's own terms -- a name refers to what
it is linked to "in a way that does not require speakers to associate any
identifying descriptive content whatsoever with the name" -- and adds that
"borrowers needn't be able to identify any of the lenders they are in fact
relying on."

Apply that to the dispute and the usual moves stop reaching. If Jews, Christians
and Muslims stand in one chain of use running back to a single dubbing, they
co-refer, and it does not matter that one party says God is tri-personal, another
that He admits no composition, and a third that associating partners with Him is
the one unforgivable sin. Those are facts about the dossiers, and on this model
dossiers are not what refers. Equally, if the chains are *distinct* — if the name
was fixed twice, on two occasions, by two acts — then no quantity of shared
description makes the users co-refer, however exalted the description is.

That is a sharper result than the dispute's own vocabulary can express, and it
cuts against the Anselmian route in
[[anselmian-co-reference-holds-only-if-the-description-does-the-referring--202609010842]]
from a direction that note does not consider. That note asks whether the
description does the referring. The causal model answers that for names, no
description ever does — not even a uniqueness-enforcing one — because reference
was fixed by an event, not by a specification.

**What this does not settle.** Whether the Abrahamic chains *are* one chain is a
historical question about transmission, and nothing in this repository has yet
asked it. The model relocates the same-God question rather than answering it: it
says the answer lies in the history of a name's use, not in the content of a
creed.
