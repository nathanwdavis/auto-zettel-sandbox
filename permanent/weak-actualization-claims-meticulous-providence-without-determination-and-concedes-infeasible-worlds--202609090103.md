Middle knowledge is not yet a doctrine of providence; it becomes one when God
uses it to choose. In Molina the thing chosen is an order of things. Middle
knowledge was necessary so that, "ex hypothesi quod hunc vel illum ordinem
rerum vellet efficere, certo praevideret ea omnia quae pro libertate
arbitrii, tam angelici quam humani, in unoquoque eorum ordinum essent
futura," and God then, "statuens collocare homines et angelos in eo ordine
rerum in quo eos collocavit, simul statuit hoc vel illo modo cum libero
arbitrio illorum cooperari"
([[molina-distinguishes-three-knowledges-and-makes-the-middle-one-depend-on-the-creature--202609090055]],
from [[molina-concordia-liberi-arbitrii--202609090050]]). The unit of choice is the whole order, and providence over free
will is exercised by placement and response — "nunc hominem variis eventibus
exercendo, nunc defectus illius tolerando et permittendo nunc illum vocando,
eique opitulando" — not by determining the act. Laing's survey supplies the
contemporary vocabulary. "God can actualize a world where His will is brought
about by the free decisions of creatures," and the actualizing is weak:
"weak actualization refers to the contribution of a being to the occurrence
of an event by placement of a free creature in circumstances in which he will
freely cause the event," as against strong actualization, in which a being
"causally determines the occurrence of an event"
([[laing-defines-middle-knowledge-as-prevolitional-knowledge-of-counterfactuals-of-freedom--202609090056]],
from [[laing-middle-knowledge-iep--202609090051]]).

That is why the objection this base holds from Grudem does not reach Molina.
Grudem argues that "every situation in which people find themselves is itself
the result of thousands, in fact millions, of individual human choices," so
that for God to "bring about a situation" those "millions of prior human
choices would have to be decided in a certain way"
([[a-situation-is-made-of-other-peoples-choices--202609081830]],
from [[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]]). The objection assumes a God who assembles circumstances
piecemeal for one creature. A God who selects an entire order of things,
foreseeing every free act in it, assembles nothing; the millions of prior
choices are part of what is foreseen, not something done to produce the
situation. The note carrying the objection already said this in its limits,
by inference; the two Molinist sources now say it in their own terms.

But the same sources concede where the pressure lies. Laing states it in his
own voice: weak actualization "implies that there may be some states of
affairs that God cannot weakly actualize, which leads to the further
conclusion that there may be some possible worlds that God cannot actualize."
Molina had said the equivalent of the knowledge: "in potestate Dei non fuit
scire per eam scientiam aliud quam re ipsa sciverit." God's options are
bounded by counterfactuals he does not author. That is the honest form of
Grudem's complaint that the Arminian residue "tends to diminish the greatness
of God—his omniscience, his omnipotence, and the absolute reliability of his
promises for the future"
([[the-dispute-is-decided-by-which-residue-honours-god-more--202609081831]]).
It lands not on the mechanics of bringing about a situation but on
feasibility: on Molinism there are worlds God might have preferred and cannot
have. Molina's reply is the list of what coheres
([[molinas-own-list-of-what-coheres-keeps-sovereignty-whole--202609090102]]);
Laing's, to the parallel objection that middle knowledge removes all divine
risk, is that the objection "begs the question of compatibilism."

**Limits.** Molina's *ordo rerum* and the contemporary possible world are not
the same concept, and glossing the first by the second is an alignment this
base makes; Molina's orders are orders of circumstances and helps, not
maximal states of affairs. Whether Grudem's objection also fails against
Craig's own formulation is not settled here, since Craig is unread and Grudem
quotes him from one essay. Laing's concession is stated as a possibility,
"there may be," and is reported at that strength.
