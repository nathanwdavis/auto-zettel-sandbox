This base can say *that* retrieval beats re-exposure
([[retrieval-not-re-exposure-is-what-makes-learning-last--202609040340]]) and
that it beats even an active elaborative rival
([[retrieval-practice-beats-not-only-rereading-but-concept-mapping-an-active-elaborative-rival--202609042200]]),
but everything it holds is about the size of the effect, not its cause. Pyc and
Rawson supply a candidate cause. Their **mediator effectiveness hypothesis** says
that a test-plus-restudy schedule improves later memory because it changes the
*mediators* the learner forms — the intermediate links between a cue and its
target (for a foreign vocabulary pair, a keyword that sounds like the cue and
points to the meaning) — and it names exactly two properties that a good mediator
must have: it must be retrievable at test time, and, once retrieved, it must
elicit the target ([[pyc-and-rawson-why-testing-improves-memory-mediator-effectiveness-hypothesis--202609042320]]).

The claim earns its place because it is a mechanism rather than a restatement of
the effect. "Testing helps" is compatible with almost any story; "testing helps
by making the cue-to-target link both more recallable and more diagnostic" is
falsifiable, because either component could fail to move. Pyc and Rawson report
that test-restudy practice improved *both* — mediators generated under
test-restudy were more likely to be retrieved and more likely to elicit the
target than those generated under restudy alone — which is why they read the
benefit as located in the quality of the link the learner builds, not in an
unanalysed property of "testing."

**Two boundaries to carry with the claim.** First, it is a hypothesis about a
paradigm this base otherwise does not hold: paired-associate foreign-vocabulary
learning with learner-reported keyword mediators. Whether the same account
explains retrieval gains for connected prose — the material the rest of this
cluster measures — is not something the paper establishes. Second, this note
rests on the paper's authoritative abstract, not its full text, which was closed
access; the abstract fixes the hypothesis and the direction of both components
but not their magnitudes. And note the mechanism is a *separate* thing from the
one comparison the Social Sciences Replication Project later tested, which
isolated mediator recall and says nothing about whether those mediators elicited
their targets ([[the-one-pyc-and-rawson-result-the-ssrp-tested-replicated-only-after-the-second-stage-sample--202609042345]]).
