The owner's account has a cell it calls *incomplete understanding*: a question held, an object in view, an answer that does not exhaust it. Question 12 of the *Summa* gives that cell a definition and then shows it operating at every level of creaturely knowledge, up to and including the sight of God's essence ([[aquinas-separates-seeing-god-from-comprehending-him-denies--202609151906]]).

**The definition.** What is comprehended is known as far as it can be known; God is infinitely knowable and no created light of glory is infinite; so the blessed see God and do not comprehend him. The reply that states this is the one to keep: "God is called incomprehensible not because anything of Him is not seen; but because He is not seen as perfectly as He is capable of being seen" (a.7 ad 2). Incomprehensibility is a limit on the *mode* of the knower, not a part of the object held back. The illustration is a theorem held on probable opinion: nothing of it is missing, and it is still not comprehended.

**The ladder.** Four rungs, each bounded the same way. Natural reason reaches "whether He exists" and what must belong to a first cause, not what he is (a.12). Grace in this life adds effects and truths reason cannot reach, "as, for instance, that God is Three and One", and still leaves the knower "united to Him as to one unknown" (a.13). The beatific vision sees the essence whole and does not comprehend it (a.7), and does not see in it everything God does or can do (a.8). Only God's own knowledge is unbounded. The deficit at each rung is described identically: not that a portion is withheld but that the knower's capacity falls short of the object's knowability.

**What stays with God even in the vision.** Article 8 supplies the one place where Aquinas names content rather than mode. The angels see the essence and yet "are ignorant of future contingent things, and of secret thoughts; for this knowledge belongs to God alone". Those two classes, what free creatures will do and what they are thinking, are the hidden tier that survives the beatific vision, and they are, not by accident, the two things the foreknowledge debate in this base has been about ([[the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914]]).

**What this does to the account.** It splits the owner's *incomplete understanding* into two kinds that the account runs together. One is incompleteness by mode (a.7): the whole object in view, the knower's grasp of it partial, and no proposition missing. The other is withheld particulars (a.8): items God knows and no creature sees, even in the vision. The first is not a mystery in the sense of a missing answer at all; it is the ordinary condition of a finite knower before an infinite object, and it does not lift in the life to come. The second is a genuine hidden remainder, and Aquinas gives it content. The account's cell should probably be two cells.

**Bearing on what the base already held.** The thinness of natural knowledge ([[natural-knowledge-of-god-is-thin-on-both-routes-for-the-same-reason--202609011042]]) is Aquinas's lowest rung, reason knowing that God is and not what he is; the sentence that note quotes from Calvin, "His essence indeed is incomprehensible", is what a.7 defines. And the incomprehensibility Maimonides appeals to for God's knowing is, in Aquinas's terms, incompleteness by mode, which is why it can be conceded without conceding that any proposition about God is false or absent.

**Limits.** Six articles of thirteen; the Latin is not on file and "comprehend" is used throughout in Aquinas's strict sense. The reading of a.8's two classes as the survivors of the beatific vision is this base's, though it is what the *sed contra* says of the angels.
