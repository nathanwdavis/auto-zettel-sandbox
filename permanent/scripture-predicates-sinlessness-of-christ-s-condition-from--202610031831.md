The New Testament does more than report that Christ committed no sin, though
it says that too: he "did no sin" (1 Pet 2:22). It describes a condition. The
child is "that holy thing which shall be born of thee" (Luke 1:35), holy before
any act of his. He "knew no sin" (2 Cor 5:21); "in him is no sin" (1 John
3:5); he is "holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners" (Heb 7:26); the
prince of this world "hath nothing in me" (John 14:30). See
[[the-new-testament-passages-call-christ-holy-from-birth-and--202610031824]].

Where the same texts join him to our nature they choose their words. God sent
his Son "in the likeness of sinful flesh" (Rom 8:3). He was made like his
brethren "in all things" (Heb 2:17) and "was in all points tempted like as we
are, yet without sin" (Heb 4:15). Set beside "in sin did my mother conceive
me" (Ps 51:5) and "for that all have sinned" (Rom 5:12), the passages place
Christ outside the inheritance as well as outside the record of acts.

They do not name a mechanism. The words original sin and sin nature, and the
question of how the inheritance was cut off in his case, belong to later
theology, which answers it in more than one way
([[augustine-and-aquinas-block-original-sin-at-the-manner-of--202610031828]]).
