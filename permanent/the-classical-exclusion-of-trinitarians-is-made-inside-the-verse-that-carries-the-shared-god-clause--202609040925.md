Q 29:46 exempts from its gentler manner of argument "those of them who do
wrong", and al-Razi reads that exception as `إلّا الَّذِينَ أشْرَكُوا مِنهم
بِإثْباتِ الوَلَدِ لِلَّهِ والقَوْلِ بِثالِثِ ثَلاثَةٍ` — except those of them
who associated partners by asserting a son for God and by the doctrine of a
third of three. The trinitarian is excluded by the same verse whose next clause
says that our God and your God is one.

**This is the tension the base had recorded as unresolved, resolved from inside
tafsir.** Every commentary captured on this verse attaches the *jizya* and the
sword to it without registering any friction with the shared-God clause, and the
base filed the juristic question — whether Christians count as *mushrikun* — as
the structural gap that would decide it. al-Razi does not answer the juristic
question, but he does something the others do not: he puts the exclusion and the
shared-God clause in the same sentence-sequence, and he does not notice a
problem. The two sit consecutively in his comment: those who say "third of
three" have committed association, `لِأنَّ الشِّرْكَ ظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌ` because
association is a great wrong; then God set out the better way "putting their
merits first" with the words that include `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد`.

**The exclusion is doctrinal, not behavioural, and that is what makes it
sharp.** Other commentators read "those who do wrong" as those who took up arms
or refused the *jizya* — a fact about conduct, which leaves the peaceful
Christian inside the gentler clause. al-Razi's reading turns on what is
believed. On his construction the party excluded from the better-way command is
picked out by the content of its Christology.

**How he gets to association, and where his premise comes from.** He does not
argue from the word "three" alone. He argues that the hypostases must be
independent essences, because the hypostasis of the Word is said to have clothed
itself in the humanity of the Messiah and the hypostasis of the Holy Spirit in
the humanity of Mary — which could not happen to what is not independent —
`ولَمّا أثْبَتُوا ذَواتًا ثَلاثَةً مُسْتَقِلَّةً فَقَدْ أشْرَكُوا`, and since
they affirmed three independent essences, they have committed association.

**A limit that has to be stated in the same breath.** That premise is al-Razi's
description of Christian doctrine, not a Christian statement of it. The
incarnation of the Holy Spirit in Mary is not asserted by the tri-personal
account this base holds from inside the tradition
([[evangelical-theology-identifies-god-by-a-tri-personal-life--202608311940]]),
which distinguishes persons without separable essences. Whether his conclusion
survives a description its target would accept is a further question, and this
note does not settle it — it records that the classical verdict, as argued here,
runs through a premise about the hypostases that the base has reason to think
misdescribes them.

**What this does to the earlier note.** It supplies from a fifth commentary the
same pattern
[[four-commentaries-read-the-common-word-as-a-summons-and-two-name-the-cross-among-its-exclusions--202609040830]]
found in the 3:64 material: where the classical tradition takes up the relation
between the two parties, it does so by naming what must be given up. It also
takes the doctrinal classification of trinitarian predication as *shirk* out of
the category of this repository's own gloss — the 08:00Z cycle's critic flagged
it as a claim with no source in the base — and grounds it in a classical text
that states it in as many words.
