Asking what role faith plays in miracles usually assumes faith does
something causal: the worker's faith releases the power, or the crowd's
unbelief blocks it. Aquinas grants the scriptural texts behind both
versions, 1 Cor 13:2 on the worker's faith and Matt 13:58 on the hearers'
unbelief. He then gives exactly two reasons why "the working of miracles is
ascribed to faith", and neither is causal. A miracle is "directed to the
confirmation of faith", and it "proceeds from God's omnipotence on which
faith relies" (ST II-II q.178, a.1 ad 5, in
[[aquinas-ascribes-miracles-to-faith-as-their-end-and-their--202609230042]]).
Faith is the miracle's goal and shares its object. It is not the miracle's
engine.

The rest of the question closes off the causal reading. Omnipotence "cannot
be communicated to any creature", so no human disposition, faith included,
can be the principle of a miracle. God is always "the principal worker" and
uses the human being as an instrument (a.1 ad 1). The strongest evidence is
Aquinas's answer about sinners: God can work a true miracle through someone
whose faith is dead, "as a man through a stick" (a.2 ad 2). If the quality
of the worker's faith were what produced miracles, dead faith could not
serve. Because the faith is only an instrument, it can.

This fits the confirming role the base already records for arguments to
God. Aquinas models miracles on natural theology: as "natural effects" lead
reason to "some knowledge of God", supernatural effects bring a person "to a
certain degree of supernatural knowledge of the objects of faith" (a.1 co.;
compare [[aquinas-makes-god-demonstrable-only-from-effects--202609011206]]).
In both cases the effect's job is to confirm, which is the same office
Plantinga assigns design arguments
([[design-arguments-can-confirm-belief-they-do-not-ground--202609011510]]).
Whether Aquinas would also deny that a miracle *grounds* faith is not
settled by q.178, which says only that the word "needs to be confirmed in
order that it be rendered credible". The grace of miracles is needed
alongside the grace of faith "that people may be confirmed in their faith".
