al-Zamakhshari glosses `سواء بيننا وبينكم` at Q 3:64 as
`مستوية بيننا وبينكم، لا يختلف فيها القرآن والتوراة والإنجيل` — even between us
and you; the Qur'an, the Torah and the Gospel do not differ over it. No other
commentary in this base states inter-scriptural agreement that plainly. And in
the next sentence he defines the word as a summons to stop saying that the
Messiah is the son of God. Both are the same reading, and the second is what the
first is about.

**The distinction the passage forces.** What is said to be undisputed across the
three scriptures is *the word* — the content that follows the clause: worship
none but God, associate nothing with Him, take not one another as lords. What is
not said to be shared is the parties' present position on it, and the invitation
is worded `تعالوا إليها حتى لا نقول: عزيز ابن الله، ولا المسيح ابن الله`, come
to it *so that* we may not say "'Uzayr is the son of God" nor "the Messiah is
the son of God". A word all three books agree on, which one party is being
summoned to come to, is not ground the parties are standing on.

**Why this sharpens rather than softens the summons reading.** It would be easy
to read this commentator as the counter-example — the one who does say the
traditions agree — and the sentence is genuinely the base's strongest classical
statement of common ground. But he places the agreement in the scriptures'
content, not in the disputants, and the same paragraph names the Christian
confession of sonship as what the invitation exists to end. He then glosses "if
they turn away" as turning away *from tawhid*, and "bear witness that we are
submitted" as what
`لزمتكم الحجة` — the proof has bound you — obliges the other party to concede,
`كما يقول الغالب للمغلوب`, as the victor says to the vanquished. His alternative
reading is harsher still: bear witness that you are unbelievers.

**What this adds to the base's reading.** The summons reading now has its
hardest case worked through: the commentator who most nearly says that the three
scriptures share this word is also explicit that coming to it costs the addressee
a doctrine. The commonality and the demand are not in tension in his text; the
demand is what he thinks the commonality consists in. Any argument that reads
Q 3:64 as recording shared ground has to answer him, not only the commentators
who never mention agreement at all.
