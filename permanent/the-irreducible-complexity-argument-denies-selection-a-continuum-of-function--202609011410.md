Behe's irreducible-complexity argument has a precise logical shape: natural
selection, on his statement of it, "requires a continuum of function to
work", and an irreducibly complex system -- one whose "removal of any one of
the parts causes the system to effectively cease functioning" -- is defined
so that no such continuum can reach it, since "any precursor to an
irreducibly complex system is by definition nonfunctional" (see
[[behe-molecular-machines--202609011400]], as summarized in
[[behe-grounds-the-design-inference-in-irreducible-molecular-complexity--202609011405]]).
The argument therefore stands or falls with a single empirical question:
whether the parts of a candidate system (Behe's flagship is the cilium)
really have no selectable function short of the assembled whole. Everything
else -- the mousetrap, the design definition as "the purposeful arrangement
of parts" -- is illustration.

Placed beside the other design arguments in this repository, that shape is
distinctive for *where* it claims selection fails. Meyer's information
argument retreats to before self-replication exists, where selection is
unavailable by definition, and the Venema-Meyer exchange accordingly turns
on where selection begins (see
[[the-information-design-argument-turns-on-where-selection-begins--202609011320]]).
Behe claims territory selection already occupies: living biochemistry, where
the mechanism is present and operating, but -- he argues -- powerless for
this class of system because the functional gradient it needs is absent.
That makes his version the bolder and the more exposed claim: a single
demonstrated functional intermediate (a working subset of the parts, or a
co-opted earlier function) breaks the argument for that system, which is
exactly the ground on which Venema's within-biology counterexamples to
information-pessimism operate, and which Meyer conspicuously declined to
defend. It is also a return move in the historical drift this repository
records elsewhere: where fine-tuning relocated the design datum upstream of
selection into physics (see
[[the-design-arguments-center-of-gravity-moved-from-organisms-to-physics--202609011015]]),
Behe moves it *down* a level instead -- from Paley's organs to single
molecules, betting that at the bottom, with "no more black boxes to
invoke", the gradualist explanation runs out of room rather than out of
scope.
