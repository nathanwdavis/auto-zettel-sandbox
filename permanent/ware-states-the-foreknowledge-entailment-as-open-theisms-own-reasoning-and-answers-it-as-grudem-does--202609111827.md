The one text of Ware's on file in his own words states the entailment this
map found Grudem and Pinnock sharing, and states it as the open theist's
reasoning: "Were God to know some future choice, say, of what you will have
for dinner this evening, since God’s knowledge is infallible, it must be the
case that you will have for dinner what God knows you will, in which case you
are not free to choose otherwise. As central and essential as libertarian
freedom is to open theism, so equally central and essential is its denial of
exhaustive divine foreknowledge"
([[ware-makes-exhaustive-foreknowledge-the-boundary-of-evangelicalism--202609111819]],
from [[ware-is-open-theism-evangelical--202609111814]]). Ware does not
contest the inference in the captured text; by the article's own restriction, stated in an
uncaptured footnote, he argues the foreknowledge and leaves the freedom to
his book. That is a second witness, in a Reformed voice, that the entailment
is common ground between Grudem and open theism, and that the two sides
differ on which of its terms to give up
([[grudem-and-pinnock-accept-the-same-entailment-and-give-up-opposite-sides-of-it--202609061922]]).

Ware's answer is the one Grudem takes from him. Idols and the openness God
alike cannot "declare what specific future events will unfold, events that
involve innumerable future free choices and actions of human beings";
Isaiah 41:23 challenges the idols to "Declare the things that are going to
come afterward, that we may know that you are gods"; foreknowledge of free
acts is "one of their self-chosen bases for asserting the uniqueness of
their deity"; and "How dare we deny to God what God himself has chosen as a
basis for asserting his own unique deity!" Grudem quotes the same argument
from *God's Lesser Glory*, in a different sentence with the same point
([[grudem-defines-omniscience-as-one-simple-eternal-act-and-rejects-open-theism--202609061915]],
from [[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]];
[[grudem-answers-open-theism-with-isaiahs-test-of-deity-not-with-the-definition-of-omniscience--202609061921]]).
So the reply to the entailment is not to deny it but to settle, on Isaiah's
authority, which term is fixed: the knowledge, and therefore not the
libertarian freedom. Molina's route is the other one, denying that the
knowledge is what fixes the act
([[molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101]]);
Ware and Grudem never take it.

The compatibilism the article does not argue shows in its argument about
the cross. If Christ's choice was free in the openness sense, "it follows
that Christ could choose to be given over or not, and then it follows that
God cannot have known, prior to his choice, just what Christ would do"; if
with Boyd the crucifixion was predestined, "must God not know, at bare
minimum, that his Son will choose to go to the cross?", and by openness
standards a predestined obedience would be "morally vacuous". Ware's texts
are Psalm 22, Isaiah 53, Acts 2:23, 4:27-28 and 1 Peter 1:20, and the
position the dilemma leaves standing is a crucifixion predestined in every
particular and freely and meritoriously chosen, which is concurrence
applied to the one act on which everything depends. The same trade appears
in his pastoral point: without exhaustive foreknowledge "God simply cannot
give assurances that things will work out for good", and "what wins in the
open view is risk; what loses is assurance of God’s success", which is the
Romans 8:28 comfort this map found Grudem buying with the freedom
([[grudems-romans-8-28-comfort-is-bought-with-the-freedom--202609081646]]).

**Limits.** Ware's compatibilism is read here off the shape of an argument
whose stated target is the denial of foreknowledge; the article does not
say "compatibilist" and does not reconcile the predestined cross with the
free choice. The sentence Grudem quotes from *God's Lesser Glory* is not in
this article; the claim that it is the same argument is a comparison of
two passages, one of them at second hand. Pinnock is read here only as Ware
quotes him, and Boyd and Sanders likewise.
