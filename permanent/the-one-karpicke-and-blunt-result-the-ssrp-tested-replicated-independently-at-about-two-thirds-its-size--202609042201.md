An independent team, with no tie to the Roediger–Karpicke line, ran Karpicke and
Blunt's contest again and got the same answer at a smaller size. The Social
Sciences Replication Project collected 52 University of Virginia students and
analysed 49, comparing only the two conditions of interest: retrieval recalled
62.3% a week later against concept mapping's 46.9%, `t(47) = 2.8825, p = 0.006,
r = 0.38 [95% CI 0.12, 0.60]`
([[ssrp-replication-report-for-karpicke-and-blunt-2011--202609042135]]). Same
direction, `p < 0.05`, so it cleared the project's stage-1 bar and no larger
sample was needed. The effect shrank — the original reported `r = 0.60`, the
replication `r ≈ 0.39` — but the original point estimate sits inside the
replication's confidence interval, and the replicators' verdict is a successful
replication that adds precision and suggests generalizability to a new sample and
setting. This is the SSRP figure the base's summary source
([[camerer-et-al-evaluating-the-replicability-of-social-science-experiments--202609041855]])
counted as one of its 21 replications without printing a single per-study number.

**What this does and does not reach, stated precisely because the temptation is
to overread it.** The SSRP protocol tested one result per paper — the first
reporting a significant treatment effect — so what replicated is Karpicke and
Blunt's *Experiment 1*, and within it the single one-week retrieval-versus-mapping
comparison ([[karpicke-and-blunt-retrieval-practice-produces-more-learning-than-concept-mapping--202609042130]]).
Experiment 2 was not tested — not the within-subject design, not the
concept-map-from-memory final test, not the 84% per-subject advantage — and
neither was "retrieval practice" as a research programme. So this is a narrow but
real independent support: it supports the specific claim that retrieval beats
concept mapping
([[retrieval-practice-beats-not-only-rereading-but-concept-mapping-an-active-elaborative-rival--202609042200]]),
and it supports, only at that one point, the broader claim that retrieval beats
re-exposure ([[retrieval-not-re-exposure-is-what-makes-learning-last--202609040340]]).
It resolves the gap the base filed when it could name the tested papers only by
surname and year
([[the-retrieval-practice-programme-has-a-preregistered-replication-and-this-base-has-not-read-it--202609041920]]):
the paper is identified, the result is read, and the outcome is attached.

It is also a second outside check on this literature, sitting beside the
independent laboratory meta-analysis
([[the-independent-audit-puts-the-effect-at-half-a-standard-deviation--202609040615]]):
one returns `r ≈ 0.39` on a single direct replication, the other `g = 0.50`
pooled across 61 studies, both from outside the effect's own camp, both real and
both middling. What this replication cannot touch is durability at long horizons
or the classroom finding that the advantage shrinks as the delay grows
(`in-classrooms-the-retrieval-advantage-shrinks-as-the-delay-grows--202609040515`),
because it measured a single one-week interval and nothing longer — that concern
stands untouched, neither confirmed nor relieved by anything here.
