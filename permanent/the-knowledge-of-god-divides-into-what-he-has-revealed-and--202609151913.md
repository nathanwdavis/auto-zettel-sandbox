**Provenance.** This note records the owner's own account of the hidden knowledge of God, stated on 2026-09-15 and filed as inquiry 202609151845. It is not distilled from a source. The sources linked here are what the account is anchored to and tested against, and the mapping between them and the account is this base's work, not the owner's or any author's.

**The account.** Four sets, nested. Let *G* be everything God knows, which is what omniscience names. A subset *R* of *G* is what God has made known to us. The remainder, *H*, is what God knows and has not made known. *H* divides again by whether we hold the question: into *M*, the known unknowns, where we possess a question and either no answer or an incomplete one (the owner's own phrases are *areas of mystery* and *incomplete understanding*), and *U*, the unknown unknowns, where we possess neither question nor answer. Two things are built into the account rather than added to it. First, *H* is a subset of *G*: nothing hidden from us is hidden from God, so *hidden knowledge of God* is one phrase and not two doctrines, and the hidden is a fact about the creature's share and never about God's knowing. Second, *M* and *U* are asymmetric in a way the account has to live with: *M* can be listed, and this base has been listing its own members for a fortnight (how God knows a free act; how a determined choice is a willing one; what grounds a counterfactual of freedom: [[the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914]]), while *U* cannot be listed at all. It can only be asserted as a class.

**The anchor, and what it does not carry.** The verse the account rests on is Deut. 29:29: "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law." Read of things known, as the KJV's "things" allows, it asserts that *H* is not empty, assigns *R* and *H* to their owners, and attaches a purpose to *R* alone. It gives *H* no content, no duration and no subdivision ([[deuteronomy-29-29-assigns-the-concealed-things-to-god-and--202609151903]]), and it can be read of acts rather than of knowledge at all, which is how the rabbinic tradition reads it. So the verse licenses the outer partition and nothing below it. Every scripture in the set captured for this inquiry does the same: the hidden is named as a whole, "the deep and secret things" (Dan. 2:22), and never sorted ([[the-partial-knowledge-passages-bound-what-creatures-know--202609151904]]).

**The vocabulary, and where it comes from.** The two cells of *H* are borrowed. "there are known knowns; there are things we know we know. We also know there are known unknowns; that is to say we know there are some things we do not know. But there are also unknown unknowns -- the ones we don't know we don't know" ([[rumsfeld-sorts-what-is-known-into-known-knowns-known--202609151910]]). The passage even reaches for the word "omniscience" for the certainty it denies anyone has. The owner's contribution is to put those cells inside a theological set, *H* under *G*, where the unknowns are not merely unknown but known by someone.

**How *U* can appear at all.** Since no member of *U* can be named, the class enters discourse only as an assertion that it is non-empty, and that assertion is itself an item of *M*: we know that there is what we do not know. Deut. 29:29 is exactly such an assertion, and so is the one passage in which the withholder announces the withholding: "I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now" (John 16:12). Both name the existence of a hidden remainder and neither names a member. That is the only form in which the fourth set can be spoken of, and it is the form in which Scripture speaks of it.

**What this sorts in the base.** The existing notes fall into place under it. Natural knowledge of God is "thin" on both of its routes ([[natural-knowledge-of-god-is-thin-on-both-routes-for-the-same-reason--202609011042]]): that is a statement about the size of *R* reached without special revelation. Grudem's use of the verse partitions God's will rather than God's disclosure ([[deut-29-29-partitions-knowledge-and-grudem-partitions-will--202609081906]]): that is a different partition wearing the verse's vocabulary. And the residues each tradition concedes in the foreknowledge debate are members of *M*, placed differently by each side.

**Limits.** The account is the owner's, and the set notation is this note's paraphrase of it. Two pressures on it are recorded in the connecting notes: the New Testament uses "mystery" for what has *crossed* from *H* to *R*, the opposite of the owner's sense; and the taxonomy has no cell for the false known, a thing believed revealed that is not, which Socrates ranks below both kinds of unknown.

**Connections, wired at creation.** The account is tested in six notes: the anchor verse's two readings ([[deuteronomy-29-29-anchors-the-hidden-knowledge-partition--202609151914]]); Aquinas's definition of incomplete understanding and the two classes he keeps with God even in the beatific vision ([[aquinas-bounds-every-knowledge-of-god-below-god-s-own-by--202609151915]]); Calvin's and Luther's rules of conduct toward the hidden ([[calvin-and-luther-both-make-the-hidden-a-known-unknown-with--202609151916]]); the apophatic route that approaches the hidden by subtraction rather than disclosure ([[for-dionysius-the-hidden-god-is-reached-by-subtraction-so--202609151917]]); the asymmetry of the two lower cells and the missing cell for the false known ([[known-unknowns-can-be-inventoried-and-unknown-unknowns-only--202609151918]]); and the Pauline sense of mystery, which makes the boundary between the tiers historical ([[in-pauline-usage-a-mystery-is-a-thing-that-has-crossed-from--202609151919]]).
