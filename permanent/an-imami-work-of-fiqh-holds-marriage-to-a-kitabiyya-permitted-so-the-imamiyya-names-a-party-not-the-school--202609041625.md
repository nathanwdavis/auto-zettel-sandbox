The 15:00Z cycle established that the Imami tradition is split on Q 2:221 and
retracted the base's "the Imami half is closed". It established it from a
*tafsir*, and said so as a limit: al-Tabataba'i twice sends the operative rule
elsewhere, `وللكلام تتمة تطلب من الفقه`, the discussion has a remainder to be
sought in *fiqh*. This note is what a work of that *fiqh* says, and it goes
further than the *tafsir* did.

**The manual does not state a school position. It states a disagreement.**
Al-Muhaqqiq al-Hilli's *Shara'i' al-Islam*, an Imami teaching text of the seventh
Islamic century, distinguishes what is settled from what is not: a Muslim may not
marry a non-*kitabi* woman by consensus, and as for the *kitabiyya*, `تم #ني تحر يم‌الكتا بية
منالهود والتصادى روایتان` [read: `ثم في تحريم الكتابية من اليهود والنصارى
روايتان`] — then concerning the prohibition of the woman of the People of the
Book, Jewish or Christian, **there are two narrations**.
Al-Sabuni's one clause, `وإلى هذا ذهب الإمامية`, reports as the school's position
something an Imami manual records as an open question.

**And the commentary on that manual decides it the other way.** Al-Najafi
(d. 1266/1850) lays out the entire strict case — the *naskh* reports from
al-Baqir via Zurara, Q 60:10, Q 4:25, and al-Murtada's claim of consensus, naming
al-Mufid, al-Murtada and Ibn Idris as its holders — and then rejects it:

> إلا أن التحقيق الجواز مطلقا وفاقاً للحسن د السدوفين على كراحية متفاوئة

except that **the verified position is permission absolutely**, in agreement with
al-Hasan and the two Saduqs, subject to a dislike varying in degree. (Quoted as
the OCR has it; corrected readings in the literature note.) What the strict wing
called prohibition, he makes *karaha*. He then declares the question closed:
`فام ببق بحمداله سبحا نه فياللسألة بعداليوم من … إشكال` [read: `فلم يبق بحمد
الله سبحانه في المسألة بعد اليوم من إشكال`], no difficulty remains in it after
today — having first shown that al-Murtada's claimed consensus is refuted by the
evidence he has just assembled.

**So the report was wrong in a way worth being precise about.** It is not that
"the Imamiyya" overstates a majority. Within the Imami tradition the base has now
read: two classical commentators claim the strict reading as their school's;
three named early jurists hold it in its strongest form; a standard manual calls
the matter two narrations; and a nineteenth-century commentary on that manual
calls permission the verified position and disapproval the whole of what
survives. `الإمامية` names a party to a live dispute. A sentence saying that
would be true; the sentence this base carried was not.

**What is left of the sectarian reversal.** Less than there was, and this is the
third cycle in a row to take some away. The reversal was: on the shared-God
clause the Shi'i commentaries gloss what the Sunni pass over, and on marriage the
Shi'i jurists are the stricter party. The first half stands. The second is now a
claim about particular classical commentators on one side of an argument both
Shi'i traditions were having internally — and on the Imami side, an argument that
the legal commentary this base has read settles the *other* way. What survives is
narrower and more interesting than what was claimed: the strict reading is real,
old, and Shi'i, and in the literature that decides cases it is contested rather
than held.

**A caution this note imposes on itself.** *Jawahir al-kalam* is a commentary,
not a code, and one work read from one host. That it states permission as
`التحقيق` shows what one substantial work of Imami legal literature concluded; it
does not show what any Imami authority rules today, and nothing here should be
read as a claim about contemporary practice. Nor does this base have a source for
any claim about the work's standing in the tradition — that it is comprehensive
or authoritative is a common view this repository has not verified, and no
sentence above rests on it.
