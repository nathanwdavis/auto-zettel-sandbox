The evil inclination of the rabbinic texts is *yetzer ha-ra*, and in Sanhedrin 91b
the phrase is written יֵצֶר הָרָע. Read in the Hebrew, the verses the rabbis attach it to are
of two kinds
([[genesis-8-21-and-6-5-call-the-heart-s-yetzer-evil-in-so--202610061554]]).

In two of them the term is already there. Genesis 8:21 calls the *yetzer* of
man's heart *ra'*, evil, from his youth, and Genesis 6:5 calls every *yetzer* of
the heart's thoughts only evil all the day. The noun and the adjective of the
later compound stand together in the verse. The English hides this: the KJV of 8:21 (on file with the base's
other KJV passages) has *imagination* and the JPS has "the devisings of the human mind are evil from
youth", so a reader of the earlier note on Sanhedrin 91b and Rashi
([[the-talmud-and-rashi-find-an-evil-inclination-present-from--202610032121]])
could not see that the inclination of Genesis 8:21 is the same word.

The rabbis take the name from 8:21 and re-read the *kol ha-yom* of 6:5. Sukkah
52a takes the inclination's first name from 8:21, "The Holy One, Blessed be He, called it
evil"
([[sukkah-52a-52b-has-god-name-the-evil-inclination-evil--202610061557]]);
Kiddushin 30b reads the same verse as the Creator calling it evil, and reads
6:5's *all the day* as *every day*, for the inclination's daily renewal
([[kiddushin-30b-has-god-create-the-evil-inclination-together--202610061556]]).
Even the birth reading of 8:21 rests on the letters of the verse: the word for
*from his youth* is written without a vav (מנעריו), which Genesis Rabbah
34:10 and Rashi
([[rashi-reads-from-his-youth-in-genesis-8-21-as-the-evil--202610032113]]) read as
*ninar*, the moment the child pushes out of the womb
([[genesis-rabbah-34-10-tells-the-antoninus-exchange-from--202610061555]]).

Genesis 4:7 is different. Its word is *chatat*, sin, crouching at the door, and
the JPS notes "Meaning of verse uncertain." There the inclination is a reading:
Sanhedrin 91b and Kiddushin 30b take the sin at the door to be the inclination
([[sanhedrin-91b-dates-the-evil-inclination-s-rule-over-a--202610032112]]). So
the rabbinic doctrine is lexically anchored in 8:21 and 6:5 and interpretively
extended to 4:7, and the earlier note's pairing of 4:7 with 8:21 joins one
verse that names the inclination to one that does not.
