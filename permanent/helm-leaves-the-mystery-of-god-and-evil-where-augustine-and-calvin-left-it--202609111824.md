Helm's post on Ware opens by setting the problem inside the tradition rather
than inside the doctrine of divine knowledge. The Reformed, "among all
theological outlooks", are "the most frequently taxed with the objection that
their views entail that God is the author of sin", and three replies have
been "repeated ad nauseam in the literature": that evil is a privation, so
that God, "who has fullness of being, cannot author evil"; that God "is the
primary cause, not to be confused with secondary, creaturely causes"; and
"that God brings about evil by willingly permitting it". They "can be
combined to make a cumulative case". Then: "There remains, of course, an
element of mystery, of how it is that the divine ordination meshes with human
sin and evil. Augustine and Calvin, for example, routinely refer to the
matter as ‘ineffable’. Bruce Ware, and others, offer 'Compatibilist Middle
Knowledge' as one way in which such ineffability can be mitigated." The
post's last paragraph returns to the same three replies after finding the
mitigation "consequently futile": "there remain the standard ways of
parrying the charge of God and evil: evil as privation, primary and
secondary causation, and the Augustinian idea of ‘willing permission’. But
the mystery remains as well. Which is how we might expect things to be"
([[helm-reads-wares-compatibilist-middle-knowledge-as-natural-knowledge-under-another-name--202609111815]],
from [[helm-shunning-middle-knowledge--202609111810]]).

Two things follow for the map of where each position puts its unanswered
question ([[the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914]]).
First, Helm's placement is Grudem's. The first of the two questions Grudem
lists as unanswerable on his own position is "Exactly how can God not be
blamed for evil when he ordains that we do evil willingly?"
([[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]];
[[the-dispute-is-decided-by-which-residue-honours-god-more--202609081831]]),
and "how it is that the divine ordination meshes with human sin and evil" is
the same joint. What differs is the attitude. Grudem ranks residues, and
prefers the position whose unanswered questions leave God greater; Helm
expects the residue, and treats a mechanism that would shrink it as suspect
for that reason, "how we might expect things to be" being the tradition's
verdict and not a defect in it. Second, Ware's project is, on Helm's
reading, the Reformed counterpart of what the Molinist replies to the
grounding objection do on the other side
([[the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104]]):
an attempt to make the residue smaller by adding a stage to God's knowing,
which the critic answers by showing that the stage is one of the old ones
under a new name.

The disagreement is narrower than it looks. Van Horn's note 59 records that
Helm "sketches a similar view" to Ware's "occasioning" passage in his own
essay "God Does Not Take Risks" (2004)
([[van-horn-records-the-concession-and-argues-that-counterfactuals-can-be-natural-or-free-but-not-middle--202609111817]],
from [[van-horn-on-incorporating-middle-knowledge-into-calvinism--202609111812]]).
If that is right, Helm and Ware share the picture of God governing evil by
what he permits and arranges, and differ on whether the picture is a kind of
knowledge (middle) and whether it reduces the mystery. Helm's answer to both
is no.

**Limits.** "Ineffable" is attributed to Augustine and Calvin by Helm without
a citation, and neither is on file for it. Helm's own essay is unread, so the
"similar view" is Van Horn's judgement, not this base's. That Helm's
placement coincides with Grudem's is this base's comparison; Helm does not
mention Grudem, and Grudem's two questions are asked of his own position,
not of the tradition.
