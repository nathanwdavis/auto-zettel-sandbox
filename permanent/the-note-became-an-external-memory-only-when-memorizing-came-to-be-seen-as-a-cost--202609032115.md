Treating notes as memory kept outside the head looks like the obvious point of
taking them, and it is not: it is a position that had to displace an older one.
Blair's evidence is a reversal in what note takers thought they owed to memory
([[blair-on-note-taking-as-transmission-and-delegated-memory--202609032105]],
[[blair-note-taking-as-an-art-of-transmission--202609032100]], p. 106). Drexel
and his seventeenth-century contemporaries saw no harm in memorising one's
notes as thoroughly as possible — the note served memory. By 1920 the medical
doctor Chavigny was recommending notes on index cards as a "personal memory on
paper," and his stated reason inverts the older view: "too much memorizing can
be harmful to the higher intellectual qualities." The memory function is handed
to paper deliberately, and on the grounds that holding it in the head costs
something better spent elsewhere. Blair sets this beside Leibniz on the
calculating machine freeing astronomers from the "servile labor of calculating"
and calls the shift one of the most significant in the history of note taking.

The consequence for any slip-box argument is that the modern slogan — *the
system remembers so you don't have to*, nobody's words but this note's — is not
a neutral description of what notes are. It is the later
side of a contested change, and it presupposes that memorising is a cost rather
than the goal — which is exactly what Drexel's generation denied.

That makes it the historical antecedent of
[[a-trusted-structure-frees-attention-from-willpower--202608311142]], where the
same trade appears three centuries later in Ahrens: a structure you can trust
is worth having because it stops you having to hold everything in mind. Ahrens
argues it as method and spends the freed capacity on switching tasks without
willpower; Chavigny argued it as hygiene and spent it on "higher intellectual
qualities." Neither depends on the other, which is what makes the agreement
worth recording — Blair is outside the Luhmann line entirely.

One limit, stated by Blair rather than inferred: she says plainly that she is
"not yet able to trace" this transformation in detail. The two ends are
documented and the path between them is not, so this note is anchored on a
before and an after and should not be read as a traced lineage. Her twentieth
-century material is also the thin end of her evidence; the article's weight is
early modern.

**Addition, 2026-09-04.** When this note was written, its premise — that
delegating to the page changes what stays in the head — was argued and never
measured, on either side. It now has one measurement behind it:
[[expecting-a-saved-copy-cost-nine-points-of-recall-in-the-same-session--202609041825]],
where believing a machine had saved what you typed cost nine points of recall
in the same session, with nothing else about the task varying. That is support
for the premise only. Chavigny's claim is that the trade is *worth making* —
that what is freed goes to "higher intellectual qualities" — and no source in
this repository measures the freed side of it. The measurement also arrives
carrying its own warnings, which the linked note carries with it: one small
unreplicated experiment, on unconnected trivia, inside a single session.
