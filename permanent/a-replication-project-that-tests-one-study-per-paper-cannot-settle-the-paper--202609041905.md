A large-scale replication project reports a rate — 62%, 36%, 30% — and the rate
is about the sample of *results* it tested, not about the *papers* those
results came from. When a paper reports four experiments and the project
replicates the first significant one, a failure leaves the other three exactly
where they were: untested. This is not a critic's objection to be argued
against the project; it is what the Social Sciences Replication Project says
about itself, in its limitations, in one sentence — for papers reporting a
series of studies "we only replicate one of those studies", so the project
"does not provide definitive insight on any of the original papers from which
we designed the replication studies"
([[the-ssrp-replicated-one-result-per-paper-and-said-so-in-its-limitations--202609041900]],
[[camerer-et-al-evaluating-the-replicability-of-social-science-experiments--202609041855]]).

The selection rule sharpens the point. The SSRP took the *first* study
reporting a significant treatment effect, which is a rule about position in a
paper, not about importance, and the project notes it cannot say which way that
biases the rate: if first studies replicate differently from later ones, its
62% is systematically too high or too low, and it does not know which. Two sets
of original authors said in published Correspondence that a later study in
their paper was the stronger one.

So a replication rate is two claims, and only one of them is licensed. It is
evidence about a *literature* — that this many high-profile findings, tested
once each at high power, did not come back. It is not evidence about any
particular paper in that literature beyond the one result tested. Reading the
rate as a verdict on the papers commits the same error in aggregate that citing
a failed replication commits singly.

The consequence cuts symmetrically, which is what makes it a rule rather than a
convenience. It protects a paper's untested experiments from being treated as
refuted — the discipline
[[the-google-effects-replication-record-reaches-only-its-priming-experiment--202609041820]]
already applies to the Google effect, now with the replicating project's own
endorsement rather than only this repository's reasoning. It equally withholds
credit: a paper whose one tested result *did* replicate has not thereby been
confirmed as a paper. A project of this design can raise or lower a single
finding, and it can characterise a field. It cannot promote or demote a
publication, and a base that cites it as though it could is borrowing
confidence the project explicitly declines to lend.
