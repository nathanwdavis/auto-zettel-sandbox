Every source in this base that argues the same-God question from a Christian
side treats Islam as the hard case and Christianity as the given. Maimonides
runs it the other way, and he does it in the operative part of a legal code.

**Islam is inside.** "With regard to any gentile who does not serve false
deities, e.g., the Arabs" — the clause is illustrative, incidental to a rule
about wine, and legally live (see
[[maimonides-classes-the-arabs-as-non-idolaters-and-the-censor-removed-the-other-half--202609042030]]).
Islam appears here not as a case to be argued but as the obvious example of the
category.

**Christianity is outside.** The sentence Maimonides wrote next was a contrast,
and the printed editions carry only its first half; the uncensored text reads
"Christians, by contrast, are idolaters." In the Messianic chapter he says it
without a censor's help: Christianity made "the majority of the world to err and
serve a god other than the Lord" (see
[[maimonides-charges-christianity-with-making-the-world-serve-another-god--202609042031]]).

That phrase is the whole point. "Serve a god other than the Lord" is an answer
to the same-God question, in the negative, about Christians — the exact verdict
that Mohler and Tachin deliver about Muslims (see
[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]).
The dispute the base has documented is not Christianity-versus-Islam with
Judaism as a neutral third. It is a three-cornered disagreement in which each
tradition has a candidate for the party that has got the object wrong, and every
candidate is somebody else.

**This also puts a date on shituf.** [[shituf-grades-the-worshipper-instead-of-identifying-the-worshipped--202609011542]]
reads the Jewish position as declining to answer the identity question and
grading the worshipper instead. That is the *later* position — the Tosafist and
Rema line, which the same translator's note flags as the softening of exactly
this passage. Maimonides does not grade the worshipper. He answers the identity
question, and answers it against Christianity. Shituf is not the Jewish view of
Christianity; it is one Jewish view, and the one it displaced is harsher.

**Two limits.** The Messianic passage is polemic, not a worked argument for where
the line falls. And nothing here is a *Muslim* judgement of Judaism; that leg is
still missing.
