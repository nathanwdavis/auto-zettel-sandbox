al-Maturidi (d. 333/944) does not gloss `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد`. He died twenty-three
hijri years after al-Tabari (d. 310/923), which puts a silent commentator inside
al-Tabari's own generation and removes the one reading of the silence this base
had left open: that the `معبود` identification was current when al-Tabari made it
and was dropped by a later tradition.

**What was open, and why it mattered.** Six cycles established that al-Tabari
glosses the clause as `ومعبودنا ومعبودكم واحد` — the one we worship and the one
you worship is one — and that no commentator captured since repeats him
([[the-maabud-identification-is-al-tabaris-own-and-the-commentaries-after-him-do-not-repeat-it--202609040820]]).
But every silent commentator in that set writes a century or more after him:
al-Zamakhshari d. 538, al-Baghawi d. 516, al-Qurtubi d. 671, al-Razi d. 606, Ibn
Kathir d. 774. A gap that wide leaves two histories compatible with the same
evidence. On one, the identification was al-Tabari's own from the start. On the
other, it was unremarkable in the third/ninth century and fell out of use — in
which case the base's finding would be about the later tradition's drift rather
than about how singular al-Tabari was. Nothing in the base could tell those
apart, because the base had nobody from the intervening generations.

**What al-Maturidi settles.** He is that missing generation: a contemporary
rather than a successor, close enough in time that his commentary is not the
later tradition looking back at al-Tabari's. On the same verse he reaches the
sentence that carries the clause and abbreviates past it:
he quotes `وَقُولُوۤاْ آمَنَّا بِٱلَّذِيۤ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْنَا وَأُنزِلَ
إِلَيْكُمْ...` and writes `إلى آخر ما ذكر`, "to the end of what was mentioned",
twice. So the drift reading fails: the identification is not something the
tradition let go of, because the commentator standing next to al-Tabari in time
does not have it either.

**A second thing it settles, which was the reason it was fetched.** al-Maturidi
is the eponym of the school that, with Ash'arism, classical Sunni theology is
built on, and he was fetched to test whether the silence belongs to a theological
family. It does not, within Sunnism: the silent set now spans both Sunni kalam
schools — Maturidi here, Ash'ari in al-Razi — as well as the Mu'tazili
(al-Zamakhshari) and the juristic and traditionist commentaries captured
earlier. The
frame within which that test is decisive is still the Sunni one — the Imami
commentaries do gloss the clause
([[the-shii-commentaries-do-gloss-the-clause-so-the-classical-silence-is-a-sunni-fact--202609041130]]) —
and this note does not disturb that. What it adds is that inside the Sunni frame
there is now no major classical theological school left to try.

**The precision this note is careful about.** "Silence" is the wrong word for
what al-Maturidi does, and the distinction is load-bearing rather than stylistic.
The other five do not reach the clause: they gloss neighbouring clauses and this
one goes unmentioned. al-Maturidi *does* reach it — he makes the whole sentence
the content of the command, calls it the good disputation itself, and says it is
of the sort `يقبلها العقل والطبع`, that reason and natural disposition accept —
and elides the clause inside the sentence he is commending. An elision inside a
quotation the commentator is actively using is stronger evidence than an absence,
because it cannot be explained by his never having got there.

**What it does not establish.** One commentator is not a generation, and this is
a single-host capture: the tafsir.app copy of the *Ta'wilat* is an empty shell
and no second host was found, so the negative rests on altafsir.com alone
(recorded in [[maturidi-tawilat-ahl-al-sunna-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609041200]]
and filed to INBOX). The other commentators of the intervening centuries are
untouched — al-Samarqandi, al-Tha'labi and al-Mawardi are on the same host, with
those death dates given by its own catalogue selector (375, 427, 450) — and any
one of them glossing the clause would narrow this claim
without overturning it. What is established is that the silence is not a
post-Tabari development, which is the reading it was fetched to test.
