In Grudem's *Systematic Theology* (2nd ed., pp. 221-222) the formal definition
of God's spirituality reads that God "exists as a being that is not made of any
matter, has no parts or dimensions, is unable to be perceived by our bodily
senses, and is more excellent than any other kind of existence" (distilled at
[[grudem-defines-god-s-spirituality-as-immateriality--202609071347]], from
[[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]]). The clause "has no parts or
dimensions" is a claim of partlessness — the content classical theology names
divine simplicity — asserted here as a component of a *different* attribute,
spirituality, and without the word "simplicity."

That placement is the point. The same denial "not divided into parts" already
turns up in this base at two other Grudem attribute-definitions: at the unity
attribute, where it is the gloss the chapter-11 outline heads "Unity
(Simplicity)," and at the act of knowing, where the second edition cashes it out
as a denial of counting, recalling, inferring and learning (see
[[grudems-incommunicable-attributes-are-denials-fenced-where-god-acts--202609050612]]
and [[grudems-simple-act-of-knowing-denies-process-not-composition--202609061920]]).
So partlessness is not, for Grudem, a doctrine given its own heading; it is a
denial that recurs across attribute-definitions — spirituality, unity, knowledge —
each time fenced by a positive that keeps God acting or being: here, that God is
nonetheless "pure being," "more real and more desirable" than any creature, and
the cause of everything else.

Two limits. This is one attribute-definition read from a single owner drop of
pp. 221-222 with no independent witness, and the recurrence claim leans on the
two linked notes for its other two data points rather than re-establishing them.
And "no parts or dimensions" in a spirituality context is at least partly a
denial of *spatial* extension (dimensions), continuous with the omnipresence
definition's denial of size, so it is not by itself the full metaphysical
partlessness of the classical doctrine — it is the clause in which that content
first appears under this heading.
