Asked "Who is God?", mainstream evangelical theology does not answer with the
attributes a philosopher would list first. It answers by naming one God who
exists eternally as three persons, and then by narrating what those persons have
done.

The National Association of Evangelicals' doctrinal basis states that there is
one God "eternally existent in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit," and
then gives its next three articles to the Son -- his deity, virgin birth,
"vicarious and atoning death," bodily resurrection, ascension and personal
return -- and to the Spirit's regenerating and indwelling work (see
[[nae-statement-of-faith--202608311920]]).

The consequence for any comparison is that omnipotence, mercy, creatorhood and
oneness -- the predicates most easily shared across the Abrahamic traditions --
are not where this tradition puts the weight. Identity is carried by the
personal relations and by a particular history. A comparison that matches
attribute-lists will therefore report far more agreement than the tradition
itself recognises.
