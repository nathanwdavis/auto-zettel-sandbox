Chapter 16's first footnote is a refusal of two labels at once. Of
*determinism*: "Though philosophers may use the term determinism (or soft
determinism) to categorize the position I advocate in this chapter, I do not use
that term because it is too easily misunderstood in everyday English," with
three reasons — it suggests choices are unreal, it suggests a mechanistic rather
than personal ultimate cause, and it lets critics group the biblical view with
non-Christian systems. Of *compatibilism*: "The view advocated in this chapter is
also sometimes called 'compatibilism,' because it holds that absolute divine
sovereignty is compatible with human significance and real human choices. I have
no objection to the nuances of this term, but I have decided not to use it"
([[grudem-defines-providence-as-preservation-concurrence-and-government--202609081824]],
from [[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]]).

Read beside two other passages, this is a habit rather than an incident. In
chapter 12 Grudem takes Augustine's "reasonable self-determination" for what he
keeps of human freedom, and the base already recorded that the phrase is valued
precisely because "his statement does not involve the terms free or freedom"
([[grudem-and-pinnock-accept-the-same-entailment-and-give-up-opposite-sides-of-it--202609061922]]).
And chapter 16 quotes Calvin at length doing the identical thing with *free
will*: Calvin grants the substance ("he acts wickedly by will, not by
compulsion") and then asks "what purpose is served by labeling with a proud name
such a slight thing?", concluding "I'd prefer not to use it myself, and I should
like others, if they seek my advice, to avoid it." Grudem quotes that with
approval and follows it.

The pattern is worth naming because of what it is not. This is not a hedge about
the content: in every case the substance is affirmed in the same breath the word
is dropped — "no objection to the nuances of this term" concedes compatibilism
while declining it, and the accompanying footnote elsewhere in the chapter says
outright that freedom of inclination "is also called 'compatibilist freedom'"
([[the-freedom-grudem-deferred-is-compatibilist-freedom--202609081827]]). The
stated reason is uniformly about the reader: the word will be heard to mean
something the writer does not mean. What the practice costs is locatability.
A reader who searches Grudem for "compatibilism" or "determinism" to find out
where he stands will find the terms only where he is declining them, and the
base's own path here is the illustration — it had to reach the position by
inference from a concession in chapter 12 before this chapter arrived to
confirm it.

**Limits.** Three instances, two of them from one chapter, and the third
(Augustine's phrase) is a case of preferring a formulation rather than an
explicit refusal of a label, so the pattern is real but the sample is small.
Calvin is quoted through Grudem, from an *Institutes* translation this
repository has not consulted, and the base's own Calvin reference note was
captured for a different purpose. Whether Grudem accepts *Reformed* and
*Calvinist* on the same terms he refuses *compatibilist* is not asked here: he
uses both freely, and his stated reason for preferring them — that they place
him "within a widely understood theological tradition" — is about belonging
rather than about precision, which is a different criterion from the one that
governs the refusals.
