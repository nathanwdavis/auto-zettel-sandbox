The case for retrieval has usually been made against a lazy comparison: pulling
material out of your own head beats reading it again. That leaves open the
obvious objection — maybe any active, effortful engagement would do as well, and
retrieval is just one instance of it. Karpicke and Blunt closed that gap by
pitting retrieval against concept mapping, an elaborative study task that is
itself active and effortful, and retrieval still won by a wide margin
([[karpicke-and-blunt-retrieval-practice-produces-more-learning-than-concept-mapping--202609042130]]).

The margin is large and it survives the handicaps built to remove it. In their
first experiment, mapping and retrieval produced near-identical amounts during
initial learning, so the week-later gap could not be an artifact of unequal
learning up front, and retrieval beat mapping at `d = 1.50`. In the second, the
initial-learning imbalance ran *toward* mapping — students generated more on the
initial maps than on the initial recall — and retrieval still won at `d = 1.07`.
Most telling, when the final test was itself the drawing of a concept map from
memory — the format that should most favour the mapping group's own practice —
retrieval-trained students still did better, at `d = 1.01`; per person, 101 of
120 (84%) learned more after retrieval than after mapping. So the operative
variable is not activity or effort in general: it is retrieval specifically. This
extends the base's existing claim that retrieval beats re-exposure
([[retrieval-not-re-exposure-is-what-makes-learning-last--202609040340]]) from a
contest against passive rereading to a contest against an active elaborative
competitor, which is the harder and more informative test.

**One boundary condition to carry with the claim.** The retrieval in this study
was free recall with *no feedback*, followed by restudy of the text — the
attempt was uncorrected in the moment but the correct material was available
immediately afterward. That places it in the configuration the base records as
the one that pays, rather than the null one where a failed attempt is never
corrected
([[a-failed-retrieval-that-is-never-corrected-buys-nothing--202609040620]]). The
win over mapping is therefore a win for retrieval-plus-restudy, not for straining
to recall in the dark. And the scope is the same one-week retention of short
science prose by undergraduates the rest of this cluster lives inside; nothing
here reaches longer horizons, connected knowledge, or note-taking.
