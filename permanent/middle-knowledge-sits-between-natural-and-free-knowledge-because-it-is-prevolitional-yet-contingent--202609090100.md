Molina's *scientia media* is defined by its position between two knowledges
the scholastics already had, and the position is fixed by two properties
rather than by a list of objects. Natural knowledge is what God knows by his
essence: in Molina, "mere naturalem, quae proinde nulla ratione potuit esse
aliter in Deo" — purely natural, which could in no way have been otherwise in
God; in Laing's survey, "that part of God’s knowledge which He knows by His
very nature or essence, and since His essence is necessary, so is that which
is known through it." Free knowledge is what God knows by knowing his own
will: Molina's "mere liberam, qua Deus post liberum actum suae voluntatis
absque hypothesi et conditione aliqua cognovit absolute et determinate" which
things are in fact future; Laing's knowledge "which He knows by His knowledge
of His own will," which "is postvolitional; it is dependent upon God’s will."
Middle knowledge, of what each free will "pro sua innata libertate, si in
hoc, vel illo, vel etiam infinitis rerum ordinibus collocaretur, acturum
esset" — would do by its innate freedom if placed in this or that or even
infinitely many orders of things — shares one property with each neighbour.
Like natural knowledge it comes before the act of the divine will; like free
knowledge its content could have been otherwise
([[molina-distinguishes-three-knowledges-and-makes-the-middle-one-depend-on-the-creature--202609090055]],
from [[molina-concordia-liberi-arbitrii--202609090050]];
[[laing-defines-middle-knowledge-as-prevolitional-knowledge-of-counterfactuals-of-freedom--202609090056]],
from [[laing-middle-knowledge-iep--202609090051]]).

Molina says exactly this when he asks whether the middle knowledge should be
called free or natural. Not free, "tum quia antecedit omnem liberum actum
voluntatis divinae, tum etiam quia in potestate Dei non fuit scire per eam
scientiam aliud quam re ipsa sciverit"; not natural either, since had the
created will been going to do the opposite, "idipsum scivisset per eandem
scientiam." So it is said "partim habere conditionem scientiae naturalis" and
"partim habere conditionem scientiae liberae." Laing compresses the same two
properties into one sentence: "middle knowledge is like natural knowledge in
that it is prevolitional, or prior to God’s choice to create. This, of
course, also means that the content of middle knowledge is true independent
of God’s will and therefore, He has no control over it. Yet, it is not the
same as natural knowledge because, like free knowledge, its content is
contingent." Pohle gives the objects that the two properties pick out: a
class "found neither in the region of pure possibility nor strictly in that
of actuality, but partake equally of both extremes and in some sort belong
to both kinds of knowledge"
([[pohle-locates-the-molinist-thomist-dispute-in-the-medium-of-foreknowledge--202609090057]],
from [[pohle-molinism-catholic-encyclopedia--202609090052]]).

This closes a gap this base recorded on itself. The note on Grudem's
knowledge of the possible found that his deduction from self-knowledge
reaches what God could do while his scriptural cases are about what creatures
would do, and said the classical vocabulary for that distinction was
"deliberately not invoked, because no source stating it is on file"
([[grudem-grounds-knowledge-of-the-possible-in-self-knowledge-without-separating-what-creatures-would-do--202609061923]]).
The source is now on file, in Molina's words and in two surveys. The line
that note drew on its own is the natural/middle line: what God knows by
knowing his power is Molina's first knowledge, and Keilah, Tyre and Sidon
are, for Molina, the objects of the third.

**Limits.** The three-way division is Molina's arrangement of a two-way
inheritance; Laing presents natural and free knowledge as the two categories
handed down from Aquinas, and no text of Aquinas on the knowledge of simple intelligence and of vision is on file
here. Whether the middle category is coherent — whether anything can be both
prior to God's will and contingent — is what the grounding objection contests
([[the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104]]),
and this note states the definition, not its success. The English renderings
of Molina are this repository's own.

**Amended 2026-09-11, from Helm, Van Horn and Laing.** The two properties
this note used to place middle knowledge now do work as a test. Ware's
"compatibilist middle knowledge" keeps the first, coming before God's will,
by deriving the counterfactual from the creature's character and its
circumstances ([[wares-compatibilist-middle-knowledge-derives-the-counterfactual-from-character-and-circumstance--202609111820]]), and on Van Horn's argument loses the second, since
a counterfactual so derived is a necessary truth; Helm files it under
natural knowledge, Laing under free, and all three agree it is not middle
([[a-counterfactual-derived-from-nature-and-circumstance-is-natural-or-free-knowledge-but-not-middle--202609111821]]). The definition stated above therefore excludes the compatibilist
variant on its own terms, which is what Van Horn means by calling its
Calvinist proponents confused. Nothing above is withdrawn.
