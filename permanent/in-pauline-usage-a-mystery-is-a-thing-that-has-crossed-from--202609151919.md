The owner's account uses "mystery" for what remains hidden. The New Testament passages captured for this inquiry use the word for the opposite ([[the-partial-knowledge-passages-bound-what-creatures-know--202609151904]]).

**The usage.** "the mystery which hath been hid from ages and from generations, but now is made manifest to his saints" (Col. 1:26); the mystery "Which in other ages was not made known unto the sons of men, as it is now revealed unto his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit" (Eph. 3:5). A *mysterion* here is a thing that was in the hidden tier and is in the revealed tier now, and it is called a mystery for where it came from, not for where it is. The word marks a crossing, and the crossing runs one way, from God's knowledge into the creature's, by an act that the passages name: "he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets" (Amos 3:7); "the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God" (1 Cor. 2:10).

**What this does to the partition.** It makes the boundary between the revealed and the hidden a boundary in time. The tiers are not fixed sets but a history of transfers, and the account's two lower sets are stages a given item may pass through: an unknown unknown becomes a known unknown when a question is put, and a known unknown becomes revealed when God answers it. Two passages describe the process from inside. Paul's "now I know in part" has a terminus, "then face to face" (1 Cor. 13:12), so the partiality of present knowledge is dated and not permanent. And John 16:12-13 is a disclosure scheduled: "I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now", followed by the promise that the Spirit "will guide you into all truth". The one passage in which the withholder speaks gives the withheld a reason, capacity, and a future.

**Aquinas's ladder is the same history stated as levels.** Grace adds to reason's knowledge truths reason could not reach, "as, for instance, that God is Three and One" (ST I q.12 a.13), and the vision adds to grace's. Each rung is the last rung's hidden tier partly transferred ([[aquinas-separates-seeing-god-from-comprehending-him-denies--202609151906]]). The base's own handoff from general to special revelation ([[natural-knowledge-of-god-is-thin-on-both-routes-for-the-same-reason--202609011042]]) is one such transfer, the first the tradition names.

**The two senses, kept apart.** The owner's sense of mystery, a question held without its answer, is the theologian's sense of the word and the sense in which the base has used it of the foreknowledge residues. The Pauline sense is a thing that has been answered. Both are legitimate and they are near-opposites, and a note on the hidden knowledge of God should say which it means; on the Pauline sense, *the mysteries* are precisely the contents of the revealed tier that were not always there. The owner's known unknowns are, on this usage, the mysteries that have not yet been made mysteries.

**Limits.** Twelve verses in the KJV, without the Greek and without any commentary on *mysterion*; the reading of the word is this base's from the two Pauline instances captured, which agree with each other but are only two. Whether every hidden thing is destined to cross, or only some, the passages do not say, and Aquinas's a.8 says some never do.
