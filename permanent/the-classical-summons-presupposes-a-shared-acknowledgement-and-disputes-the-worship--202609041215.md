Asked what makes the "common word" of Q 3:64 common, al-Maturidi answers from an
acknowledgement the addressees have already made: `إذ العبادة لا تكون إلا لله
الذي أقروا جميعاً أنه خالق السماوات والأرض، وأنه ربهم` — since worship is due to
none but God, whom **they all acknowledged** to be the creator of the heavens and
the earth and to be their Lord. The summons is not that they should come to
recognise a God they do not yet recognise; it is that they should direct worship
to the one they already grant.

**The structure that produces.** Acknowledgement (`إقرار`) of the creator is
conceded and made the premise. Worship (`عبادة`) is the disputed term, and the
charge is misdirection rather than misidentification: `لكن منهم من يعبد دون الله
أوثاناً` — some of them worship idols besides God — and some assign Him partners
in His worship. So a Muslim theologian arguing this verse works with a shared
referent he does not argue for and a divergent practice he does argue about.
The concession is anchored in scripture rather than asserted: Q 31:25 and Q 43:87,
the verses in which the addressees, asked who created the heavens and the earth,
answer "God".

**Why this is a Muslim datum for a distinction the base drew from elsewhere.**
[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]
sorts the dispute into a question about reference and a question about
description, and already carries the limit that all five of its sources are
Christian or secular. Here is a classical Muslim frame, and it is a third thing
again: not *do we pick out the same being*, and not *do we describe him alike*,
but **do you render to the one you already acknowledge what is owed to him**.
On that frame co-reference is not the conclusion of an argument but the unargued
premise of a rebuke — which is why the clause `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد` can go
unglossed in the same commentary without anything being lost: the commentator
was never trying to establish what the clause states.

**The argument he gives is from reason, and that is what makes it usable.**
Al-Maturidi's ground for the "word" is not a proof text but two rational steps:
`لأن العبادة لواحد أهون وأخف من العبادة لعدد`, worship of one is easier and
lighter than worship of a number; and `إذ ذاك جور وظلم في العقل أن ينعم أحد على
آخر، فيشكر غيره`, since it is, in reason, injustice that one should bestow favour
on another and the other thank someone else. Worship is analysed as gratitude
owed to a benefactor, so the question "whose God is it?" collapses into "who
benefited you, and whom are you thanking?" — a question of debt, not of
reference or of description.

**A second and much earlier witness to a move the base had once.** al-Razi
(d. 606/1210) grounds the gentler treatment of the People of the Book in their
own profession of oneness, `فوحدوا`, rather than in the verse's shared-God clause
([[the-gentler-argument-is-grounded-in-the-people-of-the-books-own-profession-of-oneness--202609040920]]).
That was one commentator and could have been his idiosyncrasy. al-Maturidi makes
the same move 270 years earlier, on the other verse, and grounds it one step
further back — not in what they profess about God's oneness but in what they
concede about his being creator and Lord. Two independent instances across three
centuries make this a pattern in the classical exegesis rather than a feature of
one commentary.

**What it does not license.** This is not al-Maturidi affirming that Muslims and
the People of the Book worship the same God. He says they acknowledge the same
creator, and the whole force of his sentence is that some of them nevertheless
worship what is not he. Acknowledging and worshipping come apart in exactly this
passage, and the base should not collapse them: read carelessly, `أقروا جميعاً`
becomes a co-reference proof text, and it is a premise in an argument that their
worship is misplaced. Nor is the addressee-set clean — the idol-worship charge
sits oddly on the People of the Book, and Q 39:3, which al-Maturidi cites for it,
is about the Meccan idolaters. Whether he is blurring the two groups or reporting
a charge about a subset is not something this capture settles.
