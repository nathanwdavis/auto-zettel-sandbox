The Anselmian route to co-reference works by finding a description that any
tradition can utter and that picks out God on its own (see
[[anselms-formula-identifies-god-by-description-alone--202609010840]]). The
kabbalistic prayer that states this tradition's doctrine of God most plainly
withholds exactly what that route needs -- not the possibility of addressing
God, but the availability of any *determinate* thing to say about Him.

The denials in *Patach Eliyahu* are specific in a strict sense, and the text
keeps the qualifier every time: "You are 'wise', but not with any specific
'wisdom'... You have no specific 'place'... But not that You have any specific
righteousness -- which is 'judgement', and no specific justice -- which is
'mercy'. And none of any of these attributes at all." The same qualifier
governs the sentence about names: "all the sephirot, each one has its own
name, and with them, angels are called. But You have no specific Name, for You
are He that fills all Names" (see
[[patach-eliyahu-names-the-sefirot-and-leaves-ein-sof-nameless--202609011536]]).

**What this does not license.** It does not license saying that God cannot be
referred to. The passage refers to Him continuously: it opens "Master of the
Worlds!", calls Him "'the Cause of causes', the Reason of reasons", and -- in
the sentence immediately before the one about names -- says outright, "And
outside of You, there is no uniqueness [Var. unity] among the higher and the
lower, and You are known as 'Lord over All'." A doctrine that predicates uniqueness and
supplies an appellation in the same breath is not a doctrine of referential
failure. What it denies is that any of this is a *determination*: God is known
as Lord over All without that being a specific name, wise without a specific
wisdom.

**What it does license.** Anselm's device is not a bare appellation but a
superlative that works by determination -- a being than which nothing greater
can be conceived picks out its object by fixing a maximum on a scale. On this
doctrine there is no scale to fix: the attributes the superlative would
maximise are precisely the ones denied in their specific form. So the
description does not do the referring here. Something else does -- address,
which the prayer performs from its first word and never grounds in a
description.

That is the bearing on the same-God question, and it is a sharper version of a
condition this repository already holds: co-reference by description survives
only if the description is what does the referring (see
[[anselmian-co-reference-holds-only-if-the-description-does-the-referring--202609010842]]).
Kabbalah supplies a case where a tradition confidently addresses God while
denying its own descriptions any determining power. Where that is the
situation, agreement or disagreement in description cannot settle whether two
parties are addressing the same God -- and, symmetrically, no shared formula
can establish that they are.
