The Anselmian route to co-reference works by finding a description that any
tradition can utter and that picks out God on its own (see
[[anselms-formula-identifies-god-by-description-alone--202609010840]]).
Kabbalah blocks that route at its highest level, and it does so on principle
rather than by accident: the Infinite is not the sort of thing a description
picks out.

*Patach Eliyahu*, the tradition's most widely recited statement about God,
makes the denial in every register available. Of thought: "no thought
apprehends You at all," and at the close, "there is none that knows of You at
all." Of likeness: "of You, there is no imagining, and there is no likeness, of
anything that is inside or out." Of attributes: "You are 'wise', but not with
any specific 'wisdom'... And none of any of these attributes at all." And of
names, decisively: "all the sephirot, each one has its own name, and with them,
angels are called. But You have no specific Name, for You are He that fills all
Names, and You are the completeness of everything" (see
[[patach-eliyahu-names-the-sefirot-and-leaves-ein-sof-nameless--202609011536]]).

A description picks out its object by attributing something to it that nothing
else has. Anselm's formula does this by superlative -- than which nothing
greater can be conceived. But a being of whom no attribute at all is truly
predicated, whom no thought apprehends, and who bears no name because He fills
every name, cannot be singled out that way; the superlative has nothing to grip.
So on this doctrine the Anselmian device does not fail to be *shared* across
traditions -- it fails to *refer*, for anyone, including the tradition holding
the doctrine.

Nothing here makes the same-God question unanswerable, because the Kabbalah
does not leave God unavailable. It relocates where identification happens.
Names, attributes, and relations belong to the ten sefirot: they are "called in
this arrangement" and enumerated one by one, and it is through the created
world that "higher-ones would be recognised." What can be picked out, described,
addressed, and therefore agreed or disagreed about is always the manifest God,
never the Infinite behind Him.

That is the interesting result. The level at which all monotheists trivially
have the same God -- the Infinite, since a nameless One admits no rival
description to distinguish anyone's from anyone else's -- is exactly the level
at which nobody can say anything. The level at which the traditions can be
compared is the level of the named. The same-God dispute is therefore
well-formed only downstream of Ein Sof, which is a sharper version of the
worry that the description must do the referring (see
[[anselmian-co-reference-holds-only-if-the-description-does-the-referring--202609010842]]).
