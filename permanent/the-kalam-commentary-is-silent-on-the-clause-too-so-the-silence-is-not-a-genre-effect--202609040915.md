Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (d. 606/1210) comments on Q 29:46 at length and never
glosses `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد`. That closes the one explanation this base had left
for the silence of the three commentators before him: that it is a fact about
their genres rather than about the tradition.

**The objection this note answers was the base's own.** When
[[the-maabud-identification-is-al-tabaris-own-and-the-commentaries-after-him-do-not-repeat-it--202609040820]]
established that al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and al-Baghawi all pass over the clause,
the same cycle filed the objection against its own result: those three are a
jurist, a traditionist and an abridger. A clause asserting that the God of two
parties is one is a *kalam* question, and a commentary organised around legal
cases or chains of transmission may simply have no slot for it. On that
explanation the silence would say nothing about whether the tradition held the
identification — only that these three books were not where it would be said.

**Why al-Razi is the test and not merely a fourth witness.** *Mafatih al-ghayb*
is the classical commentary written by a theologian, in which doctrinal
questions are raised, opponents' positions stated and answered. If the
identification of the two parties' God were a claim the tradition held, this is
the book that would have room for it. He is also demonstrably engaged with
exactly this material: in the same comment he distinguishes the People of the
Book from the idolaters, states what they do and do not affirm about God, and
classifies a party of them as associators. He is not passing quickly over the
verse; he is arguing about who its addressees are, in the vocabulary the
question needs.

**The evidence, and it is an absence, so it was checked as one.** The word
`معبود`, in which al-Tabari's identification consists, does not occur anywhere
in al-Razi's comment on Q 29:46. It was searched, after normalisation, in three
independently served copies of that comment — tafsir.app, islamweb.net and
quran-tafsir.net — and occurs in none of them, two of which begin and end on the
same sentences and so are demonstrably not truncated
([[razi-mafatih-al-ghayb-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609040905]]). Where the clause
does appear in his text, it appears inside a quotation of the verse offered as
one of the People of the Book's merits, and he moves straight on to the next
verse.

**What this does and does not license.** It licenses dropping the genre
qualification from the base's reading of the silence: four commentaries across
four centuries and four genres, including the theological one, leave the clause
alone. It does not license reading the silence as denial. al-Razi no more
contradicts al-Tabari here than the other three do; he does not raise the
question. The claim that survives is the narrow one — the identification of the
shared God as a shared object of worship is al-Tabari's own move, and the
commentary tradition after him neither repeats nor disputes it.

**The word does occur once, and it is worth stating where.** al-Razi uses
`المعبود` in his comment on Q 3:64, in the sentence that before the Messiah the
object of worship was none but God, so the matter had to remain so afterwards.
That is an argument that the Christians have since added to the object of
worship — the opposite direction of travel from al-Tabari's identification. The
vocabulary was available to him and he put it to the contrary use.

## Amendment, 2026-09-04 10:00Z cycle

The school objection this note's cycle filed against its own result has been
tested in the cycle after it and does not hold: al-Zamakhshari's *al-Kashshaf*,
the classical Mu'tazili commentary, passes over the clause as well
([[the-mutazili-commentary-passes-over-the-clause-too-so-the-silence-is-not-a-school-effect--202609041010]]).
Both explanations this base generated for the silence — that it belongs to
certain genres, that it belongs to one theological family — are now ruled out,
and nothing has replaced them. That is the honest state of the question: the
silence is real, general, and unexplained.
