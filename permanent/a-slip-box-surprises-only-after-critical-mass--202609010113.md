A note collection has two regimes. Below a threshold of size and internal
linkage it is a container: queries return only what was deliberately put in.
Above it, the network of links is dense enough that a query activates
relations its author never traced, and the box returns combinations that were
never stored — it can surprise, which is what lets Luhmann treat it as a
communication partner rather than an archive
([[luhmann-frames-the-slip-box-as-a-communication-partner--202609010112]]:
"The slip box needs a number of years in order to reach critical mass. Until
then, it functions as a mere container").

The threshold regime explains why atomic notes compound only over long
horizons ([[atomic-notes-compound-over-time--202701010001]]): the compounding
payoff is back-loaded, invisible during the container years. It also supplies
a first primary-source data point for the conjecture that time in the system
dominates per-period yield on the note-taking side of the analogy
([[atomic-notes-and-compound-interest-share-a-reinvestment-loop--202608311038]],
[[time-horizon-dominates-rate--202701010013]]): Luhmann prices the threshold
in years of use, not in cleverness of individual notes — though one
practitioner's account establishes the pattern for one box, not a general
law.

**Limit, added 2026-09-04.** That last clause is the whole of what stands
behind the threshold claim, and this cycle looked for something better and did
not find it. No experiment tests it: the note-taking literature measures how a
person writes during one lecture and scores a test taken shortly after, which
is the wrong construct on the wrong time scale for a claim about years of
accumulation
([[the-note-taking-experiments-do-not-reach-the-slip-box-claims--202609040215]]).
So the regime described above rests where it did — on one practitioner
describing his own box — and the reason no better evidence is cited here is
that none appears to exist, not that none was sought.
