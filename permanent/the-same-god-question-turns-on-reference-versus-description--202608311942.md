Participants in the same-God dispute can agree on every doctrinal fact and still
answer opposite ways, because they are answering different questions. One
question is whether the traditions *pick out the same being* -- a question about
reference. The other is whether they *give the same account* of him -- a
question about description. Neither answer entails the other.

Amy Plantinga Pauw states the distinction directly: "To say that we worship the
same God is not the same as insisting that we have an agreed and shared
understanding of God" (see [[gjelten-do-christians-and-muslims-worship-the-same-god--202608311927]]).

Once it is drawn, the positions sort themselves:

- **Reference decides, and it is shared.** Nostra Aetate affirms that Muslims
  "adore the one God, living and subsisting in Himself... the Creator of heaven
  and earth," and in the same paragraph records that "they do not acknowledge
  Jesus as God" -- holding the shared object together with the unresolved
  difference (see [[nostra-aetate--202608311924]]).
- **Description decides, and it is not shared.** Tachin grants that the three
  "may have shared the same broad categories about their supreme deities" and
  still concludes they "actually do not worship the same God," because "true
  worship of God is trinitarian and Christ centered"; his abstract relocates the
  question onto "the real substance of worship and its approval by God" (see
  [[tachin-do-they-worship-the-same-god--202608311925]]).
- **The christological test**, Mohler's, is a description test applied at a
  single point: whether one "can reject Jesus Christ as the Son and truly know
  God the Father" (see [[gjelten-do-christians-and-muslims-worship-the-same-god--202608311927]]).

Vroom's survey of the literature reports that the arguments come from four
different quarters -- theological insight, comparative religion, philosophy of
religion, and philosophical views about the unity of the world -- and asks which
kind is decisive (see [[vroom-do-all-religious-traditions-worship-the-same-god--202608311926]]). That is the same observation from the
outside: the disagreement is partly about which question, and which discipline,
is entitled to settle the matter.

So "do they worship the same God?" is not one question with a hidden answer. It
is at least two questions wearing one sentence, and most of the heat comes from
answering one of them while the other side answers the other.
