Al-Sabuni, a twentieth-century Sunni author, writes that the strict reading of
Q 2:221 was held by `الإمامية، وبعض الزيدية` — the Imamiyya and *some of the
Zaydis*. Al-Tabrisi, an Imami writing in the sixth/twelfth century, lists the
same three-way split and attributes the same position to
`ابن عمر **وبعض الزيدية**` — Ibn 'Umar and *some of the Zaydis* — before claiming
it as his own school's.

The phrase is the same, the attribution is the same, and it is eight hundred
years older and comes from the opposite sect. So the attribution is not a modern
Sunni gloss on Shi'i doctrine and it is not one author's summary: it is a
classical datum that a Sunni author of the twentieth century and a Shi'i author
of the twelfth report independently and identically.

**Why this was worth establishing separately from the position itself.** A claim
about what a *school* holds is exactly the kind of claim that hardens as it is
repeated, and this base had it from one witness who was outside the schools he
was describing and writing nine centuries after them. Two failure modes were
live: that a modern author had compressed a more complicated classical picture
into a slogan, or that the attribution had a single source that both later
writers were copying. Finding it in al-Tabrisi kills the first — the phrase is
classical, in the same words — and weakens the second, since the earlier witness
is Imami and the later Sunni, with nothing but the material in common.

**What it does not establish.** That *some Zaydis* held the position is now well
attested as an attribution; **that** any particular Zaydi held it is a different
claim, and neither witness names one. A Zaydi source naming the position and its
holder is what would settle it, and one exists —
[[the-zaydi-tradition-is-split-on-2-221-so-the-qualifier-some-is-load-bearing--202609041455]]
reports what the two Zaydi commentaries reachable from here actually say, which
is not what either attribution would lead one to expect.

Note also the direction of the corroboration. Al-Tabrisi does not merely report
that some Zaydis hold it; he lines them up with Ibn 'Umar, the Companion whose
dissent this base already holds from Malik's *Muwatta'*. The strict position
travels as a set — one Companion, some Zaydis, the Imamiyya — across sources
that share neither school nor century.
