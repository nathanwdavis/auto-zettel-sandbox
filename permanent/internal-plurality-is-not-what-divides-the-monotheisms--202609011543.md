It is tempting to locate the Abrahamic disagreement about God at the question
of whether the one God admits real internal distinction -- Christianity yes,
Judaism and Islam no (see
[[abrahamic-monotheisms-divide-over-internal-distinction-not-oneness--202608311941]]).
Kabbalah breaks that line, because it is a Jewish position that answers yes.

*Patach Eliyahu* asserts a unity that explicitly refuses to be counted -- "You
are He that is One -- but not in number" -- and in the next breath a real
articulation: ten sefirot, each with its own name, each with a bodily
correspondence, ordered "one long, and one short, and one intermediate," and
none of them the Infinite. That God is *within* them is stated, not hedged:
"You are He that binds them, and unites them, and, because You are within
them." The distinctions are not nominal, and they are not merely how creatures
perceive an undifferentiated simple (see
[[patach-eliyahu-names-the-sefirot-and-leaves-ein-sof-nameless--202609011536]]).

Set that beside the Maimonidean formulation the earlier note used as the Jewish
side -- a oneness "unified in a manner which [surpasses] any unity that is
found in the world," ruling out both the unity of a category containing
individuals and the unity of a divisible body (see
[[maimonides-mishneh-torah-yesodei-hatorah--202608311922]]). These are two
Jewish answers, not one, and they diverge on precisely the axis that was
supposed to separate Judaism from Christianity. Whatever divides the traditions,
it cannot be internal plurality as such, or the line would run through Judaism
before it ran between religions.

What Kabbalah does hold is a rule about *severing*: "anyone who separates one
from its companion, of these ten, is considered as though he had caused
separation in You." The offence is not distinguishing within God but treating a
distinction as detachable -- worshipping an aspect as though it stood on its
own. That relocates the dispute with trinitarianism. It is no longer "may God
be articulated?" but two narrower questions: which articulation, and whether
the persons of the Christian confession are severable in the sense the sefirot
are forbidden to be. The evangelical formula, one God "eternally existent in
three persons" (see [[nae-statement-of-faith--202608311920]]), is at least
built to fail the severability test rather than pass it, which is more than the
older framing could register.

Two cautions. Kabbalah's ten are emanated and directed -- "You are He that
directs them, and there is none who direct You" -- where the Christian persons
are not; that asymmetry is real and this note does not dissolve it. And no
kabbalist cited here draws the comparison: the parallel is this repository's,
built from what the texts assert, not a position either tradition has taken up.
