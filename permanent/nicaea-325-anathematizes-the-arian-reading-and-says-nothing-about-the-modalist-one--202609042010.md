The two readings of the divine three that this base has been treating as
symmetrically condemned were not condemned symmetrically, or in the same
document, or in the same kind of document.

**The Arian reading is anathematized at Nicaea, in the creed, by proposition.**
The clause of 325 condemns anyone who says of the Son "that he is of a different
substance or essence [from the Father] or that he is a creature", along with
four other stated Arian claims (see
[[the-nicene-anathemas-of-325-are-directed-at-one-side-only--202609042000]]).

**The modalist reading is not anathematized at Nicaea at all.** Not in the
anathema clause, which contains six propositions and no seventh; not in the
creed, whose positive phrases — "of the substance of the Father", "begotten,
not made", *homoousion* — a modalist can affirm without strain. The council of
325 names no parties and does not reach this error.

**It is anathematized in 381, by party name, in a canon.** Canon I of
Constantinople anathematizes "that of the Sabellians" inside a list of seven
parties, gives no proposition for any of them, and belongs to the council's
disciplinary decrees rather than to its creed — the creed of 381 has no anathema
clause (see
[[the-council-of-381-anathematizes-sabellianism-by-party-name-in-a-canon--202609042001]]).

So the sentence "the councils ruled out both readings" is true only if
"councils" is read across two councils, fifty-six years, and two genres of
conciliar act, with one side condemned as a stated doctrine and the other as a
name. [[the-two-kabbalistic-readings-of-the-sefirot-are-the-two-the-councils-ruled-out--202609041730]]
uses the symmetry as its structure — a left option and a right option, each
"shut" by the same authority. The symmetry is not in the sources.
