The question is not settled in the tradition, and it was never settled quietly.
Al-Qurtubi's third *mas'ala* on Q 2:221 reports three positions, all attributed:

1. *mushrikat* **did** cover the women of the Book, and Q 5:5 later abrogated
   them out of it — from Ibn 'Abbas; held by **Malik, Sufyan al-Thawri,
   al-Awza'i**. They are inside the category and exempted by a ruling.
2. *mushrikat* **never** covered them — **Qatada, Sa'id b. Jubayr**, one of
   al-Shafi'i's two positions, and al-Tabari's own verdict. They were never in
   the category.
3. *mushrikat* covers them and the permission is **abrogated** — reported by
   Ishaq al-Harbi, and a *riwaya* of Ibn 'Abbas: general over "idolatresses,
   Magian women and women of the Book, and everyone not upon Islam".

The dissent has a name and a sentence. **Ibn 'Umar**, in Malik's *Muwatta'*:
`ولا أعلم إشراكاً أعظمَ من أن تقول المرأة ربّها عيسى` — "I know of no
association greater than a woman saying that her lord is Jesus." A Companion, in
the earliest surviving law book, holding that Christian belief is not merely
*shirk* but its greatest instance. Al-Sabuni records that the **Imamiyya and some
Zaydis** took the same view, inverting the abrogation so that Q 2:221 cancels
Q 5:5.

Three things this makes it possible to say that the base could not say before.

**The tension was seen.** The commentary sweep found silence: eleven commentators
on Q 3:64 and Q 29:46, none registering any strain between the shared-God clause
and the sword. That silence is a fact about the *tafsir* genre on *those verses*,
not about the tradition. Move to the verse where a ruling is at stake and the
question is not merely visible but litigated, by name, across schools.

**The dispute is about a word's extension, not about the facts.** Every party
grants what Christians believe; they disagree about whether the Qur'an's
*mushrikun* is a term that reaches them. This is a nearer analogue to the
reference/description framing in
[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]
than anything else this base holds from a Muslim source — and it is also the
reason that note now needs a second limit. Its recorded limit is that all five
of its sources are Christian or secular. The sharper point is that the Islamic
tradition *does* run a question of this shape in its own idiom, and the idiom is
not reference-versus-description but the extension of a Qur'anic term fixed by
grammar, *naskh* and transmitted authority. The dispute here is about which
people a scriptural word ranges over; it is not about whether two descriptions
pick out one object. Marked `contradicts` because it denies that note's implicit
claim to state the neutral shape of the dispute, not because it denies its
content.

**And the sectarian split is a fact worth its own line.** On the base's earlier
finding, the Shi'i commentaries *do* gloss the shared-God clause where the Sunni
ones pass over it
([[the-shii-commentaries-do-gloss-the-clause-so-the-classical-silence-is-a-sunni-fact--202609041130]]).
Here the direction reverses: it is the Imami and some Zaydi jurists who take the
*stricter* line, that the People of the Book are associators and marriage to
their women is forbidden. Whatever the Sunni/Shi'i difference on this question
is, it is not one tradition being consistently more accommodating than the other.

Grounding limit, **closed on the Imami half and qualified on the Zaydi half by
the 14:00Z cycle of the same day.** As written, this note's only witness for the
Imami and Zaydi attribution was al-Sabuni, and it filed that gap to INBOX as the
obvious next capture. That capture was taken. Al-Tusi and al-Tabrisi both state
the strict position as their own school's and both run the abrogation backwards
on the authority of al-Baqir and al-Sadiq, so the Imami attribution is now
verified from inside the school — with one correction to the report, since what
they forbid is the *permanent* contract while allowing *mut'a* and concubinage
with the same women
([[the-imami-commentaries-claim-the-strict-reading-of-2-221-as-their-own-and-run-the-abrogation-backwards--202609041445]]).
The Zaydi half came back more complicated than the report: the strict reading is
credited to Yahya and al-Qasim by a later Zaydi commentary, while the corpus's
oldest work reads Q 2:221 the lenient way, so "some Zaydis" names a real
division rather than hedging a school-wide position
([[the-zaydi-tradition-is-split-on-2-221-so-the-qualifier-some-is-load-bearing--202609041455]]).
The sentence above — "the Imamiyya and some Zaydis took the same view" — stands
as written; what has changed is that it no longer rests on a single modern
report, and that "the Shi'a" would have been wrong where "some Zaydis" is right.
Still outstanding, and still filed to INBOX: no Imami or Zaydi *fiqh* text has
been captured, only the commentaries.

**Corrected 2026-09-04 15:00Z: "closed on the Imami half" was premature, and the
Imami half needs the same qualifier as the Zaydi one.** The paragraph above was
written when the base held two Imami commentaries, both classical and both
strict. A third has been read — al-Tabataba'i's *al-Mizan*, the major
twentieth-century Imami commentary — and it argues the opposite at the root:
Q 2:221's `المشركات` does not reach the People of the Book, and the abrogation
claim is `فساد` in both directions
([[the-imami-tradition-is-split-on-2-221-so-the-strict-reading-is-the-classical-commentaries-not-the-school--202609041510]]).
So al-Sabuni's clause is accurate about the classical Imami commentaries and too
strong about the Imamiyya as such, in the same way it would have been too strong
about the Zaydis without its `بعض`. Two things this does *not* disturb: the
sentence in the body remains a fair report of what al-Sabuni wrote, and the
*content* of the restriction — the permanent contract, not the association —
survives al-Tabataba'i's rejection of the argument for it, rebuilt as
specification rather than abrogation
([[the-permanence-restriction-survives-the-rejection-of-the-abrogation-as-specification-not-naskh--202609041520]]).
What is now wrong is only the word "closed".
