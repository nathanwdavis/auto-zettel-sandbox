[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]
sorts the dispute into two questions — does each tradition *pick out* the same
being, or does each *describe* him the same way — and treats them as the two
available answers. The semantics the distinction is borrowed from does not
divide there.

Two findings, from two survey entries, both cut the same way.

**Rigidity does not adjudicate it.** LaPorte's entry states flatly that
"Rigidity's work is compatible with descriptivism," and that the impression that
rigidity's job is to *refute* descriptivism "seems to be a mistake." The reason
is a one-way entailment: "even if rigidity does follow from anti-descriptivism,
anti-descriptivism does not follow from rigidity." And the counterexamples are
not exotic: "descriptive designators may be rigid: e.g., world-indexed
designators" (see
[[laporte-states-that-rigidity-does-not-by-itself-refute-descriptivism--202609041925]]).
So a designator can pick out the same being in every world *and* do it
descriptively. "Reference decides" and "description decides" are not
contradictories.

**And the field offers four positions, not two.** Michaelson's entry enumerates
descriptivist, causal, character and intentionalist models of what fixes
reference, and then refuses to make them rivals: "these four models aren't
strictly exclusive of each other," with classical descriptivism readable as "a
particular kind of intentionalism" and the causal theory as "a special case of
the character theory" (see
[[michaelson-sets-out-four-models-of-reference-fixing-and-declines-to-make-them-exclusive--202609041926]]).

The consequence for this repository is not that the reference/description
distinction is useless. It is that the distinction is a *theological* one — it
tracks which considerations a tradition will let settle the question — and it
has been carrying a borrowed authority it does not have. Pauw, Nostra Aetate,
Tachin and Mohler are not taking sides in a semantic controversy; they are
disagreeing about what worship requires, in vocabulary borrowed from one.
