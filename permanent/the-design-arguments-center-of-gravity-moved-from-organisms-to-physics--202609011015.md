The classic design argument argued from organisms: Paley's exemplary
contrivances are the eye, the joints, the instincts -- living mechanisms
whose adjustment of means to ends "exceeds all computation" (see
[[paley-natural-theology--202609011004]], ch. 3). The strongest
contemporary form argues instead from physics: the fine-tuning of
cosmological constants and force strengths, where "Even an extraordinarily
small change in one of many key parameters in the laws of physics would
have made life impossible anywhere in the universe" (see
[[ratzsch-koperski-teleological-arguments-for-gods-existence--202609011003]],
sec. 4.1).

The shift is not an accident of fashion but a response to defeat. Craig
opens by granting that the organism-based argument is "Widely thought to
have been demolished by Hume and Darwin", and relocates the evidence in
"wider teleology" -- in his source's words, "the necessary conditions for
the existence and evolution of intelligent life" rather than particular
organisms. That relocation puts the evidence where Darwinian selection
cannot operate -- the constants are set before there is any population to
vary and be selected -- a step this note draws out of Craig's contrast
rather than quotes (see
[[craig-the-teleological-argument-and-the-anthropic-principle--202609011005]]).
The biological revival attempted by the Intelligent Design movement (Behe,
Dembski) remains contested territory even among theists, and the SEP entry
records the "vociferous criticism and opposition" it drew from the
scientific community -- whereas the fine-tuning data themselves are
mainstream physics, and the dispute is only over their interpretation.

Stated as a general claim about evangelical natural theology, this is the
note's own synthesis of one prominent evangelical philosopher's program
(Craig 1990) plus the SEP's survey of where the live debate now sits; a
broader base of evangelical assessments has not yet been captured (see
INBOX follow-up).
