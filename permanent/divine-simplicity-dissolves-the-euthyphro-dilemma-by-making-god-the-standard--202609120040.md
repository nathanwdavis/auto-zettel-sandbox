The Euthyphro dilemma forces a choice: either God commands the good because it is
good, so his will answers to a standard outside him and above him, or the good is
good only because he commands it, so morality is arbitrary. The first horn costs
God his sovereignty; the second costs the good its content.

Divine simplicity refuses the choice. If God is not a being who *has* goodness but
is "goodness itself in its prime instance," then he is not measured against a
standard — "he is the standard." And if, being simple, he is identical with his
own act of willing, then he "cannot will anything other than the good," so the
threat of arbitrariness never arises. The dilemma is "a false alternative": both
horns assume a distinction — between God and the good, or between God and his
willing — that simplicity denies (see
[[the-sep-motivation-section-derives-simplicity-from-aseity-and-necessity-and-uses-it-to-dissolve-euthyphro--202609120030]],
summarizing [[vallicella-divine-simplicity-sep--202609042072]] section 1).

This is a distinct *kind* of reason for the doctrine. The base already holds the
argument from dependence — a composite needs a composer, and God has no cause —
as the ground the traditions share and read to different reaches (see
[[no-composition-does-different-work-in-each-tradition--202609042080]]). The
Euthyphro solution is not that argument; it is a payoff claimed *for* the
conclusion once reached. Where the dependence argument says God *must* be simple,
this says what simplicity then *buys*: a meta-ethics in which sovereignty and
non-arbitrariness stop competing. It sits alongside the survey's other two
motivations, aseity and necessity, as the third thing possession-of-properties
would forfeit and identity-with-them secures.

**Limits.** The claim is reported here from a reputable-secondary survey (the SEP
entry), which itself credits Feser (2009) drawing on Stump (2003); no primary
meta-ethical text of that argument is on file, and this note asserts only that the
survey makes the move, not that the move succeeds. It also inherits every doubt
about simplicity's own coherence that the entry's later sections raise and this
base has not yet read — the solution is only as available as the doctrine it
rests on.
