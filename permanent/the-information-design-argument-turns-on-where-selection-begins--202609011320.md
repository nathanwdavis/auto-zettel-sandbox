An openly published, both-sides exchange over the information-based
design argument inside evangelicalism's own scientific society --
Venema's PSCF review of *Signature in the Cell* and Meyer's PSCF
response (see
[[venema-seeking-a-signature--202609011310]],
[[meyer-of-molecules-and-straw-men--202609011311]]) -- reduces, on
inspection, to a single hinge: whether mutation-plus-selection is
available as a rival cause *before* self-replication exists.

Each side's case works only on its own side of that line. Venema's
refutation exhibits information gain by mutation and selection inside
living systems (antibody maturation, Lenski's populations) and extends
the mechanism backward as "a candidate for the origin of biological
information from nonliving precursors" -- conditional, by his own
statement, on "a plausible pathway leading from nonliving precursors to
a replicating entity" (see
[[venema-finds-no-thorough-search-behind-signatures-design-inference--202609011315]]).
Meyer's defense concedes the biotic examples and confines the argument
to before that pathway: selection "presupposes ... a preexisting
mechanism of self-replication", so invoking it prebiotically assumes
the information to be explained (see
[[meyer-confines-signatures-argument-to-the-prebiotic-origin-of-information--202609011316]]).
Strikingly, the two agree on the empirical state of play -- no complete
abiogenesis mechanism exists -- and read the same absence oppositely:
for Venema it is an open research front that falsifies Meyer's claim to
a completed "thorough search"; for Meyer it is accumulated knowledge of
absence that his inference requires, which Venema's concession
"effectively concedes".

This is the same structural move that decides between design arguments
elsewhere in this repository: fine-tuning displaced Paley precisely by
relocating the design datum upstream of where Darwinian selection
operates (see
[[the-design-arguments-center-of-gravity-moved-from-organisms-to-physics--202609011015]]).
The Venema-Meyer dispute is over whether the *biological*-information
argument can claim that same upstream position, or whether selection's
writ can be extended back to the origin of life itself. For the
evangelical assessment question, the exchange shows the argument's
status is contested within the design argument's own constituency on
scientific rather than theological grounds: both pieces run in the
journal of the American Scientific Affiliation (Meyer writing as "a
longtime ASA member", Venema as its commissioned reviewer), and neither
side disputes that a successful design argument would be welcome --
only whether this one succeeds.
