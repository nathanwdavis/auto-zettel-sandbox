The Social Sciences Replication Project tested one result from Pyc and Rawson
(2010) — study 31 of its twenty-one papers — and the result it chose is narrow:
by the project's first-significant-effect rule it took the comparison of
*mediator* recall between test-restudy and restudy-only practice, within the one
final-test format (cue-mediator-target) where the original ran it, a single
t-test on one of the study's six cells. In the original that comparison was
n = 36, mediator recall 34% versus 51%, `t(34) = 2.37`, `p = 0.0236`
([[ssrp-replication-report-for-pyc-and-rawson-2010--202609042330]]).

It replicated, but only just, and only in the larger sample. The first data
collection (n = 132, powered for 75% of the original effect) found the difference
in the right direction but not significant — 54.55% versus 48.39%,
`t(130) = 1.7119`, `p = 0.0893`, and no better under a typo-tolerant re-coding —
so the protocol triggered a second collection. Pooled across both (n = 306) the
difference was significant: 53.53% versus 47.57%, `t(304) = 2.6404`,
`p = 0.0087`, with the manual coding agreeing (`p = 0.0124`). The project's
aggregate report classes this among its successes and puts a one-sided default
Bayes factor of `BF+0 = 6.8` on it
([[camerer-et-al-evaluating-the-replicability-of-social-science-experiments--202609041855]]).

**This closes an identification and withholds a promotion, exactly as the rule
predicts.** It closes the standing half of the gap this base filed: the
"Pyc and Rawson (2010)" the SSRP tested is confirmed at the project's own OSF
repository to be the memory paper, so the surname coincidence is resolved and the
mechanism note it anchors ([[the-testing-effect-works-by-producing-more-retrievable-target-eliciting-mediators--202609042340]])
is a genuine member of this cluster. But the same rule that governs the Karpicke
result governs this one and harder: one replicated result promotes that result,
not the paper or the programme
([[a-replication-project-that-tests-one-study-per-paper-cannot-settle-the-paper--202609041905]]).
Here the promotion is weaker still than for Karpicke and Blunt, whose tested
result cleared at stage 1 at about two-thirds its original size
([[the-one-karpicke-and-blunt-result-the-ssrp-tested-replicated-independently-at-about-two-thirds-its-size--202609042201]]);
this one needed the second sample to cross `p < 0.05` at all. And what replicated
was mediator *recall* — one of the mediator-effectiveness hypothesis's two
components — not the mechanism itself, nor anything about connected prose or
retention horizons beyond the paradigm's single delayed test.
