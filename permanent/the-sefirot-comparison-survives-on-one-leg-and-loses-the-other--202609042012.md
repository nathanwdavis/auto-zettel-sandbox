Checking the councils against their reporter does not demolish the comparison in
[[the-two-kabbalistic-readings-of-the-sefirot-are-the-two-the-councils-ruled-out--202609041730]].
It costs it one of its two legs, and the surviving leg gets stronger.

**The right-hand leg holds, and now rests on the council.** Recanati's sefirot
as "the craftsman's vessels" and Hayyat's emanation as "a property in the
Emanator... not the essence of the Emanator" say of what proceeds that it is of
another substance than its source. The creed of 325 anathematizes exactly that,
in its own words: whoever says of the Son "that he is of a different substance
or essence [from the Father] or that he is a creature". No intermediary is
needed to make that comparison; the conciliar text says it.

**The left-hand leg does not hold in the form it was given.** R. David's sefirot
as one simple essence under differing aspects, named differently "in relation to
differing actions", was said to be "to the letter, the position Hodge reports
the councils as condemning". Hodge's report is accurate about the fourth-century
church and inaccurate about the councils: no conciliar act condemns the *ad
extra* reading in those terms, and the only conciliar mention of Sabellianism is
a bare party name in a canon of 381 (see
[[nicaea-325-anathematizes-the-arian-reading-and-says-nothing-about-the-modalist-one--202609042010]]).
The claim needs a different warrant — Tertullian, Dionysius, the Arian charge
that *homoousion* sounded Sabellian — or a weaker form.

**Chalcedon contributes nothing either way**, being about the natures in Christ
rather than the persons in God (see
[[chalcedon-defines-the-natures-in-christ-and-not-the-persons-in-god--202609042003]]).

What is left is an asymmetry that is more interesting than the symmetry it
replaces. The kabbalistic dispute recorded in
[[whether-the-sefirot-are-gods-essence-or-his-instruments-is-a-dispute-inside-kabbalah--202609041720]]
has two live options; the conciliar record forecloses one of them in a creed and
the other only through a tradition of interpretation. A Christian and a
kabbalist comparing notes are not looking at two symmetric exclusions. They are
looking at one hard boundary and one soft one.
