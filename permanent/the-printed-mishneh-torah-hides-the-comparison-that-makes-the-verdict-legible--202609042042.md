A censorship that deletes half of a contrast does not merely subtract; it
changes what the surviving half says.

In Hilchot Ma'achalot Asurot 11:7 Maimonides wrote one sentence with two limbs:
Arabs do not serve false deities, Christians by contrast are idolaters. The
printed editions keep the first limb verbatim and replace the second with a
colourless "those who worship false deities" (see
[[maimonides-classes-the-arabs-as-non-idolaters-and-the-censor-removed-the-other-half--202609042030]]).

The remaining text is not false. It is unmoored. A reader of the standard
edition sees Maimonides declining to call Muslims idolaters and has no way to
see that the clause was a comparison, or whom it was a comparison with. The
lenient judgement survives; the reason it counted as lenient does not.

This is a hazard with a shape, and this repository has met it before from the
other direction. In [[the-silence-about-the-shared-god-clause-begins-in-al-tabaris-own-generation--202609041210]]
the base found that reading the commentaries in sequence exposed a *silence* —
that al-Tabari's identification was not repeated — which no single commentary
could show. Here the sequence hides something instead: an edited text conceals
that a judgement was ever comparative.

The methodological point generalises past this case. A tradition's verdicts
reach us through a transmission that has its own interests, and the interests
show up as *shape* — which clause is kept, which is generalised, which is
dropped — more often than as outright falsification. Reading one edition of one
passage cannot detect it. What detected it here was a translator's footnote
saying what the other text reads.
