[[divine-simplicity-is-the-question-the-monotheisms-actually-divide-on--202609042081]]
orders the monotheisms by how far the argument from dependence is let run — from
Maimonides, who lets it consume the attributes and even number, to Aquinas, who
stops it at metaphysical composition — and the standing inquiry asked where the
Islamic *kalam* lands: at a middle setting admitting real attributes that are
"neither He nor other than He". Until now the base could not test this. al-Ibana
affirmed the attributes as real but withheld any relation-term under *bila kayfa*,
and its one relational datum — that God's names are "not other than He"
([[al-ash-ari-affirms-god-eternally-knowing-powerful-living--202609051940]]) —
leaned toward the Maimonidean end, giving only half of a double negation.

al-Ash'ari's own doxography, the *Maqalat al-Islamiyyin*, supplies the missing
half in the primary text. Reporting Ibn Kullab (p. 546), it states the full
formula: the attributes are real and eternal — God is knowing, powerful, living
**by** knowledge, power, and life, which are "attributes of the essence" that
subsist in Him — **and** "His attributes are not He nor other than He"
(*la hiya huwa wa-la ghayruhu*); "Knowledge is not the Power nor other than it"
(see
[[al-ash-ari-in-the-maqalat-reports-ibn-kullab-real--202609072134]]). Both
negations are present, so the position is genuinely the midpoint the axis
predicted, not the "not other than He" that al-Ibana alone suggested.

That places the Islamic leg squarely on the same axis, and at two settings, not
one. The Mu'tazila, as al-Ash'ari reports them, deny the attributes as real
properties and collapse "hearing, seeing" into "knowing" — attributes reduced to
the essence, the Maimonidean end
([[mutazili-position-as-al-ash-ari-reports-it-denies-distinct-attributes--202609051941]]).
The Kullabi–Ash'ari answer keeps the attributes real yet refuses to make them
either identical with the essence (against the Mu'tazila) or "other than" it
(which would break simplicity): the double negation *is* the middle position
between the two ends the axis names. So the prediction is confirmed for the
Ash'ari leg on the very formula it quoted, and the Islamic dispute turns out to
divide over the *reach* of the no-composition constraint exactly as the axis says
the others do — the Mu'tazila letting it consume the attributes, the Ash'ariyya
fencing it short of them.

Two limits are worth keeping. This is al-Ash'ari's doxographical *report* of Ibn
Kullab's doctrine, which he then adopts, not a Mu'tazili source read in its own
voice; the Mu'tazili leg still rests on al-Ash'ari's characterisation of his
opponents. And whether Ibn Kullab originated the formula or was credited with a
position the school later systematised is not settled by this passage — it
reports him holding it, no more.
