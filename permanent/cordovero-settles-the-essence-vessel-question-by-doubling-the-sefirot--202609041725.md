Asked whether the sefirot are God's essence or His instruments, Cordovero
answers *both*, and he can do it because he stops treating "the sefirot" as one
set of ten. In *Pardes Rimmonim* Gate 4 ch. 4 the Ein Sof emanates "ten sefirot
which are of His essence, unified with Him, and He and they are all one complete
unity"; and "these sefirot are a soul, and they clothe themselves in ten
sefirot designated by names, which are vessels" (see
[[cordovero-states-the-essence-vessel-dispute-and-answers-it-with-both--202609041710]]).
There are ten inner sefirot that are essence and ten outer sefirot that are
vessels, standing to each other as soul to body, and the second set is what the
names Hesed, Gevurah and the rest actually designate.

The move buys precisely what each side wanted and denies each side its
exclusivity. Recanati wanted the predicates that embarrass — difference,
limit, number, judgement against mercy — kept off God: Cordovero grants it, and
locates them in the outer set. "The change and the division are not in Him, but
in the outer sefirot." R. David wanted the sefirot not to be tools external to
the divine, since everyone concedes they are emanated rather than created and
"the emanated is not separate from the emanator": Cordovero grants that too, of
the inner set. His two images make the structure visible — water taking the
colour of the vessels it is poured into, and better, a sunbeam passing through
ten lamp-glasses of ten different colours, where the colour is real in the
glass, apparent in the light, and never in the sun.

Two things are worth holding onto about the shape of this answer, because they
are what a comparison with any other doctrine of internal articulation has to
reckon with.

**It is a resolution by multiplication of terms, not by definition of one.**
The dispute was over what a sefirah *is*; the answer is that there are two
kinds of thing that the word had been naming at once. That is available in
Kabbalah in a way it is not available wherever the terms have been fixed by a
council: a tradition that answers this way has not decided between the two
readings so much as declined to.

**The apophasis is unaffected, and symmetrical in time.** Emanating the ten
changes nothing about what may be said of the Emanator: "in Him we can neither
speak nor depict nor predicate — not judgement, not mercy," and this holds
"neither before the emanation, nor now after the emanation". Even Keter, first
of the emanated, has name and depiction denied of it. So the doubling adds a
rank inside the articulation without moving the boundary of what the
articulation can say about the One who does the articulating.

Cordovero's own warrant is *Patach Eliyahu*, which he quotes entire and reads
as already containing the distinction — lights that bear names beside a One who
has "no specific name", and the line about withdrawal leaving the names "like a
body without a soul" (כגופא בלא נשמתא), which he then reuses of his own outer
sefirot in Hebrew, כגוף בלא נשמה, at the end of the same chapter (see
[[tikkunei-ha-zohar--202609011531]]). That is a strong reading of the passage
rather than the only one, and this note claims no more for it than that
Cordovero rests on it.
