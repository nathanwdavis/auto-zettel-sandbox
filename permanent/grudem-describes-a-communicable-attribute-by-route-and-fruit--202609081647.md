Every Grudem attribute this base had on file until now was an incommunicable
one, and they share a form: each denies something creaturely of God and then
fences the denial at the point where God still acts or feels — independence
with creation's giving him joy, unchangeableness with his acting and
responding, unity with the emphasis of different attributes in different
contexts ([[grudems-incommunicable-attributes-are-denials-fenced-where-god-acts--202609050612]]).
The same shape recurs under spirituality ("has no parts or dimensions") and
under knowledge ("not divided into parts", cashed out as a denial of counting,
recalling, inferring and learning).

The close of the wisdom section (2nd ed., pp. 232-233) is the first material
here from the other half of the taxonomy, and the description changes kind
completely. Nothing about wisdom is stated as a denial. What the passage gives
instead, in four moves, is a procedure and a test
([[grudem-closes-gods-wisdom-in-the-readers-own-life--202609081645]]): how the
attribute is asked for (petition on the promise of James 1:5), where it mainly
comes from ("primarily from reading and obeying his Word"), what starts it (the
fear of the Lord), and how a claim to have it is checked — humility rather than
pride, "a gentle and peaceful spirit" rather than arrogance, a continual walk in
dependence. An incommunicable attribute is described by drawing a boundary
around God; this communicable one is described by naming the road from God to a
creature and the marks left at the far end of it.

Two further things this passage settles that the earlier notes could not.

**The categories are graded, not binary.** [[grudem-divine-attributes-incommunicable-and-communicable--202609050547]]
recorded, as an explicit gap, that the excerpts on file did not show whether
Grudem holds a strict boundary or a spectrum between his two classes, and that
"communicable" was being used there only as the label for the other box.
Grudem's own second-edition sentence is "God's wisdom is, of course, in part
communicable to us." *In part* is a degree word, and it makes the class not *the
attributes creatures have* but *the attributes creatures have in a measure*. That
is a partial answer to the gap, from the right edition, in Grudem's words.

**What is defined is the creature's share, not God's attribute.** The gloss
"This wisdom, or skill in living a life pleasing to God" is attached to the
wisdom that has just been asked for and received — the human possession. The
passage therefore does not say that God's wisdom *is* that skill held in a
higher degree; it says the shared portion is that skill. The question of how the
creaturely term and the divine one are related — the question classical theology
handles under analogical predication — is exactly where this would be decided,
and the passage does not raise it.

**Limits.** One passage, and the closing pastoral one of its section rather
than its definitional opening: the chapter's formal definition of God's wisdom
is not in the capture, which is why the second point above can be stated as an
absence but not as Grudem's considered view. The contrast the note is built on
also crosses grades of evidence, and this should not be forgotten in citing it:
the incommunicable definitions are first-edition sentences reaching this base
through third parties, while the wisdom material is second-edition text supplied
by the owner with page numbers. A difference of register between the two halves
of the taxonomy is the natural reading, but a difference of edition or of the
compiler's hand cannot be fully ruled out until the second edition's own
incommunicable definitions are on file. None of the other communicable
attributes has been captured.
