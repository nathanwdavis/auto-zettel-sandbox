One inference appears twice in this book with opposite functions. In chapter 12
it is a concession Grudem makes against himself: "If God knows all our thoughts,
words, and actions long before they occur, then there must be some sense in
which our choices are not absolutely free" — the price of keeping exhaustive
foreknowledge, and the reason Pinnock, accepting the same step, gave the
foreknowledge up instead
([[grudem-and-pinnock-accept-the-same-entailment-and-give-up-opposite-sides-of-it--202609061922]]).
In chapter 16 the identical inference is the weapon. Against the Arminian who
wants foreknowledge without foreordination — Cottrell: "I affirm that God has a
true foreknowledge of future free-will choices without himself being the agent
that causes them or renders them certain" — Grudem answers that foreknowledge
alone already does the damage: "even if God did not plan or cause things to
happen, the fact that they are foreknown means that they will certainly come
about," and therefore "If our future choices are known, then they are fixed. And
if they are fixed, then they are not 'free' in the Arminian sense (undetermined
or uncaused)"
([[grudem-answers-the-arminian-by-comparing-unanswered-questions--202609081825]],
from [[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]]).

That symmetry is the point. The entailment is not a cost peculiar to Calvinism
that the Arminian escapes by declining to say God *ordains*; it applies to
anyone who says God *knows*. Grudem pays it openly in chapter 12 and then shows
that the most common Arminian position pays it too without noticing, which is
why he can call open theism "the most consistent Arminian position" — a
concession about internal consistency, not about truth, since he rejects open
theism on the ground that its predictions could be wrong. The three Arminian
answers thus sort by how much of the entailment each absorbs: foreknowledge
without foreordination denies it and (on Grudem's reading) is refuted by it,
open theism accepts it and pays with God's knowledge of the future, and
Grudem accepts it and pays with libertarian freedom.

This amends a bounded claim the base made earlier. Note 202609061922 observed
that in chapter 12 "the position that would deny the entailment itself, and keep
both, is not represented," and bounded that observation explicitly to that
section rather than to the literature. Chapter 16 supplies exactly that position
— Cottrell's — states it at length and in its own words, and argues that it does
not in fact keep both. The earlier note is not withdrawn; its "not represented"
was true of the section it described, and the missing party turns out to be
represented four chapters later and answered there.

**Limits.** Grudem's inference from *foreknown* to *fixed* to *not free* is
asserted, not defended; he does not engage the standard reply that certainty of
a foreknown event need not make it necessary, which is the move a defender of
foreknowledge-without-foreordination would reach for, and no such defender is
read directly here. Cottrell is quoted from one essay in a volume this
repository has not read, and Grudem's own footnote records that Cottrell differs
from Pinnock and Rice within the same book, so "the Arminian position" in this
note means the position as Grudem assembles it. Whether the chapter-12
concession and the chapter-16 argument are meant by Grudem as one thought is not
stated by him; the pairing is this base's, drawn across two owner captures.
