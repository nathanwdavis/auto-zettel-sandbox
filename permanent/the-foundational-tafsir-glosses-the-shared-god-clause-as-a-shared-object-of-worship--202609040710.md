Asked in its own language rather than in the modern debate's, the classical
Sunni tradition does answer the same-God question, and it answers yes.

al-Tabari (d. 310/923), commenting on Q 29:46, glosses `وَإِلَهُنَا
وَإِلَهُكُمْ وَاحِدٌ` — "our God and your God is one" — with `ومعبودنا
ومعبودكم واحد`: *and the one we worship and the one you worship is one*
([[tabari-on-the-common-word-and-the-shared-god-clause--202609040705]], ad Q
29:46). The substitution is the whole of his comment on the clause. It moves
from *ilah* to *maʿbud*, from the word "god" to the object of the act of
worship, which is the identity question and not the description question: what
is said to be one is the thing both parties worship, not the account either
gives of it.

**The absence of argument is the finding, not a gap in it.** In the same verse
al-Tabari canvasses three competing readings of a different clause, weighs them,
names the strongest, answers an objection to it, and refuses an abrogation claim
on a stated methodological principle. He had the apparatus and used it earlier
in the same comment. On the shared-God clause he supplies a synonym and moves
on, with no dissenting view recorded. He does adduce prophetic reports here —
this is the point to be exact about — but what they support is the *instruction*
and its occasion: do not believe the People of the Book and do not disbelieve
them, say this instead
([[the-shared-god-formula-is-issued-as-what-to-say-when-judgement-must-be-suspended--202609040715]]).
Not one of them is offered in support of the identification itself, and none is
needed, because nothing in the passage treats it as in doubt. A commentator who
canvasses dissent where dissent exists and canvasses none here is evidence that
the identity of the object of worship was not, in this tradition at this date, a
contested question.

**Why this bears on the base's framing rather than merely adding a voice.** The
reference/description distinction was drawn here from Christian and secular
sources ([[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]),
and the standing worry about it is that it may be a Christian-philosophical
apparatus imposed on a dispute one side declines to have in those terms
([[the-muslim-consensus-statement-makes-common-ground-out-of-command-not-reference--202609040115]]).
This source cuts against that worry in one direction and confirms it in
another. It cuts against it because al-Tabari's gloss lands squarely on the
reference horn — he says the worshipped thing is one — which is what the
distinction predicts a position can do. It confirms it because he does not
*treat* it as a horn: nothing hangs on the affirmation, no consequence is drawn
from it, and it is not offered as a concession or a bridge.

**Every English rendering here is this repository's own working translation of
the captured Arabic**, and the Arabic is printed beside each rendering that
carries weight, so the translation can be checked rather than trusted
([[tabari-jami-al-bayan-on-3-64-and-29-46--202609040700]]).

**The limit that keeps this from being an irenic result.** In the same passage,
about the same people, al-Tabari endorses the reading of `إِلا الَّذِينَ
ظَلَمُوا مِنْهُمْ` ("except those of them who do wrong") on which the exception
picks out those who refuse the *jizya* and make war — the ones, in his wording,
`جادلوهم بالقتال`, to be disputed with by fighting — and glosses "and to Him we
are submitters" as submission in obedience to command and prohibition. Shared
object of worship, juristic subordination, and no tension between them. So this
note asserts only what the gloss asserts: that in Islam's foundational
commentary the object of worship is affirmed to be one. It does not assert that
this affirmation was doing ecumenical work, and the same passage shows it was
not.

**What would strengthen it.** One commentator is one commentator. al-Qurtubi,
al-Razi and Ibn Kathir on the same verse are uncaptured, and so is the juristic
literature on whether Christians count as *mushrikun*; if the gloss is
traditional rather than idiosyncratic, that is where it would show. Filed as
open work rather than assumed here.
