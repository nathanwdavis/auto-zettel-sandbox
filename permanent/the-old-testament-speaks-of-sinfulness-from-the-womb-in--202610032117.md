Among the Old Testament passages on file, two groups bear on this note's point
(the literature note,
[[the-old-testament-passages-place-sinfulness-at-conception--202610032106]],
sorts them more finely).

The first locates sinfulness at the start of life: conceived "in sin" (Ps 51:5),
"estranged from the womb" (Ps 58:3), "a transgressor from the womb" (Isa 48:8),
not clean being "born of a woman" (Job 15:14; compare 14:4), the heart's
imagination "evil from his youth" (Gen 8:21). These are psalms, wisdom,
prophecy and narrative. The second makes liability for sin personal: "every
man shall be put to death for his own sin" (Deut 24:16); "The son shall not
bear the iniquity of the father" (Ezek 18:20). These are law and prophecy.

The two groups answer different questions. The first describes a condition from
the womb or from youth, said of the wicked (Ps 58:3), of one addressee (Isa
48:8), or of anyone born of a woman (Job 15:14); the second rules on who is
punished for a sin committed. In the
captured passages neither group touches the other's question: Ezekiel 18 says
nothing about the womb, and the psalms say nothing about whose deed is
punished.

The Westminster Confession's proof texts for the corruption conveyed to Adam's
posterity are Gen 5:3; Job 14:4; 15:14; Psa 51:5 (6.3), three of them from the
first group (Gen 5:3 is not captured here), and for the inclination to evil Gen
6:5; 8:21 (6.4), the second of them in the first group. Neither
Deuteronomy 24:16 nor Ezekiel 18 appears among the chapter's proofs
([[the-westminster-confession-derives-all-actual--202610032108]]), and the
chapter does not say how the imputed guilt of 6.3 stands with Ezekiel's rule.
A reader who holds both the imputed guilt of 6.3 and Ezekiel's rule has to
supply that account from elsewhere.
