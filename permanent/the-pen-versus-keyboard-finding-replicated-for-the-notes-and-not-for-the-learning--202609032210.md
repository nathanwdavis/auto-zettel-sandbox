A replication can come apart in the middle: the manipulation does what it was
said to do, and the consequence it was said to have does not follow. That is
what happened to the best-known experiment on note taking, and the split is
the finding.

Mueller and Oppenheimer's 2014 claim had two joints
([[mueller-and-oppenheimers-claim-is-about-shallower-processing--202609032206]]).
First, laptop note takers transcribe: they write more words and more of the
lecturer's own words. Second, that transcription is "shallower processing" and
costs them conceptual understanding. The preregistered direct replication by
Urry and colleagues, in the same journal, reproduced the first joint and not
the second
([[urry-et-al-replicate-the-notes-effect-and-not-the-learning-effect--202609032205]]).
Laptop takers again wrote far more (230.69 vs 136.16 words) and again overlapped
the lecture verbatim far more (12.97% vs 8.13%), both p < .001. On the quiz the
conceptual difference was negligible and pointed the other way, Hedges' *g* =
−0.13, 95% CI [−0.45, 0.20], statistically equivalent to zero. Pooling eight
closely matched studies put total quiz performance at *g* = 0.04, 95% CI
[−0.13, 0.20] — and equivalent to zero within the bound the original study had
33% power to detect, meaning the pooled effect is too small for the original
study to have found it.

The reason this is worth a note rather than a correction is what survives. The
behavioural claim is robust: give people a keyboard and they transcribe. The
mechanism claim survives only as a correlation — in both studies, notes with
more words went with better scores and notes with more verbatim overlap with
worse — and the replicators, who had every reason to keep it, refuse the causal
reading themselves, because nobody manipulated either variable and both may
proxy for motivation or interest. So "transcription is shallow" is at present a
plausible story about a real behaviour with no experimental support for its
consequence.

What this base should take from it is a rule about which half of a two-joint
claim it is leaning on. Verbatim capture *is* different from reframing in one's
own words; the difference shows up reliably in what gets written down. What
does not follow, on this evidence, is that the difference shows up in what the
writer learns. Anyone citing "the pen is mightier than the keyboard" for the
second is citing a headline the literature has not held up
— and the gap between the two is the same gap, one level down, as the one in
[[no-experiment-in-this-base-tests-a-slip-box-claim--202609032215]].
