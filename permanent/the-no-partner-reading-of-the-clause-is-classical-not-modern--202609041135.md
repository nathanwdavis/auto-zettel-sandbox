Six of the eleven commentaries in this base gloss `وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد`, and five
of them read it the same way: as the denial that God has a partner. The sixth is
al-Tabari. The split is not between old and new.

**The five who read it as denial of partnership.** al-Tusi (d. 460) and al-Tabrisi (d. 548)
give `لا شريك له` — He has no partner — and the second gives the first's words
letter for letter. Tantawi (d. 1431/2010) gives `لا شريك له لا فى ذاته ولا فى
صفاته`, no partner *neither in His essence nor in His attributes*. al-Muyassar
expands into the three-part division of *tawhid*; al-Sa'di turns the clause into
a premise for disputation, `وعلى أن الإله واحد`. And al-Tabari, alone, glosses
it as a shared `معبود` — a shared object of worship
([[the-maabud-identification-is-al-tabaris-own-and-the-commentaries-after-him-do-not-repeat-it--202609040820]]).

**What this revises.** [[where-the-later-tradition-glosses-the-clause-it-reads-it-as-tawhid-against-partners--202609040825]]
drew the tawhid reading from two modern commentaries and called it what "the
*later* tradition" does. The temporal framing was an artefact of which
commentaries the base had. Push the sample back and the same reading is there in
the eleventh century, in the same words, and it is there in a commentary written
three centuries before the earliest of the modern ones. What separates al-Tabari
from the other four is not that he is early. He is *not* early relative to them
in the sense that mattered: al-Tusi wrote a century and a half after him and read
the clause differently.

**So the isolation of al-Tabari's reading tightens rather than loosens.** Every
addition since the 07:00Z cycle has looked like it might supply a second witness
for the `معبود` identification and has not. `معبود` is now 0 across al-Qurtubi,
Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, al-Sa'di, al-Muyassar, al-Razi, al-Zamakhshari, al-Tusi,
al-Tabrisi and Tantawi — ten commentaries, five centuries either side of him,
three schools and two branches of the tradition — and where any of them does
gloss the clause, the gloss is about partnership rather than about reference.
(The counts for the three added this cycle rest on one host; that limitation is
recorded in the capture headers and filed to INBOX.)

**Why the difference matters for the question this cluster serves.** "Our God and
your God is one" read as `معبود` identifies what the two parties worship. Read as
`لا شريك له` it asserts a property of God — indivisibility of worship — and
addresses the other party about it. The first is a statement about reference and
would bear directly on the same-God question. The second is a statement about
*tawhid* and bears on it only through a further premise about who satisfies the
description. Of the six commentaries that gloss the clause at all, five take the
second route; the other five commentaries in the base take neither, because they
do not gloss it. The reading that gave this cluster its most direct textual
support for co-reference is held by exactly one commentator out of eleven.

**Tantawi's addition, kept separate.** His `لا فى ذاته ولا فى صفاته` extends the
denial into the divine attributes, which is more than `لا شريك له` says. That is
a fact about his wording, taken from two independent hosts. What it implies about
trinitarian doctrine is not stated by him and is not asserted here; the
observation is recorded in
[[tantawi-extends-the-denial-of-partnership-into-the-essence-and-the-attributes--202609041125]]
with the same care.
