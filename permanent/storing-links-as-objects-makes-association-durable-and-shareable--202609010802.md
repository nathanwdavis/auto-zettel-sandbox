An association held only in a mind is fast but perishable and private; an
association written down as a first-class stored object — a link with an
address, a name, a place in a trail — loses none of its content when
memory moves on, and can be handed to someone else. That is the design
insight of Bush's memex ([[bush-as-we-may-think--202609010800]]): he
concedes the brain's associative speed cannot be matched, picks the one
dimension where records beat neurons — permanence — and builds the whole
device around "tying two items together" as the stored, essential act,
so that trails "do not fade", replay years later, and can be photographed
out and given away, up to a "trail blazers" economy in which the link
structure itself is the inheritance
([[bush-makes-the-stored-trail-the-unit-of-selection--202609010801]]).

The claim slots between this base's two poles of note-system design. It
is the missing step after
[[topic-head-filing-optimizes-retrieval-not-connection--202609010604]]:
Locke's 1686 machinery stores entries and spends all its rigor on finding
them again, leaving association entirely in the user's head — precisely
the perishable, unshareable part. And it is the design principle that
Luhmann's practice later realizes on paper: fixed note addresses that
make links writable-down at all, a file that behaves as a partner because
its link structure holds ideas the owner's memory no longer does
([[luhmann-frames-the-slip-box-as-a-communication-partner--202609010112]],
analogous: Bush's "enlarged intimate supplement to his memory"), and the
archive-side finding that the file's value rides on its stored shortcut
links rather than on its classification
([[serendipity-in-a-note-web-rides-on-shortcut-links--202609010304]]).
The generalization beyond Bush's one imagined device — that durability
and shareability are what turn association from a private mental event
into infrastructure — is this note's own inference, offered as the reason
link-storing systems (citation networks, hypertext, zettelkästen) keep
being reinvented wherever knowledge must outlive its first knower.
