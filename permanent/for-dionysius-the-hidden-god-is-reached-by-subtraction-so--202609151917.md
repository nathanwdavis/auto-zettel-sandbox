The owner's account, Calvin's fence and Luther's wall all treat the hidden as something that could in principle be stated and is not: a set of withheld propositions. The *Mystical Theology* describes a hidden tier of a different kind, and a relation to it that is neither inquiry nor abstention ([[the-mystical-theology-makes-unknowing-the-highest-knowledge--202609151908]]).

**Not withheld propositions but a nature beyond predicates.** What lies in the "divine Darkness" is not information God has kept back; it is God as he exceeds every affirmation and every negation. The last chapter denies of the Cause of all things "soul, or mind", "knowledge or truth", "one" and "unity" and "Godhead", and then denies the denials: it "transcends all affirmation by being the perfect and unique Cause of all things, and transcends all negation by the pre-eminence of Its simple and absolute nature". This is not a known unknown in the owner's sense, because there is no question whose answer is missing; and it is not an unknown unknown either, because it is the explicit object of a discipline. It is a region beyond the question-and-answer form altogether.

**A third posture.** The relation to it is subtraction. The initiate is to "attain unto vision through the loss of sight and knowledge", stripping predicates as the sculptor strips marble, until the ascent ends "totally dumb", and there "by a rejection of all knowledge he possesses a knowledge that exceeds his understanding". Where Calvin says adore and stop, and Luther says leave alone, Dionysius says proceed, but by removal. The hidden tier is approached and even reached, in union, without anything being disclosed. Disclosure, the movement on which the owner's partition and every scripture in this cycle's set depend, is not the mechanism here at all.

**Absorbed by Aquinas, and bounded.** Aquinas quotes this treatise at the head of his article on grace: whoever is most united to God in this life "is united to Him as to one entirely unknown", and he keeps the phrase in his own answer, "united to Him as to one unknown" (ST I q.12 a.13). But he sets it on a rung: unknowing is where grace leaves the knower with respect to *what* God is, while still adding truths, and above it stands a vision in which the essence is seen. In Aquinas's ladder the Dionysian darkness is the ceiling of this life, not of knowledge ([[aquinas-bounds-every-knowledge-of-god-below-god-s-own-by--202609151915]]).

**The parallel already on file.** Kabbalah's *Ein Sof*, which has no specific name and no specific attribute and is nevertheless addressed ([[the-nameless-infinite-defeats-reference-fixing-by-description--202609011541]]), is the same shape: a God to whom no determination applies, reached by address rather than description. The two traditions arrive at it by different routes and this base has not compared them beyond noting the shape.

**Limits.** Rolt's translation and Rolt's footnotes, whose theology is his own; the Greek is not on file. Whether the Dionysian region should be counted a fourth cell of the owner's account, or as lying outside the account's premise that the hidden is knowledge God holds and could share, is left open here; the treatise's own answer would be the latter.
