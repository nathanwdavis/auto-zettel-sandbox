The uniqueness route from Anselm's formula to "same God" has a hidden premise:
that each tradition's worship actually *refers via* the maximal description.
The formula guarantees at most one satisfier (see
[[anselm-proslogium--202609010830]]); it does not guarantee that "God" and
"Allah", as living names inside communities, get their reference from that
description rather than from somewhere else.

That premise is exactly what the semantics of names puts in question. On the
account Bogardus and Urban bring to this dispute, a name refers to "the
dominant source of information in that name's 'dossier'" -- so whether the
divine names co-refer "depends on how much weight is given to what type of
information in the dossiers of these two names" (see
[[bogardus-urban-how-to-tell-whether-christians-and-muslims--202609010833]]).
If what dominates a tradition's dossier is revelation-specific -- the one who
spoke at Sinai, the one who raised Jesus, the one who recited to Muhammad --
then a shared philosophical description sitting further down the dossier
settles nothing, and their two-part counterfactual test, not the ontological
argument, does the deciding. Their answer to the same-God question is
accordingly "it depends," not Anselm's-formula-therefore-yes.

A second limit is independent of semantics: co-reference may not be what the
dispute is about. Bogardus and Urban close by noting that worship may require
more than referring to the right being, and Tachin's position makes that the
whole question -- worship must be trinitarian and accepted by God, however
reference falls out (see [[tachin-do-they-worship-the-same-god--202608311925]]).
The ontological argument is silent on acceptability of worship.

So what the argument licenses is a conditional, not a verdict: *insofar as* a
worshipper's aim is fixed by the maximal description, all such worshippers
share one target. How much actual Jewish, Christian, and Muslim worship is
aimed that way is a question about those communities' words and practices --
one that Anselm's proof, by design, never touches.
