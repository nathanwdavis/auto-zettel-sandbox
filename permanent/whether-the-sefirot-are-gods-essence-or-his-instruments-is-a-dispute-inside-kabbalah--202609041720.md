Kabbalah does not hold one view about what the ten sefirot are. Whether they
are the essence of the Emanator (*atzmut*) or His instruments (*kelim*) is a
question the tradition put to itself and argued, and the argument is on the
record in the work that names it: Moses Cordovero's *Pardes Rimmonim* devotes
its fourth gate to it, *Sha'ar Etzem ve-Kelim*, and opens by saying the gate
exists because "a great confusion has fallen among the later kabbalists" (see
[[cordovero-states-the-essence-vessel-dispute-and-answers-it-with-both--202609041710]]).

The two sides are named and quoted there. Menachem Recanati holds that the
sefirot are "like the craftsman's vessels, with which he performs his work",
so that what is said of them — judgement and mercy, right and left, number,
limit — is not said of God at all; R. Yehudah Hayyat sharpens it to the claim
that the emanation is a *property in* the Emanator and not the essence of the
Emanator, as a ray is not the essence of the sun. R. David, in *Magen David*,
holds the contrary: the sefirot are the divine essence, and "the difference of
the names, or the number of the sefirot, does not entail multiplicity in the
Creator", because the names track differing aspects under which one simple
essence is considered relative to its differing operations.

This matters to this repository beyond Kabbalah, because a claim already
standing here assumed it. Note
[[internal-plurality-is-not-what-divides-the-monotheisms--202609011543]] reads
*Patach Eliyahu* in the essentialist sense, tags itself `contested` for exactly
that reason, and says in its own text that "how much reality those distinctions
have is the tradition's own long argument — whether the sefirot are God's
essence (*atzmut*) or His instruments (*kelim*)". Until now that sentence rested
on nothing captured: the INBOX entry of 2026-09-03 recorded that no source in
this base addressed the question. It is now sourced, and it is stronger than the
earlier note assumed. The dispute is not a modern scholar's reconstruction of a
tension; it is a sixteenth-century kabbalist reporting a fight between two
named kabbalists and refereeing it.

What follows for the reading of *Patach Eliyahu* is a caution rather than a
correction. That passage's ten are "produced", "directed", given garments and
bodies, and the One is "concealed" in them (see
[[patach-eliyahu-names-the-sefirot-and-leaves-ein-sof-nameless--202609011536]]);
those predicates are the instrumentalist's evidence, and Recanati's reading
absorbs every one of them. Cordovero's answer is not to deny that but to deny
that the two readings exclude each other — which is a further claim, not a
retreat, and is treated separately in
[[cordovero-settles-the-essence-vessel-question-by-doubling-the-sefirot--202609041725]].
