The third thing the knowability doctrine and the design arguments share is a
limit, and it is the same limit because it comes from the shared channel:
works disclose maker-attributes, not the maker's identity or inner life.

The doctrine states its own bound. Hodge's knowability chapter concedes in
the same breath that affirms knowledge: "our knowledge of God is partial and
inadequate. There is infinitely more in God than we have any idea of" (see
[[hodge-systematic-theology-volume-1--202609011033]], Part I ch. IV). Calvin
bounds it from the other end -- "His essence indeed is incomprehensible" --
and then hands the sequence over to Scripture, the next chapter's title
making natural disclosure explicitly insufficient as a guide (see
[[calvin-institutes-of-the-christian-religion--202609011032]], I.v-vi). The
proof text itself is content-thin and consequence-heavy: what the creation
makes clearly seen is "his eternal power and Godhead" -- two attributes --
and the stated yield is not salvation but inexcusability, "so that they are
without excuse" (Rom. 1:20; see
[[general-revelation-passages--202609011034]]).

The design arguments hit the same wall from their side: the inference from
contrivance concludes to *an* intelligent author, without fixing how many,
which one, or whether it is the God any tradition worships -- the
maker-not-one-maker gap (see
[[the-design-argument-concludes-to-a-maker-not-to-one-maker--202609011016]]).
That is not an accident of Paley's or Hodge's formulation but the channel's
own bandwidth showing through: an argument that reads only the works can
report only what works can carry, which is just what the doctrine, honestly
stated, always said they carry -- power and deity, enough to leave one
without excuse, not enough to name the maker. On both routes the thicker
content -- identity, triunity, redemption -- arrives only by special
revelation, which is why both Calvin's chapter order and the design
literature's "further identification step" end at the same handoff.
