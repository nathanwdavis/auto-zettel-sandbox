Splitting a note at its seams leaves each piece linkable on its own. A note carrying three ideas can only ever be cited as a lump, so its reuse is limited to contexts wanting all three at once.

This note is still an assertion of the repository's own, not a sourced claim.
What historical evidence there is bears on only half of it:
[[systematic-method-is-what-makes-a-note-usable-by-someone-who-did-not-take-it--202609032110]]
shows from Blair's early modern material that notes really were reused by
people who never did the reading, and that predictable *form* is what made
that possible — but the notes in that evidence are filed under topical heads,
and nothing in it tests one-idea-per-note as the unit. The atomicity claim
above remains unsourced and should be read as a design position.

Nor does the experimental literature help, which a 2026-09-03 search
established rather than assumed: the note-taking experiments manipulate how a
lecture is captured and measure a quiz the same day, so they never get as far
as the unit a note is filed in
([[no-experiment-in-this-base-tests-a-slip-box-claim--202609032215]]).
