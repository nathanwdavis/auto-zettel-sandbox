Two works of *fiqh* captured in the same cycle, from two traditions that do not
read each other, cite the same two eighth-century Imams — Muhammad al-Baqir and
Ja'far al-Sadiq — for opposite rules about marrying a woman of the People of the
Book. The obvious reaction is that one of them has it wrong. The obvious reaction
is wrong, and what is actually going on is more useful.

**The Zaydi manual has them permitting.** Having stated the prohibition as its own
school's rule, *Sharh al-Azhar* records the dissent: `وعن الصادق ‏ والباقر
وأبي حنيفة والشافعي: أنه يجوز نكاح الكتابيات` (the gap after `الصادق` is an
invisible bidirectional mark in the OCR, not a missing word) — from al-Sadiq, al-Baqir, Abu Hanifa
and al-Shafi'i, that marrying women of the People of the Book is permitted. A
Zaydi text is grouping the two Shi'i Imams with the two Sunni founders, on the
lenient side, against its own position.

**Imami *tafsir* has them prohibiting.** This base has held since 14:00Z that
al-Tusi carries the inversion of the abrogation as *naskh* reports from al-Baqir
and al-Sadiq, and that al-Tabataba'i had to answer those same reports to reach
his dissent. On the face of it, the two traditions flatly contradict each other
about what the Imams said.

**The Imami *fiqh* settles it by transmitting both, within a few lines of each
other.** Al-Najafi assembles the strict case from Zurara: `لا ينبغى نكاح أهل
الكتاب` — marriage to the People of the Book is not fitting — and, asked where
the prohibition lies, `قال : قوله : ولا تمسكوا بعصم الكوافر`; and the *naskh*
report, `قال : هذه منسوخة بقوله : ولاتمسكوا بعصم الكوأفر`. Both from Abu Ja'far,
al-Baqir. He then assembles the permitting case — `للنصوص المستفيضة أو المتواقرة`
[read: `المتواترة`], the widely transmitted or mass-transmitted texts — and it
includes Abu Ja'far again, through Muhammad b. Muslim: `فقال : لا بأس به » أما علمت أنه كان تحت
طللحة` — there is no harm in it; do you not know that Talha had a Jewish wife in
the Prophet's time?

**So neither tradition is misreporting.** The corpus contains both, and the
question each school faced was not *what did al-Baqir say* but *which of the
things transmitted from him governs*. Al-Tusi took the *naskh* reports and built
the school position on them; al-Najafi took the permitting reports, called them widely
transmitted, and answered the others. The Zaydi manual, reading the same
transmitted material from outside, saw the lenient reports and attributed the
lenient position. Each report is a selection made under a rule for handling
conflicting narrations, which is what a legal tradition is for.

**Why this is worth a note rather than a footnote.** It is a specific instance of
a failure this base keeps meeting in general form. A one-clause attribution of a
position to a school, or to an authority, presents a *conclusion of a weighing
process* as if it were a fact about who said what. The base has now watched the
same flattening happen at four levels: al-Sabuni flattening two Shi'i schools;
`بعض الزيدية` turning out to name a real division; the Imami manual recording
`روایتان` where the summary recorded a position; and here, an Imam transmitted on
both sides of the question he is cited to settle. The pattern is not that
attributions are unreliable. It is that an attribution is the *output* of an
argument, and quoting it without the argument loses precisely the part that
would let a reader tell whether it is contested.

**A cross-cutting detail that makes the point sharper.** Both manuals cite the
same Companion precedent, Talha's Jewish wife — the Zaydi apparatus alongside
'Uthman's and Hudhayfa's marriages, al-Najafi through Muhammad b. Muslim from
al-Baqir. The Zaydi text cites it inside the report of the view it rejects;
al-Najafi cites it as evidence for the view he adopts. The same fact, in both
traditions, doing opposite work.

**Limits.** Two works, one host each, and both read in excerpt. This note claims
what the two captures show — that each tradition's *fiqh* cites the Imams as
described, and that the Imami capture contains reports from al-Baqir on both
sides. It makes no claim about the authenticity of any of these reports, which is
a question about *isnad* that this base has no material to touch.
