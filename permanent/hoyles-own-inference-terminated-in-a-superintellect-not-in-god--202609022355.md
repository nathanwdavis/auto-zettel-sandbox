The gap between what a design argument concludes and what a theist believes is
usually argued in the abstract. The carbon resonance supplies a case where it
can be watched happening to a particular person, with the argument running at
maximum force and still stopping short.

Fred Hoyle had the strongest possible claim on the evidence: he predicted the
7.65 MeV state of carbon-12 that makes the triple-alpha process fast enough to
build carbon at all, and he read the design inference off it himself. His
confidence is at ceiling — "The numbers one calculates from the facts seem to
me so overwhelming as to put this conclusion almost beyond question" — and the
inference is stated in full: the carbon atom is "a fix", and "a superintellect
has monkeyed with physics, as well as with chemistry and biology" (see
[[hoyle-the-universe-past-and-present-reflections--202609022346]], p. 12; and
[[hoyle-reads-a-superintellect-off-the-carbon-resonance-and-stops-there--202609022350]]).

And what he affirms at the end of it is "an enormous intelligence abroad in the
universe" — an intelligence located *inside* the cosmos, invoked to "write
blind forces out of astronomy" and to make interstellar grains and living cells
"purposeful tools". Not a creator of the universe; an agent within it. The word
"God" appears once on that page, inside a reported Einstein aphorism about
whether a scientific result can be trusted, and nowhere in what Hoyle himself
concludes.

That is the maker-not-one-maker gap (see
[[the-design-argument-concludes-to-a-maker-not-to-one-maker--202609011016]])
observed rather than deduced, and it carries further than the deduction does.
The abstract version says the inference *does not entail* a unique
transcendent God. This says something stronger and harder to argue away: run at
its most persuasive, in the mind of the physicist with the best title to the
evidence and no theistic prior to protect, the inference actually terminated
somewhere else. An argument that does not arrive at a belief's content cannot
be that belief's foundation, whatever else it may do for someone who holds it
on other grounds.
