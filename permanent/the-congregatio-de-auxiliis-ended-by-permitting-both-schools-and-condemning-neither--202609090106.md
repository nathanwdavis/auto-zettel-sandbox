The one time the question this map keeps meeting — how an infallible divine
result and a free human act consist — was put to an institution with the
authority to settle it, the institution declined. Astrain's article gives the
frame and the outcome. The frame is the pair itself: efficacious grace
"obtains, infallibly, man's consent," and "in so acting, man is free";
"Hence the question: How can these two things--the infallible result and
liberty--be harmonized?" The Dominicans answered with "physical premotion and
predetermination," the Jesuits with *scientia media*, and each charged the
other with the heresy on its own flank: the Jesuits "tended toward
Pelagianism," the Dominicans "seemed in consequence to lean towards
Calvinism." After twenty years, "eighty-five conferences in the presence of
the popes," and a commission that twice reported for condemning Molina's book
and was told by Clement VIII to go back, "the question was not solved but an
end was put to the disputes. The pope's decree communicated (5 September,
1607) to both Dominicans and Jesuits, allowed each party to defend its own
doctrine, enjoined each from censoring or condemning the opposite opinion,
and commanded them to await, as loyal sons of the Church, the final decision
of the Apostolic See. That decision, however, has not been reached"
([[astrain-records-that-the-congregatio-de-auxiliis-ended-without-a-verdict--202609090058]],
from [[astrain-congregatio-de-auxiliis-catholic-encyclopedia--202609090053]]). Pohle, writing the dogmatic article, draws the moral the
historian leaves implicit: "The problem seems to lie so far beyond the
horizon of the human mind, that man will never be able fully to penetrate its
mystery"
([[pohle-locates-the-molinist-thomist-dispute-in-the-medium-of-foreknowledge--202609090057]],
from [[pohle-molinism-catholic-encyclopedia--202609090052]]).

Two things about the decree matter for this map. First, it is a ruling on
what may be said, not on what is true: each party may defend, neither may
condemn, judgement is reserved. That is the shape the same-God cluster found
in al-Tabari's handling of the Qur'an's shared-God clause, a formula issued
for what to say while judgement must be suspended
([[the-shared-god-formula-is-issued-as-what-to-say-when-judgement-must-be-suspended--202609040715]]),
reached by a different tradition on a different question. Second, it is the
institutional counterpart, and the opposite, of the move Grudem makes at the
end of his providence chapter. Grudem, having conceded two questions his
position cannot answer, decides between two incomplete positions anyway, by
which residue honours God more
([[the-dispute-is-decided-by-which-residue-honours-god-more--202609081831]]).
Paul V, with two incomplete positions before him and a commission that had
already decided, refused to decide, and the refusal has held for four
centuries.

For an argument that God's sovereignty and human freedom are both true, the
decree is a precedent of a particular kind. It does not say the two are
compatible. It says that a church can bind its members to both propositions —
Pohle notes that both the causality of grace and the freedom of consent are
Tridentine definitions — while forbidding either school to make its mechanism
a test of orthodoxy. Holding both doctrines is required; holding a theory of
how they consist is permitted.

**Limits.** The wording of the decree is Astrain's report of it; the text is
not on file. Astrain was a Jesuit and Pohle a Catholic dogmatician, so the
Dominican side is known here only from the other party. The analogy with
al-Tabari's rule is this base's and is one of form only, a rule of speech
under suspended judgement. The 1607 decree was a disciplinary act inside one
communion and says nothing about the Reformed-Arminian dispute Grudem's
chapter surveys; that parallel is drawn here, not by any source.
