There is a difference between observing that two traditions both articulate
God internally and claiming that one tradition's articulation entails the
other's. The earliest instance this repository has captured of the comparison
between Kabbalah's articulated God and the Trinity is the second kind of
claim, and much the stronger one.

Pico's cabalistic theses of 1486 assert that every Hebrew kabbalist, reasoning
from the principles of Kabbalah itself, is "inevitably compelled to concede"
about the Trinity and each divine person exactly what Catholic faith holds,
"without addition, diminution, or variation" -- and that the same principles
manifestly refute Arians and Sabellians into the bargain (see
[[pico-makes-kabbalah-compel-the-catholic-trinity--202609011806]]). That is not
a comparison. It is an entailment claim about someone else's tradition,
advanced to convert its adherents.

The distinction matters for how a weaker comparison may be argued. This
repository holds that the internal-distinction axis fails to sort Judaism from
Christianity, because Kabbalah is a Jewish position that answers yes to it (see
[[internal-plurality-is-not-what-divides-the-monotheisms--202609011543]]). That
claim survives Pico's collapsing, and needs to be kept clear of it: from "both
traditions articulate God internally" nothing follows about whether the
articulations are the same, and Pico's theses are the standing demonstration of
how quickly a structural parallel can be inflated into an identity claim once
there is something to win by it. Noticing that two doctrines share a shape is
cheap; showing that they share a referent is the whole difficulty (see
[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]).

Pico's own execution shows the seam. Where thesis 6 finally makes the mapping
concrete, the triad he assigns to the three persons is *sekhel*, *ha-maskil*,
*ha-muskal* -- intellect, knower, known -- a philosophical structure, not the
ten sefirot at all. The argument that was supposed to run on Kabbalah's own
foundations reaches, at its one specific moment, for something more tractable
than the sefirot: a triad already shaped like a Trinity.

One asymmetry to hold on to: Pico is a Christian apologist, and this note
records what Christian kabbalism asserted, not what kabbalists conceded. The
Jewish answers to this argument are not yet captured in this repository, so the
dispute is represented here from one side only.
