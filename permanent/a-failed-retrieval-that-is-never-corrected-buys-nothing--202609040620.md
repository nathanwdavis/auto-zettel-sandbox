Testing yourself is not what produces the benefit. Retrieving successfully is, or
else being told the answer afterwards. Where a study's participants got half or
fewer of the items right on the initial test and were never given feedback, the
pooled advantage of testing over restudy was `g = 0.03, CI [-0.21, 0.27],
p = .79` — nothing, with the interval straddling zero
([[rowland-meta-analysed-the-laboratory-half-and-excluded-the-classroom--202609040605]],
advance online p. 15).

The gradient above that point is orderly and steep. Still without feedback, 51%
to 75% initial success gives `g = 0.29`, and above 75% success `g = 0.56`. Add
feedback and initial success stops mattering: `g = 0.73`, the largest cell in the
analysis, "regardless of retrieval success" (p. 15). Feedback is separately the
strongest categorical moderator in the paper — 0.73 with against 0.39 without —
and the author is careful to add that even the no-feedback studies still produce
a reliable effect overall, because most retrieval attempts in them succeeded.

**Why this is the most transferable finding in the retrieval literature for this
base.** The claim in
[[retrieval-not-re-exposure-is-what-makes-learning-last--202609040340]] is that
pulling something out of your own head beats meeting it again. Read carelessly,
that says the effort is what pays. This says the opposite about the failed
attempts: effort that ends in a blank, uncorrected, is worth zero, and it is the
*successful* retrieval or the correction that carries the whole effect. Whatever
mechanism is at work, it operates on information that came back or was supplied,
not on the act of straining for it.

**And it is where the note-taking analogy finally bites.** The habit this
repository is built on — write it down, come back later, try to remember why it
mattered before reading the note — is a no-feedback retrieval attempt followed
immediately by feedback, because the note is right there. On this evidence that
is the good configuration and not the compromised one: the cell with feedback is
the strongest cell in the meta-analysis. The bad configuration is the one where
the attempt fails and nothing corrects it — a note never revisited, or a
half-remembered idea acted on without checking the source. That is a claim about
structure, though, and its transfer is an inference this note is making, not
something Rowland measured. He measured undergraduates on word lists, paired
associates and prose over minutes to weeks, and the literature he pooled says
nothing about notes at all — the negative search on his text returns zero
occurrences of `note-taking`, `external memory` and `offloading`.

**What it does to the two neighbouring notes.**
[[having-the-notes-to-hand-buys-present-performance-not-durable-knowledge--202609040345]]
found that access to notes during a test bought sixteen points immediately and
nothing a week later. Set beside this, the reason is at least a candidate: when
the notes are open, no retrieval is attempted, so there is no success to
consolidate and nothing for feedback to correct — the condition is not a weak
version of retrieval practice but an absence of it. And
[[confidence-tracks-re-exposure-while-retention-tracks-retrieval--202609040350]]
gains a mechanism for the same reason: what feels like knowing tracks having
seen, and what lasts tracks having got it back.
