The two verses are built in parallel and differ in their object. "If we say
that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves" (1 John 1:8); "If we say that we
have not sinned, we make him a liar" (1:10). The first denies a present
possession, sin that is had; the second denies a record, sins that were done.
Between them stands confession of "our sins" (1:9), in the plural. See
[[the-new-testament-passages-derive-sinful-deeds-from-a--202610032107]].

The writer includes himself in the "we", and the Westminster Confession uses
the pair for the point that sin is still had after conversion. Its proof texts
for "This corruption of nature, during this life, doth remain in those that
are regenerated" (6.5) end with 1 John 1:8, 10
([[the-westminster-confession-derives-all-actual--202610032108]]).

The same letter defines the act, "sin is the transgression of the law" (3:4),
and says "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin" (3:9). On this note's
reading 3:9 is about committing, not having, because 1:8 has already
called it self-deception for "we" to say we have no sin. The Confession cites
3:4 for its claim that every sin, "both original and actual", is a
transgression of God's law (6.6).
