When a quantity that compounds draws on a quantity that grows by fixed
increments, no increment size saves the supply side: any positive
compound rate eventually overtakes any linear rate, so the gap between
them is closed either by breaking the compounding loop or by a check
pressing on the compounder — never by the supply catching up. Malthus's
1798 ratio argument is the clean primary case
([[malthus-an-essay-on-the-principle-of-population--202609010702]]): he
grants subsistence an increment he calls beyond "any possible exertions
of mankind" — the whole earth's current produce added again every
twenty-five years — and the divergence survives the concession, 512
against 10 in nine doublings, which is why his conclusion is structural
rather than quantitative: the "strong and constantly operating check"
must fall on the multiplying side
([[malthus-pits-a-doubling-population-against-incremental-subsistence--202609010703]]).

The rate-independence is the point, and it is the same asymmetry the
base states from the other direction: a linear process is one whose
gains never join the earning base
([[linear-growth-lacks-a-feedback-loop--202701010012]]), while a
compounding one feeds every gain back in
([[reinvested-returns-compound--202701010011]]) — Malthus's populations
compound precisely because people beget people while land does not beget
land. It extends [[time-horizon-dominates-rate--202701010013]] to the
two-process case: given enough time the exponent beats not just a lower
exponent but *any* accumulation of increments. And it is the mirror of
[[long-horizon-compounding-is-bounded-by-institutional-survival--202609010509]]:
there compounding is the fragile good that institutions must protect;
here it is the pressure that some check must contain. Reading Malthus's
population case as a general law of paired growth processes — demand
compounding against linearly growing capacity of any kind — is this
note's inference, not his claim; his own empirical premises about
demography and agriculture stay with him.
