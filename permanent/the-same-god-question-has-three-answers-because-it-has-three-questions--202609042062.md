[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]
concluded that "do they worship the same God?" is "at least two questions
wearing one sentence." It is three.

1. **Reference.** Do the traditions pick out the same being?
2. **Description.** Do they give the same account of him?
3. **Address.** Is the act of worship directed to that being, under the aspect
   that makes worship worship?

The third is not a variant of the first two, and this base can now show that
rather than assert it. Maimonides' worshipper has the reference right by
stipulation — he "knows that ה' is the [true] God" — and fails anyway, because
his act is addressed to a creature. Aquinas' Catholic honouring a saint has, by
his own account, the reference right and the address right, because *dulia* is
rendered under a different aspect from latria and is a different virtue (see
[[worship-carries-an-addressee-condition-that-belief-cannot-satisfy--202609042060]]).
Neither case is settled by anything in questions 1 and 2.

Sorting the base's positions by the third question reorganises them. Mohler's
christological test and Tachin's "true worship of God is trinitarian and Christ
centered" are address claims, not description claims: they are not saying the
Muslim describes God wrongly but that his worship does not reach God. The
classical *tafsir* position — shared acknowledgement, disputed worship — is an
address claim on the other side. Shituf grades the address, which is why
[[shituf-grades-the-worshipper-instead-of-identifying-the-worshipped--202609011542]]
found it declining to answer the identity question: it was answering a different
one.

This also explains a pattern
[[rigidity-does-not-draw-the-line-the-same-god-dispute-draws--202609041935]]
noticed and could not account for — that the disputants' vocabulary of reference
does no semantic work. It does no semantic work because most of them are not
arguing about reference at all. They are arguing about address, in borrowed
words.
