Hodge reports that the councils "decided that the terms Father, Son, and Spirit,
were not expressive merely of relations *ad extra*, analogous to the terms,
Creator, Preserver, and Benefactor," and names that decision as the condemnation
of Sabellianism (see [[hodge-on-the-two-readings-the-councils-ruled-out--202609041715]]).

Go to the conciliar texts and that sentence is not in them. What is in them is a
name. Canon I of 381 anathematizes "that of the Sabellians" and stops (see
[[the-council-of-381-anathematizes-sabellianism-by-party-name-in-a-canon--202609042001]]).
The *ad extra* formulation, the analogy to Creator/Preserver/Benefactor, the
whole content of what was rejected — none of it is conciliar language.

The gap is visible inside the standard edition itself. Percival appends an
excursus to the canon precisely because the canon does not say what the parties
held, and fills the Sabellian entry by quoting William Bright: they "regarded
the Son and the Holy Spirit as aspects and modes of, or as emanations from, the
One Person of the Father" (see
[[the-standard-edition-has-to-supply-what-the-canon-left-as-a-name--202609042002]]).
That is a nineteenth-century scholar glossing a fourth-century name — the same
evidential class as Hodge, and no closer to the council.

Two consequences for this repository.

**The claim is not refuted, it is relocated.** That the fourth-century church
rejected modalism is not in doubt; the material for it is in Tertullian against
Praxeas, in Dionysius of Alexandria, in the Arians' own complaint that
*homoousion* sounded Sabellian. It is simply not in the conciliar act that has
been cited for it. A claim about what a council decided has to rest on what the
council wrote.

**And the reporter's gloss carries content the council never authorised.**
Bright's version puts *emanation* on the Sabellian side, alongside "aspects and
modes". Any comparison that sorts a doctrine of emanation by asking which
conciliar anathema it falls under is sorting it by Bright's taxonomy, not by
Nicaea's — and Bright and Hodge, writing thirty years apart in the same
tradition, are not two witnesses.
