The sentence that separates Molina from every decree-first account is a
marginal gloss on p. 324 of the *Concordia*: "Futura contingentia non quia
sciuntur a Deo ideo futura sunt, sed e contrario" — future contingents are
not future because they are known by God, but the reverse. The text beneath
it grants what a classical theist wants about the manner of the knowing: God
takes no knowledge from things, but knows everything "in sua essentia et in
determinatione liberae suae voluntatis." What it denies is the order of
dependence: "non quia cognoscit aliquid esse futurum, ideo illud futurum, sed
e contrario, quia illud futurum est ex suis causis, ideo cognoscere illud
esse futurum." For the conditional case the same is said on p. 318: a free
creature's turning one way or the other in a given order of things does "non
provenire ex praescientia Dei, quin potius ideo Deum id praescire, quia ipsa
res libero arbitrio praedicta libere id ipsum agere debet" (the page's
"praedicta" is its misprint for *praedita*), and the knowledge
by which God foresees, before creating it, what it would do is said to
"pendere ex eo quod ipsa pro sua libertate hoc vel illud sit factura et non e
contrario"
([[molina-distinguishes-three-knowledges-and-makes-the-middle-one-depend-on-the-creature--202609090055]],
from [[molina-concordia-liberi-arbitrii--202609090050]]).

Set beside Grudem this is not a disagreement about whether God foreknows
exhaustively; both say he does. It is a disagreement about which way the
dependence runs between the knowing and the thing known. Grudem's argument
against the Arminian is that "even if God did not plan or cause things to
happen, the fact that they are foreknown means that they will certainly come
about," so "If our future choices are known, then they are fixed"
([[the-foreknowledge-entailment-is-a-weapon-in-chapter-16--202609081829]],
from [[grudem-systematic-theology--202609050158]]). That inference treats the knowledge as what fixes the act.
Molina's apparatus runs the arrow the other way: the act — in the
conditional case, the act the creature would perform — is what fixes the
knowledge, and a knowledge fixed by the act leaves the act as free as it was.
Pohle reports the same principle as the one "Molinism is pledged to": "The
knowledge of God precedes as a guiding light the decree of His will, and His
will is in no way the source of His knowledge," and reports that this
independence from the decrees is why the Molinists "were most sharply
assailed by the Thomists," whose medium of foreknowledge is the decree
itself: "God foresees the (absolute and conditioned) free acts of man in the
eternal decrees of His own will"
([[pohle-locates-the-molinist-thomist-dispute-in-the-medium-of-foreknowledge--202609090057]],
from [[pohle-molinism-catholic-encyclopedia--202609090052]]). Grudem's entailment is a Reformed cousin of that Thomist
position, and Molina's reversal is aimed at exactly the step Grudem asserts
and, as the earlier note observed, does not defend.

The reversal is also a second way of disarming the argument from
foreknowledge to necessity, beside the one this base already holds.
Maimonides dissolves the argument by identifying God with his knowledge, so
that the inference from "known" to "necessary" never gets started
([[simplicity-is-maimonides-solvent-and-grudem-never-uses-it--202609081915]]).
Molina leaves the knowledge as knowledge and denies that it is the ground of
what it knows. Where Maimonides places the mystery in the identity of knower
and known and Grudem places it in the willingness of a determined choice,
Molina places it in the mode of the knowing: the middle knowledge works "ex
altissima et inscrutabili comprehensione cujusque liberi arbitrii" — by a
most profound and inscrutable comprehension of each free will. That is a
third placement of the residue to set beside the two in
[[the-same-wall-and-the-mystery-put-on-opposite-sides--202609081914]].

**Limits.** Molina's "e contrario" is asserted from the order of causes, not
argued against an opponent who holds that certain foreknowledge entails
necessity; his answer to that opponent lies in disputations this base has not
read. Whether a knowledge that depends on what a creature would do, before the
creature exists, can be true at all is the grounding objection
([[the-grounding-objection-is-molinisms-own-unanswered-question--202609090104]]),
which these pages do not face. Grudem and Molina are set side by side by this
base; neither addresses the other, and Grudem's "fixed" can be read as
certainty rather than causal fixity, in which case the two talk past one
another rather than contradict. The note records the opposition as it
stands on the surface of the two texts, and for that reason links the two
as a shared concept rather than a contradiction.

**Amended 2026-09-11, from the Westminster Confession as Helm and Van Horn
quote it.** This note called Grudem's entailment a Reformed cousin of the
Thomist decree-first position and said Molina's reversal was aimed at
exactly that step. The Reformed statement of the step is now on file:
chapter 3, paragraph 2 of the Confession, that God has not decreed anything
because he foresaw it as future or as what would come to pass upon such
conditions, which Helm reads as written to cut middle knowledge off
([[the-reformed-answer-to-the-grounding-objection-is-the-decree-and-the-confession-runs-opposite-to-molina--202609111825]]). That is Molina's "non e contrario" denied in so many words, so
the confessional order and Molina's are linked there as a contradiction,
where this note, comparing Molina with Grudem's looser "fixed", stopped at a
shared concept. Laing adds that Bañez held the counterfactuals of freedom
true in virtue of an act of God's will, which is the Thomist medium Pohle
reports, stated from the Dominican side. Nothing above is withdrawn.
