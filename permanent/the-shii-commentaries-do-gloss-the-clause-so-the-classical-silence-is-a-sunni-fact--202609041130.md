al-Tusi (d. 460/1067) and al-Tabrisi (d. 548/1153) both gloss
`وإلهنا وإلهكم واحد`. The classical silence this base spent four cycles
establishing is therefore a fact about the Sunni commentaries it sampled, not
about Islamic exegesis.

**What the silence was, and how far it was pushed.** Five commentaries were found
to pass over the clause without a word: al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and al-Baghawi
first, then al-Razi, then al-Zamakhshari. (Of the rest, al-Tabari glosses it as
`معبود`, and the two modern Sunni ones, al-Sa'di and al-Muyassar, gloss it as
the denial of partnership.) Each addition to the silent set killed an
explanation the base had generated for the silence. Adding al-Razi killed *genre*: the theologian's commentary is silent
too, so it is not an effect of writing as jurist, traditionist or abridger
([[the-kalam-commentary-is-silent-on-the-clause-too-so-the-silence-is-not-a-genre-effect--202609040915]]).
Adding al-Zamakhshari killed *school*: the Mu'tazili is silent too, so it is not
an effect of one theological family
([[the-mutazili-commentary-passes-over-the-clause-too-so-the-silence-is-not-a-school-effect--202609041010]]).

**The explanation the base rejected was right, at a boundary it had not tested.**
Both objections were raised inside the Sunni fold and answered inside it. The
school test used the widest doctrinal gap available *among Sunnis* — Mu'tazili
against Ash'ari and traditionalist — and, finding the silence on both sides of
it, concluded that school does not explain the silence. It does. The boundary is
simply not the one that was tested: cross into the Imami tradition and the clause
is glossed, by the first complete Imami commentary and by the one an Imami reader
is most likely to meet
([[tusi-glosses-the-shared-god-clause-as-no-partner-and-breaks-the-classical-silence--202609041115]],
[[tabrisi-gives-the-same-gloss-word-for-word-and-calls-the-common-word-a-call-to-tawhid--202609041120]]).

**Why this is a correction and not merely an addition.** 202609041010 stated its
own limit — it said in as many words that the survey was not exhaustive and that
the Shi'i tradition had not been touched. That limit has now been reached and it
was load-bearing. The narrow claim that note makes survives intact: within the
Sunni commentaries, silence is not a school artefact. The wider one its title
carries — that the silence is not a school artefact *simpliciter* — does not.

**What it does not license.** Two attestations are not a survey of Imami
exegesis, and the two are not independent: al-Tabrisi's gloss is al-Tusi's word
for word, and their textual relation is not something this base has captured a
source on. The Shi'i tradition's other commentaries — al-Qummi, al-'Ayyashi,
al-Tabataba'i's *al-Mizan*, al-Fayd al-Kashani's *al-Safi* — are on the same host
and untouched, and the Zaydi and Ibadi commentaries there are untouched as well.
What is established is narrower than "the Shi'i tradition glosses the clause"
and is enough to break the generalisation: **after al-Tabari, the clause does get
glossed in classical Arabic exegesis — and every classical commentary in this
base that glosses it after him is Imami.** (The two Sunni commentaries here that
gloss it, al-Sa'di and al-Muyassar, are twentieth-century.)

**A methodological residue worth keeping.** Both explanations the base generated
for the silence were tested against the widest variation it happened to have
sampled, and both were declared spent on that basis. The variation it had was
Sunni. A negative result about a "tradition" is only ever a result about the
sampling frame, and the frame here was invisible because every source in it
shared a property nobody had written down.
