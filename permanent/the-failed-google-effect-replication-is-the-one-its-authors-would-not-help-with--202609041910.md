Of the twenty-one replications in the Social Sciences Replication Project,
twenty were designed in collaboration with the original authors. The exception
is Sparrow, Liu and Wegner: they did not respond to the project's queries, and
so their replication is the only one built without the original team's help.
They supplied no materials, gave no feedback, and then, after the replication
was complete, identified design differences from the original — which left the
project recording, in its own paper, that it "cannot rule out that they
influenced the replication result"
([[camerer-et-al-evaluating-the-replicability-of-social-science-experiments--202609041855]],
[[the-ssrp-replicated-one-result-per-paper-and-said-so-in-its-limitations--202609041900]]).

This complicates a conclusion this repository has already drawn, and the honest
move is to let it. The downgrade in
[[the-google-effects-replication-record-reaches-only-its-priming-experiment--202609041820]]
rests partly on the SSRP's two failed attempts at Experiment 1. Those attempts
are now known to be the project's *weakest instance of its own method* — not
badly done, but done without the one input the project used everywhere else,
and the project says so rather than hiding it. A failure under a protocol the
original authors never reviewed is weaker evidence against the original than a
failure under one they approved, which is what the other twenty had.

Three things keep the downgrade standing anyway. The independent replication by
Hesselmann was built specifically to answer the original author's objections and
also found nothing. The audit of the original's own reporting is untouched by
any of this: its degrees of freedom and its four-word headline interaction are
problems internal to the 2011 paper
([[the-google-effect-headline-rests-on-four-words-seen-once-each--202609041835]]).
And non-cooperation is not neutral evidence. The project's closing remark on the
episode is that it "illustrates the importance of open access to all of the
materials of published studies", and an author who will not release materials
and raises design objections only once the result is known has made their
finding harder to defend, not easier.

One caveat about this note's own sourcing, since it is a note about sourcing.
Everything above is the replicating project's account of the episode. Nature
Human Behaviour published a Correspondence by Sparrow alongside the Letter,
setting out the original authors' side, and this repository has not read it.
That is a one-witness claim about a dispute, which this base's standards
normally refuse. It is recorded anyway because the exposure runs against this
base's own convenience — the unread witness would, if anything, strengthen the
concession made here — but it is a gap, and it is filed.

The general lesson is worth more than the local one. A replication's evidential
weight depends on facts about how it was *conducted* that no summary of its
result carries — whether the original authors reviewed the protocol, whether
they supplied materials, whether their objections came before or after
unblinding. A base that records only "replicated / did not replicate" has
thrown that away, and will over-read some failures and under-read others with
no way of telling which.
