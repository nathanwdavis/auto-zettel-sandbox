al-Tabrisi glosses the exception in Q 29:46 as `إلا من أبى أن يقرَّ بالجزية منهم
ونصب الحرب فجادلوا هؤلاء بالسيف حتى يسلموا أو يعطوا الجزية` — dispute with these
by the sword until they submit or pay the tax — and, a few lines later in the
same verse, glosses "our God and your God is one" as "He has no partner". He
registers no tension between the two. Neither does al-Tusi, who has Mujahid's
`بمنع الجزية` in the same section.

**The datum this generalises.** Earlier cycles recorded that every Sunni
commentary captured attaches the *jizya* and the sword to these verses without
noticing any strain against the shared-God clause, and treated that as a fact
about those commentaries. It is not confined to them. The two Imami commentaries
do the same thing, in the same verse, in the same paragraph, and one of them is
*more* explicit about the ruling than any Sunni commentary in the base: where
al-Zamakhshari reports the *dhimma* reading as one option among several,
al-Tabrisi states the rule and its terminating condition.

**Why the juxtaposition is not, by itself, an inconsistency.** Both commentators
have a reading that dissolves it, and both prefer it. Al-Tusi asks the question
outright — `فان قيل: لم استثنى الذين ظلموا؟ وكلهم ظالم لنفسه بكفره!`, why except
*those who do wrong* when all of them wrong themselves by their unbelief? — and
answers that the wrongdoing meant is wrongdoing *in conduct*, in disputing or in
something else calling for harshness. Al-Tabrisi prefers the same:
`والأولى أن يكون معناه إلا الذين ظلموك في جدالهم`. On that reading the harsh
clause is about how the other party behaves, and the shared-God clause is about
God, so the two never meet.

**And that is what makes the juxtaposition informative.** The dissolution is
available and both take it — and neither uses the shared-God clause to reach it.
Neither argues: these are people whose God is the same as ours, therefore the
exception must be narrow. The clause does no work in the passage it sits in. It
is stated, glossed in one phrase, and left; the ruling is settled from the
grammar of the exception and from Mujahid and Ibn Zayd. Across four schools and
both branches of the tradition, this base has not found a commentator who treats
"our God and your God is one" as bearing on how the other party may be treated.

**The one commentator who does connect them connects them the other way.**
al-Razi grounds the verse's leniency in the People of the Book having *professed*
oneness, and reads the exception as excluding trinitarians *as associators*
([[the-classical-exclusion-of-trinitarians-is-made-inside-the-verse-that-carries-the-shared-god-clause--202609040925]]).
That is the shared-God material doing work — and the work it does is exclusion,
not protection.

**Limit.** Two Imami commentaries, one verse, one host. This is a
cross-family confirmation of a pattern, not a survey of Imami exegesis, and it
says nothing about what the *fiqh* holds. The juristic literature that would
actually rule on whether the People of the Book are *mushrikun* remains untried
by this base, and remains where a ruling would live.
