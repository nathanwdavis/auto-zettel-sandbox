The base held this on one clause of one modern Sunni author. Al-Sabuni wrote
`وإلى هذا ذهب الإمامية، وبعض الزيدية` — the Imamiyya and some Zaydis went to
this — and the note built on it said so in its own grounding limit: no Imami or
Zaydi text was in the repository at all. Two Imami commentaries have now been
read on both of the verses at issue, and they say it themselves.

**They claim it, they do not report it.** Al-Tusi (d. 460/1067) on Q 2:221:
`وهذه الآية على عمومها - عندنا - في تحريم مناكحة الكفار، وليست منسوخة ولا
مخصوصة` — this verse stands on its generality, *in our view*, in forbidding
marriage with the unbelievers; it is neither abrogated nor restricted.
Al-Tabrisi (d. 548/1153) a century later: `وهي عامة عندنا ... وليست بمنسوخة ولا
مخصوصة`, and, having laid out the three-way split, `وهو مذهبنا` — and that is
our school's position. The markers are the point. This is not a Sunni author
attributing a view to a Shi'i school; it is the school in its own voice.

**And the abrogation genuinely runs backwards.** The strict position is not
merely a refusal to let Q 5:5 abrogate Q 2:221. On Q 5:5 al-Tusi writes
`وعندنا لا يجوز العقد على الكتابية نكاح الدوام`, reading the permission down by
Q 2:221 and Q 60:10, and then reports the reversal from the Imams themselves —
Abu al-Jarud from **al-Baqir** that the Q 5:5 permission is `منسوخ` by "do not
marry *mushrikat* until they believe", and from **al-Sadiq** that it is abrogated
by "do not hold to the bonds of unbelieving women." Al-Tabrisi gives the same
reading down in the same words, credited to `أصحابنا`. So the inversion
al-Sabuni compressed into half a sentence is, inside the tradition, a *naskh*
claim carried on the authority of the fifth and sixth Imams.

**One correction to the report, and it matters.** The one-clause Sunni summary
says the Imamiyya forbid marriage to the women of the Book. What both Imami
commentators forbid is `نكاح الدوام`, the *permanent* contract — and both, in the
same breath, hold that *mut'a* and concubinage with the same women remain
permitted: `لانه يجوز عندنا وطؤهن بعقد المتعة، وملك اليمين`. The strictness is
about the contract, not about contact at all. A base that had only the report
would have carried a position stricter and flatter than the one the school
holds, and would have had no way of knowing.

This is what closes the grounding limit recorded in
[[whether-the-people-of-the-book-are-mushrikun-is-a-disputed-question-of-law--202609041325]]
on the Imami half. That note's sectarian reversal — the Shi'i commentaries
glossing the shared-God clause where the Sunni ones pass over it, and the Shi'i
jurists taking the stricter line on marriage — now rests on Imami sources rather
than on a report of them.

**What still limits it.** Both commentaries come from one host, altafsir.com, and
no second host for either work is reachable from this environment; the claims
taken here are positive quotations, which a single institutional host threatens
weakly, but the limit is real and is recorded in both reference notes. And a
*tafsir* is not a *fiqh* text: these are the school's exegetes reading the verse,
not its jurists stating the rule in a legal compendium. Nothing above turns on
the difference, but the difference is there.

**Added 2026-09-04 15:00Z: read this note's title strictly — it says *the Imami
commentaries*, and it now has to.** A third Imami commentary has been captured on
the same two verses, and it dissents:
[[the-imami-tradition-is-split-on-2-221-so-the-strict-reading-is-the-classical-commentaries-not-the-school--202609041510]].
Al-Tabataba'i (d. 1401/1981) denies that Q 2:221 reaches the People of the Book
at all, calls the abrogation `فساد`, and answers the al-Baqir report this note
cites with the objection that an abrogator may not precede what it abrogates.
Nothing above is withdrawn: al-Tusi and al-Tabrisi do claim the position with
`عندنا` and `مذهبنا`, and the *naskh* reports are in their texts as described.
What changes is the scope a reader may take from it. Two commentaries eight and
nine centuries old are not the school, and the sentence "the Imamiyya hold X" is
not licensed by this note — which is exactly the correction the base made to the
Zaydi half a cycle earlier, arriving now for the Imami one. The `نكاح الدوام`
qualification, by contrast, gets *stronger*: al-Tabataba'i reaches the same
restriction by another route
([[the-permanence-restriction-survives-the-rejection-of-the-abrogation-as-specification-not-naskh--202609041520]]).

**Extended 2026-09-04 16:00Z: the *naskh* reports this note reports are one half
of what the corpus transmits.** Nothing here is withdrawn — al-Tusi and
al-Tabrisi do claim the strict reading as their school's, and al-Tusi does carry
the inversion as reports from al-Baqir and al-Sadiq. What has been added is the
other half. Imami *fiqh* assembles, a few lines apart, both the Zurara reports
from al-Baqir that prohibit and a report from the same Imam through Muhammad b.
Muslim that permits, `لا بأس به`
([[both-traditions-transmit-al-baqir-on-both-sides-so-each-school-s-report-of-the-imams-is-a-selection--202609041630]]).
So this note should be read as describing what these two commentators selected
from the transmitted material, not as reporting what the Imams held — a
distinction the commentators themselves do not draw, and which only became
visible once a legal work that weighs the conflict was in the base.
