Maimonides' condition on the Noahide commandments introduces a third thing to
grade, distinct from both halves of the same-God question.

Two gentiles keep the same seven prohibitions with the same precision. One
"accepts them and fulfills them because the Holy One, blessed be He, commanded
them in the Torah"; the other "fulfills them out of intellectual conviction."
The first is among "the pious among the gentiles" with a share in the world to
come; the second "is not a resident alien, nor of 'the pious among the
gentiles,' nor of their wise men" (see
[[the-noahide-covenant-turns-on-why-the-gentile-keeps-it--202609042032]]).

Neither the conduct nor the object of worship separates them. What separates
them is the *ground* — whether the obedience is referred to a commanding
authority or to the agent's own reasoning.

This is worth naming because the base has now met the same move from the other
side. [[the-muslim-consensus-statement-makes-common-ground-out-of-command-not-reference--202609040115]]
records A Common Word building its common ground out of what the two traditions
are *commanded*, rather than out of whom they refer to. Two traditions,
independently, locate the thing that matters in the standing of a command rather
than in the identity of an object.

So the map this base has been working with — reference on one axis, description
on the other — is missing an axis that both Judaism and Islam use. Call it
*ground*: on whose authority the practice is undertaken. It explains why the
same-God question can look answered and unresolved at once. Maimonides can hold
that Muslims do not serve false deities and still not count a Muslim among the
pious of the nations on those terms alone, because the two verdicts are
answers to different questions.
