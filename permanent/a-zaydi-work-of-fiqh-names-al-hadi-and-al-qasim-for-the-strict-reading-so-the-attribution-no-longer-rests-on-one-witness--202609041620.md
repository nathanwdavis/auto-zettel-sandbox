For two cycles this base has carried a claim it knew was underwritten: that the
strict reading of Q 5:5 — that the "chaste women of those given the Book" means
only those of them who converted — is the position of the Zaydi imams al-Hadi
and al-Qasim. The witness was *Tafsir al-A'qam*, a commentary written some five
centuries after them and reachable from a single host. The 14:00Z cycle said so
in its own note; the 15:00Z cycle wrote the gap into INBOX as one of two things
the entry was still waiting on. It is now closed.

**A second witness, and a better kind of one.** *Sharh al-Azhar*, the Zaydi
school's commentary on the *Azhar* by Ibn Miftah (d. 877/1472), gives the reading
and the attribution in one sentence of its own apparatus:

> وأما قوله تعال … والشحصتات من الذي ووا اكاب … فالمراد به من آمن من آهل
> الكتاب» كذا ذكره المادي والقاسم وغيرهما من أئمة أهل البيت

as for God's word *and the chaste women of those given the Book*, what is meant
is those of the People of the Book who believed; so said al-Hadi and al-Qasim and
others of the imams of the People of the House. (Quoted as the OCR has it; the
corrected reading is `الهادي` for `المادي` and `والمحصنات من الذين أوتوا الكتاب`
for the damaged Qur'anic lemma, both marked in the literature note, where the
control establishing the damage is also recorded.)

**Why it counts as independent.** Three things separate it from *Tafsir
al-A'qam*: it is a different work by a different author, it comes through a
different host — archive.org rather than altafsir.com, the first time this base
has read any Zaydi text off a second host — and, most importantly, it is a
different **genre**. The base's problem was never that al-A'qam might be lying;
it was that a *tafsir*'s report of a legal position five centuries on is one step
removed from the law, and this base had nothing at the level where the rule
actually operates. A working manual of Zaydi *fiqh*, teaching the prohibition and
citing al-Hadi and al-Qasim as its authority, is that level.

**What is confirmed, and what is still transmission.** Confirmed: that the Zaydi
tradition attributes the strict reading to these two imams, and that it does so
inside its own legal literature rather than only in an outsider's summary or a
later commentary. Not confirmed, and this note will not pretend otherwise:
al-Hadi's and al-Qasim's **own** writings remain unseen by this base. Ibn Miftah
transmits them, as al-A'qam did. Two independent transmissions are better than
one, and they are still transmissions. The one place where al-Hadi is quoted
directly here — from his *al-Manzila bayna al-manzilatayn* — is on the converse
question, whether a Muslim woman may marry a non-Muslim, on which every school
this base has read agrees; it is not evidence for the contested direction.

**The finding underneath the finding: the argument is not about abrogation.**
Every Shi'i witness this base held treated the clash of Q 2:221 and Q 5:5 as a
*naskh* problem, and the whole Imami dispute turns on which verse cancels which.
The Zaydi manual never enters that argument. It leaves Q 5:5 in force and reads
its subject as converts, so the permission is intact and empty. That is a third
mechanism for one rule, alongside the *naskh* of al-Tusi and the *takhsis* of
al-Tabataba'i — and it is immune to the objection that destroys the first, since
a verse that is not claimed to be abrogated cannot be embarrassed by the order of
revelation.
