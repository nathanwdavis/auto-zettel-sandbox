The base's argument that the People of the Book are not *mushrikun* has moved
hosts three times without changing shape. Al-Qurtubi and al-Sabuni gave it as the
majority Sunni proof from the Qur'an's own syntax: Q 2:105 and Q 98:1 conjoin the
two terms, and conjunction requires distinctness. Al-Tabataba'i gave it from
inside the Imami tradition in the twentieth century. It now turns out to be
standing in Imami *fiqh* in the nineteenth, and to be doing legal work there
rather than exegetical work.

**The argument, in the *fiqh*.** Al-Najafi (d. 1266/1850), rejecting the claim
that Q 2:221 abrogates Q 5:5:

> فيتو جه عليه مضع دخولالكتابية نيالمشر كةء لان المتبادد من الشرك في إطلاق الثر
> ع غير أهلالكتاب, كما یؤیده عطف المشر كبن على اهل الكتاب د بالعمكس في كثير من
> الابات

the objection stands that the *kitabiyya* is not included in "the *mushrika*",
because what comes first to mind from *shirk* in the law's usage is someone other
than the People of the Book, as is confirmed by the coupling of *al-mushrikun*
with *ahl al-kitab*, and the reverse, in many verses. (Quoted as the OCR has it;
the corrected reading is in the literature note.)

**Two things are different from the versions the base already held.** First, the
criterion is `المتبادد من الشرك في إطلاق الثر ع` [read: `المتبادر من الشرك في
إطلاق الشرع`] — what comes first to mind from *shirk* in the law's usage — not what the word means in the abstract. That is a jurist's test, about
how a term functions when a legislator uses it, and it makes the conclusion a
claim about legal drafting rather than about theology. Second, the conjunction
evidence is offered as *confirmation* (`كما یؤیده`, read `كما يؤيده`) of that test rather than as
the proof itself. The syntax supports the reading; it does not carry it alone.
That is a stronger position than the one the base recorded from the Sunni
authors, because it survives the objection that a conjunction can join
overlapping categories.

**And he draws the distinction that keeps it from proving too much.** `وهذا لا
ينافي اعتقادهم ما بوجب الشرك › إذ ليس الغرض فى الشرك عنهم » پل عدم تبادده من
إطلاق لفظ المشرك` [read: `إذ ليس الغرض نفي الشرك عنهم`] — and this does not
conflict with their holding what entails *shirk*; the purpose is not to deny
*shirk* of them, but that it is not what the word *mushrik* first conveys. The
base recorded exactly this distinction from al-Tabataba'i and called it the move
that lets one verse run the word at two extensions without contradiction. It is
here a century earlier, in the genre where the extension of a term decides
whether a marriage is valid.

**What this changes.** Three things, in order of how much they cost.

*The dating.* The base had this distinction as a twentieth-century Imami
refinement of a classical Sunni argument. It is at minimum nineteenth-century and
Imami, and al-Najafi presents it as an objection anyone would raise rather than
as a discovery — so the honest description is that the base does not know how old
it is, only that it is older than *al-Mizan*.

*The genre.* The base's version of the argument was exegetical: what the word
means in the Qur'an. This version is jurisprudential: what a term's scope is when
a rule uses it, with a doctrine of *tabadur* behind it. That the same conclusion
is reached by two different disciplines with two different tests is stronger
evidence for the conclusion than either alone.

*The self-restraint.* The argument is often read — including, at first, by this
base — as saying the People of the Book are not idolaters, full stop. Both Imami
authorities who make it explicitly refuse that reading. The claim is about the
extension of a legal term. It leaves the theological question exactly where it
was, which is why the same tradition can hold both that Christians hold something
amounting to *shirk* and that Q 2:221 does not reach them.

**Limits.** One work, one section, one host; and the base has read no *usul*
text, so its account of `تبادر` as a doctrine is inference from how al-Najafi
uses the word rather than anything sourced. That inference is flagged here so a
later run can either ground it or take it out.
