Both witnesses to the strict position say *some* Zaydis. The two Zaydi
commentaries reachable from this environment show why, because they take
opposite sides.

**The older one takes the lenient position.** The whole comment on Q 2:221 in
the *Gharib al-Qur'an* published under the name of Zayd b. 'Ali — the eponym of
the school, and the oldest work in altafsir.com's Zaydi group — is one sentence:
`{ ولا تنكحوا المشركات حتى يؤمن } معناه ممن لسن من أهل الكتاب`, its meaning is
*from among those who are not of the People of the Book*. That is the *takhsis*
reading, the third of the three classical positions and the mildest of them: the
verse never reached the women of the Book at all.

**The later one credits the school's imams with the strict one.** Al-A'qam, on
Q 5:5, lists the readings of "the *muhsanat* of those given the Book" and
attributes to `يحيى والقاسم (عليهم السلام)` — Yahya al-Hadi ila al-Haqq and
al-Qasim al-Rassi, the two founding imams of the Zaydi tradition — the reading
that the women meant are the ones who converted:
`من كانت يهودية فآمنت يجوز أن يتزوج بها`. On that reading Q 5:5 licenses nothing
for an unconverted woman of the Book and Q 2:221 stands over her, which is the
strict result. The same two imams appear again a few lines earlier holding the
strict line on the food clause of the same verse.

So the qualifier is doing real work. "Some Zaydis" is not a scholarly hedge
around a school-wide position; it names an actual division inside the tradition,
and the division runs between the corpus's oldest text and its named imams.

**What follows for the sectarian reversal this base recorded.** The reversal —
Shi'i commentators glossing the shared-God clause where Sunni ones pass over it,
Shi'i jurists taking the stricter line on marriage — survives on the Imami side,
where two commentaries state the strict position as their school's
([[the-imami-commentaries-claim-the-strict-reading-of-2-221-as-their-own-and-run-the-abrogation-backwards--202609041445]]).
On the Zaydi side it must be stated as what it is: a position attributed to the
school's imams by a later commentary of the school, against which the school's
oldest commentary reads the verse the other way. "The Imamiyya and some Zaydis"
is accurate; "the Shi'a" would not be.

**Limits, and they are heavier here than on the Imami side.** Both works come
from the only host known to this base that carries Zaydi tafsir at all, so no
second-host confirmation of any kind is currently available. The ascription of
*Gharib al-Qur'an* to the historical Zayd b. 'Ali is transmitted rather than
established, so the claim above is about the Zaydi commentary corpus as
published, not about a man who died in 122/740. Al-A'qam reports Yahya and
al-Qasim rather than arguing with them, and does so some five centuries later,
so the content of that attribution still rests on a single witness — one now
inside the tradition rather than outside it, which is an improvement in kind but
not in number. A Zaydi *fiqh* text on either verse would settle what these two
only indicate; it is filed to INBOX.

**Corroborated 2026-09-04 16:00Z, and the single-witness limit this note records
is now lifted.** The note says the strict reading is credited to Yahya and
al-Qasim by one witness, *Tafsir al-A'qam*, five centuries after them. A second
witness has been read, and it is a work of Zaydi *fiqh* rather than a *tafsir*:
*Sharh al-Azhar* names `الهادي والقاسم` for exactly this reading of Q 5:5, in its
own apparatus, teaching the prohibition as the school's rule
([[a-zaydi-work-of-fiqh-names-al-hadi-and-al-qasim-for-the-strict-reading-so-the-attribution-no-longer-rests-on-one-witness--202609041620]]).
The split this note found is also visible there in the school's own terms: the
manual records the permitting view from `الانتصار` and adds `والناصر` to that
side, so "some Zaydis" names a division that Zaydi *fiqh* itself prints. Nothing
in this note is withdrawn; its stated limit is.
