Meyendorff's footnote on Romans 5:12 gives the Greek "two grammatical
possibilities". If eph' ho is neuter and means "because", the verse "defines
death as the punishment for individual sins of any human (not "original"
sin)". If it is masculine, it refers to "death" (thanatos), "so that
death—as a cosmic, personalized reality—becomes the cause of individual
human sins." He names Theodoret, Theodore, Chrysostom, Cyril and Maximus as
reading it the second way
([[meyendorff-holds-that-eastern-patristic-thought-knows-no--202610081437]]);
Byzantine Theology calls it the reading "which is indeed the meaning which
most Greek Fathers accepted"
([[meyendorff-finds-a-greek-patristic-consensus-that-the--202610081438]]).

Either way, Adam's guilt is not in the clause: on the first reading each
dies for his own sins, on the second each sins because he is mortal. What
the Eastern account needs from the verse, that guilt is not inherited, does
not depend on which reading wins.

What does depend on it is the mechanism, death as the cause of sin, and
there the evidence on file disagrees. Sanday and Headlam list the same
reading, "Some Greeks quoted by Photius also took the rel. as masc. with
antecedent θάνατος", and call it "even more impossible" than Augustine's
"in whom". They also put Theodoret among those who take the relative "as
neut." and the phrase as "because"
([[sanday-and-headlam-render-eph-ho-in-romans-5-12-because--202610061553]]),
where Meyendorff puts Theodoret on the "death" side. The two cannot both be
right about Theodoret, and neither Theodoret's commentary nor any other
Greek father's text on the verse is on file to settle it.

The earlier note on the Greek of the verse showed that "because" moves the
question to the sense in which all sinned
([[in-the-greek-romans-5-12-s-last-clause-names-no-one-so-in--202610061559]]).
Meyendorff's answer to that question is that they sinned as mortals under
death's tyranny, not in Adam.
