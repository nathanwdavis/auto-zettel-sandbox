A note is only reusable by a stranger if its form, not its author's memory,
carries the meaning. Blair's early modern evidence is that this condition was
actually met: notes were bequeathed in wills, fought over in disputed
legacies, bought, and taken to order by hired professional readers, and in all
these cases notes made by one person were presumed useful to another —
consultable "in a predictable way" and applicable to purposes of their own —
even though the new owner had no memory of the reading that produced the note
and no intention of memorising it
([[blair-on-note-taking-as-transmission-and-delegated-memory--202609032105]],
[[blair-note-taking-as-an-art-of-transmission--202609032100]], pp. 104-5).
Blair's explanation is the operative part: it was the spread of *systematic*
method that let note taking be delegated and "almost mechanized," because a
book could then be worked over for its passages by explicit criteria someone
else could follow. Predictability is the property that does the work.
Placcius's note closet of 1689 is the form pushed furthest — slips hung behind
swivelling slats labelled by topical heading — and what he advertised about it
was social: it let a group pool notes and split a common task.

This is the historically attested half of the reuse claim that this base
otherwise asserts from first principles. It corroborates the *reuse* in
[[one-idea-per-note-enables-reuse--202701010002]] and says nothing about its
*atomicity*: Blair's slips are filed under topical heads, and she nowhere
discusses confining a note to a single idea, so the link is a shared concept
and not support for that note's mechanism. Read strictly, her evidence
establishes that predictable form beats private memory, which is the weaker
and older claim.

It does support [[topic-head-filing-optimizes-retrieval-not-connection--202609010604]]
on the point that note carries as its own generalization from a single case:
Locke is that note's only witness that topical filing is aimed at retrieval,
and Blair, working independently and across the whole European tradition,
defines the commonplace method the same way — passages "sorted under a
thematic or topical heading to facilitate retrieval" (p. 87). Two independent
witnesses to the purpose of topic-head filing is better than one.

Blair supplies the limit herself, and it bites on any system built from this
claim: if every text one wanted were always available to be searched afresh,
"the note itself, the selection made for later reuse, might play a less
prominent role" (p. 107). The predictability that makes a note portable is
worth paying for only while re-finding the source is dearer than re-reading
the note.
