The Westminster Confession, chapter 3, paragraph 2, as Van Horn quotes it:
"Although God knows whatsoever may or can come to pass, upon all supposed
conditions; yet hath he not decreed any thing because he foresaw it as
future, as that which would come to pass, upon such conditions." Van Horn's
gloss is that the Calvinist rejection of middle knowledge "even has
confessional status"
([[van-horn-records-the-concession-and-argues-that-counterfactuals-can-be-natural-or-free-but-not-middle--202609111817]],
from [[van-horn-on-incorporating-middle-knowledge-into-calvinism--202609111812]]).
Helm's reading of the same paragraph: it "cuts off middle knowledge", because
"God’s decrees and his foreknowledge cover the same ground, and are grounded
by the divine intuition, not by God's inspection of un-decreed
possibilities. His decrees are the ground of whatever happens." The
seventeenth-century Reformed, Twisse and Rutherford, were interested in
"cutting it off at the root, because they could not conceive of any
counterfactuals of creaturely freedom being true that were not first decreed
by God, and true because of this, and so part of his free knowledge." Hence
their answer to the objection the Molinists cannot answer: "what grounds the
truth is not a state of affairs that exists apart from the decree of God,
but only what is decreed", and "How could it be known to God that in
circumstances C, A will freely do P other than by being unconditionally
decreed by him, and so being an aspect of the divine free knowledge"
([[helm-grounds-the-reformed-refusal-of-middle-knowledge-in-the-decree--202609111816]],
from [[helm-and-more-on-molinism--202609111811]]). Laing supplies the Catholic
parallel: Bañez "also held to a belief that God knows all true
counterfactuals of freedom (which are true in virtue of an act of his will)"
([[laing-argues-that-calvinist-middle-knowledge-is-caught-in-a-dilemma--202609111818]],
from [[laing-the-compatibility-of-calvinism-and-middle-knowledge--202609111813]]),
which is Pohle's Thomist medium of foreknowledge, the decree
([[pohle-locates-the-molinist-thomist-dispute-in-the-medium-of-foreknowledge--202609090057]]);
the Confession states the same order in Protestant form.

Set beside Molina this is a contradiction in so many words, not a shared
concept. Molina's marginal rule is that future contingents are future not
because God knows them but "e contrario", and that God's conditional
foreknowledge depends on what the creature would do "et non e contrario"
([[molina-reverses-the-direction-of-dependence-god-foreknows-the-free-act-because-the-creature-would-do-it--202609090101]]).
The Confession's "hath he not decreed any thing because he foresaw it" denies
that order at the one point where Molina needs it, and Helm says that is
what it is for. It also gives confessional standing to the decree-first
order that Grudem argues from foreknowledge rather than from decree, "If our
future choices are known, then they are fixed"
([[the-foreknowledge-entailment-is-a-weapon-in-chapter-16--202609081829]]),
though the Confession makes the decree, not the knowledge, the ground.

From the Molinist side the answer is not an answer. The objection asks what
makes the counterfactual true; the Reformed reply "the decree" concedes the
objection's own point, that grounding the counterfactual in God yields
determinism, and embraces it. Laing draws the consequence for the
compatibilist who wants Molina's category anyway: grounded "in the will of
God or in the way God created the creaturely will", the position "is not in
the middle of anything." That is where Ware stands. His counterfactual is
grounded in a nature God makes and factors God arranges
([[wares-compatibilist-middle-knowledge-derives-the-counterfactual-from-character-and-circumstance--202609111820]]),
which is inside the confessional order, while the word he keeps for it names
the knowledge the Confession says God has ("whatsoever may or can come to
pass, upon all supposed conditions") but does not decree from. Helm's "odd and an inexplicable exception", and Muller's remark, reported by Van Horn from
uncaptured text, that middle knowledge is not a middle ground but the
Arminian and Jesuit position, are both about that gap between the word and
the order.

**Limits.** Twisse, Rutherford, Turretin and van Mastricht are on file only
as Helm reports them, and the Confession only as Van Horn and Helm quote it;
Helm numbers the paragraph "III" in the 2019 post. What "grounded by the
divine intuition" adds to "decreed" is not explained in the captured text.
The claim that the confessional order contradicts Molina's is this base's
reading of two sentences that were not written against each other, though
Helm's post says the paragraph was meant to exclude middle knowledge.
