`the-two-kabbalistic-readings-of-the-sefirot-are-the-two-the-councils-ruled-out--202609041730`
is one of the base's strongest comparative claims, and it hedges itself:
"Nicaea's whole achievement, *on Hodge's account of it*". Its only witness to
the fourth century is Hodge, *Systematic Theology* (1872), via
`hodge-on-the-two-readings-the-councils-ruled-out--202609041715`.

This is a groundedness asymmetry the base would not tolerate elsewhere. On the
Islamic side it read roughly twenty primary sources -- al-Tabari, al-Maturidi,
al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Tusi, al-Tabrisi,
al-Tabataba'i, Ibn Miftah, al-Najafi and others -- and made a discovery
(`the-silence-about-the-shared-god-clause-begins-in-al-tabaris-own-generation--202609041210`)
precisely by not trusting a single reporter. On the Christian side there is no
patristic or conciliar primary source in `reference/` at all: no Nicene or
Constantinopolitan creed text, no Chalcedonian definition, no Athanasius, no
Cappadocians, no anathemas in the original. A query for "Nicaea Chalcedon creeds"
reports that no note uses the term `chalcedon`.

Read the creed of 325 with its anathemas, the creed of 381, and the Chalcedonian
definition, plus one primary Sabellian-refutation text, and ask: does the
conciliar record rule out *ad extra* relations as flatly as Hodge says, or is
that a Reformed systematician's reading of it? If the sweep changes the verdict,
it changes the sefirot comparison with it.
