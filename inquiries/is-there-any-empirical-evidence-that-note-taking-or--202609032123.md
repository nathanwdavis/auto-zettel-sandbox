Filed by the 2026-09-03 gap-filling graph walk. `zettelkasten method` is a
configured topic in config.yml, and the base's grounding for it is entirely
historical, practitioner, or prescriptive. There is no empirical evidence of
any kind in it.

WHAT THE WALK FOUND. Querying "Is there empirical evidence that zettelkasten
note-taking improves thinking or writing?" matched 6 of 135 notes and surfaced
exactly ONE permanent note, [[atomic-notes-compound-over-time--202701010001]]
— which carries NO source link at all. So the base's only answer to a question
about evidence is one of its own uncited assertions.

The whole source base for the topic, by kind:
- Ahrens 202608301000 — a how-to book, and only its 11-page preview
- Luhmann 202609010111 — the practitioner describing his own practice
- Schmidt 202609010302 — an archivist describing that practitioner's file
- Locke 202609010602 — a 1686 method
- Blair 202609032100 — peer-reviewed, but HISTORY of note-taking as a practice
That is four kinds of witness and not one of them is a test. Nobody in the
base ever measured whether any of this works.

Meanwhile the base makes causal claims that would need measuring:
[[a-slip-box-surprises-only-after-critical-mass--202609010113]],
[[a-trusted-structure-frees-attention-from-willpower--202608311142]],
[[atomic-notes-compound-over-time--202701010001]],
[[one-idea-per-note-enables-reuse--202701010002]].

CANDIDATE SOURCES. The relevant experimental literature is about note-taking
and memory generally, not about slip-boxes:
1. Mueller and Oppenheimer 2014, "The Pen Is Mightier Than the Keyboard",
   Psychological Science 25(6) — the most-cited experiment on note-taking and
   understanding. Capture the REPLICATION history with it, not just the
   headline: Morehead, Dunlosky and Rawson 2019 failed to replicate key
   results. A base that cited the 2014 finding alone would be repeating
   exactly the kind of thing this repository exists to avoid.
2. Retrieval practice / the testing effect — Roediger and Karpicke 2006. Bears
   directly on Blair's Chavigny material in
   [[the-note-became-an-external-memory-only-when-memorizing-came-to-be-seen-as-a-cost--202609032115]]:
   if retrieval is what consolidates learning, then delegating memory to paper
   has a measurable cost, and Drexel's generation may have been right about
   something Chavigny dismissed. That is a genuine contradiction to look for,
   not a corroboration.
3. The generation effect, and the literature on transactive/external memory
   (Sparrow et al. 2011 on search engines and memory).

A LEGITIMATE ANSWER TO THIS INQUIRY IS "ALMOST NONE, AND HERE IS WHY THAT
MATTERS." It is very unlikely that any controlled study of zettelkasten
specifically exists. If the run establishes that the general note-taking
evidence is contested and that nothing tests the slip-box claims directly,
that is a real result and should be written as a permanent note limiting the
existing cluster — NOT recorded as a failed cycle. Do not stretch general
note-taking findings to cover slip-box claims they were never about; the
honest note is about the size of the gap between what is asserted here and
what anyone has measured.

Watch the tier rule: a psychology finding cited from a news write-up is
general-web. These should be peer-reviewed captures or they should not land.

## 2026-09-04 cycle: the note-taking half is answered, the externalized-memory half is not

**Status deliberately `in-progress`, not `answered`, and the reason matters.**
This inquiry's own stated test was met for one half of the question and not
touched for the other, and closing it on the strength of the half that was done
would strand the rest. Both halves are set out here so a later run can pick up
exactly where this one stopped.

**ANSWERED — candidate source 1, the note-taking experiments.** Both papers this
inquiry named were captured and are now in the base: Mueller and Oppenheimer
2014 (reference 202609040155, literature 202609040200) and the Morehead,
Dunlosky and Rawson 2019 direct replication (reference 202609040156, literature
202609040205). The inquiry's instruction to capture the replication history
rather than the headline was followed literally — the two were captured in the
same cycle, and neither reference note exists without the other. The finding is
the one the inquiry said would be a legitimate answer:

- The famous effect did not survive direct replication. Performance "did not
  consistently differ between any groups", and one of those groups took no
  notes at all; the replication's meta-analysis found "small (nonsignificant)
  effects favoring longhand"; the authors' own verdict is that saying which
  method is better "seems premature". Written up as permanent
  [[a-failed-replication-leaves-the-mechanism-standing-and-the-effect-unproven--202609040210]],
  whose actual claim is the asymmetry: the effect loses its warrant, the
  mechanism does not, and you may cite neither paper for the other's
  proposition.
- Nothing in that literature reaches a slip-box claim. It manipulates how a
  person writes during one lecture and scores a test taken shortly after; the
  base's claims are about accumulation, linking and a later self, over years.
  Written up as permanent
  [[the-note-taking-experiments-do-not-reach-the-slip-box-claims--202609040215]],
  which is the limiting note this inquiry asked for. It is linked `contradicts`
  into [[atomic-notes-compound-over-time--202701010001]] and
  [[a-slip-box-surprises-only-after-critical-mass--202609010113]] — not because
  anything measured refutes them, but because neither may now be reached
  without meeting the limit, and both carry it in their own bodies.
- One unexpected result worth recording: the single house practice the
  experimental literature touches is not a slip-box claim at all. It is the
  rule that a literature note must be in the reader's own words, which is
  near enough the mechanism Mueller and Oppenheimer proposed — and its
  evidential state is exactly the suspension described above.

**NOT ANSWERED — candidate sources 2 and 3, and the wider half of the
question.** This cycle read nothing on retrieval practice (Roediger and
Karpicke 2006), nothing on the generation effect, and nothing on transactive
or external memory (Sparrow et al. 2011). The sharpest of these is still the
one this inquiry identified and it has not been attempted: if retrieval
practice is what consolidates learning, then delegating memory to paper has a
measurable cost, which would bear directly against Chavigny in
[[the-note-became-an-external-memory-only-when-memorizing-came-to-be-seen-as-a-cost--202609032115]].
The inquiry was right that this is a contradiction to look for rather than a
corroboration, and looking for it is the work that remains.

**A grounding limit on what did land.** Both new sources are **abstract only** —
the full texts are not reachable from this environment, and both reference
notes say so in their first line. That is sufficient for what the two permanent
notes claim, which turns on stated designs and stated conclusions, and it is
not sufficient for any statement about either paper's methods. A related and
more uncomfortable gap: a 2018 corrigendum to Mueller and Oppenheimer exists
and could not be read at all, so this base does not know whether the original's
account was amended. Both gaps are filed in INBOX.

## 2026-09-04, second cycle: candidate source 2 is done, and it found the contradiction this inquiry told it to look for

**Still `in-progress`, and now for a smaller reason than before.** Of the three
candidate sources this inquiry named, two are answered and one is untouched. The
untouched one is candidate 3 — the generation effect, and transactive/external
memory (Sparrow, Liu and Wegner 2011 on search engines and memory). Nothing else
of this inquiry's original scope remains open.

**WHAT THIS CYCLE DID.** Candidate source 2, retrieval practice, is in the base,
and — unlike the two note-taking papers, which are abstract-only — it is in the
base **from full texts**. Three sources, all reached through the second author's
own laboratory publications page at Purdue after the publisher returned its
usual 403:

- Roediger and Karpicke 2006, *Test-Enhanced Learning* (reference 202609040305,
  literature 202609040310) — the paper this inquiry named.
- Agarwal, Karpicke, Kang, Roediger and McDermott 2008, *Examining the Testing
  Effect with Open- and Closed-Book Tests* (reference 202609040315, literature
  202609040320) — not named by this inquiry, and the most important thing this
  cycle found. See below.
- Karpicke 2025, *Retrieval-based learning* (reference 202609040325, literature
  202609040330) — the field's current survey, used only for replication status
  and for what it does not cover.

**THE CONTRADICTION, FOUND WHERE THIS INQUIRY SAID TO LOOK AND NOT IN THE PAPER
IT NAMED.** This inquiry predicted that if retrieval is what consolidates
learning, then delegating memory to paper has a measurable cost, and that this
would bear against Chavigny in
[[the-note-became-an-external-memory-only-when-memorizing-came-to-be-seen-as-a-cost--202609032115]].
The prediction was right and the source was wrong. Roediger and Karpicke 2006
cannot deliver it: every condition in it happens inside the head, and a full-text
search confirms the paper never discusses notes or external storage at all. It
gives the *mechanism* and no measurement.

The measurement is in the 2008 paper, which manipulates exactly the right thing
— whether the learner can see the written material, defined by its authors as
"view notes and textbooks while taking the test" — and then waits a week. Result:
having the material to hand was worth sixteen points of immediate performance
and nothing at all a week later. Written up as
[[having-the-notes-to-hand-buys-present-performance-not-durable-knowledge--202609040345]],
which is linked `contradicts` into the Chavigny note and states in its own body
exactly how narrowly: what is contradicted is the premise that memory can be
handed to paper at no cost to what the head knows, not Chavigny's recommendation
and not his claim about higher intellectual qualities, which nobody has measured
on either side.

That paper was found by following a citation rather than trusting a summary. The
2025 survey mentions it in one paragraph, on the forgetting measure; the paper's
own abstract reports the same data on the retention measure; the two read very
differently and both are true. Had this cycle cited the survey's sentence and
stopped, the base would now hold an overstated claim.

**THE OTHER TWO NOTES.**
[[retrieval-not-re-exposure-is-what-makes-learning-last--202609040340]] states
the core finding and is careful about its evidential standing: the field's own
survey calls the effect replicated hundreds of times, but that survey is written
by the original paper's co-author and the three independent meta-analyses it
names were not read. That is filed as follow-up work rather than papered over.
[[confidence-tracks-re-exposure-while-retention-tracks-retrieval--202609040350]]
records the metacognitive dissociation both papers measured, and ends on the
sharpest question this cycle produced: the *feeling* of having a trustworthy
structure is generated by re-exposure rather than by retrieval, and that feeling
is currently the whole of the evidence anyone offers for this practice.

**WHAT WOULD FINISH THIS INQUIRY.** Candidate 3 — the generation effect, and
transactive memory (Sparrow et al. 2011, DOI 10.1126/science.1207745, verified to
exist via Crossref this cycle; Unpaywall reports is_oa false, so it will need the
access ladder, and the author-lab route that worked three times today should be
tried first). Its replication history must be captured with it, as this base did
for Mueller and Oppenheimer. And the standing gap named in
[[the-note-taking-experiments-do-not-reach-the-slip-box-claims--202609040215]] is
unchanged and is now measured rather than assumed: a 31-page 2025 survey of what
makes knowledge durable never once uses the words note-taking, notes, external
memory or offloading.
