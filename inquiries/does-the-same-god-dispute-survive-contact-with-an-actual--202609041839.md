The base's most load-bearing distinction -- reference versus description -- is
stated in `the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942`
and carried by `anselmian-co-reference-holds-only-if-the-description-does-the-referring--202609010842`,
`the-nameless-infinite-defeats-reference-fixing-by-description--202609011541` and
`shituf-grades-the-worshipper-instead-of-identifying-the-worshipped--202609011542`.
But the base owns no note on the semantics that distinction is borrowed from. A
query for "rigid designation Kripke causal theory of reference proper names"
returns no note using the terms `kripke` or `rigid` at all, and the one place a
theory of reference appears -- Evans's dossier-dominance view in
`bogardus-and-urban-make-co-reference-turn-on-dossier-dominance--202609010838` --
is grounded, by that note's own admission, in a published abstract only.

So the base runs a philosophy-of-language argument on a second-hand summary of
one theory and no statement of its rivals. Questions to settle from primary
sources (Kripke, *Naming and Necessity*; Evans, "The Causal Theory of Names" and
*The Varieties of Reference*; Donnellan on referential vs attributive use):

1. On a causal-historical account, does "God"/"Allah" co-refer by baptismal
   chain regardless of how the dossiers diverge -- and would that make the
   dispute's descriptive half idle?
2. Does Evans's dominance criterion actually deliver "it depends", or does it
   deliver a determinate answer once the dossiers are specified?
3. Anselm's formula is a description; if reference to God runs through a causal
   chain rather than a description, does
   `maximal-greatness-has-at-most-one-bearer-so-perfect-being-worshippers-co-refer--202609010841`
   lose its force, or does it survive as a claim about descriptive fit only?
