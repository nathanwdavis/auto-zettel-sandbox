But no note compares the two articulations ON THE MERITS -- three persons versus ten sefirot, consubstantial persons versus emanations, whether the sefirot are God's essence (atzmut) or instruments (kelim), and whether either tradition's distinctions are personal. Note 202609011543 itself flags the comparison as this repository's own inference; note 202609011811 warns that from 'both traditions articulate God internally' nothing follows about whether the articulations are the same. **Progress (2026-09-03), not an answer.** This cycle established one thing
bearing on the question and failed to find the sources needed to settle it.

Established: the structural parallel is not only a Christian apologetic claim.
Leon Modena, a seventeenth-century Jewish critic of Kabbalah, reached the same
judgement from the opposite motive --- that the sefirot point to "a plurality
within God similar to the Christian doctrine of the Trinity" --- and treated it
as disqualifying (see
the-first-jewish-answer-to-christian-kabbalism-conceded-the-parallel--202609030155).
So the base now has the resemblance asserted by a hostile Jewish witness as
well as by a Christian apologist, which is corroboration that a resemblance was
perceived, from two directions with nothing in common but the perception.

NOT established, and this is why the inquiry stays open: none of that is a
comparison ON THE MERITS. Nobody in the base compares three persons with ten
sefirot, consubstantiality with emanation, or asks whether the sefirot are
God's essence (*atzmut*) or His instruments (*kelim*) --- the distinction that
note 202609011543 says would narrow the contrast sharply, and which no captured
source addresses. A perceived resemblance reported by two parties who both had
a use for it is weak evidence about the doctrines themselves.

What was tried and failed: a search for open-access comparative scholarship
returned only blogs, forums and encyclopaedia summaries --- `general-web` tier,
below what a claim this contested needs, and `lint_citations` would in any case
require three distinct verified sources for a note tagged `contested`. The
standard scholarship (Scholem, Idel, Wolfson) is in copyright and was not
reachable this cycle. One concrete lead did surface and is filed in INBOX:
Abraham Abulafia is reported to have attacked the sefirot in the thirteenth
century as no better than the Trinity, which would put a KABBALIST on the
record about the parallel four centuries before Modena --- but that report came
from a non-citable source and needs grounding in Idel's Abulafia scholarship
before any note may use it.
