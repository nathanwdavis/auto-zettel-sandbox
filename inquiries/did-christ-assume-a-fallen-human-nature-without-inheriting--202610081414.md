## 2026-10-08

Answered 2026-10-08. Yes, on two writers' terms, by two routes. Both Irving and Meyendorff give Christ a fallen human nature and deny that he is a human person, so guilt, which belongs to persons, has no subject in him. Meyendorff denies inherited guilt to everyone (what passes from Adam is mortality), so Christ needs no exemption; Irving keeps original sin passing by ordinary generation and blocks it at the manner of Christ's conception, bearing our guilt only by imputation. They differ on what 'fallen' contains: mortality and the innocent passions for Meyendorff (with John of Damascus), the evil propensities as well for Irving, which is where Westminster's 'corruption of nature' bites. Open: Meyendorff's 1987 SVTQ essay, the Greek fathers on Romans 5:12 in their own words (Theodoret is placed on opposite readings by Meyendorff and by Sanday and Headlam), Maximus, and the modern literature (INBOX 2026-10-08).
