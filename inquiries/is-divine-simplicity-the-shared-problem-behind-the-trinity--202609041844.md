The base holds all three legs of this and has never joined them.
`maimonides-denies-any-composition-in-gods-unity--202608311932` ends by naming
the challenge outright -- "any account on which the one God contains real
internal distinctions has to answer it" -- and nothing in the base answers it.
Meanwhile `whether-the-sefirot-are-gods-essence-or-his-instruments-is-a-dispute-inside-kabbalah--202609041720`
and `cordovero-settles-the-essence-vessel-question-by-doubling-the-sefirot--202609041725`
are a dispute about composition; `evangelical-theology-identifies-god-by-a-tri-personal-life--202608311940`
sets a tri-personal life against a list of attributes; and
`tantawi-extends-the-denial-of-partnership-into-the-essence-and-the-attributes--202609041125`
extends the denial of partnership "neither in His essence nor in His attributes",
which is the Islamic form of the same worry.

Yet a query for "divine simplicity and the doctrine of divine attributes" returns
no note that is about it, and the term appears nowhere in the base. Nor does the
Ash'ari/Mu'tazili dispute over whether the attributes are eternal and distinct --
`eternity` is a term the base never uses -- although that dispute is the closest
Islamic analogue to both the sefirot question and the Trinitarian one.

Work it from primaries (Aquinas, ST I q.3; Maimonides, Guide I.50-60; al-Ash'ari
and 'Abd al-Jabbar on the attributes; a Cappadocian or Augustinian statement of
how the persons are not parts) and settle whether the three traditions face one
problem with three answers, or three problems that only look alike because
"composition" is doing different work in each. This bears directly on
`abrahamic-monotheisms-divide-over-internal-distinction-not-oneness--202608311941`
and `internal-plurality-is-not-what-divides-the-monotheisms--202609011543`, which
may be understating how much the divide is about simplicity rather than plurality.
