## Answer (2026-09-03 maintenance cycle)

**The test this inquiry set was met: an existing permanent note had to change.**
[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]],
the base's most-linked note, now carries a paragraph recording that its framing
was assembled entirely from Christian and secular sources and is partial
everywhere else. It was not retracted, because the qualification runs in two
directions and only one of them goes against it.

**The reference/description axis is not foreign to Islamic material.** The
Qur'an makes a reference claim in its own voice, telling Muslims to say to the
People of the Scripture "our Allah and your Allah is One, and unto Him we
surrender" (29:46), and asking them "Dispute ye with us concerning Allah when He
is our Lord and your Lord?" (2:139) -- while elsewhere rejecting trinitarian
language outright. On the reference question as the base poses it, the Qur'an
answers yes, in as many words. See
[[the-quran-asserts-co-reference-and-difference-in-one-address--202609032315]].

**But the dichotomy is incomplete, and that is the real finding.** A Common Word
Between Us and You (2007), the open letter from 138 Muslim scholars to the
Christian churches, never asks whether the two worship the same God. It
establishes common ground in what God *commands* -- love of God, love of
neighbour -- concedes the formal differences and lets them stand, and declines
to press the shirk clause in Qur'an 3:64, the very verse it takes its title
from, routing it through al-Tabari toward religious freedom instead. The frame
has no slot for that answer. See
[[a-common-word-locates-the-shared-ground-in-command-not-reference--202609032320]].

Set beside the Jewish material, this produced an unplanned result: the Rema
grades the worshipper, A Common Word grades nobody and changes the subject.
Two traditions, two *different* third answers. "Neither reference nor
description" describes where these traditions do not go, not a shared
alternative they hold.

**The cheap, checkable piece is done.** raw/202609032255-quran-co-reference-passages.txt
adds 2:139, 3:64 and 29:46 complete, in the same Pickthall edition from the same
API as the original capture, and the Qur'an reference note records the
selection defect in its first capture explicitly rather than quietly correcting
it. raw/ is immutable, so nothing was edited; the first capture stands and the
second sits beside it.

**What this answer does NOT cover, and it is a real limit.** Candidates 2, 3 and
4 of this inquiry -- Saritoprak in his own voice, Al-Ghazali's *Faysal
al-Tafriqa*, Ibn Taymiyya's *al-Jawab al-Sahih* -- were not reached. A Common
Word is an irenic document written to find common ground, which makes it strong
evidence of what Muslim scholars would say jointly to Christians and weak
evidence of how the question is argued adversarially. The base now has a Muslim
source that declines to press the shirk objection and none that presses it. That
gap is filed as its own INBOX entry rather than left inside this closed inquiry,
and both permanent notes state the limit in their own bodies.
Filed by the 2026-09-03 gap-filling graph walk. This is a STRUCTURAL gap, not
a missing-source gap: the base's largest cluster answers a question about
Islam almost entirely in non-Muslim voices.

WHAT THE WALK FOUND. Querying the base for "How do Muslim theologians
themselves argue about whether Christians worship the same God?" matched 5
notes and returned ZERO literature notes. Every argued source it did return is
Christian or secular:

- Gjelten, "Do Christians And Muslims Worship The Same God?" — NPR journalism
  (reputable-secondary)
- Beckwith x2 — a Catholic philosopher
- Bogardus & Urban, Faith and Philosophy — analytic philosophy of religion
- Tachin, Unio Cum Christo — a REFORMED theology journal, so this is a
  Christian theologian, despite the title being the base's most direct-looking
  match for the question
- Nostra Aetate — a Catholic conciliar declaration

The base holds exactly one Islamic primary text, reference
[[quran-tawhid-passages--202608311923]], which is scripture rather than
anyone's argument. Zeki Saritoprak is NAMED in
[[shared-abrahamic-descent-does-not-settle-the-reference-question--202608311943]]
and in the Wheaton literature note, but only secondhand, as a person quoted in
the NPR piece — there is no reference note for him and nothing of his was
captured.

Contrast the Jewish side, which this base argues from INSIDE its own tradition
across six captured sources: Maimonides 202608311922, Ramban 202609011530,
Shulchan Arukh with the Rema's gloss 202609011532, Tiqqunei ha-Zohar
202609011531, Sefer Yetzirah 202609011825, and Dweck on Modena 202609030150.
That asymmetry is the finding. The same-God cluster is 26 permanent notes and
it is the largest thing this base has built.

A SPECIFIC, CHEAP, CHECKABLE PIECE OF IT. The Qur'an capture
(raw/202608311923-...) holds Surah 112, 4:171 and 5:73. All three mark
DIFFERENCE — divine oneness, "say not 'Three'", and "they surely disbelieve
who say: Allah is the third of three". The capture does NOT hold 29:46, which
is the most on-point verse in the Qur'an for this exact question and which
runs the other way: "our God and your God is One, and unto Him we surrender."
A capture assembled to bear on the same-God question that includes the three
rejecting verses and omits the affirming one is selection-shaped, whether or
not it was meant that way, and every note resting on it inherits that shape.
raw/ is immutable, so this needs a NEW capture, not an edit; the same
public-domain Pickthall edition via the AlQuran Cloud API that produced the
first one will do, and the existing reference note can gain a second
excerpt_capture rather than being rewritten.

CANDIDATE SOURCES, in rough order of expected reachability:
1. "A Common Word Between Us and You" (2007), the open letter from 138 Muslim
   scholars to Christian leaders, full text at acommonword.com. Openly
   published, directly on point, and collective rather than idiosyncratic.
2. Zeki Saritoprak — already named in the base secondhand; find something he
   actually wrote.
3. Al-Ghazali, Faysal al-Tafriqa bayna al-Islam wa-l-Zandaqa (the criterion
   distinguishing Islam from unbelief), which is the classical text on who
   counts as outside the faith and why.
4. Ibn Taymiyya, al-Jawab al-Sahih li-man baddala din al-Masih — the major
   classical Muslim response to Christianity, and a hostile witness, which is
   useful for the same reason Modena was useful on the Jewish side: it argues
   the question rather than smoothing it.

WHAT WOULD COUNT AS ANSWERING THIS. Not "add a Muslim source". The test is
whether any existing permanent note has to change. Specifically:
[[the-same-god-question-turns-on-reference-versus-description--202608311942]]
is the single most-linked note in the entire base (in-degree 12) and its four
sources are Gjelten, Nostra Aetate, Tachin and Vroom — no Muslim among them.
If the reference/description framing is one that Muslim theologians do not
actually use, that note is a Christian-philosophical frame presented as the
neutral shape of the dispute, and the run should say so plainly rather than
adding a corroborating footnote.
