Asked by the user (2026-09-01). Candidate common ground to examine: both rest on general revelation (creation as a channel of knowledge of God, Rom 1:19-20, Ps 19); teleological arguments are the inferential form of what the knowability doctrine asserts dogmatically; both deliver only a 'thin' natural knowledge (power, wisdom, deity - not Trinity or redemption), connecting to [[beckwith-locates-co-reference-in-general-revelation--202609010837]] and the Anselm cluster; and the doctrine's innate/acquired distinction (Calvin's sensus divinitatis vs demonstration) locates where design arguments fit and where they are contested.

**Answer (2026-09-01):** Three common elements, one per result note. (1) One
channel: both are built on general revelation -- the doctrine asserts the works
are legible (Calvin I.v, Hodge ST I ch. IV, Rom 1:19-20 / Ps 19), and the
design argument's minor premise ("the world everywhere exhibits marks of
design") is that same assertion doing inferential work. (2) One content, two
offices: the classic Reformed statements hold the knowledge as innate/possessed
(Hodge: "not due to a Process of Reasoning"; Calvin's seed of religion) while
still stating the teleological syllogism -- the arguments vindicate and
articulate rather than originate. (3) One limit: natural knowledge is thin on
both routes for the same reason -- works disclose maker-attributes ("eternal
power and Godhead", inexcusability not salvation), matching the design
argument's maker-not-one-maker gap; both hand off to special revelation for
identity. Grounded in verbatim captures of Calvin (Institutes I.iii, I.v, Allen
trans.), Hodge (ST I, three excerpts), and Rom 1:18-21 / Ps 19:1-4 (KJV).
