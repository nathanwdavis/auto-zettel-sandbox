Follow-up to inquiry 202609011030 and permanent note design-arguments-can-confirm-belief-they-do-not-ground--202609011510, which states the confirm/ground distinction abstractly from Plantinga but carries no concrete case. Candidate case: the Hoyle state (the 7.65 MeV excited state of carbon-12 predicted by Hoyle in 1953), where the discoverer's own reaction, a believing physicist's use of the same evidence, and the later lattice-QCD reassessment of how fine the tuning actually is together separate confirming from grounding.

**Answer (2026-09-02):** Confirming and grounding are different relations.
Grounding is a relation between a person's belief and what it rests on;
confirming is a relation between evidence and a hypothesis — evidence confirms
when it raises the hypothesis's probability, which is compatible with the
result staying low, with the confirmation being defeasible, and with the
argument's conclusion being weaker than the belief. So one argument can be a
genuine confirmer and nobody's foundation. The Hoyle state is the worked case,
in three parts, one per result note. (1) It does not deliver the content:
Hoyle predicted the 7.65 MeV level, read the design inference off it at
ceiling confidence ("almost beyond question"), and terminated in "an enormous
intelligence abroad in the universe" — an agent within the cosmos — never in
God. (2) Its confirming use is issued as non-coercive: Polkinghorne states the
aim as "not logical coerciveness but insightful understanding" and names the
exits (happy accident, or multiverse) rather than closing them. (3) Its weight
is revisable and has in fact been revised — Livio et al.'s ~60 keV tolerance,
Oberhummer et al.'s ~0.5% window, then the lattice recomputation's ~2% in mq,
with error bars "compatible with a vanishing" derivative, i.e. with no
fine-tuning at all — and no believer's confidence tracked those moves, which is
what an increment rather than a foundation predicts. Grounded in bounded
captures of Hoyle (Engineering and Science, Nov. 1981, p. 12, verified against
a rendered page image because the PDF is an OCR'd scan), Polkinghorne (Faraday
Paper No. 1, 2007, pp. 1-3), and Epelbaum et al. (Phys. Rev. Lett. 110, 2013,
pp. 1, 3-4). Left open, in INBOX: Kragh's history of the resonance's anthropic
reading, which the Letter cites but does not summarise.
